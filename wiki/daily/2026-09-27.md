## 1. Batched Contextual Reinforcement: A Task-Scaling Law for Efficient Reasoning

**年**: 2026 | **Tier**: 1 | **分野**: tech

**Key Insight**: 共有コンテキスト内で複数タスクを並行処理する際、タスク数の増加に伴い、タスクあたりの消費リソースが単調減少しつつ高い処理精度を維持する「タスクスケーリング則」が存在する。

### Abstract

Large Language Models employing Chain-of-Thought reasoning achieve strong performance but suffer from excessive token consumption that inflates inference costs. Existing efficiency methods such as explicit length penalties, difficulty estimators, or multi-stage curricula either degrade reasoning quality or require complex training pipelines. We introduce Batched Contextual Reinforcement, a minimalist, single-stage training paradigm that unlocks efficient reasoning through a simple structural modification: training the model to solve N problems simultaneously within a shared context window, rewarded purely by per-instance accuracy. This formulation creates an implicit token budget that yields several key findings: (1) We identify a novel task-scaling law: as the number of concurrent problems N increases during inference, per-problem token usage decreases monotonically while accuracy degrades far more gracefully than baselines, establishing N as a controllable throughput dimension. (2) BCR challenges the traditional accuracy-efficiency trade-off by demonstrating a "free lunch" phenomenon at standard single-problem inference. Across both 1.5B and 4B model families, BCR reduces token usage by 15.8% to 62.6% while consistently maintaining or improving accuracy across five major mathematical benchmarks. (3) Qualitative analyses reveal emergent self-regulated efficiency, where models autonomously eliminate redundant metacognitive loops without explicit length supervision. (4) Crucially, we empirically demonstrate that implicit budget constraints successfully circumvent the adversarial gradients and catastrophic optimization collapse inherent to explicit length penalties, offering a highly stable, constraint-based alternative for length control. These results prove BCR practical, showing simple structural incentives unlock latent high-density reasoning in LLMs.

## 2. Coexistence after Preference Change: Reciprocal Standing and the Limits of Self-Validating Assent

**年**: 2026 | **被引用**: 2 | **Tier**: 1 | **分野**: systems_engineering

**Key Insight**: 現在の同意は過去の変化を遡及的に正当化できず、支配関係の形成による選好変化の問題は消滅しない

### Abstract

What would make human–AI coexistence more than a stable arrangement whose participants have been made to approve it? This paper examines that question under a conditional prospect of transformative power: the capacity to shape another subject's preferences, practical capacities, and opportunities to question a shared arrangement. It does not assume that present AI systems are conscious or that superintelligence is imminent. Drawing on work on consent, adaptive preferences, non-domination, and artificial servitude, it develops paired thought experiments in which sincere assent remains compatible with importantly different relations. The argument separates three judgments: the permissibility of an earlier transformation, the standing and present choices of the resulting subject, and the justification of an ongoing relationship. These judgments cannot be inferred from one another merely because the subject now approves. A dormant mechanism for excluding future dissent can leave a particular act of consent intact while undermining reciprocal coexistence; a wrongful transformation can leave a present subject whose wish not to be restored deserves consideration. Applying the same reasoning in both directions prevents human cognitive vulnerability from licensing paternalism and artificial origin from licensing permanent instrumental subordination. Creation, copying, emergency restraint, and mutually chosen change test the limits of the account. A critical reading of the Trinity Accord then locates the value of early questions in preserving a defeasible point of challenge, not privileged authorship or a mandate over future minds. The contribution is a bounded account of reciprocal standing under preference change: later assent cannot alone vindicate its production, earlier wrong cannot alone dispossess the present subject, and present agreement cannot alone close the relationship to future claims. ## 3. Reinforcement Learning: A Survey

**著者**: Leslie Pack Kaelbling, Michael L. Littman, Andrew Moore | **年**: 1996 | **被引用**: 8768 | **Tier**: 1 | **分野**: operations_management

**Key Insight**: エージェントが未知の動的環境との相互作用を通じて累積報酬を最大化するための、探索と開発のトレードオフおよび遅延報酬問題に対処する数理的・アルゴリズム的基礎理論。

### Abstract

This paper surveys the field of reinforcement learning from a computer-science perspective. It is written to be accessible to researchers familiar with machine learning. Both the historical basis of the field and a broad selection of current work are summarized. Reinforcement learning is the problem faced by an agent that learns behavior through trial-and-error interactions with a dynamic environment. The work described here has a resemblance to work in psychology, but differs considerably in the details and in the use of the word ``reinforcement.'' The paper discusses central issues of reinforcement learning, including trading off exploration and exploitation, establishing the foundations of the field via Markov decision theory, learning from delayed reinforcement, constructing empirical models to accelerate learning, making use of generalization and hierarchy, and coping with hidden state. It concludes with a survey of some implemented systems and an assessment of the practical utility of current methods for reinforcement learning.
