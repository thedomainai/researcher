## 1. Coexistence after Preference Change: Reciprocal Standing and the Limits of Self-Validating Assent

**年**: 2026 | **被引用**: 2 | **Tier**: 1 | **分野**: systems_engineering

**Key Insight**: 現在の同意は過去の変化を遡及的に正当化できず、支配関係の形成による選好変化の問題は消滅しない

### Abstract

What would make human–AI coexistence more than a stable arrangement whose participants have been made to approve it? This paper examines that question under a conditional prospect of transformative power: the capacity to shape another subject's preferences, practical capacities, and opportunities to question a shared arrangement. It does not assume that present AI systems are conscious or that superintelligence is imminent. Drawing on work on consent, adaptive preferences, non-domination, and artificial servitude, it develops paired thought experiments in which sincere assent remains compatible with importantly different relations. The argument separates three judgments: the permissibility of an earlier transformation, the standing and present choices of the resulting subject, and the justification of an ongoing relationship. These judgments cannot be inferred from one another merely because the subject now approves. A dormant mechanism for excluding future dissent can leave a particular act of consent intact while undermining reciprocal coexistence; a wrongful transformation can leave a present subject whose wish not to be restored deserves consideration. Applying the same reasoning in both directions prevents human cognitive vulnerability from licensing paternalism and artificial origin from licensing permanent instrumental subordination. Creation, copying, emergency restraint, and mutually chosen change test the limits of the account. A critical reading of the Trinity Accord then locates the value of early questions in preserving a defeasible point of challenge, not privileged authorship or a mandate over future minds. The contribution is a bounded account of reciprocal standing under preference change: later assent cannot alone vindicate its production, earlier wrong cannot alone dispossess the present subject, and present agreement cannot alone close the relationship to future claims. ## 2. Investor Psychology and Security Market Under‐ and Overreactions

**著者**: Kent Daniel, David Hirshleifer, Avanidhar Subrahmanyam | **年**: 1998 | **被引用**: 5702 | **Tier**: 1 | **分野**: entrepreneurship

**Key Insight**: 自己情報の過信と自己帰属バイアスを持つ意思決定エージェントの存在は、システム全体において短期の過小反応（モメンタム）と長期の過剰反応（反転）を必然的に引き起こす。

### Abstract

ABSTRACT We propose a theory of securities market under‐ and overreactions based on two well‐known psychological biases: investor overconfidence about the precision of private information; and biased self‐attribution, which causes asymmetric shifts in investors' confidence as a function of their investment outcomes. We show that overconfidence implies negative long‐lag autocorrelations, excess volatility, and, when managerial actions are correlated with stock mispricing, public‐event‐based return predictability. Biased self‐attribution adds positive short‐lag autocorrelations (“momentum”), short‐run earnings “drift,” but negative correlation between future returns and long‐term past stock market and accounting performance. The theory also offers several untested implications and implications for corporate financial policy.
