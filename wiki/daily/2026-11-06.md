## 1. 久那斗 Kunato Core Ver.3.0.0: Truth-Preserving Deliberation, Refutation Persistence, and Epistemic Boundary Control

**年**: 2026 | **Tier**: 1 | **分野**: religious_studies

**Key Insight**: 真理保持と反駁可能性を両立させる形式的枠組み：マルチエージェント推論システムの基礎原理

### Abstract

Abstract We present Kunato Core Ver.3.0.0, a formal framework for truth-preserving deliberation, refutation persistence, grounded justification, dependency-safe rollback, and epistemic boundary control. Kunato v3 addresses a general problem in scientific reasoning, artificial intelligence, hypothesis management, and multi-agent deliberation: how a reasoning system can preserve constraints already established by semantics, evidence, refutation, and dependency analysis while remaining open to new claims and legitimate revision. The framework treats accepted knowledge as a protected epistemic state and separates proposition generation from authority to modify that state. Its governing rule is: no valid warrant, no epistemic state transition. Kunato distinguishes linguistic expressibility from model-theoretic constructibility. Given background-compatible worlds B and a candidate model set M(h), a hypothesis h is constructible only when B intersect M(h) is nonempty. Linguistic expression therefore does not by itself establish possibility. Semantic terms are versioned so that changes in definition constitute explicit semantic revisions rather than silent reinterpretations of previously evaluated claims. Under monotone evidence accumulation, the surviving model set S_t(h) is nonincreasing: S_(t+1)(h) is a subset of S_t(h). Once S_t(h) becomes empty, additional monotone evidence cannot revive the refuted hypothesis. Reopening instead requires an explicit revision of evidence, background constraints, semantic representation, or test validity, and reassessment does not itself constitute renewed support. This establishes a formal distinction between evidence accumulation and belief revision while preserving previously earned refutations under unchanged conditions. Kunato v3 implements a zero-trust epistemic architecture. External humans, language models, software agents, documents, and other generators may submit claims but cannot self-certify admission-critical properties such as constructibility, refutation status, independent support, semantic compatibility, or conflict status. These properties are derived by the verifier from declared registries. Accepted-state transitions require context-bound write certificates; stale, forged, context-mismatched, or proposition-mismatched authorization fails closed. Source identity alone provides no epistemic privilege when semantic and evidential conditions are otherwise identical. Support is represented through explicit alternative support sets and evaluated using grounded least-fixed-point semantics. Declared observations, discharged proof obligations, or trusted axiomatic roots initiate grounded support, whereas pure circular support cannot bootstrap epistemic authority. When premises are invalidated, a dependent claim remains justified if and only if at least one complete support set remains disjoint from the invalidated premises. Independently supported conclusions therefore survive rollback, while unsupported and false remain formally distinct categories. The framework establishes false-root closure and premise-laundering results. If every complete support path for a dependent closure ultimately contains the same invalid premise, invalidation of that premise eliminates all recorded justification within the closure. The worst-case rollback radius is unbounded as the dependency graph grows. Moreover, adding downstream mathematics, simulations, terminology, citations, intermediate propositions, computational complexity, or rhetorical elaboration cannot repair a support path that still contains an invalid premise. Only a genuinely independent support path can restore justified inferential authority. This yields a minimal stopping rule for reasoning branches whose indispensable roots have been invalidated. At the deliberative boundary, Kunato separates participation from epistemic write authority. Any unrestricted channel capable of bypassing verification makes the authorization invariant impossible to guarantee. Conversely, given sound declared roots, an intact verifier and certificate mechanism, complete mediation, and the specified transition function, the authorization invariant is preserved across every finite reachable transition sequence. A sequence of external inputs that never reaches ACCEPTED status has zero effect on the accepted-signature set. The framework therefore controls epistemic state transitions without requiring classification of participants by identity, intention, belief, religion, nationality, moral character, or presumed honesty. This architecture reaches an explicit fixed point rather than asserting absolute self-grounding. Any finite verification chain must ultimately terminate in trusted roots or become circular. Kunato therefore states its strongest guarantee conditionally: sound roots + intact verifier + complete mediation -> authorization invariant preserved. This fixed point also identifies the irreducible boundary of the framework. Internal authorization integrity does not imply ontological truth if trusted semantic or evidential roots are false, corrupted, or inadequate. Authenticated provenance is not equivalent to truth, and an arbitrarily mutable or bypassable verifier cannot guarantee accepted-core integrity. Kunato v3 therefore does not claim to be a truth oracle, a universal natural-language semantic compiler, or a detector of deceptive persons or intentions. Its formal objective is narrower: to prevent unsupported claims from acquiring epistemic authority within a protected reasoning state. The reference implementation computationally audits constructibility, evidence contraction, refutation persistence, self-attestation exclusion, source-identity independence, semantic drift, conflict quarantine, certificate integrity, grounded support, dependency-safe rollback, false-root propagation, premise-laundering resistance, fail-closed authorization, adversarial-stream noninterference, transition-invariant preservation, and the evidence-root boundary. The reported verification includes 34,782 dependency-safe rollback cases, 1,024 premise-laundering extensions, a 1,000-input non-accepted adversarial stream, and finite false-root dependency trees, with no recorded audit failures. Machine-readable results are reproducible byte-for-byte, including the terminal newline, across the tested Python hash seeds. Kunato Core Ver.3.0.0 thus defines an epistemic security kernel for scientific reasoning, AI-assisted research, automated hypothesis management, truth-maintenance systems, belief revision, knowledge-base integrity, and human-AI or multi-agent deliberation. Its fixed point is not absolute truth certification, but preservation of justified epistemic state relative to explicit semantic and evidential roots. The resulting operational principle is concise: preserve meaning, verify premises, retain valid refutations, reject self-certified authority, prune invalid roots, preserve independent support, and reason only over the grounded residual structure.

## 2. Adaptation in Natural and Artificial Systems

**著者**: John H. Holland | **年**: 1992 | **被引用**: 35555 | **Tier**: 1 | **分野**: complexity_science

**Key Insight**: 非線形な環境における適応は、スキーマの再結合と並行表現による選択（遺伝的アルゴリズム）を通じて、効率的な探索と利用のバランスを最適化するプロセスとして定式化できる。

### Abstract

Genetic algorithms are playing an increasingly important role in studies of complex adaptive systems, ranging from adaptive agents in economic theory to the use of machine learning techniques in the design of complex devices such as aircraft turbines and integrated circuits. Adaptation in Natural and Artificial Systems is the book that initiated this field of study, presenting the theoretical foundations and exploring applications. In its most familiar form, adaptation is a biological process, whereby organisms evolve by rearranging genetic material to survive in environments confronting them. In this now classic work, Holland presents a mathematical model that allows for the nonlinearity of such complex interactions. He demonstrates the model's universality by applying it to economics, physiological psychology, game theory, and artificial intelligence and then outlines the way in which this approach modifies the traditional views of mathematical genetics. Initially applying his concepts to simply defined artificial systems with limited numbers of parameters, Holland goes on to explore their use in the study of a wide range of complex, naturally occuring processes, concentrating on systems having multiple factors that interact in nonlinear ways. Along the way he accounts for major effects of coadaptation and coevolution: the emergence of building blocks, or schemata, that are recombined and passed on to succeeding generations to provide, innovations and improvements. Bradford Books imprint

## 3. Dissecting racial bias in an algorithm used to manage the health of populations

**著者**: Ziad Obermeyer, Brian W. Powers, Christine Vogeli, Sendhil Mullainathan | **年**: 2019 | **被引用**: 5641 | **Tier**: 1 | **分野**: law

**Key Insight**: 客観的ニーズの代替（プロキシ）としてリソース消費量（コスト）を学習目標に設定すると、システム本来の目的を歪める致命的なバイアスが発生する。

### Abstract

Racial bias in health algorithms The U.S. health care system uses commercial algorithms to guide health decisions. Obermeyer et al. find evidence of racial bias in one widely used algorithm, such that Black patients assigned the same level of risk by the algorithm are sicker than White patients (see the Perspective by Benjamin). The authors estimated that this racial bias reduces the number of Black patients identified for extra care by more than half. Bias occurs because the algorithm uses health costs as a proxy for health needs. Less money is spent on Black patients who have the same level of need, and the algorithm thus falsely concludes that Black patients are healthier than equally sick White patients. Reformulating the algorithm so that it no longer uses costs as a proxy for needs eliminates the racial bias in predicting who needs extra care. Science , this issue p. 447 ; see also p. 421
