## 2. Coexistence after Preference Change: Reciprocal Standing and the Limits of Self-Validating Assent

**年**: 2026 | **被引用**: 2 | **Tier**: 1 | **分野**: systems_engineering

**Key Insight**: 現在の同意は過去の変化を遡及的に正当化できず、支配関係の形成による選好変化の問題は消滅しない

### Abstract

What would make human–AI coexistence more than a stable arrangement whose participants have been made to approve it? This paper examines that question under a conditional prospect of transformative power: the capacity to shape another subject's preferences, practical capacities, and opportunities to question a shared arrangement. It does not assume that present AI systems are conscious or that superintelligence is imminent. Drawing on work on consent, adaptive preferences, non-domination, and artificial servitude, it develops paired thought experiments in which sincere assent remains compatible with importantly different relations. The argument separates three judgments: the permissibility of an earlier transformation, the standing and present choices of the resulting subject, and the justification of an ongoing relationship. These judgments cannot be inferred from one another merely because the subject now approves. A dormant mechanism for excluding future dissent can leave a particular act of consent intact while undermining reciprocal coexistence; a wrongful transformation can leave a present subject whose wish not to be restored deserves consideration. Applying the same reasoning in both directions prevents human cognitive vulnerability from licensing paternalism and artificial origin from licensing permanent instrumental subordination. Creation, copying, emergency restraint, and mutually chosen change test the limits of the account. A critical reading of the Trinity Accord then locates the value of early questions in preserving a defeasible point of challenge, not privileged authorship or a mandate over future minds. The contribution is a bounded account of reciprocal standing under preference change: later assent cannot alone vindicate its production, earlier wrong cannot alone dispossess the present subject, and present agreement cannot alone close the relationship to future claims. ## 3. Gradient-based learning applied to document recognition

**著者**: Yann LeCun, Léon Bottou, Yoshua Bengio, Patrick Haffner | **年**: 1998 | **被引用**: 57486 | **Tier**: 1 | **分野**: finance_corporate

**Key Insight**: 空間的対称性と局所的接続性という幾何学的制約をネットワーク構造に埋め込むことで、高次元データからの効率的な特徴抽出と汎化が可能になる。

### Abstract

Multilayer neural networks trained with the back-propagation algorithm constitute the best example of a successful gradient based learning technique. Given an appropriate network architecture, gradient-based learning algorithms can be used to synthesize a complex decision surface that can classify high-dimensional patterns, such as handwritten characters, with minimal preprocessing. This paper reviews various methods applied to handwritten character recognition and compares them on a standard handwritten digit recognition task. Convolutional neural networks, which are specifically designed to deal with the variability of 2D shapes, are shown to outperform all other techniques. Real-life document recognition systems are composed of multiple modules including field extraction, segmentation recognition, and language modeling. A new learning paradigm, called graph transformer networks (GTN), allows such multimodule systems to be trained globally using gradient-based methods so as to minimize an overall performance measure. Two systems for online handwriting recognition are described. Experiments demonstrate the advantage of global training, and the flexibility of graph transformer networks. A graph transformer network for reading a bank cheque is also described. It uses convolutional neural network character recognizers combined with global training techniques to provide record accuracy on business and personal cheques. It is deployed commercially and reads several million cheques per day.
