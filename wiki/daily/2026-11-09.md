## 1. Coexistence after Preference Change: Reciprocal Standing and the Limits of Self-Validating Assent

**年**: 2026 | **被引用**: 2 | **Tier**: 1 | **分野**: systems_engineering

**Key Insight**: 現在の同意は過去の変化を遡及的に正当化できず、支配関係の形成による選好変化の問題は消滅しない

### Abstract

What would make human–AI coexistence more than a stable arrangement whose participants have been made to approve it? This paper examines that question under a conditional prospect of transformative power: the capacity to shape another subject's preferences, practical capacities, and opportunities to question a shared arrangement. It does not assume that present AI systems are conscious or that superintelligence is imminent. Drawing on work on consent, adaptive preferences, non-domination, and artificial servitude, it develops paired thought experiments in which sincere assent remains compatible with importantly different relations. The argument separates three judgments: the permissibility of an earlier transformation, the standing and present choices of the resulting subject, and the justification of an ongoing relationship. These judgments cannot be inferred from one another merely because the subject now approves. A dormant mechanism for excluding future dissent can leave a particular act of consent intact while undermining reciprocal coexistence; a wrongful transformation can leave a present subject whose wish not to be restored deserves consideration. Applying the same reasoning in both directions prevents human cognitive vulnerability from licensing paternalism and artificial origin from licensing permanent instrumental subordination. Creation, copying, emergency restraint, and mutually chosen change test the limits of the account. A critical reading of the Trinity Accord then locates the value of early questions in preserving a defeasible point of challenge, not privileged authorship or a mandate over future minds. The contribution is a bounded account of reciprocal standing under preference change: later assent cannot alone vindicate its production, earlier wrong cannot alone dispossess the present subject, and present agreement cannot alone close the relationship to future claims. ## 2. Artificial Intelligence: the global landscape of ethics guidelines

**著者**: Anna Jobin, M. Ienca, E. Vayena | **年**: 2019 | **被引用**: 2368 | **Tier**: 1 | **分野**: ai_governance

**Key Insight**: 規範やガイドラインの策定において、高次の基本原則（透明性・公正性等）ではグローバルな合意が形成されやすい一方、その具体的解釈や実装方法、優先順位においては不可避的に深刻な分岐が発生する。

### Abstract

In the last five years, private companies, research institutions as well as public sector organisations have issued principles and guidelines for ethical AI, yet there is debate about both what constitutes "ethical AI" and which ethical requirements, technical standards and best practices are needed for its realization. To investigate whether a global agreement on these questions is emerging, we mapped and analyzed the current corpus of principles and guidelines on ethical AI. Our results reveal a global convergence emerging around five ethical principles (transparency, justice and fairness, non-maleficence, responsibility and privacy), with substantive divergence in relation to how these principles are interpreted; why they are deemed important; what issue, domain or actors they pertain to; and how they should be implemented. Our findings highlight the importance of integrating guideline-development efforts with substantive ethical analysis and adequate implementation strategies.

## 3. Dissecting racial bias in an algorithm used to manage the health of populations

**著者**: Ziad Obermeyer, Brian W. Powers, Christine Vogeli, Sendhil Mullainathan | **年**: 2019 | **被引用**: 5641 | **Tier**: 1 | **分野**: law

**Key Insight**: 客観的ニーズの代替（プロキシ）としてリソース消費量（コスト）を学習目標に設定すると、システム本来の目的を歪める致命的なバイアスが発生する。

### Abstract

Racial bias in health algorithms The U.S. health care system uses commercial algorithms to guide health decisions. Obermeyer et al. find evidence of racial bias in one widely used algorithm, such that Black patients assigned the same level of risk by the algorithm are sicker than White patients (see the Perspective by Benjamin). The authors estimated that this racial bias reduces the number of Black patients identified for extra care by more than half. Bias occurs because the algorithm uses health costs as a proxy for health needs. Less money is spent on Black patients who have the same level of need, and the algorithm thus falsely concludes that Black patients are healthier than equally sick White patients. Reformulating the algorithm so that it no longer uses costs as a proxy for needs eliminates the racial bias in predicting who needs extra care. Science , this issue p. 447 ; see also p. 421

## 5. Reinforcement Learning: A Survey

**著者**: Leslie Pack Kaelbling, Michael L. Littman, Andrew Moore | **年**: 1996 | **被引用**: 8768 | **Tier**: 1 | **分野**: operations_management

**Key Insight**: エージェントが未知の動的環境との相互作用を通じて累積報酬を最大化するための、探索と開発のトレードオフおよび遅延報酬問題に対処する数理的・アルゴリズム的基礎理論。

### Abstract

This paper surveys the field of reinforcement learning from a computer-science perspective. It is written to be accessible to researchers familiar with machine learning. Both the historical basis of the field and a broad selection of current work are summarized. Reinforcement learning is the problem faced by an agent that learns behavior through trial-and-error interactions with a dynamic environment. The work described here has a resemblance to work in psychology, but differs considerably in the details and in the use of the word ``reinforcement.'' The paper discusses central issues of reinforcement learning, including trading off exploration and exploitation, establishing the foundations of the field via Markov decision theory, learning from delayed reinforcement, constructing empirical models to accelerate learning, making use of generalization and hierarchy, and coping with hidden state. It concludes with a survey of some implemented systems and an assessment of the practical utility of current methods for reinforcement learning.
