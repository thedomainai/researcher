# PEAR: A Knowledge-guided Autonomous Pipeline for Ptychography Enabled by Large Language Models

**Authors**: Xiangyu Yin, Chuqiao Shi, Junjing Deng, Yimo Han, Yi Jiang | **Year**: 2024 | **Cited by**: 3 | **Kind**: article | **Relevance**: agents: supporting

## Abstract

The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) has catalyzed significant progress in autonomous experimentation and data analysis within the field of microscopy. Recently, large language models (LLMs) have attracted great research interests due to the popularity of OpenAI’s ChatGPT platform [1]. LLMs, which are neural network models trained on extensive datasets, demonstrate “intelligent” capabilities including comprehensive knowledge, common sense, and basic logics [2]. They can be easily adapted to many natural language processing tasks such as search, summarization, reasoning, and planning. Recent breakthroughs have further expanded their utility by enabling the integration of specialized knowledge, tools, and additional modalities (e.g., images). These attributes are particularly beneficial for computational imaging techniques such as ptychography, where the quality of images is heavily influenced by the selection of experimental and reconstruction parameters. Previous works have utilized Bayesian optimization with Gaussian processes to facilitate automatic parameter tuning and experimental designs [3,4]. However, the approach can be less efficient in more routine tasks for experienced researchers whose existing knowledge can guide them obtaining useful results within a few reconstructions. Here we report our next-generational workflow named “Ptychographic Experiment and Analysis Robot” (PEAR), which mimics human experts’ thought process and automates ptychography based on domain knowledge embedded within multiple customized LLMs or AI agents. As illustrated in Figure 1, PEAR interacts with users through natural language in the form similar to a ChatGPT’s prompt. When a user initializes a reconstruction task (Figure 1a), the ptychography agent starts a conversation to gather basic information such as sample type, beam voltage, and probe defocus (Figure 1b). This agent is a customized LLM with a comprehensive knowledge base of electron/X-ray microscopy, ptychography, reconstruction techniques, and even instrument-specific information. Based on user’s inputs, the ptychography agent further locate data path, determine reconstruction parameters, and pass them to the coding agent, which automatically generates a reconstruction script (Figure 1c) and carry out the corresponding reconstruction (Figure 1d). Next, the diagnosis agent performs a set of evaluations and describe any artifacts or issues in the results (Figure 1e). Lastly, the ptychography agent utilizes the feedback and attempts to suggest a better reconstruction strategy to the coding agent (Figure 1f). Steps c-f are repeated until PEAR obtains the best reconstruction. All intermediate results, diagnosis reports, and AI’s “thought process”, are visible to users during the entire process. Figure 2 shows selected ptychographic reconstructions of 2D SnSe materials generated by PEAR. The workflow first processed a dataset acquired with a focused probe and determined that the reconstructed object (Figure 2a) has poor quality due to insufficient probe overlap. PEAR then obtained a better image (Figure 2b) from a different dataset acquired with a defocused probe. After exploring more advanced techniques, the workflow outputs its best results (Figure 2c) by using multislice ptychography and the detector de-blurring method. These choices are in good agreement with experienced scientists. To protect users’ privacy and ensure consistent performance, PEAR is designed to utilize local computing resources and knowledge repository. We also released a single-agent version of PEAR in the form of a custom ChatGPT named “foldsliceGPT”[5], which contains most of PEAR’s knowledge and serves as an AI assistant for the fold_slice fork [6] of the PtychoShelves package [7] . The GPT leverages OpenAI’s web platform for textual conversation and code execution, thus providing basic features including script generation, experimental designs, simulation, and rule-based diagnosis for X-ray/electron ptychography [8].
