# Anatomical Review of "CAST: Game Solvers as Turn-Level Teachers for LLM Agents" Epistemological, Ontological and Comparative Perspectives on Solver-Guided Credit Assignment in Long-Horizon Reinforcement Learning for Language Agents

**Authors**: Syed Muntasir Mamun | **Year**: 2026 | **Cited by**: 0 | **Kind**: article | **Relevance**: post_training: supporting

## Abstract

K SC This anatomical review dissects the CAST framework (Wang et al., 2026), which converts changes in a game solver's state value into turn-level advantages that are injected into reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for large language model (LLM) agents. The paper is examined chapter by chapter. Core postulates, hypotheses, empirical tests and results are refined; strengths and limitations are catalogued; the contribution is situated against leading theories of credit assignment, process supervision, on-policy distillation and potential-based reward shaping. Epistemological and ontological assessments are offered, together with projections of future research trajectories in solver-guided and value-network-guided agent training. The review concludes that CAST constitutes a theoretically grounded and practically efficient advance in turn-level credit assignment, while remaining constrained by the availability and quality of domain solvers or approximate value functions.
