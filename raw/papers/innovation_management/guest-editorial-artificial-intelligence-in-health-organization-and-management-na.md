## Abstract

Artificial intelligence in health organization and management: navigating innovation and transformationArtificial intelligence (AI) has moved from conceptual possibility to practical reality in health care.Across health organizations and management, AI is no longer limited to experimental domains; it now informs everything from patient scheduling and supply chain logistics to clinical decision support and population health analytics.The impetus behind this special issue of the Journal of Health Organization and Management lies in the recognition that AI is a sociotechnical innovation -a confluence of algorithms, data, human expertise and organizational cultures -that challenges established paradigms and brings forth new questions about efficiency, equity, workforce dynamics, policy and ethics.The special issue presents a diverse suite of contributions that collectively illuminate the multifaceted impact of AI.Several papers address the operational and analytical dimensions of AI.Other contributions focus on risk management and resilience as well as papers examining the future of precision oncology and AI in Belgium, which outline scenarios and policy responses, illustrating the interaction between technological potential and strategic governance.Several papers interrogate the human and organizational dimensions of AI adoption and the sociotechnical perspective on professional adaptation and training in the age of AI offers insight into how roles are redefined when AI becomes part of the workforce, while a psychometric evaluation of a Turkish instrument measuring perceptions of AI in healthcare provides tools for assessing readiness and attitudes.Assessing AI literacy and attitudes among medical students emphasizes the need for future professionals to develop a critical understanding of AI to integrate it effectively into practice.The issue also includes forward-looking perspectives that situate AI within broader technological trends.These diverse issues covered in this special issue speak to the depth and richness of the research in this still embryonic yet burgeoning field.Collectively, these papers highlight that AI is not a monolithic technology but a spectrum of tools and practices embedded in varied contexts.AI in health organization and management is framed in contemporary literature as a vehicle for both transformation and tension.On one hand, AI promises enhancements in diagnostic accuracy, personalized treatment, administrative efficiency and operational performance.It enables clinicians to draw insights from vast datasets, supports real-time monitoring and automates routine tasks, thus freeing up human resources for more patient-centred activities.For instance, analyses have shown that integrating big data, analytics, AI and machine learning can improve operational effectiveness, lower expenses and foster scalable, resilient healthcare systems.AI-driven revenue cycle management automates billing and coding, reduces errors, speeds up claims processing and stabilizes finances.In supply chain management, AI supports demand forecasting and inventory optimization, reducing waste and ensuring timely availability of medical supplies.AI also facilitates patient engagement and remote monitoring through wearables and telemedicine platforms, enabling early detection of health issues and improving continuity of care.At the same time, the literature warns us that the integration of AI in health organizations is fraught with ethical concerns, sociotechnical complexities and implementation challenges.Ethical issues include data privacy, algorithmic bias and transparency.AI systems rely on vast datasets containing sensitive health information; inadequate protections risk
