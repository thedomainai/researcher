# Enhancing mass transport in redox flow batteries with 3D-printed triply periodic minimal surface electrode structures

**著者**: Maxime van der Heijden, M. Barzegari, J. Aarts, B. Liu, Antoni Forner‐Cuenca
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 1
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: イノベーション管理

## Abstract

Triply periodic minimal surface (TPMS) structures offer a compelling route for designing porous electrodes with tunable architectures for electrochemical applications such as redox flow batteries. In this study, we fabricated carbonaceous TPMS electrodes, including gyroid, diamond, IWP, and cubic geometries, using digital light processing, and evaluated their performance in single-electrolyte flow cells using an organic redox couple. The printing process and subsequent thermal treatment produced conductive porous electrodes that closely replicated the intended TPMS geometries, preserving the characteristic features of each design. Through a detailed analysis of pressure drop, surface area, and electrochemical behavior, we identified the diamond design as particularly effective in enhancing mass transport while maintaining low hydraulic resistance. We further assessed gyroid structures, selected for being the most well-known among TPMSs, across a range of porosities, finding that higher porosity improved flow performance despite a reduction in surface area, an advantageous trade-off for electrolytes with facile kinetics and low ionic conductivity. Comparison with a commercial carbon felt electrode revealed comparable ohmic resistance and favorable flow characteristics, though reduced electrochemical surface area limited the achievable current densities. To isolate mass transport effects, a kinetically facile organic redox couple was employed, after which a representative 3D-printed gyroid electrode was evaluated in a proof-of-concept full-cell vanadium redox flow battery. A full-cell test demonstrated the feasibility of printed electrodes, achieving a voltage efficiency of 76% at a current density of 50 mA cm−2. These findings demonstrate the potential of architected 3D printed electrodes for future redox flow batteries and highlight the importance of tuning geometry and porosity to balance power output, mass transport, and system-level pumping losses.
