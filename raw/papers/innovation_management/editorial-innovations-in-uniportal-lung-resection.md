# Editorial: Innovations in uniportal lung resection

**著者**: Yojiro Yutaka, Calvin Sze Hang Ng
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: イノベーション管理

## Abstract

Importantly, technological innovation must be accompanied by systematic surgical education. The transition from multiportal to uniportal surgery involves fundamental changes in instrument manipulation, visualization, and coordination between the surgeon and surgical assistants. In our previous study, we evaluated the safe introduction of uVATS in an academic center, focusing on the learning curve, stepwise surgical training, and structured team-based education [2]. Our experience highlights the importance of establishing a standardized educational framework that enables surgeons to acquire new technical skills while maintaining procedural safety. Such considerations are increasingly relevant as thoracic surgery evolves toward diversified approaches, including uVATS, reduced-port RATS, and single-port robotic surgery. Furthermore, the integration of surgical techniques with multidisciplinary technologies will be essential for future progress. In their review, Chang et al. discussed the convergence of uniportal surgery with advanced imaging, bronchoscopic localization, neoadjuvant treatment, and emerging hybrid therapeutic approaches. These developments extend the concept of innovation beyond surgical access and highlight the importance of coordinating diagnostic, systemic, and local treatments within a comprehensive patient-centered strategy. Such integration reflects the broader evolution of minimally invasive thoracic surgery, as discussed in our previous Editorial [3]. Despite these promising developments, several challenges remain. Much of the available evidence is derived from retrospective studies conducted at experienced institutions, and long-term oncological outcomes remain insufficiently established for newer techniques. Standardized training, objective assessment of surgical proficiency, prospective comparative studies, and economic evaluations will be essential to establish the safety, reproducibility, and clinical benefits of these evolving approaches.Ultimately, the future of minimally invasive thoracic surgery should be guided by a patient-centered philosophy. Uniportal VATS, reduced-port RATS, and single-port robotic surgery should be regarded as complementary options rather than competing techniques, with the optimal approach selected according to individual patient characteristics and surgical complexity. Continued innovation should prioritize oncological completeness, procedural safety, preservation of pulmonary function, and meaningful improvements in patient recovery. We hope that the contributions to this Research Topic will stimulate further innovation, critical evaluation, and international collaboration toward these shared goals.
