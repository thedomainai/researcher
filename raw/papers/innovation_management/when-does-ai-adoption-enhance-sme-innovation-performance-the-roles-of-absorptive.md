# When does AI adoption enhance SME innovation performance? The roles of absorptive capacity, entrepreneurial agility, and digital maturity

**著者**: Ali Zokaei Kuhbanani, Léo‐Paul Dana, Mohammad Hadi Bolandian, Zahra Najafi Kalyani, Mohammadreza Parsanejad
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: イノベーション管理

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence (AI) adoption is increasingly viewed as a driver of innovation in small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). Yet adopting AI tools does not necessarily mean that firms can convert them into innovation outcomes. Drawing on dynamic capabilities theory and absorptive capacity theory, this study examines when and how AI adoption intensity enhances SME innovation performance. We propose a capability-building model in which AI adoption intensity strengthens absorptive capacity, absorptive capacity enhances entrepreneurial agility, and entrepreneurial agility improves innovation performance. We further examine digital maturity as a boundary condition that strengthens the AI adoption–absorptive capacity relationship and the downstream indirect pathway to innovation performance. The model was tested using survey data from 294 SMEs operating in an emerging economy context and partial least squares structural equation modeling. The results show that AI adoption intensity positively affects absorptive capacity, absorptive capacity strongly enhances entrepreneurial agility, and entrepreneurial agility improves SME innovation performance. However, the direct effect of AI adoption intensity on innovation performance is not significant, whereas the total and serial indirect effects are significant, indicating an indirect-only mediation mechanism. The findings also show that digital maturity positively moderates the relationship between AI adoption intensity and absorptive capacity and strengthens the serial indirect effect through absorptive capacity and entrepreneurial agility. PLSpredict results further indicate satisfactory indicator-level predictive relevance for SME innovation performance. The study contributes to AI innovation research by showing that AI adoption becomes innovation-enhancing not through adoption alone, but through absorptive capacity, entrepreneurial agility, and digital maturity.
