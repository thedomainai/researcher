# Eco-conscious synthesis of a starch-based magnetic hydrogel for effective methylene blue decontamination in wastewater treatment

**著者**: Anibal Alviz-Meza, Silvia Álvarez-Torrellas, Ysabel Huaccallo-Aguilar, María Verónica Carranza‐Oropeza, Juan Manuel Garrido-Zoido
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: イノベーション管理

## Abstract

Abstract Novel magnetic hydrogels based on native Peruvian Amarilla Reyna potato starch were synthesized via radical copolymerization, followed by in situ co-precipitation of magnetite, nanoparticles for Methylene Blue (MB) removal from aqueous matrices. The materials were characterized by FTIR, XRD, SEM-EDS, VSM, swelling measurements, and pH PZC determination, confirming successful polymer grafting, a homogeneous Fe₃O₄ incorporation, magnetic recoverability, and a high swelling capacity (352.1 g/g). The magnetic hydrogel achieved a MB removal efficiency of 96% and an equilibrium adsorption capacity of 1148.3 mg/g at pH 10 and 30°C. Adsorption kinetics followed the pseudo-first-order (PFO) kinetic model, reaching the equilibrium within 3 h, while the good fitting to the Langmuir isotherm model confirmed a monolayer adsorption behaviour. Thermodynamic analysis revealed that the adsorption process was spontaneous and exothermic, while Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations indicated a highly delocalized positive charge over the MB molecule, providing a molecular-level explanation for its weaker competition with small inorganic cations for negatively charged adsorption sites in complex aqueous matrices. Likewise, the hydrogel retained over 90% MB removal efficiency after five consecutive adsorption-desorption cycles, demonstrating an excellent reusability of the material. Tests in real water matrices showed a decreasing in the MB removal efficiency, c.a. 30-38%, due to ionic competition for the active sites and organic matter interference. The synthesized magnetic hydrogel demonstrated environmental safety, with no significant toxicity after treatment of the real effluents. These findings highlighted the potential of the obtained magnetic hydrogel as a sustainable and reusable adsorbent for dyes removal from complex aqueous matrices.
