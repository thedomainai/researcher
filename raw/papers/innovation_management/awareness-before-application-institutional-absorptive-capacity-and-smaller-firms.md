# Awareness Before Application: Institutional Absorptive Capacity and Smaller Firms’ Take-Up of Public Innovation Support

**著者**: Jeffrey Morales, Roberto Jalón
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: イノベーション管理

## Abstract

Public innovation policies are often designed to reach smaller firms, yet most never participate. We ask where participation is lost along the access process. Using six waves of Chile's National Innovation Survey covering 2011--2022, with the complete sequence estimated on the three harmonized waves from 2017 to 2022, we decompose access into three sequential transitions---awareness, application, and receipt---and observe awareness directly for the full surveyed population rather than inferring it from application behavior. Only 1.6--2.8% of formal firms apply for public innovation support, and roughly 91% do not know that the main R&D tax incentive exists, so coverage is lost overwhelmingly before application. A binding constraint on qualified labor is associated with awareness but not with application conditional on awareness; internal R&D displays the opposite pattern. Formal cross-stage tests and equivalence tests, with inference adapted to the small number of industry clusters, confirm that the qualified-labor constraint is more strongly associated with awareness than with conditional application. Awareness accounts for the largest share of the coverage gap between smaller and larger firms under two size definitions. Among informed non-applicants, however, firms reporting such constraints are more likely to cite bureaucratic difficulty, so administrative burden is unequally distributed without being the binding participation margin. We introduce the concept of institutional absorptive capacity to explain why formally neutral innovation policies can generate unequal access, and shift the policy focus from administrative simplification toward active outreach.
