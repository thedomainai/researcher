# Chemical characterization, antibacterial activity, in vitro cytotoxicity, and selectivity of the lipid extract from Sargassum filipendula against multidrug-resistant bacteria

**著者**: Marisa Castro Jara, Diego Serrasol do Amaral, Leonardo Wernerb, Wellington Da Rocha da Silva, Fábio Augusto do Nascimento Fialho
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: イノベーション管理

## Abstract

Abstract Antimicrobial resistance in multidrug-resistant (MDR) bacteria necessitates new therapeutic alternatives, and marine macroalgae represent an underexplored source of bioactive lipids. This study characterized the lipid extract (LE) of Sargassum filipendula and evaluated its antibacterial and cytotoxic profiles against MDR hospital isolates. LE composition was determined by GC-MS, and its thermal behavior by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC). Antibacterial activity was evaluated using the broth microdilution method (CLSI M07) against Acinetobacter baumannii , Escherichia coli , Klebsiella pneumoniae , Pseudomonas aeruginosa , Staphylococcus aureus , and coagulase-negative staphylococci, followed by determination of the minimum bactericidal concentration. Cytotoxicity was assessed in L929 and MDBK cell lines using MTT, Neutral Red, and Lactate Dehydrogenase assays; Hemolytic activity was evaluated using erythrocytes; and selectivity indices (SI) were calculated for each bacterial species. Palmitic acid (61.07%) and oleic acid (15.90%) were the predominant fatty acids. TGA revealed a total mass loss of 82% across two decomposition stages (296°C, 445°C), and DSC detected no phase transitions, indicating the absence of amorphous or crystalline domains. The LE exhibited bacteriostatic activity against all isolates, with MIC values ranging from 1.18 mg/mL ( S. aureus ) to 2.5 mg/mL ( P. aeruginosa ). Cytotoxicity assays yielded IC 50 values ranging from 0.16 to 1.25 mg/mL, and the hemolytic IC 50 was 12.81 mg/mL. However, the SI values remained below the safety threshold for all tested species (1.45–3.90). The S. filipendula LE is a candidate source of antibacterial compounds against MDR bacteria, although purification processes and in vivo validation are required to establish its therapeutic potential.
