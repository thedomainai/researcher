# Strategic Reskilling and Knowledge Retention in the Global Energy Sector: An Integrated Framework for LNG Operations

**著者**: S. Al Yazidi
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: オペレーションズリサーチ

## Abstract

Abstract The global energy sector is undergoing a period of unprecedented transformation driven by digitalization, demographic workforce change, and the accelerating energy transition. These forces are reshaping operational roles, introducing new competency requirements, and increasing the risk of critical knowledge loss, particularly within high-reliability industries such as liquefied natural gas (LNG) operations. At the same time, many organizations are experiencing the retirement of highly experienced personnel, creating a widening gap between emerging workforce capabilities and legacy operational expertise. This paper presents an applied study of workforce transition challenges within the LNG sector and proposes a structured capability development framework to address them. Drawing on industry practice and a case study from Oman LNG, the research examines how reskilling initiatives and knowledge-retention mechanisms can be integrated into a unified workforce strategy. The study identifies common limitations in traditional training approaches, including fragmented development programs, insufficient transfer of tacit knowledge, and limited alignment between learning activities and evolving digital roles. To address these gaps, the paper introduces the Predict–Prepare–Preserve–Perform (4P) Framework, a practical model that aligns proactive workforce planning, targeted reskilling pathways, structured experiential learning, and performance reinforcement. The framework emphasizes experience-based development, mentorship, and progressive responsibility as essential enablers of operational readiness and workforce confidence. The findings highlight that effective workforce transformation requires more than technical training; it demands deliberate preservation of operational expertise and strong integration between learning functions and business strategy. The paper offers actionable insights for energy organizations seeking to sustain safety, reliability, and operational excellence during periods of significant demographic and technological change.
