# AI-Driven Reservoir Modeling for Field-Scale Production Optimization in a Mature Offshore Oil Field: A Gulf of Suez Case Study

**著者**: S. Negahban, N. S. Ibrahim, H. Alaa, M. G. Aboelhassan, M. Abudooh
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: オペレーションズリサーチ

## Abstract

Abstract This paper presents a field-scale, AI-driven reservoir modeling and optimization framework applied to a mature offshore field in the Gulf of Suez. This asset, producing since the late 1960s, is characterized by complex geology, high water cut, and more than 50 years of production and injection history. Traditional reservoir simulation workflows in such environments are often time-intensive and resource-constrained, limiting their effectiveness for rapid decision-making. The proposed approach is a fully data-driven workflow that integrates automated data preprocessing, AI-based history matching which leads to optimization of production and water injection profiles. Artificial neural networks were trained on historical field data from more than 400 wells to capture nonlinear relationships between water injection, production, pressure, and artificial lift parameters. The models were evaluated against a three-year withheld historical dataset and were then used to generate base forecasts under current operating conditions. Thousands of operational scenarios were then evaluated to identify the optimized gas lift distribution and water injection strategies under field constraints. The AI-based model successfully history-matched over five decades of field-wide oil, gas, water, and pressure data. For the final three-year withheld period, field-level MAPE was 5–8% for oil rate and 5% for water rate across the two reservoirs; gas-rate error was higher. Scenario-based forecast identified operating cases with approximately 2–3% higher oil production and 15-20% lower gas lift requirement relative to the modeled no-action base case. This relative forecast difference is used as a screening indicator and has not been field-validated or formally uncertainty-quantified. The modeled water-injection scenarios indicated opportunities to reduce forecast water production in selected well patterns while maintaining or improving forecast oil output. The workflow also identified preliminary infill locations for further evaluation. The results demonstrate that AI-driven, top-down reservoir models can accelerate scenario evaluation in mature assets and provide a practical tool for reservoir-management decision support. The workflow provides a rapid, data-informed screening and decision-support capability for late-life fields with limited modeling resources. Field implementation, surveillance, and post-implementation evaluation are required to confirm the operational value of individual recommendations.
