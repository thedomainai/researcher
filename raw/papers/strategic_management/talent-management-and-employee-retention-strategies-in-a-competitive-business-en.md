# Talent Management and Employee Retention Strategies in a Competitive Business Environment

**著者**: Saddam Hussain
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経営戦略

## Abstract

Talent management and employee retention have become critical strategic priorities for organizations operating in increasingly competitive, technology-driven, and knowledge-intensive business environments. Organizations face growing pressure to attract employees with appropriate capabilities while simultaneously developing, engaging, and retaining highperforming and high-potential talent. Employee turnover can generate substantial direct and indirect costs through recruitment expenditure, onboarding requirements, productivity disruption, loss of institutional knowledge, weakened customer relationships, and increased workload for remaining employees. This paper examines the relationship between talent management practices and employee retention through a systematic conceptual and literature-review approach. It analyzes major talent-management dimensions, including strategic workforce planning, recruitment and selection, onboarding, learning and development, career management, performance management, compensation, recognition, succession planning, employee engagement, leadership, workplace flexibility, and employee well-being. Drawing on human capital theory, social exchange theory, the resource-based view, psychological contract theory, and the Job Demands-Resources framework, the paper develops an integrated model explaining how organizational practices can influence employees' intention to remain. The analysis suggests that retention is strengthened when employees perceive competitive and fair rewards, meaningful career opportunities, supportive leadership, recognition, psychological safety, work-life balance, organizational justice, and a credible long-term employment relationship. The paper further argues that retention strategies should be differentiated because employees leave organizations for different reasons and not all turnover is equally harmful. The study concludes that sustainable talent management requires organizations to integrate employee development and experience with workforce analytics, leadership accountability, internal mobility, succession planning, and business strategy rather than relying primarily on financial incentives.
