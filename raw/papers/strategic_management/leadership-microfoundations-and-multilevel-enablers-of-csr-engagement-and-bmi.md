# Leadership Microfoundations and Multilevel Enablers of CSR Engagement and BMI

**著者**: Desislava Dikova, Mariola Ciszewska‐Mlinarič
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経営戦略

## Abstract

This chapter examines how CEO characteristics and leadership orientations shape the deployment of business model innovation (BMI) in small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). Drawing on survey evidence from Polish firms, it highlights the microfoundational role of managerial experience, education, and external networks in driving sustainability commitments, which in turn are translated into firm performance through BMI. The analysis shows that social commitment and managerial expertise are particularly influential, while external networks amplify social but not environmental engagement. Additional comparisons reveal that firms demonstrating high ambidexterity, balancing exploration and exploitation within BMI, achieve superior performance outcomes and stronger innovation capabilities than firms with no dominant logic of business model change. These findings underscore the role of leadership as a bridging mechanism that aligns vision, stakeholder expectations, and organizational capabilities to enact BMI under conditions of institutional ambiguity and resource constraints. The chapter concludes with implications for managers and policymakers and foreshadows how governance structures can sustain these leadership-driven innovation efforts, a theme developed further in the next chapter.
