# Beyond governance: How executive management nationality shapes report readability in Saudi Arabia

**著者**: Shehabaddin Abdullah Abdulwadod Al-Dubai‎
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経営戦略

## Abstract

This study examines how board effectiveness and CEO nationality jointly influence the readability of English-language annual reports in Saudi Arabia. Using a panel of 78 firm-year observations from firms in the MSCI Tadawul 30 Index between 2020 and 2022, readability is measured using the Flesch Reading Ease (FRE) Index. The analysis examines whether the effects of board meeting frequency and board independence on report readability differ between firms led by domestic and foreign CEOs. The findings show that annual reports are, on average, “very difficult” to read. In direct-effect models, neither board meetings nor board independence are significantly related to readability. Interaction models, however, reveal contingent effects. Under foreign CEOs, more frequent board meetings are associated with significantly higher readability, while the effect is negligible for domestic CEOs. Board independence has opposing effects: it reduces readability under foreign CEOs but improves it for domestic CEOs. Hausman-Taylor estimation confirms that the results are robust to endogeneity. The findings show that governance mechanisms do not operate uniformly and should be evaluated alongside CEO nationality. Findings are consistent with the interpretation that their effect on disclosure clarity depends on the institutional, linguistic, and cultural alignment between the board and the CEO.
