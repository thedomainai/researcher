# Skill Mismatch and Underemployment in the Digital Era: A Theory-Driven Systematic Literature Review on Human Capital Paradox and Future Workforce

**著者**: Sekar Widyasari Putri, Moses Glorino Rumambo Pandin
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経営戦略

## Abstract

The digital transformation accelerated by the development of artificial intelligence, automation, and the implementation of Industry 4.0 and Industry 5.0 has fundamentally changed the structure of the labor market. These changes have widened the gap between workforce competencies and job needs, giving rise to increasingly complex skill mismatch and underemployment phenomena. This condition challenges the basic assumption of Human Capital Theory, which states that investment in education and skills development will increase productivity, employability, and success in the labor market. In fact, many individuals with highly educated levels remain employed in jobs that do not match their competencies, giving rise to a phenomenon conceptualized in this study as the Human Capital Paradox. This study aims to systematically synthesize the development of literature on skill mismatch and underemployment, evaluate the relevance of Human Capital Theory in the digital era, and develop a conceptual framework that explains the relationship between technological change, workforce capabilities, and labor market dynamics. The study used the Systematic Literature Review (SLR) method with PRISMA 2020 guidelines through a synthesis of 59 Scopus-indexed articles from various disciplines. The study results show that skill mismatch is a multidimensional phenomenon that includes vertical mismatch, horizontal mismatch, skill gap, and skill obsolescence, which significantly contribute to underemployment, decreased productivity, wage penalties, low job satisfaction, and decreased worker welfare. This study expands Human Capital Theory by developing the Human Capital Paradox Framework, which views underemployment as a consequence of a dynamic mismatch between human capital accumulation and changing competency needs in the labor market. This framework provides a theoretical contribution to the development of human resource management science while also serving as a basis for formulating talent development strategies, employment policies, and education systems that are more adaptive to the future of the world of work.
