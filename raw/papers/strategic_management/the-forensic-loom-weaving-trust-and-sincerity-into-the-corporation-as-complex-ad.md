# The forensic loom: weaving trust and sincerity into the corporation as complex adaptive system

**著者**: Robert L. Oprisko
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経営戦略

## Abstract

Purpose This paper introduces the Forensic Loom, a diagnostic framework that treats organizational trust as something that can be verified rather than assumed. It proposes that leadership sincerity, whether a leader is performing the scope they claim, can be examined systematically instead of judged impressionistically. Trust is grounded in what a leader does, not in what a leader is believed capable of doing. Design/methodology/approach The paper is conceptual. It proceeds phenomenologically, grounding a Bohrian complementarity account of the corporation as simultaneously unitary and multiple. It extends Elliott Jaques' stratified systems theory by arguing that five functions are inevitable in any viable corporation. This claim is grounded in Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety. A cybernetic and telemetric architecture, drawn from the tradition of Wiener, Ashby and Beer, operationalizes the framework for measurement. Findings The paper argues that a form of recurring and inevitable failure, which it terms the corporate mask, involves actively performing a scope above one's function and span of discretion. This failure is a rational, locatable source of coordination loss that propagates across hierarchical levels. It is a structural consequence of how formal and functional scopes diverge and never fully close. It proposes level-specific and seam-specific instruments that render this failure measurable and correspond structurally with Beer’s viable system model. Research limitations/implications This paper is conceptual and does not include a case study. The correspondence with Beer's viable system model is structural rather than recursive. A full demonstration that each level recursively embeds the viable system model's own five systems is noted as a direction for future research. Originality/value Existing integrations of stratified systems theory and the viable system model are structural and capability-focused. This paper's contribution is regulatory: it proposes using cybernetic telemetry to assess whether a leader is performing the function of their level in real time, rather than whether they are capable of doing so. In doing so it grounds institutional trust in verified behavior rather than in reported capability.
