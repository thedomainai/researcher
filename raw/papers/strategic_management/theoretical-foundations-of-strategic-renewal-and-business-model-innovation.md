# Theoretical Foundations of Strategic Renewal and Business Model Innovation

**著者**: Mariola Ciszewska‐Mlinarič, Piotr Wójcik
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経営戦略

## Abstract

This chapter analyzes the theoretical underpinnings of strategic renewal (SR) and business model innovation (BMI), showing how organizations achieve transformation in today’s volatile environment. SR is framed as more than product or operational changes: it involves deliberate shifts in vision, structures, capabilities, and identity to renew long-term competitive advantage. Drawing on several core perspectives, including the resource-based view, dynamic capabilities, evolutionary, institutional, and learning theories, the chapter shows that successful renewal depends on overcoming inertia and path dependence, balancing adaptation and proactive transformation, and navigating tensions between top-down leadership and distributed agency. The chapter positions BMI as the central mechanism for enacting strategic renewal. BMI represents systemic reconfiguration of how value is created and captured, reaching beyond incremental innovation to encompass activity redesign, new partnerships, and sustainable business models. Leading frameworks highlight aspects such as novelty, efficiency, lock-in, complementarities, and the strategic use of ecosystem and circularity approaches. This chapter culminates with an integrated conceptual model that will guide subsequent empirical analysis. This model links the antecedents of strategic renewal—environmental pressures and organizational readiness—with the renewal logic (adaptation versus proactive creation), initiation (leadership versus distributed agency), structural choices, and key drivers and barriers such as resources or learning.
