# Strategic leaders with a ‘difference’: how unconventional backgrounds and paradoxical qualities shape decisions and outcomes

**著者**: Ananya Sengupta
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経営戦略

## Abstract

This doctoral thesis studies strategic leaders with a ‘difference’, specifically two under explored groups of strategic leaders, with the aim of exploring how these differences shape their decision-making process and ultimately outcomes in the teams and organisations that they lead. The first group comprises strategic leaders with unconventional backgrounds: CEOs with criminal or addiction histories. The second group comprises CEOs with paradoxical demographic attributes relating to education and experience. My research is anchored in the Upper Echelons Theory (UET) while at the same time drawing on perspectives from psychology such as post-traumatic growth theory and from organisational studies such as paradox theory. From a methodological perspective, I used DA and fsQCA to shed new light on the cognitive and relational black boxes that have limited our understanding of the strategic decision-making process. In Chapter 2 I advance a Qualities-Decision-Outcomes (QDO) framework, building on UET, which acts as the underpinning conceptual framework for the three papers in the thesis. All papers in the thesis explore different aspects of the QDO but all aim to explore cognitive and/or relational black boxes of strategic decision-making, and the conceptual paper develops a framework that supports this objective. Chapter 3 uses a discourse analysis of autobiographies to study the lived experience narratives of four strategic leaders with criminal and addiction histories, who have undergone a transformative post-traumatic growth experience. The study explored how these leaders use discursive resources including persuasive rhetorical strategies and arguments, drawing on “interpretative repertoires” for meaning-making that becomes a part of their “construed reality”. My analysis identifies three interrelated discourses of transformative leadership, enduring gratitude and purposeful inclusivity that form a narrative arc through which they (re)construct and legitimise their personal and professional identities. Chapter 4 uses fsQCA analysis on a bespoke dataset created from publicly available secondary data on Information Technology (IT) sector Multinational Enterprises (MNEs) in the S&P600 Small Cap Index, to explore how the combination of paradoxical demographic attributes relating to CEOs’ education and work experience, along with decisions on ambidextrous expansion and other contextual factors, influence performance outcomes of the firm. This study finds that paradoxical demographic attributes of CEOs, particularly experience have significant influence on firm performance in short-term periods of high disruption, albeit success can also be achieved by opportunistically using different strategies. Over more stable periods post the disruption, diversity continues to be important but other attributes such as expertise and leadership also become important. Further diversity is a double-edged sword which leads to both positive and negative outcomes. Thus, the mere presence of demographic attributes is not sufficient to prevent adverse outcomes. From these findings I infer that CEO cognition likely plays a mediating role in the process that transforms the existence of paradoxical demographic attributes into firm success, thus foregrounding the importance of CEOs strategically adopting a “paradox mindset”. In doing so, I introduce the concept of ‘paradoxical strategic leadership’. This concept is explored further in Chapter 5, which is a conceptual paper that draws parallels between the “perceptual process” of the UET and the process of navigating paradox under paradox theory and theorises how the adoption of a paradox mindset can transform the perceptual process into the process of paradoxical strategic leadership. The paper demonstrates how concepts from paradox theory can be integrated into strategic leadership studies to enable a better understanding of the complexities and competing tensions that strategic leaders grapple with. Thus, the paper offers a window into the cognitive black box of the process of paradoxical strategic leadership in the upper echelons. In this paper I also advance a novel framework that provides a theoretical lens for sensemaking of past strategic decisions under conditions of paradox. In addition, it acts as a practice tool that can help strategic leaders to pro-actively adopt a paradox mindset for more effective decision making when faced with paradoxical tensions. As a whole, the thesis makes several conceptual and empirical contributions to upper echelons literature while taking an inter-disciplinary view of strategic leadership studies and employing methodological pluralism.
