# Touchdown or fumble: strategic talent acquisition in the National Football League

**著者**: Sandra W. DeGrassi, Randall D. Harris, W. Scott Sherman
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経営戦略

## Abstract

Research methodology This case is based on multiple media reports before and during the Kansas City Chiefs’ 2024 season, specifically in early October 2024, after two of the Chiefs’ wide receivers were injured. Primary sources included YouTube recordings of interviews with Brett Veach, numerous Chiefs’ players and officials, various commentators discussing the team’s activities and the ESPN six-part documentary series “The Kingdom.” These were supplemented with various published, online and broadcast sports reports. The YouTube videos were transcribed using Transkriptor, a web-based AI application. The ESPN documentary audio “The Kingdom” was transcribed from closed caption text. A complete set of references is contained in the case footnotes and the teaching notes’ reference section. Case overview/synopsis Brett Veach, General Manager of the Kansas City Chiefs National Football League (NFL) team, needed to find an additional wide receiver. The Chiefs and most NFL teams typically had six wide receivers on the team. The Chiefs had only four wide receivers in early October after their top two wide receivers suffered long-term injuries and might not play again in the 2024 season. Veach had until the NFL’s November 5th trade deadline to identify a candidate and negotiate a trade with another NFL team. The Chiefs were trying to get to their third consecutive Super Bowl, a first in the NFL and had 12 more regular-season games plus up to three playoff games to play. Could Veach find an available wide receiver that could meet the Chiefs’ on-the-field needs, fit with the Chiefs’ culture and save the Chiefs’ chance at a third Super Bowl by the November 5th trade deadline? Complexity academic level The case was designed for both undergraduate and graduate Human Resource Management courses. The case may be useful in introductory-level human resource management courses, as well as in upper-level talent acquisition or strategic human resource management courses that cover selection. The case has been classroom tested at the undergraduate and graduate levels, in both in-seat and asynchronous online modalities. The case is designed to apply the Attraction-Selection-Attrition (ASA) framework (Schneider, 1987) to guide a selection decision. The case explores three general themes within the ASA framework: understanding overall organizational culture; analyzing organizational skill gaps; and making selection decisions from a pool of imperfect candidates. The case takes a behind-the-scenes look at NFL talent acquisition as an application of the ASA framework.
