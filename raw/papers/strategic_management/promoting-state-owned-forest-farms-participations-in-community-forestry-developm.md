# Promoting state-owned forest farms’ participations in community forestry development in China: the roles of multiple institutional pressures

**著者**: Shuqiang Li, Suliman Almojel
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経営戦略

## Abstract

Socio-economic transformation has posed new challenges to the effectiveness of community forestry (CF) management. In response, China has introduced the Joint Management of Forest Land between State-Owned Forest Farms and Communities (SOFFs-CJM) model, which aims to strengthen CF governance and foster the revitalization of traditional forest regions. The key to the success of this model lies in incentivizing the active participation of SOFFs. Grounded in institutional theory, this study conducts an empirical analysis of SOFFs’ joint operation decisions using data from 187 forest farms across four provinces—Fujian, Jiangxi, Guangxi, and Zhejiang. Logit models, mediation effect models, and moderation effect models are integrated to examine the decision-making behavior of SOFFs. The results show that: (1) coercive pressure, mimetic pressure, and normative pressure all positively influence SOFFs to engage in joint management with communities, with coercive pressure exerting the most significant impact. (2) Organizational legitimacy plays a mediating role in the relationship between institutional pressures and the decision-making of SOFFs. Specifically, institutional pressures indirectly affect joint management decisions through performance legitimacy, task legitimacy, and value legitimacy. (3) The management system and nature of SOFFs moderate the impact of institutional pressures on joint management decisions. County-owned SOFFs enhance the influence of mimetic pressure on joint management decisions and weaken the impact of normative pressure, while corporate-owned SOFFs reduce the influence of coercive pressure and normative pressure. (4) Coercive pressure and normative pressure have a complementary effect on joint management decisions, and normative pressure has an inverted-U-shaped relationship with joint management decisions. Therefore, the government can promote the participation of SOFFs in community forestry by improving coercive pressure, appropriately guiding normative pressure, enhancing mimetic pressure, and designing differentiated policies based on the management system and nature of the forest farms.
