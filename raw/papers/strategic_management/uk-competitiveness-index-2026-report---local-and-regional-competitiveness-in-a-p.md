Competitiveness Index (UKCI), first published in 2000, benchmarks the competitiveness of the UK's localities using a 3-Factor model of inputs, outputs and outcomes.This 2026 edition, the thirteenth, covers 349 localities and compares their performance with 2022, a period marked by geopolitical uncertainty, trade disruption and cost-of-living pressures.• The City of London remains by far the most competitive locality (UKCI 778.7), but since 2022 it has also experienced the largest fall in competitiveness relative to the UK average (-66.2points).This may reflect concerns about its position as a global financial centre, with a number of UK-listed companies moving their listings elsewhere, particularly to the USA.• Of the larger urban areas outside London, Edinburgh, with its financial specialism and government role, is the most competitive (UKCI 117.7), overtaking Milton Keynes, which led in 2022.• Manchester's competitive position is improving (UKCI 113.3, ranked 31st), but the Greater Manchester Combined Authority as a whole ranks only fifth among combined authorities and city regions (UKCI 96.7), showing that competitiveness is unevenly distributed across the wider area.• Belfast records the largest improvement among the core cities, rising from 96.7 to 100.1 to move just above the UK average, driven by increases in highly qualified residents and business registrations.• The least competitive locality is Blaenau Gwent in Wales (UKCI 72.5), with a slight decrease in UKCI score since 2022; however, this should be set against a longer-run improvement relative to the UK average.• Only 101 of the 349 localities covered have a UKCI score at or above the UK average of 100.This reflects the concentration of the most competitive localities in London and the South East, two regions that together account for 27.9 percent of the population of Great Britain.• Localities experiencing larger improvements in UKCI score are generally more rural, although a number of localities in Inner London also fall into this group.Larger declines are generally found in more densely populated areas, including Outer London, traditionally more prosperous areas in the South East, and former industrial cities.• As a positive sign for ambitions to 'rewire the state' and support broader growth and prosperity, there is some evidence of convergence.Since 2011 less
