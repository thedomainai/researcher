# HUMAN RESOURCE MANAGEMENT AND WORKFORCE PLANNING

**著者**: Sixbert Sangwa, Placide Mutabazi
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経営戦略

## Abstract

Human resource management (HRM) determines how enterprises acquire, deploy, reward, support, and retain the capabilities required to deliver their purposes. Yet staffing is not simply a matter of matching vacancies to applicants: HR decisions distribute opportunity, organize work, influence financial exposure, and condition the credibility of organizational commitments. This chapter develops a critical, integrative account of strategic HRM and workforce planning across twelve functional domains, connecting human-capital economics, the resource-based view, the ability–motivation–opportunity framework, organizational justice, and psychological-contract theory with empirical selection research and evidence-based evaluation. A transparent, purposive synthesis of secondary scholarship and authoritative institutional material situates the analysis in Rwanda, using the August 2026 Labour Force Survey and documented enterprise structures, alongside international comparative evidence. A fully identified hypothetical Kigali service-line expansion demonstrates demand forecasting, full-time-equivalent staffing, internal supply, vacancy gaps, recruitment costs, and sensitivity testing. Editable diagnostic tables and an original workforce-decision framework translate theory into operational practice, while an AI-assisted hiring assessment rubric makes fairness and privacy controls inspectable. The central argument is that effective HRM is neither personnel administration nor indiscriminate investment in people. It is a context-dependent, accountable configuration of work requirements, capabilities, rights, incentives, information, and managerial decisions. Empirical findings and illustrative calculations are distinguished from normative commitments and propositions requiring further testing.
