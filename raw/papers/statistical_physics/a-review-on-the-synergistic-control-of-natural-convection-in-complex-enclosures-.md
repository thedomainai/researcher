# A Review on the Synergistic Control of Natural Convection in Complex Enclosures: The Interplay of Geometry, Nanofluids, Magnetic Fields, and Porous Media

**著者**: Ria Egami, Zeeshan Badshah, Zeeshan Badshah, Mahmoud M. Selim, Ibrahim Elbatal
**年**: 2027 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 統計力学・熱力学

## Abstract

Natural convection in complex enclosures is an important component in passive thermal management systems such as solar collector, electronic cooling, heat exchanger and energy storage systems. However, the relatively low heat transfer capacity of natural convection has stimulated a lot of research aimed at more advanced methods of improving and controlling heat transfer. The present review critically summarizes the latest numerical study on the synergistic control of natural convection due to the interaction between four factors: geometry modification, nanofluids, magnetic field, and porous media which are collectively called GNMP interactions. A systematic literature-screening approach was used, whereby 220 potentially relevant studies were first retrieved from Scopus, Web of Science and Google Scholar and finally 72 high quality studies were selected for the detailed analysis. The effects of corrugated walls, baffles, wavy surfaces, complex inner bodies, nanoparticle suspensions, magnetohydrodynamic control and porous structures on the average Nusselt number, entropy generation, and Bejan number are compared. The synthesis indicates that modifying the geometry and the addition of nanoparticles in general improves heat transfer by breaking down the thermal boundary layers and increasing the effective thermal conductivity. Average Nusselt number enhancements can be up to 2030% contingent upon geometry, sort of nanoparticles and working conditions. The convective heat transfer, on the other hand, is often drastically reduced by Lorentz- force damping of the buoyancy-driven circulation at strong magnetic fields, which is usually observed when the Hartmann number is increased. It can either accelerate or retard the convection in porous media, depending on the model adopted (DarcyBrinkman Forchheimer) and the porosity and permeability of the media. One of the main conclusions is that heat-transfer improvement can be associated with higher thermodynamic irreversibility associated with higher velocity gradients, magnetic damping, and/or viscous dissipation. Thus, the systems of GNMP must be optimized using a simultaneous first-law and second-law evaluation. This review gives a coherent perspective for the understanding of conflicting results, outlines research needs, and suggests ways to design natural- convection systems that are efficient, controllable, and thermodynamically sustainable.
