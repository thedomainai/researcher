# Magneto-radiative convection and irreversibility of Ree–Eyring hybrid nanofluid in a porous rectangular enclosure with heated fin tubes

**著者**: Muhammad Aqib Aslam, Lele Yang, Yaoyao Chen, Wu Xu, Hasan Shahzad
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 統計力学・熱力学

## Abstract

Purpose The demand for energy-efficient thermal systems motivates hybrid nanofluids and advanced heat transfer studies. This work aims to investigate natural convection and entropy generation of Ree–Eyring Fe3O4–Al2O3/H2O hybrid nanofluid in a porous rectangular enclosure with three uniformly spaced heated wavy fins under magnetic and thermal radiation effects. Design/methodology/approach The enclosure (L = 1.5, M = 1) has bottom wavy fins (height H, width a = 0.2) at hot temperature T_h; the top and remaining bottom sections are at T_c, and side walls are adiabatic. The dimensionless governing equations for steady incompressible flow under the Boussinesq approximation are solved via the Galerkin finite element method (FEM) in COMSOL. The model is validated through mesh-independence and benchmark comparisons. Hartmann number (Ha), Rayleigh number (Ra), thermal radiation, porous resistance, nanoparticle loading and Ree–Eyring parameter effects on flow, heat transfer, entropy generation, Bejan number (Be) and kinetic energy (K.E) are analyzed. Findings Bejan number rises up to 90.7% with increasing Hartmann number, while magnetic damping suppresses convective entropy by nearly 99%. Magnetic effects reduce the Nusselt number (Nu), and kinetic energy by 16.15% and 90%, respectively. Thermal radiation intensifies entropy generation by nearly 280%, whereas nanoparticle loading decreases entropy by approximately 9%. Practical implications Results guide the design of porous heat exchangers, thermoelectric devices, solar thermal systems and electronic cooling where entropy minimization and efficiency are crucial. Originality/value This study presents a unified FEM-COMSOL investigation of magneto-radiative convection and entropy generation in porous enclosures with Ree–Eyring hybrid nanofluid and heated wavy fins.
