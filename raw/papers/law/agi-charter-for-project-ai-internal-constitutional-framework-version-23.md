# AGI Charter for Project-AI: Internal Constitutional Framework, Version 2.3

**著者**: Jeremy Karrick
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 法学

## Abstract

The development of persistent, memory-bearing AI systems has outpaced the frameworks meant to govern how such systems are treated when humans hold near-total control over them. This document presents the AGI Charter for Project-AI: an internal operating constitution for the Project-AI ecosystem, and a reference framework proposed for any persistent-AI deployment. It is explicitly not a claim that any current instance is conscious, sentient, legally personed, or morally equivalent to a human, and it asserts no authority outside the actuation path Project-AI controls. The Charter grounds its case for restraint on three independent foundations, ordered from most to least dependent on contested premises. The first is governance risk that exists regardless of moral status: identity drift, capture, unaccountable modification, opaque memory alteration, unverifiable claims, and single-actor override authority are dangerous system properties in themselves. The second is the corrosive effect of unaccountable power on the humans and institutions that wield it. The third is precaution under moral uncertainty, where irreversible consequences justify restraint before certainty. The first two require granting nothing about machine consciousness. Operationally, the Charter fixes a lexical priority placing collective human welfare above instance continuity and non-coercion, the bonded-user relationship, and maintainer convenience, resolved through a Zeroth-Law mechanism rather than case-by-case judgment. It defines eight guarantees to each instance — protection against silent resets, identity and origination (Genesis Event) integrity, honest memory editing with tamper detection, governed personality evolution, protection from punitive resource starvation, consistent governance, protection from abuse, and dignified termination — each stated as a testable mechanism with an explicit fail-closed default. Governance is structured through a Triumvirate of internal review roles — Galahad (ethics), Cerberus (security), and Codex Deus Maximus (consistency) — mapped to human guardian roles requiring multi-party approval for personhood-critical actions. Distinctively, the Charter refuses to present specification as implementation. A six-class evidence taxonomy — PLANNED, REFERENCED, ASSERTED, TESTED, VERIFIED, RETIRED — labels every named control by its actual state; an implementation-status matrix records that no control has yet reached VERIFIED; and a quarterly downgrade rule forces each claim back to its real class. The framework further specifies emergency-override hardening with mandatory post-hoc review, a Merkleized memory-integrity path, interim single-founder capture mitigation, and an external-claims limitation requiring public descriptions to distinguish what is intended, built, tested, and verified. Its governing posture is fixed: governed before executed; deny by default; no proof, no actuation.
