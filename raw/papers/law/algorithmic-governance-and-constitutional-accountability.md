# Algorithmic Governance and Constitutional Accountability

**著者**: Aneesh V. Pillai, Susan Zacharia
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 法学

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence (AI) has evolved from being a simple technological breakthrough to becoming a crucial instrument in governance, transforming the way states provide welfare, administer justice, and oversee economic and social activities. India’s rapid digital evolution, propelled by key initiatives like Digital India, Aadhaar, and India Stack, has embedded algorithmic systems into the heart of state operations. These technologies offer promises of efficiency, transparency, and inclusivity; however, they also pose challenges to the normative principles of constitutional governance. When algorithmic systems, often characterized by a lack of transparency, bias, and accountability, make decisions affecting fundamental rights, traditional doctrines of administrative law such as reasoned decision-making, proportionality, and natural justice face significant challenges. This study examines the intersection of algorithmic governance and constitutional accountability in India. It argues that the lack of a coherent, rights-based regulatory framework for AI undermines the constitutional guarantees of equality, non-discrimination, and due process, as outlined in Articles 14, 15, and 21 of the Indian Constitution. By drawing on international developments, including the European Union Artificial Intelligence Act (2024), the OECD’s Principles on Artificial Intelligence (2019), and the UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence (2021), this study situates India’s regulatory gap within a global comparative framework. This suggests that India needs to adopt a rights-focused, multilevel regulatory structure that balances innovation with constitutional safeguards and aligns with its commitment to democratic accountability. The research identifies systemic challenges in India’s AI governance, such as institutional fragmentation, regulatory capture, opacity in algorithmic decision-making, and limited technical and judicial capacity. It concludes that the future of AI governance in India must be guided by constitutional morality and the normative commitment to dignity, equality, and liberty that are central to the Indian Republic. Accordingly, this study proposes a normative framework based on transparency, accountability, fairness, proportionality, and participatory oversight. It recommends the establishment of an Artificial Intelligence Commission of India, mandatory algorithmic impact assessments, and enforceable rights to explanations and appeals. Embedding constitutional values into AI regulation is crucial to ensure that India’s digital future remains democratic, inclusive, and just, where algorithms support justice rather than replace it.
