# From Colonial Constitutionalism to Algorithmic Governance: The Evolution of Constitutional Freedom, Liberty and Human Dignity in India in the Age of Artificial Intelligence

**著者**: Kunal Agarwal, Suryan Vats Upadhyaya, Neelabh Nayan Kalita
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 法学

## Abstract

In the past ten years, artificial intelligence (AI) has moved from the margins of policy to the centre of constitutional debate. Automated decision systems now shape welfare distribution, policing, credit scoring, content moderation and electoral communication, and courts and legislatures are asking whether inherited constitutional notions of liberty, equality and dignity can meaningfully apply to code. This paper traces India's constitutional journey from colonial subject state to rights-bearing republic and compares it with the markedly different experiences of the United States and the People's Republic of China. It then examines how each jurisdiction is reshaping its fundamental rights regime in response to algorithmic governance, through a comparative public law analysis across three themes: the institutional design of AI regulation, the approach of courts to algorithmic accountability, and the treatment of privacy and data protection. The paper presents India as a middle-ground state that adopts the American language of judicial review while building a centralised regulatory structure that resembles the Chinese model in form, though not in intent. Tables and figures are used throughout to visualise the comparative findings. Keywords: Right to privacy; Algorithmic Governance; Data Protection; Judicial Review; Human Dignity; comparative constitutional law
