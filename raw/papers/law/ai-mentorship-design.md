# AI MENTORSHIP DESIGN

**著者**: Roman Shaban
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 法学

## Abstract

The emergence of generative artificial intelligence creates new opportunities for designing personalized systems of mentorship that extend beyond conventional digital tutoring, automated instruction, and information retrieval. Conversational AI can provide explanations, generate adaptive exercises, simulate professional scenarios, offer formative feedback, and support reflection on individual learning processes. However, the availability of sophisticated AI assistance does not necessarily produce sustainable learning, professional competence, or independent judgment. This conceptual paper introduces AI Mentorship Design as a systematic approach to creating personalized human–AI developmental environments that combine educational psychology, self regulated learning, adaptive instruction, metacognitive monitoring, and competency-based assessment. The paper proposes the Personalized Digital Mentorship Architecture (PDMA), a theoretical framework consisting of eight interconnected components: developmental goal modeling, baseline competency assessment, dynamic learner profiling, adaptive mentoring strategies, structured practice, metacognitive regulation, independent performance evaluation, and ethical governance. A central distinction is made between AI-supported task performance and genuine competency development. While conventional assistance systems frequently optimize the immediate quality of generated outputs, a developmental mentorship system should prioritize the learner's increasing ability to perform tasks accurately, critically, and independently. The framework integrates principles of scaffolding, retrieval practice, deliberate practice, self explanation, formative assessment, and gradual withdrawal of assistance. It also examines psychological risks, including automation bias, cognitive dependency, unrealistic performance expectations, and the displacement of human mentorship. Recent experimental evidence demonstrates both the potential benefits and limitations of AI supported learning, indicating that educational outcomes depend substantially on instructional design, user behavior, and institutional context. The proposed architecture provides a foundation for designing personalized digital mentors in education, professional development, research training, creative practice, and lifelong learning. It also offers testable propositions and evaluation criteria for future empirical investigation
