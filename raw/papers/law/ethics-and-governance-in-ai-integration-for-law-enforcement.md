# Ethics and Governance in AI Integration for Law Enforcement

**著者**: Yasmine Ezzeddine
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 法学

## Abstract

The deployment of artificial intelligence (AI) in law enforcement raises urgent ethical and governance questions that intersect with public perceptions, legal frameworks, and operational practice. This chapter examines these interdependencies using Serge Moscovici’s Social Representation Theory to illuminate how societal narratives, media discourse, and lived experiences shape the legitimacy of AI-enabled policing. It evaluates contemporary ethical frameworks, transparency, accountability, and fairness, and situates them within recent regulatory developments such as the European Union’s AI Act, algorithmic impact assessments, and municipal transparency initiatives. Drawing on empirical studies and case-based examples, the chapter interrogates algorithmic bias, the “black box” problem, and the implications of biometric surveillance for due process and civil liberties. The analysis argues that effective governance requires binding legal safeguards, independent oversight, and participatory mechanisms that centre affected communities in decision-making. The chapter concludes with prescriptive recommendations for policymakers and policing practitioners: strengthen enforceable AI governance instruments; operationalise participatory models of oversight; and create interoperable standards for auditing and transparency. Ultimately, it contends that aligning technological innovation with democratic accountability is essential to ensure AI serves public safety without compromising rights.
