# GOVERNANCE OF GENERATIVE AI IN ORGANIZATIONS: INTERNAL POLICIES ON USE, TRACEABILITY, AND ACCOUNTABILITY FOR MACHINE-GENERATED CONTENT

**著者**: André Henrique de Oliveira Santos, Luiz Arnoni Fraga Lizardo
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 法学

## Abstract

Generative artificial intelligence entered organizations faster than the structures capable of governing it: in 2025, 79% of organizations reported using it in at least one business function, up from 33% in 2023, while only slightly more than half actively mitigated the risk of content inaccuracy and less than a third that of intellectual property infringement. This article analyzes corporate governance of generative AI in three articulated dimensions: internal use policies, traceability of generated content, and accountability for the damages arising from it. The qualitative, documentary investigation articulated the consolidating normative framework, the European Artificial Intelligence Regulation, Bill No. 2,338 of 2023, pending a vote in the Chamber of Deputies as of September 2026, the General Data Protection Law and the Brazilian Internet Civil Framework, with management standards, the ISO/IEC 42001 standard and the NIST risk management framework with its generative AI profile, and with recent literature on governance and civil liability of platforms, based on searches conducted in September 2026. The results propose an internal policy model in eight components, from the inventory of permitted uses to incident response, situate traceability, logging of interactions, identification of user and model version, labeling of synthetic content and human review trail, as the evidentiary prerequisite of any accountability, and systematize the allocation of responsibilities among developer, deployer, user and organization. It is concluded that, in the vacuum between mass adoption and the specific law still in progress, the documented internal policy fulfills a function analogous to that of supervision in administrative contracts: it is what separates the diligent organization from the negligent one when machine-generated content causes harm.
