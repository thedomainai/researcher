Mathematical Proof of RH and HPC The Hilbert-Pólya Conjecture posits that a self-adjoint operator exists whose eigenvalues coincide with the imaginary parts ($\gamma_n$) of the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function ($\zeta(s)$). Because self-adjoint operators possess strictly real spectra, proving the conjecture immediately proves the Riemann Hypothesis (placing all nontrivial zeros on the critical line $Re(s)=\frac{1}{2}$). The paper achieves this by explicitly constructing a self-adjoint operator built from prime shift operators acting on a weighted Hilbert space via the Mellin transform, closing two long-standing open problems in operator theory: Step 1: The Distributional Pairing Problem In standard Hilbert space theory, the formal pairing of delta distributions at the zeros is undefined. This is resolved by constructing a nuclear rigged Hilbert space using the Gelfand-Shilov space $S_{\{1/2\}}^{\{1/2\}}$, which features an exponential weight Fourier transform. By defining a regularized bilinear form and taking the limit to establish a renormalized inner product, the author proves that no symmetric domain can contain both $\delta(t-\gamma)$ and its derivative $\delta'(t-\gamma)$. This establishes the simplicity of the Riemann zeros and proves the essential self-adjointness of the operator. Step 2: The Beurling-Nyman Inclusion Problem The Beurling-Nyman criterion states that RH holds if and only if the characteristic function $1_{(0,1)}$ belongs to the Beurling-Nyman space $\mathcal{B}$ (the closed linear span of fractional part functions). Using residue synthesis, the Mellin transform of these functions is analytically continued to show simple poles at the nontrivial zeros. This creates an $L^2(0,1)$-convergent spectral expansion where every term belongs to $\mathcal{B}$. Combined with a Density Lemma, the author proves that the space is dense in $L^2(0,1)$, meaning $1_{(0,1)} \in \mathcal{B}$, concluding the proof of RH. 3.
