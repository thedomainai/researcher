# From Algorithms to Accountability: Rethinking Ethics, Law, and Governance in Artificial Intelligence

**著者**: ARJOO
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 法学

## Abstract

Artificial Intelligence (AI) has moved from being primarily a computational technology to becoming a decision-making infrastructure that increasingly influences education, healthcare, finance, employment, public administration, security, media, and everyday communication. The growing use of generative AI and autonomous AI systems has created significant opportunities for innovation, but it has also intensified concerns relating to privacy, discrimination, transparency, misinformation, intellectual property, cybersecurity, human autonomy, and accountability. The central challenge is therefore no longer only how intelligent an AI system can become, but how responsibly its capabilities can be designed, deployed, monitored, and governed. This paper examines the relationship between AI ethics, legal regulation, and governance, with particular emphasis on the Indian legal environment and its interaction with international approaches. The study analyses the Information Technology Act, 2000, the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, and the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, alongside international instruments including the European Union Artificial Intelligence Act, UNESCO’s Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence, the OECD AI Principles, and the NIST Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework. The analysis identifies a recurring governance gap: ethical principles often remain voluntary, while existing laws may regulate specific harms without providing a comprehensive AI lifecycle governance mechanism. To address this gap, the paper proposes an integrated Ethics-Law-Risk-Accountability (ELRA) governance framework based on risk classification, impact assessment, traceability, human oversight, continuous monitoring, auditability, and clearly assigned responsibility. A mathematical risk formulation is also proposed to support proportional governance decisions. The paper concludes with practical recommendations for responsible AI adoption in India, including AI impact assessments, algorithmic documentation, human oversight, independent auditing, incident reporting, AI literacy, and continuous regulatory adaptation.
