# Borrowed Shame: Hao Hongmei as a Narrative Resource for Sun Shaoping's Moral Image

**著者**: Khoo-Hong Chang
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 法学

## Abstract

This article reconsiders one of the most frequently moralized episodes in Lu Yao's Ordinary World: Hao Hongmei's theft of handkerchiefs at the end of high school and Sun Shaoping's intervention on her behalf. Existing readings often treat the incident as evidence of Hao's vanity, compromised character, or eventual moral education. Such interpretations remain too close to the evaluative grammar supplied by the novel itself. Through close reading informed by affect theory, the anthropology of the gift, class analysis, and rhetorical narrative ethics, this article argues that the episode organizes a transfer of moral value. Hao's shame originates in a social relation. Graduation gifts impose a debt of reciprocity on a student whose poverty has to remain concealed if she is to preserve ordinary membership in the class. Once she is caught, however, the narrative reallocates agency and interpretive authority. Sun explains her motives, pays for the handkerchiefs, controls the circulation of knowledge, and converts the objects from evidence of theft into gifts that can safely re-enter social exchange. Witnesses then ratify his kindness, while Hao is reduced largely to fear, tears, and gratitude. Her crisis therefore becomes the condition under which Sun's goodness can appear as exceptional, magnanimous, and independent of romantic self-interest. I call this process borrowed shame: the narrative conversion of one character's socially produced humiliation into another character's ethical credit. The article further shows that this structure reappears in Hao's later widowhood and remarriage, where her vulnerability again tests and enlarges male moral character. A counterfocal reading does not deny Sun's compassion. It distinguishes the ethical merit of his action within the story from the unequal distribution of voice, action, and value through which the novel makes that merit legible.
