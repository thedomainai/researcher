# Order, Role, and Individual : A Critique of Modern Legal Person Theory

**著者**: Johan Hermansson
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 法学

## Abstract

In this dissertation, I investigate how the legal person should be understood within legal theory. I do so through a critique of two fundamental theses of modern legal person theory: (i) the realist thesis that posits that legal persons are human individuals, groups of human individuals, or other kinds of non-legal entities; and (ii) the legal subject thesis that posits that all legal subjects are legal persons. My critique is developed in five substantive chapters. In chapter two, I begin by examining the intellectual history of the legal person. The person has not always been understood as an individual. Nor have all legal subjects always been understood as persons. These conceptual metamorphoses are demonstrated through an analysis of how the Latin term ‘persona’ and its modern equivalent ‘person’ have been used in selected historical sources. Chapter three addresses the debate between realists and legalists on whether the legal person is a legal or non-legal entity. I systematise the different variants of the realist and legalist theses, critically evaluate the arguments for and against them, and conclude that their tenability can be questioned. I continue in chapter four by discussing the possibility of a terminological solution to the realist/legalist debate. I do so by systematising the different senses in which realists and legalists have used the term ‘legal person.’ In an elaboration of previous theory. I argue for a distinction between the legal person qua (i) individual, individual in a (ii) purely passive, (iii) represented passive, or (iv) active role, and (v) partial legal order. In chapter five, I instead suggest an ontological solution to the realist/legalist debate: the legal person is more accurately conceptualised as a combination of these senses than as a single one. According to my revised legalist thesis, a legal person is a partial legal order protecting an individual in a passive role through empowering and obligating this individual in a direct active role and/or one or several other individuals in indirect active roles as its representatives. The legal person is a legal construction, but it is a legal construction of human individuals and other non-legal entities. Chapter six is dedicated to a more recent debate on whether all legal subjects are legal persons. According to my core thesis, legal personhood consists of a core of conceptually and contextually necessary legal positions: standing and capacity for disposition of property. This allows for a clear distinction between legal subjects with a limited legal subjectivity and legal persons with a more substantive legal personhood. In chapter seven, the conclusions of the dissertation are combined into a theoretical whole: the Partial Legal Order Theory of Legal Persons, a novel theory of legal persons. The practical utility of this theory is demonstrated through three case studies on the rights of animals, AI, andcorporations.
