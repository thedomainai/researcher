# Beyond data protection: algorithmic decision-making, administrative justice and constitutional accountability in India

**著者**: Aman Sonkar
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 法学

## Abstract

Purpose This paper aims to argue that algorithmic decision-making (ADM) in India should be governed primarily through constitutional and administrative law rather than being treated solely as a data protection issue. It examines whether Articles 14 and 21 of the Constitution, together with the proportionality doctrine developed in Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India, provide an adequate framework for ensuring accountability, transparency and procedural fairness in ADM. The paper further identifies the institutional and legal gaps that permit welfare, policing and surveillance algorithms to operate without meaningful constitutional safeguards. Design/methodology/approach The study adopts a doctrinal legal methodology based on constitutional provisions, judicial decisions and statutory texts. It undertakes a comparative analysis of regulatory frameworks in the European Union, the United Kingdom and Canada to identify principles relevant to the Indian context. The analysis is supported by three case studies involving Aadhaar-linked welfare delivery, predictive policing systems and automated facial recognition technologies to evaluate the constitutional implications of algorithmic governance. Findings The study finds that India’s existing regulatory framework fails to provide adequate legal authorization, reason-giving obligations, contestability mechanisms and independent oversight for high-risk ADM. It demonstrates that these deficiencies are constitutional failures rather than merely regulatory gaps. The paper proposes a three-tier transparency model together with legislative, institutional and judicial reforms, including algorithmic impact assessments, an independent oversight authority and structured judicial review, to ensure constitutionally compliant deployment of government algorithms. Research limitations/implications The analysis is confined to doctrinal legal research and selected case studies from welfare administration, policing and surveillance. It does not include empirical evaluation of algorithmic systems or technical performance testing. Nevertheless, the study provides a constitutional framework for future interdisciplinary research on ADM governance, administrative accountability and judicial oversight in India. Practical implications The proposed framework offers practical guidance for legislators, policymakers, regulators and courts in developing constitutionally compliant governance mechanisms for ADM. The recommendations support the introduction of algorithmic impact assessments, transparency obligations, independent oversight and effective procedural safeguards for public-sector AI systems affecting constitutional rights. Social implications Strengthening constitutional accountability for ADM can enhance transparency, protect fundamental rights and improve public trust in digital governance. The proposed reforms seek to reduce arbitrary decision-making, welfare exclusion, discriminatory policing and unchecked surveillance by ensuring that algorithmic systems remain subject to the rule of law and procedural fairness. Originality/value Unlike existing scholarship that primarily analyses ADM through data protection law, this paper repositions algorithmic governance within the constitutional framework of administrative justice. It demonstrates that established principles under Articles 14 and 21 already provide the normative basis for regulating government algorithms and argues that the principal deficit lies in institutional implementation rather than constitutional doctrine. The proposed transparency model integrates comparative regulatory insights with Indian constitutional jurisprudence to provide a novel framework for algorithmic accountability.
