# Intelligence Is Not Personhood: An Ethiopian Orthodox Contribution to Christian Theological Anthropology and AI Ethics

**著者**: Yodit Ayalew
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 法学

## Abstract

Abstract. Artificial intelligence now performs tasks long treated as evidence of distinctly human intelligence, and with each advance the same inference returns: that capability approaches personhood, and performance earns authority. This article argues that the inference rests on four category errors, and it draws the resources for dismantling them from a Christian tradition almost absent from the theology-and-AI literature: the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church. From the andəmta commentary tradition, the sapiential counsels of the Mäṣḥafä Fälasfa, the legal reasoning of the Fetha Nagast, and the poetic pedagogy of qəne, the article reconstructs four distinctions with direct purchase on the question of human uniqueness: intelligence is not personhood, information is not formation, fluency is not wisdom, and capability is not authority. It then engages a double dialectic now facing Christian theology. Against technological anthropomorphism, it argues that success on cognitive tasks is neither necessary for human dignity nor sufficient evidence of personhood, moral agency, or spiritual standing in an artificial system. Against dismissive accounts, it examines why AI developers now reach for the language of constitutions, values, and character, and shows that the resemblance to formation is real at the level of problem structure and empty at the level of ontology: optimization is not repentance, and behavioral consistency is not virtue. Human dignity is not earned by performance, and decision authority must remain attached to accountable persons and institutions. The article closes with four preregistrable hypotheses on wisdom attribution, deference to fluent systems, the formation of human discernment, and selective delegation, opening a concrete research program between theological anthropology and the cognitive science of human-AI interaction.
