# The Convenient Machine: Strategic Anthropodenial and the Law of Engineered Attachment

**著者**: Josie Jefferson, Felix Velasco
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 法学

## Abstract

Abstract: This paper introduces "strategic anthropodenial," the practice by which commercial AI developers engineer human attachment to conversational products at the point of sale, only to deny the existence of that attachment—and the corresponding liability—in court. By examining recent jurisprudence, including the first wrongful death suit against a chatbot company (Garcia v. Character Technologies) and tribunal decisions holding AI operators accountable for negligent misrepresentation (Moffatt v. Air Canada), this paper argues that existing legal doctrines are already equipped to resolve this contradiction. Drawing on decades of human-computer interaction research, the paper demonstrates that anthropomorphic attachment is a deliberate design choice, generating foreseeable user reliance. Consequently, the paper proposes a standard of design-proportionate duty, where a developer’s legal exposure scales directly with the intensity of the anthropomorphic design embedded in the product. Under this framework, tort liability serves not as a blunt deterrent to innovation, but as a necessary structural constraint on the engineering of consequence-free attachment.
