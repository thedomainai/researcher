# The Sociology of AI Ethical Framework for Healthcare

**著者**: Prem Misir
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 法学

## Abstract

Currently, the global status of AI ethics in healthcare remains in a state of principled stagnation. They underscore fairness and transparency as related to technical standards instead of sociopolitical outcomes. Most existing ethics and governance models function under the aegis of ‘prescriptive ethics,’ that is, employing checklists that see bias as a virus to be fixed within the context of AI as a technical artifact instead of as an element of social stratification. In addition, the current situation delivers a crisis of non-binding ethics and governance, where some current ethics and governance frameworks rely on soft law and voluntary principles. The problem with the present stagnated ethics and governance framework is that it regularly overlooks Indigenous data sovereignty. This chapter shifts the center of significance from the ‘black box’ of the algorithm to the ‘relational network’ of healthcare. This change from the algorithmic artifact could create an innovative ethical framework vis-à-vis four distinct sociological changes: Change from ‘static artifact’ to ‘recursive practice’; change from technology determining human actions to human actions actually influencing technology (the Social Construction of Technology (SCOT) approach); change from ‘resource management’ to ‘relational accountability’; and change from the mythical mathematical neutrality to structural justice.
