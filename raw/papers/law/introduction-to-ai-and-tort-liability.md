# Introduction to AI and Tort Liability

**著者**: Juan Diaz-Granados
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 法学

## Abstract

The introductory chapter to this book provides the contextual background for the discussion and outlines the book’s legal problem, aims, theoretical framework, key doctrinal challenges, scope and general structure. The chapter explains how artificial intelligence (AI) challenges the foundations of tort law. As AI evolves from rule-based programs to autonomous, generative and agentic systems, it increasingly infers, acts and self-modifies without direct human instruction, creating new forms of harm and raising complex questions of liability. Traditional tort doctrines—such as fault, causation, foreseeability and compensable harm—struggle to address AI’s architectural complexity. In response, the book advances a dynamic model of AI tort liability that reconceptualizes tort law as both reparatory and regulatory. The chapter introduces the tort liability matrix, which reallocates and recalibrates liability according to the degree of public or private risk and benefit involved. Grounded primarily in the common law traditions of the United States, the United Kingdom and Australia and enriched by comparative insights from the European Union, this book focuses on negligence as the core analytical framework. It ultimately proposes a prospective, policy-oriented approach that steers innovation toward the public good while preserving fair redress.
