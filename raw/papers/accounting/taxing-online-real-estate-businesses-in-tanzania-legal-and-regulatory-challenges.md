# Taxing Online Real Estate Businesses in Tanzania: Legal and Regulatory Challenges in the Digital Economy

**著者**: Edina Mwamlima
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 会計学

## Abstract

The digital transformation of Tanzania's real estate sector has significantly changed property advertising, brokerage, rental management, and transactions. Property owners, brokers, and digital intermediaries increasingly rely on websites, online marketplaces, and social media platforms to generate income. While Tanzania has gradually expanded its tax laws and electronic transaction regulations to cover digital economic activities, taxing online real estate businesses remains challenging due to legal and regulatory hurdles. This article critically assesses whether Tanzania’s legal and institutional framework is sufficient for taxing online real estate, highlighting key obstacles to effective tax collection. Using doctrinal legal analysis of relevant legislation, regulations, and literature, alongside comparisons with other jurisdictions and international trends, it shows that the issue is no longer the absence of laws but their fragmented implementation and enforcement. Major challenges include identifying the income earner, defining the roles of property owners, brokers, and platforms, establishing taxable nexus in cross-border deals, obtaining reliable third-party information, and integrating electronic payments and registration data. The article contends that while mandatory electronic payments and stricter taxpayer ID rules improve traceability, they are insufficient without interoperable systems and clearer duties for digital intermediaries. It advocates clearer laws for online real estate agents, stronger third-party reporting, integrated data systems, and technology-driven tax administration. These reforms are essential to enhance compliance, ensure legal certainty, promote fairness, and support sustainable growth of Tanzania’s digital economy.
