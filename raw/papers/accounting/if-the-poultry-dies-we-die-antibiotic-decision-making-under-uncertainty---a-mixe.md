# "If the poultry dies, we die". Antibiotic Decision Making Under Uncertainty - A mixed-methods study of Poultry Farmers in Nepal

**著者**: Anica Buckel, Diletta Topazio, Sakar Shivakoti, Megha Raj Banjara, Nabaraj Adhikari
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 会計学

## Abstract

Antibiotics play an important role in disease management in poultry, but their excessive or inappropriate use can contribute to antimicrobial resistance (AMR), making bacterial infections more difficult to treat. Understanding farmers’ antibiotic-use decisions is important for designing effective stewardship interventions, yet research has often focused on knowledge and attitudes as primary drivers. We used an explanatory sequential mixed-methods design, beginning with a survey of 107 poultry farmers assessing antibiotic-related knowledge and practices, perceived financial scarcity, temporal discounting, and risk preferences, followed by qualitative interviews and focus group discussions. Knowledge and attitude measures were largely unrelated to practice, and farmers often reported knowingly departing from recommended stewardship practices. Only confidence in managing poultry health without antimicrobials was associated with fewer misuse practices after correction for multiple testing. Among the psychological measures, higher risk tolerance was linked to more self-treatment and over-the-counter purchasing, whereas greater perceived financial scarcity was linked to less over-the-counter purchasing. In interviews, farmers often framed antibiotic use as a necessity rather than a choice. They described a production environment that felt uncertain, in which alternative disease prevention and management strategies seemed unreliable. In this context antibiotics may function as a means of protecting their flocks and investments against disease-related losses amid debt, market uncertainty, and distrust of alternatives. Antibiotic stewardship efforts may therefore need to go beyond information provision to address farmers’ fears and concerns related to immediate flock losses and support access to trusted, demonstrably effective alternatives.
