# Transforming Thoracic Surgery with Artificial Intelligence and Extended Reality

**著者**: Quinten Jonathan Mank
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 会計学

## Abstract

Anatomical lung resections, especially segmentectomies performed through VATS or RATS, require the surgeon to know the patient’s bronchovascular anatomy precisely. Conventional 2D CT leaves the surgeon to reconstruct this 3D anatomy mentally. Static preoperative imaging also fails to capture how the lung deforms during surgery. This thesis investigates how artificial intelligence (AI), 3D reconstruction, physics-based modelling and extended reality (XR) can support thoracic surgery across three phases: preoperative planning, intraoperative guidance and postoperative evaluation. Part I replaces a manual, open-source reconstruction workflow, which takes 1.5–2 hours per patient, with deep learning models (nnU-Net). These models segment pulmonary arteries and veins automatically in under 5 minutes, with Dice scores of 0.91–0.92. They also detect and measure thoracic lymph nodes automatically (Dice 0.74). In congenital lung abnormalities, viewing AI-generated models in virtual reality improved anatomical understanding and made lung-sparing segmentectomies possible. Part II introduces simulated reality: deformable 4D lung models built with AI and the finite element method (Pulmo-SR), which showed high anatomical accuracy and a latency of 0.23 seconds. This part also describes the first clinical use of AI-driven augmented reality in robotic lung surgery, with real-time instrument recognition. It then covers automated recognition of anatomical orientations in surgical video. Part III shows that postoperative AI-based 3D reconstruction found clinically relevant differences between the intended and the achieved resection in nearly one-third of segmentectomies planned on 2D imaging. This makes the method useful as an objective audit tool.
