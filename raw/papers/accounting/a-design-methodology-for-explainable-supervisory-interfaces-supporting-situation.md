# A Design Methodology for Explainable Supervisory Interfaces Supporting Situation Awareness and Human-AI Collaboration in Augmented SOCs

**著者**: Kahina Amokrane, Reda Yaich, Emeline DURIMEL, Guillaume Giraud, Christelle Fouquiau
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 会計学

## Abstract

The increasing integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) agents into monitoring environments, such as Security Operations Centers (SOCs), is significantly transforming analysis, decision-making, and incident response activities. While these agents allow the automation of certain processes, they also raise new challenges regarding transparency, oversight, and human–AI coordination. Operators must be able to understand the status and behavior of AI agents, oversee their actions, adjust task delegation, and maintain awareness of their own ability to intervene. In this paper, we propose a methodology to design an explainable monitoring dashboard intended to support interaction between a human operator and a set of AI agents. The design principles are based on the concepts of Situation Awareness and Human-AI Teaming, as well as recommendations from research in Human-AI Interaction. The methodology is applied to the context of augmented SOC where an analyst monitors and analyzes security events in real time to identify potential threats, assess alerts, and determine appropriate responses. More specifically, the dashboard is structured around four complementary dimensions: (i) visualizing the operational status of the AI agents, (ii) maintaining a shared understanding of the collaborative activity between humans and the agents, (iii) supporting adjustable task delegation through supervision and reallocation mechanisms, and (iv) taking into account the operator’s cognitive and operational state. This approach leads to the definition of an interface that incorporates mechanisms for transparency, explainability, and human control, aimed at improving understanding of AI agents’ behavior while keeping the operator at the center of the decision-making loop
