# Post Occupancy Evaluation of LEED BD+C- and LEED O+M-Certified Buildings in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia

**著者**: Alya Shuailah, Mady Mohamed
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 会計学

## Abstract

As Saudi Arabia advances toward its Vision 2030 goals, sustainable practices in the built environment have emerged as a national priority. LEED (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design) certification aligns with the Kingdom’s commitment to reducing energy use, enhancing environmental performance, and improving quality of life. This research aims to assess the relationship between different types of LEED certification—Building Design and Construction (BD+C) and Operations and Maintenance (O+M)—and occupant satisfaction in terms of indoor environmental quality (IEQ) in office buildings in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia. The study evaluates satisfaction levels across five key IEQ factors: thermal comfort, indoor air quality, visual comfort, acoustic comfort, and overall IEQ satisfaction. Framed as an exploratory two-case post-occupancy evaluation (POE) combining a questionnaire survey with semi-structured interviews, the study found no statistically significant difference between the two buildings except for “thermal comfort”, which favored the O+M-certified building (p = 0.03, Cohen’s d = 0.47); however, this difference does not remain significant after correction for multiple comparisons. Both pathways were associated with generally acceptable indoor environmental conditions in the two buildings studied. Given the two-case design and contextual differences between the buildings, the findings are exploratory rather than evidence of certification-pathway effects.
