# AI and Digital Colonialism in Africa: Artificial Intelligence, as Currently Built and Governed, Is Extractive by Default

**著者**: Getty Musiiwa
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 会計学

## Abstract

This African Position Paper examines how artificial intelligence reproduces structural asymmetries between Africa and the centres of digital power, and sets out what institutions should do about it. It tests a claim now common in public debate: that AI is inherently an instrument of digital colonialism. The evidence does not support that strong claim. It does support the paper's central argument: AI, as currently built and governed, is extractive by default. Without deliberate intervention, the systems that gather training data, employ data workers, host computation and deploy autonomous agents move value and interpretive authority away from African communities. The evidence includes: an original calculation from Common Crawl's October 2026 archive, which shows thirty detectable African languages together making up 0.053% of web pages, against 42.6% for English; Africa's share of global data-centre capacity, which is under 1%; documented pay and conditions in outsourced AI data work in Kenya. The paper also documents Africa's response, which it calls the "awareness record". This includes the Malabo Convention, the AU Data Policy Framework, the AU Continental AI Strategy, the Kigali Declaration, Kenyan court rulings against Meta, the African Content Moderators Union, and open African-language models such as InkubaLM. Its conclusion is that the gap is no longer awareness but enforcement, capacity and ownership. It compares African approaches with those of Latin America, India, China, Russia, France and the EU, Germany and the Nordic open-source tradition. It analyses agentic knowledge production and model collapse as emerging risks. It also reports a reflexive audit in which an AI research agent served as both research instrument and object of study. The paper proposes the Extraction Stack, a five-layer framework covering language, labour, data, infrastructure and agents. It closes with policy recommendations addressed to the African Union, member states, universities and archives, funders, AI developers and communities, and includes an institutional self-audit checklist.
