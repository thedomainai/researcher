# Smart Soil Diagnostics for Sustainable Agriculture: Combining Micronutrient Sensing, Pesticide Monitoring, and AI-Assisted Soil Management

**著者**: G. Tejaswini, N. Srinivasa Rao, M. Padmaja
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: オペレーション管理

## Abstract

Soil management for sustainable agriculture requires timely information about nutrient availability and contaminant burdens at a spatial resolution that conventional sampling alone may not provide.Micronutrient deficiencies can limit crop productivity, while excessive or persistent pesticide residues may affect soil organisms, water quality, and foodsystem safety.This review examines how proximal soil sensors, portable analytical devices, laboratory reference methods, Internet of Things (IoT) connectivity, and artificial intelligence (AI) can be combined into a practical soil-diagnostics framework.Electrochemical sensors and ionselective electrodes are promising for selected ionic species and pH, whereas optical and spectroscopic methods can support rapid screening when models are calibrated for local soil conditions.Pesticide monitoring presents a more complex analytical challenge because residues vary in chemistry, sorption, degradation, and extraction efficiency; field sensors therefore complement rather than universally replace validated chromatographic methods.AI can fuse sensor signals with soil texture, moisture, weather, crop history, and laboratory measurements to generate spatially explicit recommendations, provided that training data are representative and uncertainty is reported.The article proposes an integrated workflow, compares sensing options, identifies qualityassurance requirements, and outlines a phased implementation strategy.The central conclusion is that sustainable smart-soil management should be built around sensor-laboratory complementarity, transparent decision rules, and agronomic validation-not sensor readings or AI predictions in isolation.
