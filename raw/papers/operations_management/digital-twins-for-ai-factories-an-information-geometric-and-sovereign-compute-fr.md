# Digital Twins for AI Factories: An Information-Geometric and Sovereign-Compute Framework for Planning, Energy-Constrained Operation and Lifecycle Optimisation

**著者**: Syed Muntasir Mamun
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: オペレーション管理

## Abstract

Demand for AI factories—specialised high-density computing infrastructure for the development and operation of artificial intelligence models—is accelerating. Planning and operating these facilities differs fundamentally from conventional data centres because of extreme power densities, rapid hardware iteration, and binding constraints on electricity and cooling. Digital twins, understood as high-fidelity virtual representations that couple physical (thermal, electrical, mechanical) and logical (compute, network, workload) layers, offer a means of testing configurations, identifying bottlenecks and simulating energy and performance outcomes before capital is committed, and of supporting continuous optimisation once facilities are operational. This paper develops an information-geometric and active-inference account of such twins as statistical manifolds on which natural gradient flows minimise variational free energy. It situates the twin within the joint optimisation of capacity, energy, cooling and cost; examines the problem of long-term investment under rapid technological change; specifies data and organisational requirements for accuracy; assesses lifecycle value and evidentiary standards; bounds confidence in simulation relative to reality; and evaluates the business case against capital-light alternatives. Drawing on the architecture of a Global South Compute Bank, the paper further shows how digital twins can be institutionalised within fractional compute reserves, clearinghouse mechanisms and cognitive monetary policy so that capacity is recycled rather than stranded. The resulting framework treats the AI factory as a jointly optimised manifold whose continuous representation by a physics-informed, information-geometric twin is the single most important institutional change required for efficiency, resilience and commercial viability over a five-year horizon.
