# From Diagnosis to Digital Execution: AI-Enabled Materials Management for High-Intensity Operations

**著者**: Angie Milne, Lee Vettese
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: オペレーション管理

## Abstract

Abstract In high-intensity energy operations, material availability depends on more than the accuracy of an enterprise resource planning inventory balance. A component may be recorded as available and still be unusable because its location, condition, certification or readiness for the work cannot be confirmed. The resulting searches, expediting, emergency purchasing and workarounds create a form of operational friction that is often managed locally until the cumulative effect appears in maintenance delay, lost productivity, excess working capital or avoidable logistics activity. A diagnostic-led transformation method is presented using practitioner evidence from energy and other asset-intensive operations. The method combines site engagement, benchmarking, value stream mapping, direct process observation and data analysis to identify where material flow is breaking down. Critical-to-quality requirements are then translated into a future-state operating model before the digital architecture is selected. Typical execution controls include master-data remediation, barcode or Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) identification, mobile warehouse tasks, two-way Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) integration, automated exception workflows, supplier-performance visibility, risk-based inventory policy and AI-enabled optimisation where the data and decision are suitable for probabilistic modelling. Across the case evidence, digitally controlled execution produced material improvements in processing speed, traceability, training burden, inventory performance and logistics flow. The strongest results did not come from technology deployed in isolation. They came from removing process variation, clarifying ownership and decision rights, improving data quality and then using digital tools to enforce the redesigned flow. The approach is particularly applicable to Middle Eastern operations, where large-scale energy investment, complex contractor ecosystems, remote and offshore logistics, localisation objectives and high expectations for uptime increase the cost of material uncertainty. Materials management is therefore treated here as an operational assurance discipline supported by digital and AI capability, rather than as a transactional warehouse function.
