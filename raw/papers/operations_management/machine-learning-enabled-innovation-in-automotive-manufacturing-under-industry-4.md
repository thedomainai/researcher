# Machine Learning-Enabled Innovation in Automotive Manufacturing Under Industry 4.0: A Review

**著者**: L. O. Ajuka, D. Nasamu, Okoruwa V.O., I. Aderibigbe, M. K. Odunfa
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: オペレーション管理

## Abstract

Automotive manufacturing is transitioning toward Industry 4.0 cyber physical production systems, where IIoT sensing, connectivity, and edge–cloud computing in practice link physical processes with enterprise decision layers. In this context, machine learning (ML) supports quality inspection, predictive maintenance, process optimisation and adaptive control, production planning and scheduling, logistics and traceability, and energy and sustainability management. Large-scale industrialisation remains constrained by heterogeneous and imbalanced data, labelling cost, model drift under non-stationary operations, and fragmented governance across plants. Additional barriers include limited guidance on machine learning operations for monitoring, reproducibility, and safe updates in cyber physical environments, alongside IT/OT integration constraints across PLC/SCADA/MES/ERP stacks. As models become more complex, the lack of standardised explainable AI (XAI) practices for safety- and quality-critical decisions limits auditability and regulatory acceptance. This study synthesises the end-to-end pathway from shop-floor data generation to enterprise-scale deployment, organising evidence around ISA-95/IEC 62264 enterprise-to-control integration and RAMI 4.0 aligned reference architectures. We propose a taxonomy linking plant data sources and digital infrastructure to ML tasks and measurable innovation outcomes, and map evidence maturity from laboratory validation to pilot and sustained deployment. The synthesis indicates that the most mature clusters occur in vision-based inspection and asset health monitoring, whereas closed-loop optimisation, adaptive scheduling, and cross-enterprise sustainability analytics remain less mature due to real-time, safety, and governance requirements. In conclusion, we recommend future research focusing on reproducible benchmarking, robust multi-plant generalisation, explainable and verifiable ML for safety-critical production, hybrid digital-twin architectures, and energy-aware AI practices to enable scalable and trustworthy Industry 4.0 deployment.
