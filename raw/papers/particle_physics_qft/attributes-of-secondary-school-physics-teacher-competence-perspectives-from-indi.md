# Attributes of secondary school physics teacher competence: perspectives from Indian educators

**著者**: Deena Davis, Benny Thomas, Hementhakumara V
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 素粒子・場の量子論

## Abstract

Physics education is epistemologically distinctive, yet persistent student misconceptions, low student achievement, and inadequate subject-specific teacher preparation frequently hinder effective teaching. While generic teacher competence frameworks exist, they are largely derived from well-resourced Western contexts and fail to account for how competence is enacted under the unique systemic constraints of the Global South. To address this gap, this qualitative exploratory study investigates the core attributes of a competent physics teacher from the perspectives of 22 experienced secondary educators in India. Participants were selected through maximum variation purposive sampling across diverse curricular, institutional, and geographic contexts. Data were collected through semi-structured interviews and analyzed using a hybrid deductive-inductive thematic network approach. Three interrelated dimensions of competence emerged: cognitive foundations, the enactive domain of competence, and the professional self. The findings indicate that Indian physics teachers rely on adaptive expertise, drawing on experimental, technological, and mathematical skills, alongside a strong physics identity, to navigate systemic constraints such as examination-driven curricula and resource deficits. The study offers empirically grounded insights for developing discipline-specific competence frameworks. It also provides practical recommendations to improve teacher education, instructional quality, and students’ engagement in physics.
