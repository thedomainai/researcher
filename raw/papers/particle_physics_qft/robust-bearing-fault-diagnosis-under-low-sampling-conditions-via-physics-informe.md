# Robust bearing fault diagnosis under low sampling conditions via physics-informed feature engineering and optimized SVM with discriminant learning

**著者**: Gigih Priyandoko, Aviv Yuniar Rahman
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 素粒子・場の量子論

## Abstract

The evaluation of bearing condition through low-rate current assessments is appealing for economical motor oversight, though 10 Hz data collection is unable to identify the traditional high-frequency bearing elements employed in spectral MCSA. This research evaluates whether slower current envelope and statistical variations remain discriminative. The proposed pipeline combines several different features, including statistical, entropy, autoregressive, Teager energy operator, and ball-oriented descriptors. These are combined with Fisher-score feature selection, linear discriminant analysis, and an RBF support vector machine with confidence-weighted trial voting. Twenty-one independent laboratory trials were analyzed, including seven healthy trials, seven ball-fault trials, and seven inner-race-fault trials. The data were split into seven groups, with each group having one full trial in each group. All of the data preparation, feature selection, and model tuning were done in the training group. The configuration that was selected yielded 70/72 accurate out-of-fold window predictions (97.22%; Wilson 95% CI: 90.4-99.2%) and 21/21 correct trial decisions (100%; Wilson 95% CI: 84.5-100%). Trial-level accuracy was 100% in every outer fold (mean 100%, SD 0 percentage points); however, this reflects only three test trials per fold. The results show that this is possible in one controlled motor-bearing setup, but not in terms of external robustness or the direct recovery of the bearing's characteristic frequencies. Before industrial generalization, validation across motors, loads, noise levels, fault severities, and an external dataset is required.
