# Magnetic resonance imaging and spatial localized spectroscopy using intermolecular multiple-quantum coherences: techniques and clinical utility for brain

**著者**: Dan E. Demco, Radu Fechete, Ruben Emanuel Nechifor
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 素粒子・場の量子論

## Abstract

This review is focused on the investigation on brain microstructure using advanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and spectroscopy (MRS). These echniques were introduced in the last decades using NMR based on intermolecular multiplequantum coherences (iMQCs), excited and detected in the presence of the long-range dipolar inter-nuclear couplings induced by correlated gradients. The origin of multiple spin echoes and nonlinear coherence transfer echoes based on COSY revamped by asymmetric Z-gradient echo detection (CRAZED) in liquids are discussed using quantum-mechanics formalism. The accent of this review is on the intermolecular zero-quantum coherences (iZQCs) and intermolecular double-quantum coherences (iDQCs) excitation, evolution, and detection in the presence of correlated field gradients using the CRAZED pulse sequences. The applications of this pulse sequence to reduce the effect of inhomogeneous magnetic fields and to increase the image resolution is reviewed in the field of magnetic resonance imaging and localized spectroscopy with the accent on brain investigations. The physics related to the used of CRAZED methods is also addressed to produce fundamentally different contrast than does conventional imaging. The effects of spatial inhomogeneity and temporal instability of the NMR made at ultra-high magnetic field are nearly eliminated using iZQCs, thereby achieving a resolution enhancement. This will open new frontiers in the nonlinear spatial localized NMR spectroscopy and magnetic resonance imaging. The large nuclear magnetization induced by different hyperpolarization methods allows detection of intermolecular multiple-quantum coherences in liquids, for nuclei in low abundance and/or having low gyromagnetic ratio, and even in low density matter like gases.
