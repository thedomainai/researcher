# Dynamic brand experience and customer equity: effects on purchase intention through brand relationship quality

**著者**: Sayani Biswas, P. Aruna
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: meta_analysis | **分野**: マーケティング

## Abstract

Purpose With the digitally immersive brand environment, brand-consumer engagements have transformed from rigid brand-focused interactions into fluid technology-enriched encounters. The present research focuses on the impact of Dynamic Brand Experience (DBE), with AI-powered personalisation, AR/VR interfaces, and frictionless omnichannel integration, on consumer behavioural outcomes in India's beauty industry. It particularly assesses how DBE helps develop customer equity elements and how it combines with Brand Relationship Quality (BRQ) in shaping purchase intention. Design/methodology/approach A cross-sectional descriptive study design was adopted, collecting information from 413 Indian beauty product consumers who had already experienced brand technologies. Purposive sampling was employed to make the participants’ experiences meaningful. A structured questionnaire was administered, and the data were analysed using partial least squares structural equation modelling (PLS-SEM) in SmartPLS 4, assessing the measurement model (reliability, convergent and discriminant validity), the structural model (R-square, effect sizes, and bootstrapped path significance), and specific indirect effects, with common method bias evaluated through Harman's single-factor test. The Value Equity scale did not meet reliability standards and was excluded from the model. Findings Dynamic Brand Experience shapes purchase intention chiefly by building relationship equity ( β = 0.493), which is by far the strongest antecedent of Brand Relationship Quality ( β = 0.731; f² = 1.150), and BRQ is the dominant driver of purchase intention ( β = 0.597). The specific indirect effect RE → BRQ → PI ( β = 0.437) is the single most influential pathway, with the serial route DBE → RE → BRQ → PI ( β = 0.215) also substantial. Brand Equity is behaviourally negligible, and the Value Equity scale could not be validly measured and was excluded; the study therefore advances no claim regarding value equity. Moderation by income and gender is identified as a direction for future empirical research. Originality/value This research contributes to theory by bringing together Customer Equity Framework and Service-Dominant Logic in a digital brand setting, and providing empirical evidence for the asymmetrical effects of customer equity dimensions on consumer intention. It highlights the move from symbolic brand heritage to value-based, emotionally resonant experiences, especially in digitally engaged emerging economies. By explicitly delimiting the scope of the current empirical investigation to mediation, the study also delineates a clear agenda for future moderation research involving income and gender as boundary conditions. Research limitations/implications A cross-sectional study design restricts longitudinal insight into changing brand-consumer relationships. Experimental or longitudinal study designs, or cross-cultural settings, in future research can increase generalisability and identify dynamic behavioural trends. Additionally, the moderation effects of income and gender on the BRQ–purchase intention pathway, while theoretically conceptualised in this study, were not empirically tested and represent a key limitation of the current work. Additionally, some contextual data cited in this study (e.g., Deloitte, 2023 ; KPMG, 2020 ) originate from industry reports rather than peer-reviewed sources. While these are used solely to establish market context, future studies should anchor contextual claims more firmly in peer-reviewed empirical literature on Indian digital consumer behaviour. Practical implications This research suggests the importance for beauty retailers to make digitally immersive experiences like AR try-ons, AI-powered consultations, and personalised recommendation engines the focal point to tap into consumer value and participation. Old brand equity strategies based on heritage should give way to adaptive, personalised branding campaigns. Since Brand Relationship Quality cannot equally mediate consumer responses, retailers should actively seek to establish trust, satisfaction, and emotional attachment. Firms should consider adopting a two-pronged strategy in digitally fragmented markets like India: high-tech, immersive technologies for urban consumers and mobile-optimized, utility-based solutions for mass segments. Interactive, ongoing conversations through influencers and user-generated content are among the key drivers of brand visibility and consumer co-creation in digitally fragmented markets.
