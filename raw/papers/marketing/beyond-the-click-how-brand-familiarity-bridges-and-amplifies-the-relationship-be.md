# Beyond the Click: How Brand Familiarity Bridges and Amplifies the Relationship Between Online Customer Experience and Shopping Behavior Across Three Asian Markets

**著者**: Altaf Hussain Pirzado, Abu Muslim Jamal, Saif Hasan, Shaban Mussa Kalinga, Nahom Haileselassie Bihon
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: マーケティング

## Abstract

This study examines the influence of online customer experience on online shopping behavior and investigates the dual role of brand familiarity as both a mediating mechanism and a moderating condition across Pakistan, China, and Bangladesh.Data came from a cross-sectional survey of 887 online consumers from Pakistan (n = 308), China (n = 292), and Bangladesh (n = 287). Grounded in the Stimulus–Organism–Response (S-O-R) framework, the analysis used SPSS 28 and Hayes’ PROCESS macro: regression, bootstrapped mediation (Model 4), moderation (Model 1), and one-way ANOVA with Tukey post hoc tests.Online customer experience was positively associated with online shopping behavior (β = 0.60, p < 0.001) and brand familiarity (β = 0.57, p < 0.001). Brand familiarity was positively associated with online shopping behavior (β = 0.41, p < 0.001), partially mediating the relationship between online customer experience and online shopping behavior (indirect effect β = 0.23, 95% CI [0.15, 0.32]). Brand familiarity also strengthened this relationship (interaction β = 0.12, p < 0.001). Cross-country comparisons revealed significantly higher levels of all three constructs in China than in Pakistan and Bangladesh.This study extends the Stimulus–Organism–Response (S-O-R) framework by examining brand familiarity as an organismic state with two roles. It investigates whether brand familiarity mediates the relationship between online customer experience and shopping behavior and whether it strengthens that relationship. By examining these mechanisms in three Asian e-commerce markets at different stages of digital maturity, the study provides insight into how online experiences relate to shopping behavior. The findings also point to the value of combining high-quality online experiences with strategies that build brand familiarity.
