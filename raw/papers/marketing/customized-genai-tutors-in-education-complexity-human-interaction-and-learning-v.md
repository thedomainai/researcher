# Customized GenAI tutors in education: complexity, human interaction, and learning value

**著者**: Giulia Pavone
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: マーケティング

## Abstract

Purpose This study aims to examine how learning value and usage intentions emerge in artificial intelligence (AI)-enabled educational services when generative AI (GenAI) tutors are customized for specific pedagogical roles. Drawing on a service-dominant logic perspective, it investigates how perceived topic complexity and learners’ need for human interaction shape learning outcomes, value creation and continued usage intentions. Design/methodology/approach Two field studies are conducted, with customization operationalized as an ex-ante pedagogical role configuration. Study 1, a hybrid professor-supported course, uses thematic analysis guided by Bloom’s Taxonomy to examine perceptions of a customized GPT-based tutor. Study 2, a fully autonomous online module, applies pre/post-tests and moderated mediation analysis. Findings GenAI tutors configured for lower-order cognitive support (memorization and comprehension) are perceived as valuable. However, effectiveness depends on contextual conditions. In fully AI-mediated learning, perceived topic complexity negatively affects memorization, comprehension and perceived value. Under higher topic complexity, learners with stronger needs for human interaction perceive greater pedagogical value in the GenAI tutor. Continued usage intentions are driven by comprehension and perceived value rather than memorization. Practical implications Findings highlight the importance of explicit pedagogical role definition, alignment between intended and enacted functions and adapting AI integration to learner needs. GenAI tutors configured to support foundational learning activities appear particularly suitable for lower-order cognitive processes, while complex topics may require hybrid human–AI configurations that preserve social support. Originality/value This research conceptualizes customization as a service design principle in human–AI educational services and shows how value creation depends on service conditions using Bloom’s Taxonomy.
