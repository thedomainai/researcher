## Abstract

Episode summary: Consumer support is cratering while enterprise customers get dedicated engineers, fifteen-minute response times, and SWAT-team-style incident management. We unpack the role of technical account managers, how SLAs are actually structured, and what happens when support is expert-to-expert instead of bot-to-everyone. The gap between tiers isn't just widening—it's becoming two completely different categories of service. Show Notes The gap between consumer and enterprise customer support isn't just large—it's becoming two entirely different product categories. While most users hit AI chatbots that Gartner says have driven consumer satisfaction down twelve points year over year, enterprise customers with budgets above $250,000 in annual contract value get something fundamentally different: a dedicated technical account manager embedded in their operations. The TAM role is the biggest structural differentiator. Unlike tier-one agents who wait for tickets, TAMs sit in your Slack, know your architecture, and prevent problems before they happen. They conduct quarterly architecture reviews, run health checks, and advocate for your priorities inside the vendor's engineering organization. This comes at a cost—premium support can add 25-35% to annual contracts—but for companies where an hour of downtime costs six figures, the math works. Service level agreements form the reactive complement to proactive TAM support. Enterprise SLAs specify response times as tight as fifteen minutes for severity-one outages, with critical situation management teams that run war rooms and provide thirty-minute status updates. These guarantees are backed by service credits and termination clauses, though credits never approach the actual cost of an outage. The consumer model, by contrast, is designed for deflection rather than resolution—bots handle simple queries poorly while humans become harder to reach, creating an experience that Forrester found 63% of consumers say has worsened since chatbots were introduced.
