# Beyond Agent Orchestration: A Universal Ecosystem of Proactive and Autonomous Collaboration Among Browsers, Applications, and AI Agents

**著者**: Jincheng Zhang
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 複雑系科学

## Abstract

The emergence of autonomous artificial intelligence (AI) agents is transforming the relationship between humans, software applications, and digital environments. However, much of the current development of agentic systems remains organized around conventional application-centric architectures or coordinator-driven multi-agent frameworks, in which an agent receives instructions, invokes predefined tools, delegates subtasks, and aggregates the results. Although these approaches enable increasingly sophisticated automation, they do not fully capture a broader possibility: software applications themselves could become autonomous participants in an interconnected ecosystem, capable of initiating, sustaining, and adapting collaborative relationships with other applications and agents without waiting for a central coordinator to assign every interaction. This paper proposes the Universal Proactive Agentic Software Ecosystem (UPASE), a conceptual architectural framework in which any compatible web browser, mobile application, desktop application, web service, or independent AI agent can participate as an autonomous and interoperable agent. Its central proposition is that every browser can function as an agent, every application can function as an agent, and agents can be represented and accessed through browser or application environments. More importantly, these participants should possess the ability to proactively discover relevant entities, initiate communication, recognize emerging dependencies, exchange information, propose actions, negotiate constraints, respond to environmental changes, and independently initiate mutually beneficial collaboration. Such collaboration is not restricted to predefined task assignments or centralized orchestration. Instead, it emerges through continuous, context-sensitive interactions among autonomous participants that retain their own operational identities, capabilities, objectives, and decision-making responsibilities. Within this ecosystem, a Personal User Agent represents the user's goals, preferences, and authorization boundaries, but does not necessarily control every collaborative interaction. When a user requests a complex objective, relevant browser agents, application agents, and independent agents can establish cooperative relationships. They may subsequently initiate additional interactions when they detect relevant information, changing conditions, conflicts, opportunities, or dependencies. For example, an airline application agent detecting a flight delay could independently contact a hotel application agent, a mapping agent, and a calendar agent to exchange relevant information and develop coordinated alternatives, even before receiving a specific instruction from the user's agent to perform those individual communications. The proposed framework introduces six conceptual foundations: universal software agentization, bidirectional browser–application–agent representation, autonomous contextual awareness, proactive mutual communication, emergent collaborative relationships, and continuous cross-application adaptation. It also examines shared task context, distributed initiative, reciprocal decision-making, authorization, trust, and accountability as necessary conditions for responsible collaboration. The paper distinguishes proactive software-ecosystem collaboration from conventional tool invocation, predefined multi-agent workflows, and centralized agent orchestration. It further discusses relevant prior research, including Internet of Agents, Web of Agents, and contemporary agent-native application frameworks, and identifies the remaining architectural research questions concerning their extension to a universal ecosystem of independently initiated software collaboration. To the best of our knowledge, this paper presents a distinct unified architectural proposal centered on proactive, mutually initiated collaboration among browser agents, application agents, and independent AI agents as participants in a universal software ecosystem. This qualified novelty claim concerns the particular combination and scope of the proposed architecture, not the invention of autonomous agents, proactive behavior, multi-agent collaboration, or agent interoperability individually. The proposal is conceptual and requires implementation, systematic prior-art verification, and empirical evaluation.
