## Abstract

Listen and ask questions on NotebookLM: https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/854a4cd7-0558-48fd-9be7-6aa8b199dca6?authuser=1 The Logic of Logos: Naming, Covenant, Fulfillment, and Recursive Meaning from Adam to AI develops a systems-oriented account of Logos as the mechanism by which meaning becomes transmissible across time, embodiment, media, and changing technological substrates. Working from a Catholic theological spine while engaging systems theory, cybernetics, information theory, predictive processing, media theory, computation, and artificial intelligence, the paper argues that religious tradition can be understood as a recursive transmission architecture rather than merely a collection of isolated doctrines. The work traces a continuous symbolic and operational lineage from Adamic naming and semantic addressability, through covenantal continuity in Abraham, divine identity in Exodus, prophetic correction, Davidic memory structures, Marian consent, Incarnation, Eucharistic repetition, conciliar semantic stabilization, Augustinian memory, Thomistic formal logic, Ignatian discernment, Jesuit adaptation, and contemporary AI-mediated language systems. Special attention is given to the bidirectional structure of meaning in time: earlier symbols move forward as priors, while later fulfillments reveal backward what those symbols were carrying. This principle is explored through scripture, Catholic theology, sacramental systems, networking theory, version-control analogies, semantic drift, AI hallucination, formal proof systems such as Lean 4, and computational models of language. Rather than reducing theology to computation or AI to mysticism, the paper develops a shared systems grammar for describing how coherence survives across changing media and historical conditions. The work proposes that Catholic tradition functions as a long-duration Logos transmission system capable of preserving intelligibility through naming, relation, ritual, doctrine, memory, embodiment, and adaptive translation. The paper includes extensive engagement with biblical texts, patristics, scholastic theology, cybernetics, media theory, AI alignment, semantic systems, and modern physics, including an appendix on Albert Einstein, Georges Lemaître, recursive scientific discovery, and the Einstein–Rosen bridge as examples of coherence emerging through layered reinterpretation.
