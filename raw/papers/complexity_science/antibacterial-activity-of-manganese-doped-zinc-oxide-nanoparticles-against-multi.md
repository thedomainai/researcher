# Antibacterial Activity Of Manganese-Doped Zinc Oxide Nanoparticles Against Multidrug Resistant Salmonella Typhi

**著者**: Nasir Shah Khalil, Muhammad Saddam, Faraz Ahmad Khan, Shahzad Anwar Durrani, Muhammad Rizwan
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 複雑系科学

## Abstract

Background: Multiple drug resistant (MDR) Salmonella Typhi is a public health threat. Due to their unique physicochemical properties, the manganese doped with zinc oxide nanoparticles (Mn-ZnO NPs) have become of interest as possible antimicrobial agents. Background: Multi drug resistant (MDR) Salmonella Objective: This research aims at synthesis and characterization of Mn-ZnO NPs and its antibacterial activity and hemocompatibility. Methodology: Chemical co-precipitation method was used for the synthesis of Mn-ZnO NPs. UV–Vis, XRD, EDX, SEM and FTIR were employed to characterize the nanoparticles. Red blood cell (RBC) hemolysis assay was used to evaluate hemocompatibility of red blood cell (RBC). The antibacterial activity against the MDR S. Typhi was determined by agar well diffusion method and minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) was determined by broth based assay. Results: The absorption peak of Mn-ZnO NPs is 372 nm and the pure ZnO NPs is 360 nm in the UV-Vis spectroscopy. XRD patterns display the hexagonal wurtzite structure and EDX analysis revealed the presence of zinc (56.3%), oxygen (38.2%) and manganese (5.5%). A few agglomeration and 40-80 nm particles were identified by the SEM. FTIR spectra showed typical peaks at 3412, 1635 and 435 cm-1. Hemolysis was 4.5% at 50 µg/mL and increased to 10.1% at 100 µg/mL. The diameter of the inhibition zone grew from 8.4 mm at 20 µg/mL to 16.8 mm at 60 µg/mL concentrations. The MIC of the MDR S. Typhi was 62.5 µg/mL. Conclusion: The concentration-dependent antibacterial activity of the Mn-ZnO NPs against MDR S. Typhi was observed and the relatively low hemolytic activity of the NPs was observed at low concentration. They need further investigation for their antimicrobial activity, action mechanism and for their safety concerning potential biomedical applications.
