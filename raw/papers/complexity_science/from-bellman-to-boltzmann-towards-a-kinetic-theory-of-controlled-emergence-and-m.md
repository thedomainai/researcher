# From Bellman to Boltzmann: Towards a Kinetic Theory of Controlled Emergence and Meta-Cognition

**著者**: Jia Liu, Alvaro Velasquez, Susmit Jha, Nathaniel D. Bastian
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 複雑系科学

## Abstract

We propose a research vision for a kinetic theory of collective intelligence in large-scale multi-agent learning systems. Contemporary multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) provides powerful tools for optimizing individual agents, while mean-field game theory characterizes population-level decision-making in the large population limit. Yet, these frameworks offer limited mathematical understanding of how higher-order collective structures (e.g., coordination, specialization, adaptive communication, and meta-cognition) emerge from microscopic interactions among heterogeneous learning agents. Inspired by statistical mechanics, we argue that a mesoscopic kinetic description can provide a missing bridge between individual learning dynamics and macroscopic collective behavior. Building on existing connections between Boltzmann-type equations and mean-field games, we envision Bellman--Boltzmann dynamics in which Bellman decision making is coupled with Boltzmann-type interaction dynamics over heterogeneous agent states, including policies, beliefs, memory, and other cognitive variables. This perspective raises fundamental questions about the well-posedness and mean-field limits of such systems, entropy and Lyapunov principles for learning dynamics, phase transitions in collective behavior, and the emergence and control of collective meta-cognition. In this paper, we outline an ambitious mathematical research program spanning microscopic agent models, kinetic equations, macroscopic limits, and controllable emergence and meta-cognition, together with algorithmic and experimental directions for large-scale MARL and multi-agent AI. Our central thesis is that collective intelligence should be studied not only as an optimization problem, but as an emergent dynamical phenomenon governed by principles analogous to those of statistical mechanics.
