# Human Factors in Offshore Small Modular Reactors: A Review with Respect to Ambient Intelligence and Autonomous Systems Design and Operation

**著者**: P. G. Fatolitis
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 複雑系科学

## Abstract

Abstract Offshore Small Modular Reactors (SMRs) represent a promising advancement in nuclear energy, offering flexible, scalable power generation while mitigating land-based challenges. However, their deployment in potentially harsh marine environments introduces unique human factors (HF) considerations that include human-machine team performance and trust, operator situation awareness, workload and vigilance, and means of human-systems interaction. This abbreviated review proposes a summary of the relevant literature, with an emphasis on Ambient Intelligence (AmI) for enhancing human operator decision support, which includes the application of autonomous and remote machine SMR operations. AmI may be fundamentally described as a socio-technical environment that integrates sensors, artificial intelligence and machine learning (AI/ML) with human performance dimensions to enhance human-machine system safety and efficiency. Elements of this environment include context awareness and adaptivity in complex, dynamic environments, in which automated AI/ML systems can recognize and adapt to user state and environmental context. Tailored AI/ML responses help to facilitate safe and effective human-systems interaction. System opacity and effective human-machine teaming in such an environment is also a factor, which raises questions related to levels of automation that are applied in design and operations. Questions related to both human-automation trust and human-machine team performance are also notable. The objective of this review is to identify salient synergies and gaps between HF principles and emerging technologies. AmI systems, leveraging sensor networks, AI-driven analytics, and adaptive interfaces, can provide real-time contextual awareness, predictive alerts, and personalized decision aids to mitigate human cognitive workload during routine operations, maintenance, and emergency responses. Further, autonomous and remote operations such as robotic inspections, drone-based monitoring, and teleoperations are desirable in that they reduce human exposure to radiological and environmental hazards in offshore settings. Drawing from multidisciplinary sources including nuclear engineering, human-computer interaction, ergonomics, and maritime safety studies, this review provides a general literature-based analysis to support the design and operation of SMRs from an HF perspective. The aim of this work is to identify fundamental, relevant safety and efficiency-related HF principles and to propose an integrated HF framework for offshore SMRs, fostering resilient systems that enhance operational reliability, reduce human error, and support sustainable nuclear deployment in the energy transition era.
