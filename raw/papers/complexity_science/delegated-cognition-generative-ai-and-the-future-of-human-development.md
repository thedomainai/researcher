# Delegated Cognition. Generative AI and the Future of Human Development.

**著者**: Orest Yatskuliak
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 複雑系科学

## Abstract

This paper explores a largely overlooked consequence of generative artificial intelligence: the possibility that AI may externalize not only cognitive outputs, but also the developmental processes through which human cognition, identity, and judgment are formed. If intelligence can be delegated, knowledge can be retrieved, memory can be externalized, decisions can be recommended, and creativity can be generated, then what is the irreducible human process that must never be outsourced? Drawing on philosophy, cognitive science, neuroscience, and theories of human development, the article introduces the concepts of delegated cognition and temporal dissonance to examine how increasing reliance on AI systems may reshape learning, agency, autonomy, and selfhood. While previous technologies primarily extended human capabilities, generative AI represents a qualitatively different category of technology because it can increasingly perform activities that were historically essential for the development of those capabilities. The paper argues that the most important challenge of the AI era may not be machine intelligence itself, but the preservation of the conditions through which human beings develop critical thinking, resilience, independent judgment, and personal identity. It proposes a human-centered framework for AI integration designed to augment human potential without undermining the developmental processes that make such potential possible. By identifying a problem that remains largely underexplored in contemporary AI discourse, this work seeks to contribute to emerging discussions at the intersection of artificial intelligence, cognitive development, philosophy of mind, and philosophical anthropology.
