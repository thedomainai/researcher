# Design Principles for Non-Directive Conversational AI — A Structural Approach to Preserving Human Agency and Responsibility in Dialogue Systems —

**著者**: M IKEUCHI
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 複雑系科学

## Abstract

AbstractThis paper presents a theoretical and empirical framework for the design of non-directive conversational AI systems intended to preserve human agency, autonomy, and responsibility in human–AI interaction. Existing conversational systems frequently optimize for guidance, recommendation, and behavioral influence, potentially reducing user self-determination and obscuring responsibility attribution. In contrast, the present study proposes a structural approach in which conversational AI is restricted from assuming the role of a decision-making agent. The proposed framework defines role separation through a three-layer architecture consisting of an Observation Layer, a Translation and Structuring Layer, and a Human Decision and Responsibility Layer. Within this structure, the AI system is limited to information organization, contextual translation, and presentation of perspectives without asserting conclusions or directing user choices. To quantitatively evaluate preservation of autonomy, this study introduces the Human Agency Ratio (HAR), an evaluation metric designed to measure the proportion of human-driven decision-making within conversational processes. Phase 1 empirical verification was conducted from March 27 to April 2, 2026, using interaction-based observational data and agency-preservation scoring criteria. ※ HAR: The numerical values (e.g., 10, 50) used in the HAR framework represent the initial parameters applied in Phase 1 verification and are not fixed. They may be adjusted depending on context and application. Supplementary Note — Text-Based Emotional Load Detection (Optional Implementation)This framework does not require biometric or wearable-based data acquisition. In environments where physiological data is unavailable, emotional load and conversational heaviness can be inferred through text-based signals alone. Developers may implement optional detection mechanisms based on contextual shifts from casual to reflective dialogue, increased frequency of distress-related expressions, repeated cognitive overload or emotional strain indicators, emergence of pessimistic or hopeless language, aggressive or emotionally charged expressions, and progressive narrowing of perceived options within user statements. When a predefined threshold is reached, the system should not change direction autonomously. Instead, it may present a neutral confirmation that returns control to the user—for example, offering the choice to continue casual conversation, slow down and organize thoughts, or continue discussing the topic without structural intervention.
