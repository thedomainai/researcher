# The employability paradox: artificial intelligence, critical thinking and the cross-cultural competencies we are dismantling

**著者**: Anne Cullen, Dennis McCornac, Kathryn Swift
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 複雑系科学

## Abstract

Purpose This paper argues that the institutional response to employer concerns about graduate unreadiness constitutes a misdiagnosis. Current employability frameworks are dismantling the critical thinking and cross-cultural competencies that graduates, and the labour markets they enter, most need. The paper repositions universities as irreplaceable sites for developing cognitive and intercultural capacities that neither employers nor artificial intelligence can generate independently. Design/methodology/approach The paper analyses Australian higher education policy, accreditation frameworks, ranking systems and national data documenting the decline of Asian Studies. Drawing on scholarship on employability, critical thinking and intercultural competence, and on the implications of artificial intelligence (AI), it uses the Australian case to show how structural incentives drive curricular drift away from foundational intellectual development. Findings Accreditation and accountability frameworks have produced cumulative drift toward narrow vocational competencies at the expense of critical thinking and cross-cultural competence. The collapse of Asian Studies in Australia shows how employability metrics undervalue preparation whose benefits are long term. Post-pandemic graduates are entering AI-shaped labour markets that most demand the capacities universities have spent decades quietly removing. Originality/value The paper frames the employability paradox, showing that the institutional cure is worsening the condition it claims to treat. Its original contribution is to connect the decline of Asian Studies, the logic of accreditation and the demands of AI-enabled workplaces in a single policy argument with direct implications for universities, accreditors, policymakers and teachers.
