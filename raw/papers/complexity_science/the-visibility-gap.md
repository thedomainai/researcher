# The Visibility Gap

**著者**: Eugene Y. Chan
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 複雑系科学

## Abstract

Organizations are investing in AI faster than they are building ways to show what it returns. In a survey of 467 working professionals in organizations that use AI, 67% agree that AI is producing meaningful value, yet only 21% report a formal method for measuring it, and 31% do not know whether one exists. Confidence that the organization knows whether its AI investments are delivering is high essentially only where a method exists. The gap runs through how benefits are perceived. Respondents see tasks getting faster (81%) more readily than they see the saved time redirected toward higher-value work (68%), and those who see the time redirected rate AI’s overall value far more favorably. What people can see also depends on where they sit: 75% of Managers & Executives say it is clear how AI use in their work group contributes to outcomes that matter, against 56% of individual contributors, while the costs of verifying outputs and worry about status look alike at both levels. The study is a cross-sectional sample of self-reported perceptions, so it shows that a visibility gap exists and where it tends to appear, but not whether any particular organization has it. That requires measuring behavioral value directly, by asking the people doing the work and the people overseeing it the same questions and comparing the answers.
