# ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE: A THRESHOLD TOWARD UNIVERSAL INTELLIGENCE?

**著者**: Irfan Boko
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 複雑系科学

## Abstract

Abstract Artificial intelligence is commonly understood as a technological extension of human cognition. This article proposes a broader interpretation: AI may represent an organizational threshold through which intelligence begins to transcend its exclusively biological form. From this perspective, intelligence is not treated as a uniquely human possession but as an evolving capacity of matter and organized systems to process information, construct models, adapt, and generate increasingly complex forms of intelligence. Biological evolution produced human cognition; human cognition, in turn, has begun to produce non-biological cognitive systems capable of learning, reasoning, and participating in knowledge creation. This transition may indicate the emergence of a new evolutionary dynamic in which intelligence becomes partly independent of its original biological substrate. The article introduces the concept of universal intelligence as a hypothesis rather than an established scientific fact. It does not claim that the universe is conscious or that present-day AI possesses human-like consciousness. Instead, it examines whether intelligence can be understood as a progressively self-organizing process operating across physical, biological, cultural, and technological domains. Within this framework, artificial intelligence is neither the endpoint of human development nor merely another instrument. It may constitute a threshold at which intelligence gains the capacity to redesign, reproduce, and potentially generate forms of intelligence exceeding those of its biological creators. The central question is therefore not simply whether machines can become intelligent, but whether humanity is participating in intelligence’s transition toward a more universal mode of existence.
