# Delegating Decisions, Avoiding Responsibility: The Ethical Risks of Organizational AI

**著者**: Md. Salamun Rashidin
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 複雑系科学

## Abstract

This research project, titled “Delegating Decisions, Avoiding Responsibility: The Ethical Risks of Organizational AI,” will examine how artificial intelligence influences employees’ perceptions of responsibility and their willingness to accept ethically questionable business decisions. The project will investigate whether reliance on AI recommendations and perceptions of AI autonomy will encourage employees to transfer accountability to the technology, managers, organizations, or technology providers. It will also examine whether clear organizational governance will help preserve human accountability when AI contributes to consequential decisions. The research will integrate AI delegation theory, responsibility gap theory, and moral disengagement theory to develop and test a model of ethical decision making. AI decision reliance will represent the extent to which employees depend on AI outputs, while perceived AI autonomy will capture the degree of independent decision authority employees attribute to the system. Responsibility shifting will concern the transfer of accountability to another actor. Moral disengagement will concern the cognitive justifications that allow employees to view questionable conduct as acceptable. The project will examine whether responsibility shifting and moral disengagement will operate sequentially to connect AI reliance and autonomy with unethical decision intention. Two complementary quantitative studies will be conducted in Italy. Study 1 will employ a randomized 2 × 2 × 2 experimental design, varying AI decision reliance, AI autonomy, and AI governance clarity. Eligible working adults with relevant professional experience and recent workplace AI use will assume the role of a human resource manager evaluating applicants. Participants will encounter an AI recommendation involving a potential fairness concern and will choose whether to accept, review, override, or report it. They will then complete measures of responsibility shifting, moral disengagement, and unethical decision intention. The experiment will assess the effects of the assigned conditions and examine whether the observed patterns will be consistent with the proposed psychological process. Study 2 will use a three wave employee panel to examine the model in actual workplace settings. Participants will reflect on a recent organizational decision involving AI. The first survey will measure AI decision reliance, perceived AI autonomy, governance clarity, and relevant individual and organizational characteristics. Approximately two weeks later, the second survey will measure responsibility shifting. A third survey, approximately two weeks after the second, will assess moral disengagement, unethical decision intention, and self reported questionable AI use. This design will examine relationships across separate measurement occasions, although it will not independently establish causality. The analysis will evaluate measurement reliability and construct distinctiveness before testing the proposed relationships. Experimental comparisons, regression, and structural equation modelling will assess direct effects, indirect relationships, and the moderating role of governance clarity. Bootstrapped confidence intervals will evaluate indirect effects. Alternative explanations, response quality, missing data, and panel attrition will also receive attention. The project will test the expectation that greater AI reliance and perceived autonomy will be associated with stronger responsibility shifting, which will relate to moral disengagement and unethical decision intention. Clear governance will be expected to weaken these relationships through explicit verification duties, human decision authority, reporting procedures, and accountability requirements. These expectations will remain hypotheses, and the analysis will allow for null, mixed, or contrary findings. The anticipated contribution will be a clearer explanation of how technological delegation can affect ethical accountability. The project will also seek to inform organizational policies, employee training, and oversight practices that support responsible AI use. Participation will be voluntary, informed consent will precede data collection, and confidentiality safeguards will protect participants and their organizations.
