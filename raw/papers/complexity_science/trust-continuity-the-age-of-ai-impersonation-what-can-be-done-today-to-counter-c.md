# Trust Continuity, the Age of AI Impersonation: What Can Be Done Today to Counter Conversational Impersonation and AI-Assisted Chat Intrusions Through Governance-Centric Approaches-Conversation Integrity, Attribution, Privacy Preservation Using AIVORIX OS™

**著者**: Nicole Crossleytownsend
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 複雑系科学

## Abstract

This paper examines the growing challenge of conversational impersonation, AI-assisted chat intrusions, and trust validation in increasingly complex digital communication environments. As large language models (LLMs), autonomous agents, and AI-enabled collaboration systems become more capable, organizations face emerging risks associated with account compromise, identity spoofing, social engineering, behavioral mimicry, and AI-generated impersonation attacks that may be difficult to detect using traditional authentication mechanisms alone. The motivation for this research was influenced by professional discussions within the LinkedIn AI and cybersecurity community regarding the current limitations of large language models in detecting sophisticated conversational impersonation attacks. These discussions highlighted concerns that as defensive technologies evolve, malicious actors may continue adapting their methods, creating an ongoing challenge for enterprises, government agencies, critical infrastructure operators, healthcare organizations, financial institutions, and technology providers. The paper explores the concept of trust continuity as a potential future cybersecurity and governance framework. Rather than focusing solely on identity verification, passwords, multifactor authentication, or network-based attribution, the research investigates whether future AI ecosystems may require persistent governance capabilities capable of evaluating whether users, agents, and systems continue behaving in ways consistent with historically trusted patterns. Using the conceptual AIVORIX OS™ framework, the paper examines governance-centric approaches involving behavioral lineage analysis, institutional memory, lifecycle traceability, conversational integrity monitoring, privacy-preserving attribution, cryptographic evidence preservation, and human oversight. The research presents a detailed scenario demonstrating how future governance architectures may assist in identifying conversational trust disruptions without exposing personal Internet Protocol (IP) addresses or relying upon invasive user surveillance. The paper also discusses practical measures available today, including continuous identity verification, adaptive authentication, behavioral analytics, immutable audit logging, out-of-band verification procedures, AI-assisted anomaly detection, and governance-based security controls. In addition, the research references several established cybersecurity and identity-security organizations whose technologies currently support portions of these defensive capabilities. While AIVORIX OS™ is presented as a conceptual governance-centric AI operating system architecture and not a currently deployed solution, the paper contributes to ongoing discussions regarding the future of AI governance, operational accountability, trust validation, identity assurance, privacy protection, and cybersecurity resilience. The research is intended for cybersecurity professionals, AI researchers, governance practitioners, policy makers, enterprise architects, risk management leaders, government agencies, critical infrastructure operators, and technology organizations seeking to better understand how trust, attribution, accountability, and governance may evolve as AI-assisted communications become increasingly prevalent.
