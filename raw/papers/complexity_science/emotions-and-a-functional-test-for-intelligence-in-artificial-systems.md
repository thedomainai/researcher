# Emotions and a Functional Test for Intelligence in Artificial Systems

**著者**: Jeroen Terpstra
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 複雑系科学

## Abstract

Whether large language models have emotions is usually argued on the wrong ground, either by appeal to conscious feeling or by pointing at emotion-like internal representations. A functional analysis is more productive, but the version currently on offer is incomplete. This paper sets out a layered functional model of the psyche, developed from more than twenty years of clinical practice, and uses it to specify three jointly necessary conditions for a system to have emotions rather than to imitate them: reorganization (a stimulus of emotional significance produces sustained, multisystem change in processing), plasticity (the episode durably alters how the system appraises afterwards), and stakes (a persistent internal state whose degradation the reorganization functions to prevent, or whose maintenance it functions to secure). Current language models satisfy at most the first. Because intelligence, on this model, is the coordination of feeling, valuing and thinking, the same three conditions mark the threshold at which genuine artificial intelligence begins; this is stated as the Law of Heron. The account also corrects a claim I have made previously, that affect requires a body: what emotion requires is not a body but stakes, of which a body is the evolutionary, not the only conceivable, implementation. The three criteria are offered as a falsifiable test.
