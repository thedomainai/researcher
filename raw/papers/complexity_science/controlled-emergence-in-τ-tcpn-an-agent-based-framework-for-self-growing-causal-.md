# Controlled Emergence in τ-TCPN: An Agent-Based Framework for Self-Growing Causal Topology under Axiomatic Constraints

**著者**: You Zhang
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 複雑系科学

## Abstract

Modern AI systems largely operate as fixed-architecture function approximators: topologies are predefined, and learning adjusts weights inside a closed graph. We argue that the next breakthrough in autonomous intelligence lies not in scaling fixed networks, but in controlled emergence—letting causal topology itself grow, repair, and prune under hard axiomatic constraints. This paper presents τ-TCPN (timed, typed, controlled-emergence Petri-net), a dual-track (thinking / execution) agent simulation framework in which nodes and edges are not prescribed but emerged. A Gap detector senses broken causal input; a candidate generator proposes new bridge nodes (bypass or forward-extension); a local Proof checker validates candidates against non-cycle, arithmetical, and safety predicates; STDP-like co-firing strengthens legal bridges; a dormant-wake operator opens the gate when causal evidence accumulates; and a pruning operator recycles valueless transients. Across four stress scenarios (single-cut, cascaded cut, secondary destruction of a self-grown bridge, multi-source competition with planted loops), the system autonomously rebuilt causal paths with main-chain fake-spike ratio < 2%, zero false Proof rejections in 465 calls, and stable node-population dynamics via pruning. We report the full mathematical specification, the six life-cycle operators, the logged empirical traces, and a conjecture: useful inductive biases for AGI-scale systems may be reverse-extracted from repeated emergence patterns rather than hand-designed upfront. Keywords: controlled emergence, self-organizing topology, causal spike, Kuramoto coupling, STDP, Petri net, axiomatic AI, neuro-symbolic growth, spike causality
