# Digital Well-Being and Self-Regulation in the Age of Generative Artificial Intelligence

**著者**: Roman Shaban
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 複雑系科学

## Abstract

The rapid integration of generative artificial intelligence (AI) into education, professional work, personal decision-making, and everyday life creates new possibilities for cognitive assistance, productivity, and psychological support. At the same time, increasingly intensive interaction with AI systems introduces challenges related to attentional fragmentation, information overload, habitual reliance, cognitive fatigue, and the management of personal boundaries. Traditional approaches to digital well-being have frequently emphasized screen time, problematic technology use, and digital disengagement. However, such perspectives are insufficient for understanding generative AI, whose effects depend not only on the duration of interaction but also on the cognitive functions delegated, the structure of the interaction, and the degree of autonomy retained by the user. This article conceptualizes AI-related digital well-being as a dynamic condition in which individuals are able to obtain meaningful benefits from generative AI while maintaining effective attentional control, manageable information demands, intentional digital habits, psychological boundaries, and independent decision-making. Drawing on research in digital well-being, metacognition, cognitive offloading, technostress, self determination, and behavioral self-regulation, the article proposes the BOUND-AI framework. The framework integrates seven regulatory processes: purposeful initiation, attention organization, information filtering, boundary negotiation, deliberate cognitive delegation, reflective monitoring, and intentional disengagement. Practical strategies are proposed for regulating AI-assisted workflows, preventing unnecessary information expansion, reducing impulsive interaction patterns, establishing sustainable digital routines, and preserving individual agency. The article also outlines an empirical research agenda for evaluating the relationship between intensive generative AI use, self-regulatory practices, perceived cognitive burden, and psychological well-being. The central proposition is that healthy AI integration should not be defined by minimizing technological engagement, but by developing the ability to regulate when, why, how, and to what extent artificial intelligence participates in human cognitive and everyday activities
