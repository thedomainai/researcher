# The Human Intelligence Imperative: Human Cognitive Agency in the Age of Artificial Intelligence

**著者**: Life Edet
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 複雑系科学

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence is rapidly expanding the amount of intelligence that individuals and organizations can access, generate, and operationalize. As AI systems become increasingly capable of analysis, synthesis, generation and recommendation, a critical question emerges: what happens to human cognitive agency when machines can perform more of the thinking involved in everyday decisions? This conceptual paper examines the changing relationship between human cognition and artificial intelligence, focusing on the distinction between cognitive assistance and cognitive dependence. It argues that the central challenge of the AI era is not simply developing more intelligent machines, but ensuring that humans remain capable of framing problems, evaluating information, challenging machine-generated outputs, integrating perspectives, exercising judgment, and taking responsibility for decisions. The paper proposes a Human–AI Cognitive Loop in which human reflection and problem framing precede AI-assisted exploration, followed by verification, human judgment, action, and reflection. It further introduces NeuralFusion’s Four Brains Framework Analytical, Intuitive, Associative, and Reflective as a conceptual model for examining cognitive integration, alongside the proposed Cognitive Fragmentation Index™ (CFI™) as a potential framework for future empirical investigation. These constructs are presented as research propositions rather than validated psychological measures. The paper concludes by outlining a research agenda for studying cognitive agency, AI-assisted decision-making, cognitive offloading, and the development of human-centered cognitive technologies.
