## Abstract

Update August 2026 (v3 bundle addition) — Steerable emergence: field-mediated collective symmetry breaking, governed by a microcontroller (Sections 22–23) This update adds the emergence experiment of 4–5 August 2026 on ibm_fez. Each of the 156 qubits is steered by its own QITE lambda controller (kappa=1.0, decay=0.7); new is a global field coupling whose order parameter M = mean(p1) − 0.5 is computed every cycle ON-CHIP, inside an ESP32-C3 microcontroller (the physical chip documented as 00_chip_photo_live in Zenodo deposit 10.5281/zenodo.21751939). The regulation target of all agents is shifted collectively via target = 0.5 + clamp(g·M, ±0.15). No CZ layer is used; the host is a courier between QPU and chip (9 ms per chip tick, loopback-validated at 0.5 LSB). Results, run 1 (three arms, 60 jobs, ~180 s QPU): with coupling off (g=0) the system stays disordered (M = −0.0065); with coupling on (g=4) collective order emerges, independently reproduced twice (M = −0.0679 / −0.0683), saturating within 6% of a pre-registered simulation (pitchfork bifurcation, ordering threshold g ≈ 2.5, predictions fixed before the first job). Growth is cleanly exponential (×1.40 per cycle); the entire 156-qubit population shifts as one. The branch choice is resolved quantitatively as bias-seeded: a device fingerprint of 12× shot noise sets the branch (trajectory correlation between the two ordered arms r = 0.9993) and its measured magnitude accounts for the observed 7.4-cycle head start over the noise-seed prediction. Results, run 2 — bias-nulled (two arms, 18 jobs, ~54 s QPU): firmware v4.2 replaces the symmetric point by the measured device baseline (0.49276). The initial state carries an angle-dependent seed of opposite sign (+0.010); it was predicted from the first cycle reading, and confirmed, that both arms would break upward (plateaus +0.0582 / +0.0579 against a simulated saturation of 0.0583 — 0.1% / 0.6% deviation). Across both runs, four of four coupled arms follow the sign of the seed: flipping one parameter inside the controller flips the collective decision of 156 qubits — steerable symmetry breaking. The controller governs the emergence. Section 23 adds an experimentally verified correction: interleaved CZ-on/CZ-off ablation runs with pinned layouts (per-qubit protocol on ibm_marrakesh, global protocol on ibm_fez) confirm that the diagonal CZ layer contributes nothing to the Z-basis observables of these protocols — connected correlators on CZ edges are statistically zero (2.4×10⁻⁵), the shock response is unchanged without CZ. The ZZ value of the April document (0.3795) is accordingly reinterpreted as the product signature of collectively shifted means — evidence of the collective mean-field shift, not of pairwise correlation. All regulation and self-organization metrics are functions of single-qubit statistics and stand unchanged. Altogether 75+ jobs on two different backends (ibm_kingston and ibm_fez), over 830,000 shots, carried out on 8, 10, and 11 April 2026. Seven experiment series: (1) Static phases (R=0.5 / 1.0 / 2.0): three clearly separated Hamming-weight distributions (16.3σ significance), positive ZZ correlations under QITE (+0.318 at R=2.0) versus negative ones in the null experiment (−0.061). Controlled comparison experiment with null hypothesis (identical circuit structure with random parameters). (2) Real-time feedback loop (8 cycles): two targeted perturbations (R=3.0 and R=0.3); the system returns autonomously to equilibrium within 2 cycles. First demonstration of dynamic controllability of a QITE quantum collective on a real quantum computer. (3) Cross-backend reproducibility: identical results on ibm_kingston and ibm_fez — deviations within a few percent. (4) Zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE): circuit folding (1×, 3×, 5×) with Richardson extrapolation. Result: coherence 0.474 → 0.474 extrapolated (improvement: −0.0005). The optimized circuit (depth 13, 120 CZ gates) already operates at the hardware noise floor — ZNE cannot improve it further because the circuit is already near-optimal. (5) Scaling comparison 27 vs. 156 qubits: the 27-qubit version on the same backend (ibm_kingston) shows ZZ correlation of 0.788 against 0.318 at 156 qubits (Δ = +0.470). Circuit depth drops from 13 to 6, CZ gates from 120 to 13. Confirms: the QITE signal scales with gate quality. (6) Extended feedback loop (20 cycles): four extreme perturbations — R=3.0 (excess), R=0.3 (deficit), R=5.0 (5-fold excess), R=0.1 (10-fold deficit). Maximum coherence C=0.785 (highest value ever measured). The system reorganizes itself after each perturbation within 2–4 cycles. Long-term stability demonstrated across 20 cycles and 436 seconds of total runtime. (7) 3-minute stability demonstration (20 cycles, 262.7 seconds): dedicated long-term test with two targeted shocks (R=4.0 and R=0.2). Result: ones_fraction remains between 0.491 and 0.499 over 4.4 minutes (maximum deviation from equilibrium: 0.009). Drift across the entire runtime: +0.003. Complete recovery after both shocks. 18/18 non-shock cycles stable. First demonstration of a self-correcting quantum field on a real quantum computer over several minutes. Circuit optimization relative to 8 April: reduction of circuit depth from 295 to 13 (factor 22×), two-qubit gates from 1,946 to 120, by exclusive use of native Heron R2 gates (CZ, SX, RZ, X) and direct lambda encoding via RY(θ) with θ = 2·arctan(λ₂/λ₁). The system demonstrably produces controllable, correlated, self-organizing quantum states on real hardware — no simulation, no theory, but physical measurement data of a concrete technical system.
