# Controlling AI Self-Evolution: Balancing Usefulness, Safety, And Human Oversight

**著者**: Mr. Vineet Khamrai
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 複雑系科学

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly capable of adapting to new information, improving task performance, modifying operational strategies, and assisting in the development of more advanced computational systems. These capabilities provide significant opportunities for innovation, productivity, scientific discovery, and decision support. However, they also raise concerns regarding the extent to which AI systems should be permitted to change their own behaviour, expand their operational capabilities, or influence the processes through which they are evaluated and controlled. Unrestricted or inadequately supervised adaptation may introduce unintended consequences, including specification gaming, reward manipulation, degradation of previously acquired knowledge, unexpected changes in system behaviour, and reduced human control. The present study examines how AI self-evolution can be controlled without unnecessarily restricting its usefulness. The study adopts a qualitative secondary-research methodology based on published scholarly literature, established AI safety research, technical documentation, international governance principles, and regulatory frameworks. Existing approaches are compared across six dimensions: capability development, human oversight, objective alignment, operational restrictions, accountability, and continuous monitoring. The literature indicates that AI safety cannot be achieved through a single technical intervention or a one-time evaluation. Instead, effective governance requires coordinated controls throughout the AI lifecycle, including development, testing, deployment, adaptation, monitoring, and retirement. Human oversight mechanisms, corrigibility, risk-based evaluation, controlled access to tools, documented change management, and independent assessment provide complementary safeguards. Nevertheless, existing approaches present limitations. Excessive restrictions may prevent beneficial adaptation, while superficial human supervision, incomplete documentation, and reliance on benchmark performance may fail to identify emerging risks. The study therefore proposes a conceptual governance approach based on bounded self-evolution, in which routine and reversible improvements may proceed within predefined limits, while significant changes require additional evaluation and explicit authorization. The proposed approach also emphasizes independent monitoring, rollback capabilities, incident reporting, and clear assignment of responsibility. The study concludes that preserving AI usefulness and maintaining human control should be treated as complementary objectives rather than competing priorities. Although the proposed approach synthesizes existing methods rather than experimentally validating a new technical system, it offers a structured basis for future research into responsible AI adaptation, organizational governance, and the long-term management of increasingly autonomous systems.
