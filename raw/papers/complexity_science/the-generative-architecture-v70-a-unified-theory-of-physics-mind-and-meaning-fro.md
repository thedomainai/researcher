## Abstract

The Generative Architecture v7.0 presents a unified mathematical framework in which reality is generated from a single primitive: the first distinction D. From D arise three stable organisational forms — Possibility (P), Coherence (C), and Boundary (B) — which serve as the working axioms of the architecture. These axioms are not independent primitives but canonical projections of D onto structural, constraint, and boundary domains. The core thesis is that reality is made of distinction: matter is distinction made persistent, energy is distinction with the capacity for change, information is distinction with a boundary, consciousness is distinction aware of itself. The architecture introduces the compressed geometric form, a minimal state quadruple (M,N(M),Σ(M),C) that unifies structure, constraint, boundary, and coherence. All dynamics follow from a single operator, the generative derivative DG(x), whose vanishing defines the fixed‑point condition of the system. The classical seven‑stage generative loop — imbalance, action, feedback, pattern, tendency, development, renewed imbalance — is shown to be the operational expression of this derivative. The loop is the universal engine of the architecture: every derived phenomenon is a regime, projection, or consequence of its iteration. The theory derives collapse, development, and geometry as the three dynamical regimes of coherence ascent, yielding an Einstein‑like field equation in the geometric phase. It develops predictive memory compression, the seven cognitive operators, the emotional field, and the phase architecture as cognitive‑semantic projections of the same generative mechanism. A full quantisation scheme is presented, including the functional Schrödinger equation, path integral formulation, and the emergence of an effective Planck constant from coherence fluctuations. Multi‑agent systems are treated through the Dingus extension of the coherence tensor, introducing a metric, connection, and curvature governing identity transport, collapse propagation, and relational teleodynamics. A renormalisation‑group formulation identifies stable and unstable fixed points across development, identity, meaning, and geometry. Numerical simulations validate the architecture across physical, cognitive, and multi‑agent domains: weak‑field general relativity reproduced with correlation 0.999, a sharp phase transition with critical exponent ~0.5, a conserved generative invariant G=D+I′, a stable RG fixed point in the development field, and long‑term cognitive stability with 80–100 simultaneous identity peaks. This document provides the complete, self‑contained exposition of the Generative Architecture v7.0, from first principles to numerical evidence, including implications for physics, artificial intelligence, cognitive science, and philosophy.
