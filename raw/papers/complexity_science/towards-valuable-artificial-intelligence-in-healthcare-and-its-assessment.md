# Towards Valuable Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare and its Assessment

**著者**: Bart-Jan Boverhof
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 複雑系科学

## Abstract

We have posited 3 research questions central to this dissertation: Research Question 1: How can the value of medical AI best be determined? Research Question 2: How can the cost-effectiveness of medical AI best be determined? Research Question 3: How can generative AI be leveraged to optimize the HTA process? Research question 1 is addressed in Chapters 2 and 3. Chapter 2 presents a method for thinking about value in radiology AI through the Radiology AI Deployment and Assessment Rubric (RADAR), which is rooted in the tradition of value-based healthcare. Together, these chapters present frameworks for broad valuation of medical AI. Chapter 3 adopts the perspective of health technology assessment and proposes a framework for broad valuation of medical AI by 29 assessment elements organized across four focus areas. Research question 2 is addressed in Chapters 4 and 5. Chapter 4 presents a systematic literature review that critically examines existing health economic evaluations of medical AI, assessing their scope, reporting quality, and the technological maturity of evaluated systems. This chapter addresses the second research question by identifying current methodological practices, gaps, and limitations in how costeffectiveness of AI is currently determined. Chapter 5 provides a practical application of health economic evaluation methodology by conducting a cost-effectiveness analysis of AI-based glaucoma screening compared to opportunistic case-finding. This chapter demonstrates how cost-effectiveness can be determined through the development of a health-economic model combining a decision tree for screening simulation with a Markov model for disease progression. Together, these chapters explore ways in which quality and relevance of cost-effectiveness studies of medical AI can be improved. While the first two research questions focus on AI as the object under evaluation, a parallel development warrants attention: the potential of AI to serve as a tool within the HTA process itself. The emergence of generative AI and large language models has created new opportunities to enhance and streamline labor-intensive workflows, such as those introduced by the EU-HTAR as mentioned before. The third research question is addressed in Chapter 6. This chapter presents the development and validation of an AI-based tool for automated PICO extraction from HTA documents and clinical guidelines, designed to support the joint clinical assessment process under the EU-HTAR. The chapter provides proof-of-concept evidence that generative AI can meaningfully contribute to the HTA process.
