# The Invisible Footprint: Awareness, Attitudes, and Designable Solutions for Sustainable Human-AI Interaction

**著者**: Jasmine Mckenzie, Mark Holden, Wesley Junkins, Alaina Smith, Juan Gilbert
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: HCI

## Abstract

What people know about an AI system shapes their relationship with it just as much as what that system can do. As generative AI (GenAI) becomes embedded in student life, an open question in human-AI interaction is how new information, including a system's environmental footprint, reshapes that relationship. This study tested whether a short micro-learning intervention could shift students' attitudes, subjective norms, perceived behavioral control, and intentions toward more sustainable use of GenAI. Using a two-arm design, 125 undergraduate and graduate students across four universities were randomly assigned to view a five-minute video on GenAI's water, energy, and hardware costs either before or after completing surveys measuring these constructs. Following the intervention, students showed large gains in perceived knowledge and behavioral intention, and a moderate gain in perceived behavioral control; attitudes and subjective norms did not change significantly. A regression model explained 72% of the variance in intention, with attitudes being the strongest predictor and knowledge showing no independent effect. These findings suggest that informing students about GenAI's environmental costs can measurably shift how they relate to the technology. That attitude change drives intention to act, not knowledge alone, which is a finding with direct implications for human-AI interaction design. The results extend responsible-AI discourse to include environmental stewardship, pointing to concrete strategies such as usage feedback, eco-mode defaults, and AI-literacy curricula to embed sustainability in everyday human-AI interaction.
