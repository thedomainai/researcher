# Explainable AI for Fraud Prevention: Building Trust and Sustainability in Digital Economies

**著者**: Parteeban M. Varatharajoo, Nur Haryani Zakaria, Juhaida Abu Bakar, Aniza Md Din
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: HCI

## Abstract

The global digital economy faces a serious threat from online fraud, as increasingly complex schemes jeopardise consumer confidence and financial stability. Strong fraud prevention systems are now essential as Malaysia develops its digital economy through initiatives such as MyDIGITAL. This study examines the potential of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) to enhance online fraud prevention strategies, with a focus on its applicability in Malaysia’s cultural and legal contexts. Financial institutions, regulators, and end-users gain a better understanding and increased trust in AI-driven decision-making by adopting XAI’s transparent framework. To prevent fraud, the study examines the efficacy and scalability of various global solutions, including anomaly detection and behavioural analytics, in Malaysia. Since Malaysia’s Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) and new AI governance frameworks demand algorithmic accountability and responsible data usage, regulatory compliance receives extra attention. This chapter examines the application of XAI technologies in fraud detection, employing a mixed-methods approach that emphasises their impact on user trust, operational efficiency, and detection accuracy. Expert interviews and surveys targeting Malaysian financial and technology stakeholders offer qualitative insights that complement quantitative data from fraud incidence reports and industry case studies. To determine the factors that facilitate and hinder the adoption of XAI in Malaysia, the study also examines how society and culture perceive AI transparency. According to research, XAI can significantly enhance stakeholder trust and the accuracy of fraud detection, thereby creating a safer online environment. However, there are obstacles to overcome, such as a lack of technical expertise, concerns about data privacy, and the need for contextualised solutions. This work contributes to the conversation about responsible AI by offering researchers, industry leaders, and policymakers practical suggestions for aligning international innovations with Malaysia’s distinct socioeconomic and regulatory environment.
