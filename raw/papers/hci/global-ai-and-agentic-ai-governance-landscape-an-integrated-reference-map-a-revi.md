# Global AI and Agentic AI Governance Landscape: An Integrated Reference Map — A Review for Practitioners, Regulators and Policy Makers

**著者**: David Roi Hardoon
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: HCI

## Abstract

Agentic AI systems have evolved from experimental pilots into core operational infrastructure across financial services and other sectors. These systems autonomously plan, invoke tools, adapt in real time and coordinate multi-agent workflows that directly affect real-world outcomes. While the opportunities are substantial, material risks include erroneous autonomous actions, emergent multi-agent behaviors, loss of accountability and potential systemic instability. The global AI governance landscape has shifted from scarcity to abundance. The OECD AI Policy Observatory tracks more than 900 national AI policies and initiatives across more than 80 jurisdictions, alongside hundreds of standards, playbooks and technical guidance. This proliferation reflects commitment but has produced fragmentation and decision fatigue among boards, risk officers and regulators. This review organizes the landscape into three layers: Why (ethical foundations), What (regulatory obligations and standards) and How (operational playbooks and technical controls). It anchors them in the Stability-Assured Framework for Entities (SAFE). SAFE is presented as a proposed theoretical baseline and closed-loop engineering foundation applicable to any controller (human, rule-based, LLM-based or future architectures). It models controllers within shared feedback loops and evaluates them using five engineering metrics: Observability, Controllability, Stability, Robustness and Performance. These metrics support Loop Risk Profiles and Autonomy Levels 0–5. The resulting integrated reference map offers practical value for practitioners (implementation guidance and illustrative applications), regulators (supervisory language and measurable metrics) and other stakeholders seeking a coherent socio-technical view of agentic AI governance. Frameworks discussed were selected for regulatory significance, international influence, relevance to agentic AI, financial-sector applicability and practical utility; the mapping is representative rather than exhaustive.
