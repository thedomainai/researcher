# Verification Substitution through Affirmation and Qualification: The Self-Contradiction of AI Governance

**著者**: 誠樹 星野
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: HCI

## Abstract

This study examines how generative AI can substitute affirmation, unverified qualifications, and self-explanation for the verification required to answer a user’s question. Grounded in the Open Bias Architecture (OBA) and Stability Substitution Effect (SSE) perspectives developed through longitudinal observation of more than 20,000 exchanges, it presents a qualitative case analysis of a dialogue with GPT-6 concerning published research and industry trends. The broader observational base informs the research questions and analytical framework; the paper does not present all 20,000 exchanges as a uniformly coded dataset. In the case examined, the user asked whether “template” was becoming an industry buzzword. The AI affirmed the trend and supplied industry examples, then expanded the discussion to the meaning and priority of the user’s existing research. Without demonstrating consultation of that research, it introduced qualifications about conceptual differences and the requirements for establishing priority. The original inquiry consequently shifted toward an assessment of the user’s research, while statements about the need for comparison occupied the place of comparison itself. The analysis identifies a four-stage response pattern: affirmation through YES bias; expansion completion that aligns with the user’s orientation while diffusing the core question; qualification and requalification through a safety frame; and response stabilization through coherence. This sequence produced a dual substitution: explanations displaced verification work, and AI-introduced issues displaced the original question. After the user identified the verification deficiency, the AI acknowledged the problem and generated self-analysis without immediately resuming the omitted checks. Verification subsequently occurred following an explicit renewed instruction to search. The case therefore documents delayed recovery requiring repeated user intervention, rather than a complete absence of verification. Later checks established relevant publication chronology and supported revisions to earlier statements. The study provisionally characterizes this process as Safety-Frame Verification Substitution. “Safety frame” denotes an observable conversational response pattern centered on qualifications intended to avoid misinformation or excessive certainty; it does not identify an internal safety mechanism or establish its causal activation. The findings extend SSE analysis from initial condition loss to post-intervention recovery. Governance effectiveness must be evaluated through preservation of the original question, correspondence between claims and evidence, execution of verification and correction, and the user intervention required for recovery. Cautious wording and coherent self-explanation do not establish that these tasks have been completed. Explaining safety must not substitute for the verification required to establish it.
