# Decision-Centric Energy Operations: Integrating Edge Intelligence, Operational Digital Twins, Industrial Work Surfaces, and Agentic AI

**著者**: Kunal Bhatia, Mohamed Abdulrahman Alzaabi, Daxil Mehta, Shalin Dhar, Siddharth Setia
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: HCI

## Abstract

Abstract Energy operators are not short of data. Most assets already have sensors, historians, supervisory systems, engineering tools, dashboards, digital twins, and a growing number of AI pilots. The harder problem starts after an event is detected. Engineers still have to bring together operating trends, work history, procedures, constraints, and approval requirements from several systems before they can make a safe decision. This paper proposes a decision-centric operating architecture that connects edge intelligence, an operational digital twin, an industrial work surface, and agentic AI within a human-in-the- loop model. The layers are intentionally separated. Edge intelligence qualifies events near the asset. The digital twin adds asset context, operating state, constraints, and history. The work surface places the case into investigation, approval, and execution workflows. Agentic AI supports the engineer by retrieving evidence, invoking approved tools, checking rules, and preparing an explainable recommendation. The pattern is intended for workflow-level deployment rather than unconstrained automation. A representative implementation pattern shows how a qualified trigger initiates context retrieval from live data, engineering documents, maintenance history, procedures, prior events, and constraint models. The agent prepares a recommendation package that exposes evidence, assumptions, alternatives, constraint checks, confidence levels, and required human approval before action. The contribution is a practical reference pattern for moving from data visibility to decision execution. The paper emphasizes bounded AI use, evidence traceability, workflow ownership, auditability, and human authority. It does not argue for transferring accountability to autonomous systems. Instead, it shows how AI can reduce investigation effort, improve decision consistency, and give engineers and operators a clearer basis for action.
