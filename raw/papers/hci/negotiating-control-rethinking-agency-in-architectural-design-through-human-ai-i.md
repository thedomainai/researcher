# Negotiating Control: Rethinking Agency in Architectural Design through Human-AI Interaction

**著者**: Isabella Shi
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: HCI

## Abstract

Machine intelligence is entering architectural design as part of a longer history of changing design media. Like that of other tools, the designer’s relationship with AI creates unique conditions for how control, authorship, and decision-making sit in the design process. This raises the central question of this thesis: how can artificial intelligence be treated as a design participant in architectural form-making so that agency can be negotiated? This thesis positions AI not as a replacement for the designer, but as an augmenting partner whose role must be actively negotiated. It explores how humans and machines can work through form together while retaining intention. Following Nicholas Negroponte’s argument that "a designer of a certain expertise and a machine of some scholarship [...] must share the labor of establishing a common language” (Negroponte 1970), this thesis treats language as the condition for negotiation: not only spoken or written language, but also the architectural language through which form is described, interpreted, and transformed. The project began by testing large language models, which showed that general language alone was not enough to support meaningful discussions of form. To negotiate with AI, architectural knowledge had to be made more explicit and systemic. Drawing from Operative Design, the thesis develops an initial operational knowledge base: a shared architectural language on form. Through a language-negotiation tool, this knowledge is treated as malleable through conversation and discussion between the designer and the machine. In the next stage of the thesis, this negotiated operative knowledge base was treated as the basis for early form generation using the form generation interface. The interface allows designers to work with AI through operations, visual outputs, revisions, and feedback. A chosen direction can be continued or challenged, making the design process more visible. Each design option can be exported as images, models, and a design direction guide for further exploration, as well as a JSON file that records the designer’s newly created shared language. This thesis does not seek to automate architectural design or produce finished buildings. Instead, it focuses on an earlier and more ambiguous stage of design to ask how AI might participate in that moment without replacing the designer’s judgment. By first establishing a shared operative language and then using that language as the ground for form generation, the project proposes a human-AI workflow in which AI becomes part of a loop of interpretation rather than a tool that simply produces an output. Agency, in this case, is not held entirely by the human or handed over to the machine. It moves through the exchange. The value of AI in design, therefore, does not come from perfect obedience or full autonomy, but from a productive middle ground where AI understands enough to participate while departing enough from expectation to open new formal possibilities.
