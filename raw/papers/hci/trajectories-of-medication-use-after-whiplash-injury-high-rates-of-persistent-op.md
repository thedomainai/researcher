# Trajectories of medication use after whiplash injury: high rates of persistent opioid and sedative use.

**著者**: Harpa Ragnarsdóttir, Lárus Steinþór Guðmundsson, Freyja Jónsdóttir, Hjalti Már Björnsson
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: HCI

## Abstract

Introduction: We aimed to quantify longitudinal changes in medication redemption before and after motor vehicle collision (MVC)-related whiplash injury and to examine whether early postinjury pain and disability are associated with subsequent medication trajectories. Methods: In this single-center nested prospective cohort study, 167 adults with MVC-related whiplash injury were included. Subacute measures of pain intensity (Visual Analog Scale) and disability (Neck Disability Index) were linked to nationwide prescription registry data covering 12 months before and after injury. Three medication categories were examined: nonopioid analgesics, opioids, and sedatives/hypnotics. Outcomes included dispensed defined daily doses and user trajectories (nonuse, prior use, new use, and new persistent use). Mixed-effects models and logistic regression analyses were applied. Results: The proportion of individuals redeeming analgesics increased from 19% to 35% and opioids from 34% to 51%, while sedatives/hypnotics showed a modest, nonsignificant increase (13%-17%). Among new users, 26% of opioid users and 43% of sedative/hypnotic users progressed to persistent use. Higher disability and pain were associated with greater medication use. Women had higher odds of initiating and persisting in opioid use. Conclusion: Medication redemption increases after MVC-related whiplash injury, with substantial progression to persistent use among new opioid and sedative/hypnotic users. Early symptom burden and patient characteristics may help identify individuals at risk, supporting guideline-concordant prescribing, careful monitoring of prolonged medication use, and nonpharmacological management where appropriate.
