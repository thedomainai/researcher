# Human–Robot Interaction

**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0

## Abstract

Human–robot interaction (HRI) studies robots&s; ways of communicating and behaving around humans, as well as humans&s; behaviours around robots in natural settings. This chapter focuses on underlying concepts such as user-centred design, social robotics, and emotion AI to understand how to programme robots&s; appropriate behaviour when interacting with humans. Concepts include communication etiquette such as verbal and non-verbal communication, personal space around robots, and how to approach system design from cultural and affective perspectives. The chapter also discusses real-life experiences related to HRI. Research conducted from the UK deployment of the Pepper robot assistant in care homes will be reviewed to determine when robots can cause distrust, confusion or negative emotional reactions from their users. Due to experiences like these when placing sociable robots in care home settings, it is important to spend enough time doing methodical design research. Poorly though-out designs can lead to socially awkward, unethical, and unusable robots. The content covered in this chapter provides an interdisciplinary approach to topics from psychology, design, and engineering to allow practitioners to design robots with which people can build trust, understand clearly, and experience positively. Appropriate HRI allows robotic technology to benefit humans by ensuring it acknowledges social rules, norms, cultural differences, and ethics as it enters more of our physical and social environments.
