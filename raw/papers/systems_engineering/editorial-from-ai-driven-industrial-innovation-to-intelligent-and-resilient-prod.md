## Abstract

As 2026 draws to a close, it is an appropriate moment to reflect on how industrial engineering and production management have evolved over the past year.In my 2025 editorial [1], "Advancing Industrial Innovation and Human-Centric Technologies in the Era of AI," I argued that artificial intelligence (AI) was no longer merely an emerging technology but was becoming a foundational capability of modern industrial systems.The research published in the International Journal of Industrial Engineering and Management (IJIEM) demonstrated how AI, digital twins, predictive maintenance, and intelligent scheduling were contributing to increasingly responsive and human-centric industrial ecosystems.One year later, this observation remains relevant, but the discussion has advanced.The central question is no longer whether AI and digital technologies will transform production management, but how their integration can deliver measurable improvements in operational performance, resilience, sustainability, and human well-being.Technological advancement does not automatically translate into better production management, and the availability of intelligent technologies does not necessarily lead to better managerial decisions.Looking across the research published in 2026, several interconnected trends are shaping the future of production management.First, the transition from AI adoption towards intelligent and increasingly autonomous decision-making is becoming more evident.While earlier research emphasized predictive analytics, monitoring, and the optimization of individual processes, current developments increasingly address AI-supported production planning, adaptive scheduling, and integrated decision-support systems.Digital twins are evolving from virtual representations of physical assets towards environments for evaluating operational scenarios and supporting decisions.However, the growing autonomy of intelligent systems also raises important questions regarding reliability, explainability, human oversight, and their actual contribution to industrial performance.Second, resilience and adaptability are becoming fundamental dimensions of production performance.Traditional production management has largely emphasized efficiency, productivity, and cost optimization.Increasing uncertainty and disruptions have demonstrated that these
