# Automation Without Isolation: Preserving Human Performance in Remote Drilling Operations

**著者**: M. Duarte, S. CEDANO, A. Torrealba, M. Kvalo, E. Laird
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: システム工学

## Abstract

Abstract The O&G industry continues to expand the use of remote operating models to assist drilling activities, enabled by advances in automation & connectivity. This paper examines the application of three remote models commonly deployed in drilling operations: Remote Support, Remote Monitoring, & Remote Operation. While these models differ in system complexity, degree of automation, & operational authority, their effectiveness is not governed by technology alone. Outcomes are strongly influenced by Human Factors (HF) embedded within each model. Drawing on practical HF & resilience engineering concepts, this paper demonstrates how cognitive workload, mental model alignment, attention management, and decision-making dynamics shape performance across all remote configurations. As systems become more automated & separated from the rig site, human interaction with complex interfaces increases, amplifying risks associated with misinterpretation of data, delayed intervention, & plan continuation bias. This work highlights that resilient remote drilling operations require more than robust digital infrastructure. Effective implementation depends on clear role definitions between the sharp end & blunt end of the system, standardized interfaces that support shared mental models, & organizational frameworks that balance efficiency with adaptive capacity. Recognizing the human element is essential to ensuring that remote drilling models enhance safety, reliability, & operational performance.
