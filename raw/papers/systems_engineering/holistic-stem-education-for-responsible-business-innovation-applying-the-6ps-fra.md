# Holistic STEM Education for Responsible Business Innovation: Applying the 6Ps Framework to IoT Cybersecurity and Business Resilience Dr Muhammad Jamro (M.Jamro@herts.ac.uk) School of Physics, Engineering and Computer Science University of Hertfordshire, United Kingdom

**著者**: M. Y. Jamro

## Abstract

The growing adoption of Internet of Things (IoT) devices and operational technology (OT) is transforming business operations through remote monitoring, automation, improved efficiency and data-informed decision-making. However, increased connectivity can expose physical infrastructure to cyber risks, with potentially significant operational, financial, safety and supply-chain consequences. Reported cyberattacks on supermarket refrigeration systems in France during October 2026, followed by further incidents affecting European and United Kingdom businesses in early November, provide an applied case study for examining these challenges. French authorities subsequently attributed the principal 11–12 October campaign to Shahid Kaveh, an offensive cyber unit affiliated with Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps; attribution of the 12–13 October, the Dixell and Copeland were hacked during a cyberattack (ANSSI, 2026; Brandy et al., 2026). This article explores why IoT cybersecurity should be addressed through holistic science, technology, engineering and mathematics (STEM) education, connecting technical knowledge with organisational governance, business resilience and professional responsibility. It proposes the 6Ps framework: Partners, Process, Product, Platform, Performance and Protection, as an organising approach to responsible business innovation. The framework is integrated with a Hybrid CDIO-Scrum approach and a staff-led Student Engagement Partnership (SEP), supporting interdisciplinary learning and the supervised use of artificial intelligence (AI). The article distinguishes publicly reported incident information from documented technical vulnerabilities that have not been established as causes of the reported attacks. It develops layered business protection controls, prototype-to-product readiness considerations, risk-management practices and human accountability requirements, including safeguards for AI-assisted operational decision-making. A proposed three-hour interdisciplinary workshop uses simulated industrial scenarios and open-source tools to develop students’ understanding of IT/OT security, procurement, incident response and business continuity. The SAFER decision protocol (Step back, Assess the risk, Find resources, Evaluate options, Respond) is adapted to support timely, evidence-informed responses to operational incidents, with an accompanying decision card. The article presents the framework and educational activities as proposed approaches for further evaluation, rather than empirically validated interventions. It concludes that responsible digital innovation requires secure engineering, informed procurement, lifecycle management, resilient operations and accountable human judgement. A starter survey is provided in an appendix to support future evaluation and stakeholder feedback.
