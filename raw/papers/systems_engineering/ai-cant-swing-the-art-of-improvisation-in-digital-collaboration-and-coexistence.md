# AI Can’t Swing: The Art of Improvisation in Digital Collaboration and Coexistence

**著者**: Fumi Kurihara
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: システム工学

## Abstract

The ICIS 2026 conference theme invites us to consider how different worlds can or should coexist, create tensions, or require distinct solutions. My artefact, a short film entitled AI Can’t Swing, addresses a central sociotechnical tension: the difference between patterned systems and improvisational human behaviours and relations. The film stages embodied dance experiments that enact the tensions between algorithmic and AI constraints1 and human improvisation. The claim is not that swing dance would be “more human” than AI, but that it makes visible forms of collaboration that are difficult to capture when human action is translated into data. Both AI systems and swing dance involve patterns. The difference lies in how the variation within these patterns is treated. In many AI systems, variation is something to predict, reduce, optimise, or correct.2 This is especially true in probabilistic AI systems, where surprise may be built into the technology itself but is produced through computational logics rather than embodied and relational responsiveness (Berente et al., 2021; Raisch & Krakowski, 2020). By contrast, in swing dance, relational variation is the source of connection. It is where play, responsiveness, and human relationships emerge.
