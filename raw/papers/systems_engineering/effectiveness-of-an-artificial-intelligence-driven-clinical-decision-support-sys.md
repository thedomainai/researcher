# Effectiveness of an Artificial Intelligence-Driven Clinical Decision Support System on Pediatric Critical Care Nursing Competencies, Patient Safety, and Clinical Outcomes

**著者**: Neama Salah Abd Elaziz Soliman Elgendy, Nesma Ahmed Abd Elaleam Abo Hamed, Basma G. Mohammed, Maha Ebrahim Fouda
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: システム工学

## Abstract

Background: Pediatric critical care nursing requires rapid clinical assessment, accurate interpretation of patient data, timely recognition of deterioration, and evidence-based decision-making. Artificial intelligence (AI)-driven clinical decision support systems may enhance nurses' clinical competencies by providing real-time risk alerts, supporting clinical decisions, and facilitating early intervention. However, their effectiveness in improving pediatric critical care nursing performance, patient safety, and clinical outcomes requires further evaluation. Aim: This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of an AI-driven clinical decision support system on pediatric critical care nursing competencies, patient safety, and clinical outcomes. Methods: A quasi-experimental research design with pre- and post-intervention assessments was proposed for implementation in the Pediatric Intensive Care Unit at Sohag University Hospitals, Egypt. A purposive sample of 100 pediatric critical care nurses and 100 critically ill children was proposed. Data collection tools included a structured nurses' assessment questionnaire, an AI-supported clinical decision-making knowledge questionnaire, a clinical competency observational checklist, a patient safety assessment checklist, and a pediatric clinical outcomes assessment record. The intervention would integrate AI-supported early warning alerts, clinical risk assessment, evidence-based decision support, and structured nursing responses into routine pediatric critical care practice. Nursing competencies, adherence to patient safety practices, and selected clinical outcomes would be assessed before and after implementation. Results: Following the intervention, the percentage of nurses with "Good" knowledge levels significantly increased from 10.0% to 72.0% ( 48.50, p < 0.001), and the mean total knowledge score rose from 13.8 ± 3.4 to 21.4 ± 2.6 (t = 18.25, p < 0.001). Observational clinical competency levels shifted significantly, with "Good" competency increasing from 12.0% to 75.0% ( 46.85, p < 0.001), showing the largest domain gain in interpretation and response to AI alerts (+2.0 points, p < 0.001). Post-implementation process fidelity revealed high procedural adherence among 68.0% of nurses. Patient safety metrics improved dramatically, with timely recognition of clinical deterioration increasing from 56.0% to 86.0% ( χ² = 24.12, p < 0.001). The mean time to recognize clinical deterioration was slashed from 18.6 ± 7.4 to 9.2 ± 4.1 minutes (t = 12.45, p < 0.001), and the time to first intervention dropped from 14.3 ± 6.2 to 7.1 ± 3.3 minutes (t = 11.20, p < 0.001). Regarding pediatric clinical outcomes, uniform numerical reductions were observed in unplanned support escalations (24.0% to 15.0%, p = 0.095) and CPR events (6.0% to 3.0%, p = 0.248), though they lacked statistical significance. Crucially, the median PICU length of stay decreased significantly from 6.0 to 5.0 days ( = -3.12, p = 0.002). Conclusion: The integration of AI-driven clinical decision support significantly strengthens pediatric critical care nursing competencies, standardizes practice behaviors, accelerates life-saving response times, and optimizes PICU bed utilization. AI serves as a powerful clinical safety net, though active clinical judgment and professional nursing accountability remain fundamental.
