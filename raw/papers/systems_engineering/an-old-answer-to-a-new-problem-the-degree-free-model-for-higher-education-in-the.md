# An Old Answer to a New Problem: The Degree-Free Model for Higher Education in the Age of AI

**著者**: Boris Gorelik
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: システム工学

## Abstract

Generative AI has not created the governance crisis in higher education credentialing; it has made it harder to ignore. The academic degree is higher education’s principal instrument for governing access to occupations. Its signaling value depends on assessed work reflecting individual competence. AI weakens that link: in blind marking, AI-written exam answers went largely undetected and, on average, outscored students, while detection tools trail each new model. Strengthening surveillance restores procedural control only at the cost of assessment validity. Drawing on credentialism and signaling theory, this paper argues that the response may need to include institutional redesign, not only pedagogical adjustment. It proposes a degree-free model, illustrated by the yeshiva: a centuries-old institution organized around formation, community, and recognized mastery rather than certification. Labor markets already partly follow this logic, as employers substitute direct observation of performance for the degree within three to five years of hire. Three recommendations follow. Outside safety-critical fields, institutions could stop issuing degrees while continuing to teach. Public investment should shift from surveillance-based assessment toward authentic evaluation. Reform should be differentiated by field, retaining mandatory credentialing in licensed and safety-critical professions. The model is offered as a proposition for debate, not a blueprint.
