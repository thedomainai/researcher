It situates advanced urban artificial intelligence within a broader methodological architecture that begins with uncertainty, measurement validity, and research design, then proceeds through statistical explanation, causal inference, predictive machine learning, scalable engineering, and applied governance. The argument throughout is that urban analytics becomes publicly valuable only when relational modeling, visual evidence, and large-scale computation are joined to defensible assumptions, explicit evaluation logic, and accountable institutional practice. Graph transformers are treated as powerful tools for representing networked dependencies across roads, transit systems, freight corridors, and governance jurisdictions, while vision models are positioned as mechanisms for converting complex street-level conditions into analyzable evidence about safety, accessibility, compliance, and service performance. Across chapters, the manuscript develops frameworks, protocols, scorecards, and governance artefacts that support research reproducibility, operational deployment, and policy interpretation. Particular attention is given to global applicability across South Asia, Europe, Africa, and the Americas, where infrastructure maturity, sensing capacity, and regulatory expectations vary significantly. The book is designed for researchers, practitioners, and policymakers seeking an academically grounded yet implementation-aware foundation for trustworthy urban AI.
