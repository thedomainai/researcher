## Abstract

Three claims are being made to Congress and the public:1. That the urgent question is whether to pause AI development.2. That we need standards to make AI safe.3. That the choice is between moving fast and moving carefully. All three are misleading. The real situation is this:· Pausing is not the issue. A pause on a system that cannot be certified changes nothing.The debate over pausing is a distraction from the question that matters.· A stochastic system cannot be certified for safety. A large language model that samplesfrom a probability distribution cannot be verified to behave correctly per instance. Nostandard can fix that, because the problem is the architecture, not the paperwork.· A deterministic option exists. Systems can be built that behave the same way every time,that can be formally verified, and that fit the safety standards already written and alreadyenforceable. One such approach is ST-T1024. There are others. The point is that the choiceis not "fast vs. careful." It is "unverifiable vs. verifiable." Congress does not need to write a standard for stochastic AI. https://drive.google.com/file/d/1_n_b8wHu33TCFaKEH8ThLHolWgA9rjqM/view?usp=drivesdk Congress needs to recognize that the standard for deterministic systems already exists, and that the question is whether we require safety-critical AI to be built that way.
