# A LITERATURE REVIEW OF RADIAL BASIS FUNCTION NEURAL NETWORKS FOR BLAST EXPLOSIVE PREDICTION

## Abstract

This review paper examines the development and application of Radial Basis Function Neural Networks (RBFNN) for predicting blast explosive effects. Addressing the complexities of nonlinear and multi-physics blast phenomena, it highlights the advantages of data-driven approaches over traditional analytical methods. The paper presents the fundamentals of RBF networks, including their architecture, training techniques, and optimization strategies, emphasizing their capacity for universal function approximation and adaptability. A broad range of application domains such as mechanical control systems, energy and environmental modelling, medical diagnostics, and materials science underscore the versatility of RBF models. Performance evaluations demonstrate their robustness and accuracy in handling nonlinearities and uncertainties, although challenges related to computational complexity and data limitations persist. The review discusses hybrid modelling approaches and emerging methodologies like quantum kernels and interpretable artificial intelligence to address existing gaps. Ultimately, this synthesis underscores the significant potential of RBF neural networks in advancing blast effect prediction and suggests pathways for future research integrating novel computational techniques to further enhance prediction accuracy and reliability.
