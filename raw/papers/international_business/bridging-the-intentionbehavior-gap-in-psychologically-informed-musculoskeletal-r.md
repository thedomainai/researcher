# Bridging the intention–behavior gap in psychologically informed musculoskeletal rehabilitation: A con-vergent mixed-methods investigation and the triadic care model

**著者**: Atena Mahmoudi
**年**: 2027 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 国際経営

## Abstract

While clinical guidelines strongly endorse psychologically informed physiotherapy (PIP) and cognitive-behavioral techniques (CBT) for musculoskeletal pain, routine clinical uptake remains inconsistent.Most implementation research describes clinician attitudes without modeling the cognitive and systemic mechanisms that impede translation into daily practice.This study investigates the "intention-behavior gap" in psychologically informed musculoskeletal rehabilitation among Iranian physiotherapists using the COM-B (Capability, Opportunity, and Motivation -Behavior) framework and introduces the Triadic Care Model as an operational solution.A convergent mixed-methods design (QUAN + QUAL) compliant with the Good Reporting of A Mixed Methods Study (GRAMMS) guidelines was conducted between 2023 and 2024.The quantitative phase surveyed 450 licensed physiotherapists using the validated Psychological Practice and Skills Tool (PPST).Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) tested hypothesized paths linking psychological self-efficacy, systemic barriers, postgraduate education, and clinical utilization.The qualitative phase comprised semi-structured, in-depth interviews with 20 practicing physiotherapists analyzed via reflexive thematic analysis.Quantitative and qualitative findings were integrated via a joint display matrix.A pronounced intention-behavior gap was identified: 78.2% (n=352) of clinicians strongly endorsed the bio psychosocial model, yet only 24.4% (n=110) routinely implemented psychological techniques.SEM demonstrated excellent fit (χ2/df=2.15,CFI=.96,TLI=.95,RMSEA=.048[90% CI: .041-.055],SRMR=.042).Psychological self-efficacy directly predicted technique utilization (β=.41, p<.001), while postgraduate training indirectly enhanced utilization via self-efficacy (β=.34, p<.001).Systemic barriers exerted a potent negative direct effect on implementation (β=-.46,p<.001).Qualitative synthesis revealed four systemic-cognitive impediments: (1) The Reimbursement Trap and Tariff Disincentives, (2) Sociocultural Stigmatization and Somatic Fixation, (3) Referral Anxiety and Boundary Ambiguity, and (4) Didactic versus Experiential Pedagogical Deficits.Physiotherapists experience high motivational readiness but severe capability and opportunity deficits.Bridging this gap requires moving beyond generic bio psychosocial advocacy toward structural reimbursement reform, experiential micro-skills training, and the adoption of the Triadic Care Model, a structured interprofessional framework linking the physiotherapist, clinical psychologist, and patient through a warm-referral pathway.
