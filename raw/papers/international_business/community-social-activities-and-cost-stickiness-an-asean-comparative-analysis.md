# Community social activities and cost stickiness: An ASEAN comparative analysis

**著者**: Komang Ayu Krisnadewi, Luh Gede Sri Artini, I Wayan Shandyasa, Ni Nyoman Sinta Suwandani, Cynthia Prisilia Winawan Komang
**年**: 2027 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 国際経営

## Abstract

This study investigates whether costs associated with community-based social activities exhibit asymmetric adjustment behavior. It then explores how socially embedded commitments shape cost rigidity in multinational settings. Prior research on cost stickiness has largely explained asymmetric cost behavior through economic adjustment costs and managerial discretion, whereas studies linking corporate social responsibility to cost rigidity remain predominantly descriptive and confined to single-country contexts. Limited attention has been paid to the underlying mechanisms through which community-focused social engagement may constrain firms’ ability to adjust costs downward. Understanding how socially embedded activities influence cost behavior is critical for firms engaged in sustained community relationships, where cost decisions may be constrained by relational obligations and reputational considerations rather than purely economic factors. Using 3,376 firm-year observations from publicly listed companies in Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam, and the Philippines from 2015 to 2023, this study applies the asymmetric cost behavior model to measure cost stickiness. Data on community social activities (COM) were sourced from CSRHub, while financial data were obtained from OSIRIS. Regression analysis and country-specific comparisons were conducted. Findings reveal that costs associated with COM do not uniformly exhibit high stickiness at the aggregate level. However, substantial cross-country variations are observed, suggesting that the degree of social embeddedness has a distinct influence on cost adjustment behavior across contexts. Results indicate that socially embedded costs may resist downward adjustment not because of regulatory constraints but due to implicit relational and reputational commitments. This study contributes to the management accounting literature by introducing a social capital theory-based explanation for asymmetric cost behavior. It highlights that cost rigidity can arise from non-economic constraints embedded in firms’ social relationships. Findings encourage a rethinking of cost flexibility assumptions in organizations with sustained community engagement.
