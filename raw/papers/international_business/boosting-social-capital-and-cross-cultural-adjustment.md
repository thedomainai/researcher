# Boosting Social Capital and Cross-Cultural Adjustment

**著者**: Ameer Asra Ahmed, P. Vinish, C. M. Charithra, Sharath Ambrose, S. Baranidharan
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 国際経営

## Abstract

Expatriates play a crucial role in facilitating knowledge transfer, executing strategies, and fostering global integration within multinational corporations (MNCs). They serve as operational assets and strategic representatives, embodying the ethos and vision of their parent organizations while navigating the socio-cultural complexities of their host countries. Often, they balance aligning with the strategic goals of the parent organization and immersing themselves in the host nation's socio-cultural fabric. This study evaluates the influence of expatriate personality and cultural intelligence on social capital and cross-cultural adjustment among employees in select MNCs in India. A survey involving 385 employees from various roles and departments used a reliable questionnaire. Confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) and structural equation modeling (SEM) validated the model and tested the hypotheses. The findings reveal strong positive associations between expatriate personality, cultural intelligence, and social capital. However, neither expatriate personality nor cultural intelligence showed a significant direct effect on cross-cultural adjustment once both were estimated together in a single structural model with bootstrapped confidence intervals. Mediation analysis confirmed that social capital fully mediates the relationship between expatriate personality and cross-cultural adjustment and, once cultural intelligence and relevant controls (international experience, host tenure, and language proficiency) were modeled jointly, also fully mediates the relationship between cultural intelligence and adjustment. Language proficiency emerged as an additional significant predictor of both social capital and adjustment. This indicates that expatriate personality and cultural intelligence support cross-cultural adjustment primarily by enabling relationship building and access to social resources rather than through unmediated, direct pathways. The study offers practical recommendations for improving expatriate support systems and fostering long-term adjustment in culturally complex environments.
