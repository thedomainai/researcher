# Evaluating intercultural competence in a structured international engineering workshop

**著者**: Pitjutha Juangphanich, Weerachai Chaiworapuek, Patamaporn Sripadungtham
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 国際経営

## Abstract

Short-term international engineering workshops have grown as a practical response to curriculum constraints on extended international mobility, yet methods for evaluating how intercultural competence (IC) develops within these programs remain limited. This study evaluates self-reported IC outcomes among undergraduate engineering students who participated in a structured workshop in Thailand. Post-program data were collected from 89 students at 10 institutions in eight countries (response rate=74.2%), using a mixed-methods design combining a survey instrument across four dimensions with open-ended responses. The results reveal that attitudinal competencies outpaced behavioral skills, with significant variation across nationality and English proficiency groups. Engineering terminology was linked to reduced behavioral engagement beyond general English proficiency, even among intermediate-level speakers. Host-country participants showed a possible developmental pathway distinct from visitor groups, one that sojourner-centered models may not fully capture. This study provides empirical evidence that disciplinary context and participant characteristics relate to IC development in ways general IC frameworks have not fully addressed, applying existing theory to an engineering-specific, short-term setting. The approach also provides valuable insights for program designers and educators, supporting more equitable IC development that better prepares graduates for multinational professional environments.
