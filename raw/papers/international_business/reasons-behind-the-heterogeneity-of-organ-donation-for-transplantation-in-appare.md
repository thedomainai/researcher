# Reasons behind the heterogeneity of organ donation for transplantation in apparently homogeneous countries

**著者**: Francisca González Cohens, Fernando M Gonzalez
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 国際経営

## Abstract

Solid organ transplantation is of unquestionable value in the treatment of end-stage and irreversible failure of these organs.It is evident that the supply of organs from deceased donors falls far short of the demand, a gap that has globally driven efforts to increase donation rates, albeit with markedly uneven results across different continents.Initially, low donation rates were attributed to factors external to the procurement process itself, such as insufficient public education or awareness regarding organ donation, lack of trust in the fairness and equity of the healthcare system, religious beliefs, restrictive legal frameworks, and deficiencies in communication campaigns aimed at promoting donation.However, these factors do not fully account for the problem.For instance, in some lowincome countries, donation rates are nearly nonexistent because healthcare systems fail to adequately address chronic diseases in general and organ failure in particular.In other contexts, competing health priorities contend for limited financial resources, leading healthcare administrators to prioritize certain acute conditions over others when allocating admission to intensive care units (ICUs).As a result, potential organ donors may be excluded from admission to these units.In countries with greater availability of critical care beds for potential donors, the provision of medical, surgical, and neurological care depends significantly on the academic training, dedication, and commitment of medical residents.These professionals must also contend with their own beliefs regarding the ethical dimensions-such as beneficence, honesty, equity, and justice-of providing advanced care to patients who are near death with the aim of preserving their organs for transplantation.It is within this group of countries that the implementation of clinical management models in ICUs may help explain differences in donation rates among neighboring and ostensibly similar nations-for example, the United States and Canada, Spain and France, or Argentina and Chile.A systematic analysis of bottlenecks and the optimization of the organ donor procurement process are likely key factors in increasing overall rates of solid organ transplantation. / 33
