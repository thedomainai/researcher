# Artificial Intelligence and Digital Transformation in Higher Education: A Systematic Review of Opportunities, Challenges and Emerging Trends

**著者**: Ms. Komal Kamlesh Gaikwad
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: systematic_review | **分野**: リーダーシップ・組織行動

## Abstract

Artificial Intelligence AI is getting more and more involved in higher education, it is reshaping teaching, learning , assessment, academic administration, research, and even how institutions decide things. In the last few years the fast rise of generative artificial intelligence GenAI, plus intelligent tutoring systems, learning analytics, automated assessment, recommendation systems, and AI assisted administrative tools has opened up new possibilities for universities, yet it also brings worries like academic integrity, data privacy, algorithmic bias, digital inequality, transparency, and changing professional roles. This paper actually uses a secondary-data based systematic review, looking at research and policy sources about AI and digital transformation in higher education. The review brings together outcomes from academic studies, other systematic reviews, and international policy frameworks, sort of to map out the main uses, opportunities, obstacles, and the kind of emerging trends that are starting to show up. What the literature suggests is that AI in education is not only about making content. It also includes adaptive learning, personalized tutoring, intelligent assessment, profiling and prediction, and support for institutional management. At the same time, good adoption does not just happen, it needs institutional policies, AI literacy, ethical safety nets, teacher professional development, careful responsible assessment approaches, and a human centered way of putting things into practice. A number of recent international frameworks stress human agency, ethics, AI competencies, and responsible usage, so there is a clear direction there. Overall the review shows that AI is best understood as augmentation tech with AI to help educators and learners not to flip a switch and replace the educational roles that already exist. For digital transformation a balance must exist, with technical novelty on one side and pedagogical sense together with ethical norms institutional realities and human factors, on the other. None of those should be treated as optional because leaving one out tends to break the system quickly.
