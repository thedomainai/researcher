# AI-Augmented Leadership: An Umbrella Review Protocol

**著者**: Sebastiano Rapisarda, Laura Dal Corso
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: リーダーシップ・組織行動

## Abstract

In recent years, the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into leadership practices has emerged as a critical factor for organizational success and development. AI is defined as “the ability of a system to correctly interpret data, learn from this data, and use this data to achieve specific goals and tasks through flexible adaptation” (Khawaja & Hamdan, 2023, p. 804). In an increasingly competitive global market, the integration of technological and innovative solutions lies at the center of organizational strategies. The concept of leadership, defined as the art of influencing a group of workers to achieve shared goals, is undergoing a profound transformation. The rise of AI is prompting a shift toward “e-leadership” models. E-leadership is defined as the process of social influence, facilitated by technology, that modifies the behaviors and performance of individuals and groups (Rapisarda & Dal Corso, 2025). In this scenario, leaders must possess technical acumen and strategic vision to effectively leverage AI, aligning it with talent acquisition strategies and organizational objectives (Anvari, 2025). However, integrating AI into leadership comes with its own set of challenges. One key concern is that overreliance on technology could lead to a decline in intuition and emotional intelligence use, potentially resulting in cognitive offloading (Jing et al, 2026). Therefore, an effective leader must balance using AI with maintaining personal connections, which are essential for team cohesion and fostering innovation. At the same time, leaders face significant ethical and psychological challenges, including employee resistance to adopting AI, concerns about data privacy, and AI-driven job insecurity. Therefore, leaders must be transparent and accountable in establishing codes of ethics, monitoring the use of technology, and supporting employees (Fu & Zhang, 2026; Wang et al., 2026). This represents a necessary condition for cultivating trust in human-AI collaborative relationships within leadership in organizations. Therefore, integrating AI into leadership requires a shift in mindset. Leaders must become ambassadors and champions of technological change, as well as facilitators of a culture of continuous learning. Although a substantial body of literature, including systematic reviews, has examined AI in recent years, to our knowledge, no comprehensive umbrella review has synthesized findings from multiple reviews. Such an overview could clarify how AI can be integrated into leadership and organizations. Furthermore, it could serve as a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners by offering guidance on best practices to help leaders drive innovation within their organizations. We formulated the following research question: “What empirical evidence is available regarding the integration of artificial intelligence into leadership?” In particular, the objectives of this review will be to: (1) Provide a comprehensive overview of all reviews concerning the integration of artificial intelligence into leadership. (2) Provide best practices for integrating AI into leadership. (3) Identify any knowledge gaps in the topic. (4) Disseminate review findings in the published literature.
