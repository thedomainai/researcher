# Strange New Schools: Factors Influencing the Adoption of Artificial Intelligence in Pre-K to 12 School Psychology

**著者**: Erin Lau
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: リーダーシップ・組織行動

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence (AI) tools are being widely used in the workforce, generating emerging debates about their role in human-centered service fields. While they have the potential to transform school psychology by improving productivity for basic tasks and allowing more time for service delivery, adoption of AI is inconsistent. Further, there is limited existing empirical research examining adoption patterns and perceptions (Farmer et al., 2025). This cross-sectional study used a multimethod approach to examine factors influencing school psychologists’ adoption of AI. School psychologists (N = 116) completed a survey assessing constructs from the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT; Venkatesh et al., 2003) and open-ended questions about organizational and individual barriers and facilitators and task boundaries for AI use. Multiple regression analysis revealed that the overall UTAUT model predicted behavioral intention to use AI (p < .001). Additionally, performance expectancy, effort expectancy, and social influence were positive predictors of behavioral intention (p < .001), while facilitating conditions were a negative predictor of behavioral intention (p = .02). This finding suggests that facilitating conditions may operate through individual expectancy beliefs and social pressures, and poor training and support may reduce intention to use AI. Qualitative findings revealed themes highlighting the importance of organizational support, including resources, infrastructure, organizational climate, communication, ethical concerns, and shared beliefs. Individual barriers included ethical concerns, knowledge gaps, concerns with dependence and workflow, attitude, and concerns with human connection. The implications for this study center around the need for organizational (i.e., school or district) support, training, resources, and culture to develop individual skills and attitudes. Limitations of this study and future directions for research were discussed.
