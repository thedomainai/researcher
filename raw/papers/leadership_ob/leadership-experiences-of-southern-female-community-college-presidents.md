# Leadership Experiences of Southern Female Community College Presidents

**著者**: Jala Daniel
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: リーダーシップ・組織行動

## Abstract

The purpose of this phenomenological study was to examine the lived leadership experiences of Southern female community college presidents. The study explored the participants' leadership styles, successes, and difficulties encountered while rising to and serving in the community college presidency. This research also explored what they believed were effective leadership characteristics and how mentoring helped them to reach the presidency. Semi-structured interviews were conducted with 13 Southern female community college presidents across five states. This study used a phenomenological approach to capture lived presidential leadership experiences, incorporating narrative elements of personal career ascension. Semi-structured interviews were analyzed using inductive thematic analysis. Findings revealed that the presidents viewed honesty, communication, and a caring attitude as important qualities for effective leadership. Data from the MLQ Self-form revealed the majority of the presidents displayed transformational leadership behaviors. Thematic analysis revealed that the Chief Academic Officer position was the most coveted vice-presidential position in preparation for a community college presidency. Positive, effective mentoring, regardless of the mentor's gender, was vital for them to achieve their presidency. Challenges endured on their ascent to the presidency included geographic mobility and maintaining a personal work-life balance. Specific challenges endured within their presidencies included patriarchal attitudes and the influences of city, state, and federal politics. African American women presidencies were negatively affected by the intersection of race and gender.
