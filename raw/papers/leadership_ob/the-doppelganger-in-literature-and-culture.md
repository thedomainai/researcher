# The Doppelganger in Literature and Culture

**著者**: Pamela Bedore, Anita Duneer
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: リーダーシップ・組織行動

## Abstract

This chapter sets up a historical and theoretical framework for the study of the doppelganger as a conceptual tool for literary and cultural analysis. Locating the myth of Narcissus as a foundational narrative, it draws together early twentieth-century approaches to the doppelganger by Otto Rank, Ernst Jentsch, and Sigmund Freud in reading the uncanny profusion of split-self nineteenth-century doppelgangers and in identifying recurring tropes that continue to be adapted in contemporary literature, film, and popular media. The imagined presence of the doppelganger is read as integral to notions of authorship, from the Romantic evocation of the creative Muse to postmodern doublings of authors through characters, imposters, and even readers. A discussion of W. E. B. Du Bois’s concept of double consciousness theorizes negotiations between the split self and performativity, addressing the double bind of authenticity and self-representation, and the ways in which contemporary authors and filmmakers claim the imaginatively subversive power of the double. A survey of doppelganger tropes deployed across a variety of genre fictions reveals the widespread use of the double in popular literature to reflect cultural and technological anxieties from uncanny algorithms to AI. The chapter concludes with an organizational framework for the chapters that follow.
