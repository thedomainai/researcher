# AI-Enabled Integrated Talent Platforms: Accelerating Readiness for Critical Roles in Energy Operations

**著者**: S. Handayani
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: リーダーシップ・組織行動

## Abstract

Summary The energy industry continues to face increasing workforce complexity driven by digital transformation, changing demographics, evolving competency requirements, and the need to maintain operational continuity across critical business functions. While organizations have invested heavily in digital technologies for operational excellence, succession planning and workforce readiness management remain areas where decision making is frequently influenced by fragmented information, limited visibility of workforce capabilities, and subjective assessments. These challenges become increasingly significant in large organizations where talent mobility, cross functional experience, and diverse competency profiles make successor identification more complex. A major downstream energy company identified several challenges in its talent management and succession processes. Historically, succession planning activities relied heavily on management discussions, performance records, current job assignments, and managerial recommendations. This approach often created limitations in identifying hidden talent, recognizing cross functional experiences, and objectively evaluating workforce readiness for critical positions. The organization required a more comprehensive system capable of capturing broader dimensions of employee capability while providing greater transparency, consistency, and governance throughout the succession planning process. To address these challenges, the organization implemented an integrated talent ecosystem consisting of a Career Management Platform (CMP) and Capability Development Platform (CDP). CMP centralized succession planning activities using a meritocracy based point system incorporating capability, experience, aspiration, and achievement dimensions, while CDP connected competency assessments, capability gap identification, and structured development interventions. Together, the platforms established a closed loop talent management process that improved visibility into workforce capability and readiness. Although the implementation significantly improved governance, transparency, and workforce visibility, several limitations remained. Successor rankings continued to rely on predefined weighting mechanisms and rule based logic, making it difficult to evaluate the relative importance of different experiences, identify hidden talent across functions, and anticipate future succession risks. To address these limitations, the organization identified an AI-enabled Workforce Intelligence Layer as the next stage of maturity aligned with its Human Capital 5.0 transformation roadmap. The proposed AI capabilities include successor readiness prediction, intelligent talent discovery, dynamic successor evaluation, development path recommendations, and succession risk forecasting. This paper presents the implementation of CMP and CDP as the current state of workforce readiness management while proposing an AI-enabled evolution toward predictive workforce intelligence.
