# Artificial intelligence (AI) in construction finance: transforming cost estimation, risk management and financial performance

**著者**: Jack S. Cook, Karla Paul
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: コーポレートファイナンス

## Abstract

Purpose Artificial intelligence (AI) is transforming construction finance by improving cost estimation, cash flow forecasting, financial risk assessment, procurement and decision support. However, research remains fragmented across AI subfields and financial functions, limiting a comprehensive understanding of how AI creates financial value within construction organisations. This study aims to synthesise contemporary literature and develop an integrated conceptual framework of AI-enabled construction finance. Design/methodology/approach A structured integrative literature review was conducted using a transparent search and screening protocol. Forty-six peer-reviewed studies published between January 2021 and June 2026 were systematically identified, screened and coded using Gioia’s hierarchical coding methodology to examine AI subfields, construction finance applications, organisational benefits, implementation challenges and emerging research directions. Findings Machine learning (ML) and optimisation dominate existing research because of their widespread application to cost estimation, cash flow forecasting, financial risk assessment and resource optimisation. Computer vision, natural language processing, robotics and automated planning remain comparatively underexplored but demonstrate growing potential to enhance project controls, contract administration and operational efficiency. The review further shows that successful AI implementation depends not only on predictive capability but also on high-quality data, enterprise integration, governance, organisational readiness and workforce capability. Practical implications Construction organisations should align AI initiatives with financial strategy while strengthening data governance, enterprise integration and workforce capabilities. These complementary capabilities enhance forecasting accuracy, financial resilience, resource allocation and long-term organisational performance. Originality/value Unlike previous reviews, this study integrates fragmented evidence into a coherent conceptual framework linking AI subfields, construction finance functions, organisational implementation factors and financial performance outcomes. The framework identifies extant research concentrations, highlights important knowledge gaps and provides a foundation for future empirical research while offering practical guidance for organisations seeking to strengthen AI-enabled financial decision-making.
