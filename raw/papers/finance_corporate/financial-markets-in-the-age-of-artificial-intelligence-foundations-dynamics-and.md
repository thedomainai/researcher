# Financial Markets in the Age of Artificial Intelligence: Foundations, Dynamics and Risks

**著者**: Issam BELKADI
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: コーポレートファイナンス

## Abstract

Abstract Financial markets are undergoing a profound technological transformation, of which artificial intelligence (AI) constitutes the primary driver. This chapter proposes a theoretical and conceptual analysis of the AI-induced transformation of financial markets, structured around three complementary dimensions: the technological foundations (machine learning, deep learning, natural language processing, generative AI), the transformation dynamics affecting financial actors (forecasting, algorithmic trading, risk management, financial inclusion via FinTech as a channel extending AI-driven financial services to underserved populations), and the emerging systemic risks (algorithmic flash crashes, technological concentration, model bias and opacity, cyber risks). Drawing on an original analytical framework combining the Efficient Market Hypothesis (Fama, 1970), the Adaptive Markets Hypothesis (Lo, 2004), complexity theory and recent literature on explainable AI (XAI), we propose a taxonomy of AI applications in finance and an original conceptual model “the Financial AI Cycle Model (FACM)” structured around four sequential quadrants (Innovation, Diffusion, Crowding, Regulation) formalizing the mechanisms through which AI creates and then erodes value in financial markets. In response to the identified systemic risks, we propose the DARE regulatory framework (Detection, Attenuation, Resilience, Explainability) as a conceptual governance architecture. Our analysis raises a fundamental theoretical tension: while AI is hypothesized to improve informational efficiency and liquidity in the short run, it may paradoxically undermine systemic stability in the long run by amplifying correlations and herd dynamics, a proposition derived from the FACM and advanced as a theoretical hypothesis rather than an established empirical finding.
