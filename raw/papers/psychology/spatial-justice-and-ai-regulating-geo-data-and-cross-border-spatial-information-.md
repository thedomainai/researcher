# Spatial Justice and AI: Regulating Geo-Data and Cross-Border Spatial Information Flows

**著者**: Suvankar Dey
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 心理学

## Abstract

The accelerated adoption of artificial intelligence in geospatial technologies has posed new challenges to the manner in which societies manage territorial knowledge and provide equal spatial relations. This chapter is a critical discussion of the regulatory environment in relation to AI-mediated geo-data collection and transnational flows of spatial information that discusses how the existing governance frameworks fail to resolve core issues of territorial equity, digital sovereignty, and algorithmic justice. By considering a variety of different regulatory paradigms in different jurisdictions and being involved in the experiences of affected communities, this work makes a case that the current legal practices have portrayed spatial data as the technical infrastructure and not as the politically fraught terrain tightly linked to issues of power, representation, and territorial authority. The discussion shows how geospatial technologies involving AI enhance historical trends of spatial inequality and establish new domains of algorithmic territorial governance, which are not regulated according to conventional means. Based on the interdisciplinary approach, this chapter presents an argument that to realise spatial justice in the algorithmic age, the data governance needs to be radically reimagined by placing community autonomy, territory rights, and democratic accountability at the centre. The paper ends with the suggestion of an interdisciplinary regulatory approach that transcends the individualized nature of privacy protection to collective spatial data regulation, presented in practical advice to policymakers, technology developers, and civil society organizations in an effort to make AI-driven spatial systems beneficial instead of detrimental to the goal of spatial justice.
