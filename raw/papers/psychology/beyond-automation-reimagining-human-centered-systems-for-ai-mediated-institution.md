# Beyond Automation: Reimagining Human-Centered Systems for AI-Mediated Institutions

**著者**: Taha Khan
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 心理学

## Abstract

ABSTRACT PurposeThis paper examines how AI and automation can reinforce existing institutional weaknesses when technological efficiency becomes disconnected from human realities, contextual judgment, ethical responsibility, and institutional coherence. It argues that the central challenge is not automation itself, but the institutional conditions in which it is designed, governed, and implemented. Design/Methodology/ApproachThe paper adopts a conceptual and interdisciplinary systems approach to examine how institutional systems translate human realities into formal representations, priorities, and organizational responses. It develops and examines five interacting institutional mechanisms: representational reduction, priority alignment, resolution-consequence disconnect, adaptive compensation, and institutional reinforcement. Building on this mechanism analysis, the paper develops the concept of Invisible Institutional Contributions to describe consequential human contributions that remain incompletely represented within formal institutional systems. Informed by the Human-Centered Ethical Systems Framework (HCESF), the paper then develops five human-centered system design principles: reality-aligned institutional design, responsibility–authority alignment, consequence-aware system design, structured contextual adaptability, and institutional learning and adaptation. FindingsThe analysis suggests that AI can amplify institutional fragmentation when embedded within poorly aligned structures. Technological optimization may strengthen reliance on measurable indicators while overlooking contextual judgment, informal coordination, preventive problem-solving, and adaptive human contributions. Institutions may therefore appear technologically efficient while remaining dependent on unrecognized human adaptation. ImplicationsThe paper argues that structural redesign should precede or accompany automation expansion. Institutions should assess role clarity, authority, information flows, consequences, contextual adaptability, and learning capacity to ensure that automation supports rather than undermines institutional coherence. Originality/ValueThe paper shifts attention from AI as an isolated technological intervention toward the institutional environments in which AI operates, offering a human-centered systems perspective on why automation can reproduce or amplify existing institutional conditions.
