# Learning how to use a Decision Support System: a simulation of Human-AI interaction frames.

**著者**: Corentin Boidot, Antoine Mourato, Léo Facca
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 心理学

## Abstract

The integration of AI in Decision Support System faces a hard limit: automation bias (Cummings, 2004). While AI may outperforms human operators in certain tasks, the added value of keeping humans-in-the-loop becomes questionable if they merely validate AI decisions. However, the ideal tool should enable complementary team performance (CTP). CTP describes a situation where the human-AI (HAI) team overcomes both AI capacity and the operator alone in terms of performance, which is incompatible with automation bias. CTP describes a one-time performance, and is not easy to achieve: it means operators stay on their mind mostly when they are correct, and change their mind mostly when the AI is correct, which is optimistic, as shown in a recent meta-analysis (Vaccaro et al., 2024).However, one observation of CTP doesn’t preclude automation bias in the long-run. Beyond a static conception of performance, the dynamics of these interactions have been studied through several theoretical perspectives (Kumar et al., 2021; Williams et al., 2023; Gonzalez et al., 2026).The stress is often put on the evolution of trust in AI through time (Chong et al., 2022), but the issue of learning effects in the task, independent of trust, is poorly understood. What is the impact of AI assistance on human skill acquisition? This question has been mostly answered in educational contexts (Fan et al., 2025; Kosmyna et al., 2025), where the delegation of cognitive tasks to the AI would hinder the learning process of the children. In operational contexts, the fear of deskilling is often expressed by experts like physicists (Natali et al., 2025), but empirical evidence is missing, to our knowledge. This gap in the literature is understandable as the inquiry is challenging: in any domain, future experts are few, their tasks to learn are numerous, and the deployment of measurement settings on long duration might be difficult.To explore this question without conducting such longitudinal studies, we propose to build a model of human learning of a technical task with an advisor. Our work develop a simulation setting on a classification task to depict possible quantitative effects of advice-taking on performance evolution through time. This modeling scheme allowed us to investigate the impact of the advisor on skill development. While most people expect that a more accurate advisor would lead the learner to higher performances, Vaccaro et al. (2024) suggest that advisors with lesser performance would be more prone to inducing CTP. This claim follows from static experimental contexts: we hereby test it in a dynamical setting. We expect that in the long-run, deskilling could happen, and would affect not-only degraded-mode performance, but also maximal running performance. Is the performance of the advisor the main factor determining performance evolution, or could the assistants have an intrinsic complementarity with the learner, depending on their classification patterns? The type of AI could lead to different results, in terms of performance, reliance, and of dependence.
