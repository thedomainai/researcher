# Does Technostress Impair Critical Thinking? Artificial-Intelligence Self-Efficacy and the Preliminary Moderating Role of Technostress in University Students

**著者**: Sandra Lizzette León Luyo, Heyner Yuliano Márquez Yauri, Moisés David Reyes Pérez, Christian David Corrales Otazú, Sarita Jessica Apaza Miranda
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 心理学

## Abstract

Background: The digitization of higher education has raised concerns about whether technology-related stress impairs higher-order cognition. However, the direct relationship between technostress and critical thinking remains largely untested, and the role of students' confidence in using artificial intelligence as a potential protective resource is unknown. Methods: A cross-sectional survey was conducted with 340 undergraduate students from public and private universities in Peru. Three validated instruments measured critical thinking (Yoon's Critical Thinking Disposition Inventory, 13 items), artificial intelligence self-efficacy (General Self-Efficacy Scale for Artificial Intelligence, 6 items), and technostress (RED-Technostress scale, 18 items). Data were analyzed using partial least squares structural equation modeling. Significance was determined through 5,000-resample bootstrapping with percentile confidence intervals, and out-of-sample predictive relevance was evaluated using PLSpredict and the cross-validated predictive ability test. Results: Artificial intelligence self-efficacy had a strong positive effect on critical thinking (β = 0.42, p &lt; .001, f² = 0.20). Technostress showed no significant direct effect (β = −0.06, p = .50). A preliminary interaction indicated that technostress strengthened the positive relationship between artificial intelligence self-efficacy and critical thinking (β = 0.15); this interaction was significant under the two-stage approach (p = .012) but only marginal under the full bootstrap (p = .07). The model explained 17% of the variance in critical thinking, and out-of-sample predictive relevance was limited. Conclusions: Technostress did not impair critical thinking in this sample. Artificial intelligence self-efficacy was the only robust predictor, and its contribution appeared to intensify at higher levels of technostress, although this moderating effect requires replication. These findings suggest that fostering students' confidence in using artificial intelligence may be more effective for protecting critical thinking than interventions focused on reducing technostress.
