# Perceived therapeutic relationships with AI mental health chatbots: A mixed methods systematic review (Preprint)

**著者**: Ming Wai Wan, Francesca Hill, Joseph Firth, Anamaria Churchman
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: systematic_review | **分野**: 心理学

## Abstract

BACKGROUND AI guided chatbots are increasingly integrated into digital mental health interventions, delivering increasingly flexible and contextualised dialogue. Yet it remains unclear whether users of AI guided DMHIs perceive any “relationship” with a chatbot analogous to that found in traditional psychotherapy, in which the therapeutic relationship is recognised as a pivotal mechanism of change, and whether such perceptions relate to intervention engagement or effectiveness. OBJECTIVE This systematic review aimed to synthesise what is known in the about users’ perceived “therapeutic relationship” with AI chatbots in DMHIs. The second objective was to evaluate whether, in quantitative studies, perceived relational constructs were associated with intervention outcomes. METHODS Five databases were searched for quantitative and qualitative peer-reviewed studies examining perceived therapeutic relational constructs (e.g., trust, bond, empathy) between a user and AI chatbot in a DMHI. For eligibility, studies must have examined the direct experience of using the AI-guided chatbot-based support designed to enhance mental health or psychological wellbeing by primarily psychological means and not combined with in-person therapy. RESULTS Of the 24 eligible studies, most DMHIs included text-based chatbot-delivered interaction as a feature of a broader CBT-based programme. Nine of the 12 quantitative syntheses evaluated the therapeutic bond, based on brief questionnaire subscales, typically reporting mostly high therapeutic bond, even after relatively brief exposure. However, few other perceived relational constructs were studied. Addressing the second aim, no studies investigated whether a perceived therapeutic relationship with a DMHI chatbot was associated with user outcomes following chatbot use. However, the qualitative synthesis of 14 studies found that users’ perceived relationships with DMHI chatbots were shaped by feeling understood, empathised with and cared for; sometimes treating the agent as a real person or friend; valuing its non reciprocity and non judgement - indicating that a traditional therapeutic relationship may not always be necessary for a chatbot to be experienced as helpful; and perceiving relational ruptures including feeling misunderstood and unheard, emotional mismatch, inconsistency and poor conversational flow. Many studies were of moderate quality; studies with declared commercial interests covered the therapeutic bond in more surface-level ways. CONCLUSIONS Interest in how users perceive relational qualities with AI guided chatbots has grown, yet evidence is lacking on whether such perceptions improve outcomes or sustain engagement. Some users clearly perceive relational qualities with DMHI chatbots, but it remains unclear what accounts for individual differences in human-AI relational perceptions beyond limitations inherent to current DMHI technologies. More rigorous and longitudinal research, alongside validated measures tailored to “human-AI relationships”, is needed to determine whether - and in what ways - these perceptions contribute to DMHI engagement, adherence, or therapeutic benefit, and to identify when relational perceptions may create challenges for users with heightened vulnerability.
