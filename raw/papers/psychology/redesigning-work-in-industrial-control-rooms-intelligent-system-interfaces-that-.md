# Redesigning Work in Industrial Control Rooms: Intelligent System Interfaces That Preserve Human Decision Authority While Maintaining Efficiency

**著者**: Peta L. Chirgwin
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 心理学

## Abstract

While significant research has focused on the technical performance of intelligent systems and the design of human-machine interfaces, comparatively little attention has been given to the redesign of work itself. This introduces emerging sociotechnical risks, including authority ambiguity, skill degradation, automation complacency, reduced situational engagement, and diminished adaptive capacity during abnormal operating conditions. This paper introduces Human Authority-Centred Work Design (HACWD), a methodology that treats human authority as a property of the whole work system rather than as an individual trait or a final approval step. HACWD extends the author's previously developed Human Authority Index (HAI), which conceptualises human authority as a measurable sociotechnical property comprising four domains: Decision Clarity, Cognitive Sustainability, Psychosocial Safety and Work Design. Where the HAI assesses the extent to which intelligent systems preserve meaningful human authority, HACWD translates these principles into a work design model built around four interdependent design domains: Interface Design, Work Design, Capability Design and Authority Design. Using autonomous mining operations as an illustrative context, the paper examines how intelligent systems may reshape conventional control-room roles and proposes the human authority engineer or decision architect as an example of a future operational role. This is research in progress: the HAI weightings, items and interpretation bands are preliminary and are the subject of ongoing field, psychometric and cross-sector validation. The paper positions human authority as a critical organisational capability and argues that future operational success will depend not only on technological sophistication, but on the ability of organisations to redesign work, workforce capability and decision structures around effective human-machine collaboration.The research investigates: 'How should work, roles, and decision authority be redesigned in intelligent industrial operations to preserve and enhance human capability while realising the benefits of automation and AI?'
