# Human-in-the-Loop Intelligent Automation: A Review of Trust, Oversight, and Workforce Implications

**著者**: A. Shaji George, B.Roshini
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 心理学

## Abstract

Intelligent automation is now used to retrieve information, predict, recommend, generate content, route workflows and take physical or administrative action. Human-in-the-loop (HITL) design is often suggested as a possible fail-safe but it does not necessarily mean that a person is always in control. Oversight can be undermined by lack of time, authority, competence, independent evidence, or feedback or by a lack of real accountability despite the title of oversight. In this paper we explore HITL intelligent automation from three intertwined perspectives: calibrated trust, operational oversight, and workforce implications. It combines existing human-automation research, guidance for human–AI interaction, risk-management standards, regulation, and evidence of labor. A threat model encompasses technical misfortune, adversarial manipulation, cognitive and organizational failure, and harmful deployment of workforce. The mathematical formulation determines the automation modes based on expected error consequence, uncertainty, review cost, workload and skill preservation. The suggested architecture is that of separating the execution of the model from the risk routing, human decision making, dual authorization, audit, appeal, and learning feedback. A seeded synthetic experiment with 10,000 binary decisions is conducted for the comparisons between automation-only, passive universal review, risk-adaptive HITL, and dual control. With the declared generator, adaptive review achieves a 83.6% to 85.0% accuracy rate, while reviewing 37.1% of cases, and dual control achieves a 87.0% accuracy rate with a 5.2% rate of high impact error. These are indicative values and not measurements taken in the workplace. The review finds that effective HITL systems must have meaningful intervention capability, calibrated reliance, representative evaluation, protected worker voice, continuous competence and evidence of oversight functioning under realistic workload and failure.
