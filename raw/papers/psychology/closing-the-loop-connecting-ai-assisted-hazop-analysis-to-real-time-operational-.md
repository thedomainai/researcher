# Closing the Loop: Connecting AI-Assisted HAZOP Analysis to Real-Time Operational Safety Intelligence

**著者**: Bjarne André Asheim
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 心理学

## Abstract

Abstract Process safety management in oil, gas and petrochemical facilities generates substantial safety knowledge through systematic Hazard and Operability (HAZOP) studies. That knowledge is, however, largely dormant in practice. Captured in static documentation, disconnected from operational systems, and unavailable to operators when needed. Simultaneously, control room operators face increasing volumes of data without real-time access to the structured safety reasoning developed during plant design. This paper presents an integrated approach to closing this loop, connecting AI-assisted HAZOP analysis to real-time operational safety intelligence through a shared knowledge representation framework based on Multilevel Flow Modelling (MFM). MFM is a deterministic, physics-based modelling methodology that represents a process plant as a network of functional goals, flows, and causal relationships. The same MFM model that enables automated HAZOP scenario generation can be deployed in a real-time decision support system to monitor plant operations, identify deviations, indicate likely root causes, and warn of potential consequences, all drawing from the pre-computed safety knowledge generated during HAZOP analysis. The paper describes the architecture of this dual-use safety model, the methodology for generating structured HAZOP outputs from MFM, the mechanism for deploying the same model in real-time control room assistance, and the approach for maintaining this connected safety knowledge base through plant lifecycle changes. Experience from AI-assisted HAZOP applications and control room decision support deployments demonstrates that the integrated approach improves hazard coverage, accelerates HAZOP execution, and provides operators with contextually relevant safety intelligence in real time. The paper also discusses the requirements for establishing a continuous Process Safety Management (PSM) evergreening cycle enabled by this technology integration.
