# Negotiating AI at work: motivational tensions and the redefinition of marketing expertise

**著者**: Judy Cheung, Miaolei Jia, Bingjie Li
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 心理学

## Abstract

Purpose Artificial Intelligence Marketing (AIM) tools are increasingly transforming how marketing work is performed. However, its impact on individual practitioners remains underexplored. This study aims to examine how marketing professionals experience and negotiate the contradictory motivational consequences of artificial intelligence (AI)-enabled work. Rather than treating AI as either empowering or threatening, the study explores how these effects coexist and how practitioners respond to them. Design/methodology/approach Twenty in-depth interviews were conducted with marketing practitioners using an exploratory, theory-informed qualitative design. Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs, Self-Determination Theory and the Job Characteristics Model were used as complementary sensitising lenses to orient interpretation, while coding procedures informed by grounded theory techniques were used to develop themes inductively from the data. Findings The findings reveal that AIM simultaneously empowers and destabilises practitioners. AI enhances efficiency, capability, strategic focus, confidence and creative possibilities, while also generating uncertainty regarding task ownership, professional identity, technological dependence and future relevance. These effects are not experienced as separate outcomes. Instead, practitioners continually negotiate competing tensions by reframing expertise, selectively integrating AI, preserving human control over consequential tasks and reaffirming distinctively human sources of professional value. Motivation therefore emerges as dynamic and continually reconstructed rather than as a stable outcome of AI adoption. Research limitations/implications The design prioritises interpretive depth rather than statistical generalisability. Future research could develop measures of AI-enabled empowerment, destabilisation and negotiation using larger and more diverse samples. The research sample did not analyse differences across career stages, and warrants future investigation. This paper examines broad AIM tools rather than isolated technologies. Future studies should explore how particular AI capabilities change dimensions of professional value and motivation. The findings highlight negotiation as a continuing process, but the cross-sectional design provides only a snapshot of the process. Longitudinal research could establish whether empowerment-destabilisation tensions persist, intensify, diminish or change form over time. The findings suggest potentially important roles for trust, employee participation, work design, organisational support and AI governance. Future research could investigate the organisational conditions that facilitate productive negotiation of AI-related tensions for marketing practitioners. Practical implications Organisations should consider: Evaluating AI in terms of how it changes employees’ experiences of expertise, ownership, autonomy and professional value; Approaching AI capability development as workforce transformation, rather than technical training alone; Preserving meaningful human ownership of work; Addressing the efficiency-intensification paradox. Providing employees with meaningful agency over AI adoption; Treating AI governance as a crucial part of the employee experience. Social implications AI simultaneously empowers and destabilises marketing practitioners, generating tensions around expertise, autonomy, identity and employability. Rather than passively accepting change, individuals actively negotiate these tensions through adaptation, reframing and human–AI collaboration. AI’s long-term impact will depend not only on technological capabilities, but also on how organisations sustain meaningful work, professional development and human agency. Originality/value This study contributes a new tension-based framework that explains how motivation is continuously constructed under AI-driven work environments. In addition, it advances the understanding of AI-enabled work in three ways. Firstly, it conceptualises employee motivation as shaped by simultaneous empowerment and destabilisation rather than by uniformly positive or negative effects. Secondly, it shows how AI redefines marketing expertise from task execution towards judgment, interpretation, contextual understanding and human–AI orchestration. Thirdly, it identifies ongoing negotiation as a central process through which practitioners sustain professional value and motivation under technological change.
