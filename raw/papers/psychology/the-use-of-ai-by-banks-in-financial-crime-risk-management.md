# The Use of AI by Banks in Financial Crime Risk Management

**著者**: Adam Abukari, Julia Werberich, Ololade Durodola
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 心理学

## Abstract

Financial Crime Risk Management (FCRM) is one of the top priorities of senior managers in the banking industry. The use of AI tools, products and services by banks is on the rise. Using AI in FCRM is a natural progression in combating financial crime such as fraud detection. It also enables financial institutions such as banks to reduce costs as well as to improve customer satisfaction and their performance ratings with regulators, including the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) and Competitions Market Authority (CMA). Current approaches to managing financial crime risk (FCR) may become outdated if banks do not align themselves with the opportunities that can be derived from the use of AI. AI already in use to combat fraud and other financial crimes in banks includes AI tools and products that have successfully mitigated against money laundering, blocked suspicious transactions and phishing emails, and managed sanctions risks, while strengthening Know Your Customer (KYC) processes. The use cases of AI within banks, such as pattern recognition and data analysis as well as machine learning for predictive analysis, are explored further in this chapter. Despite the risks and vulnerabilities associated with the use of AI to counter financial crime such as algorithmic bias and data privacy, financial institutions and their employees have increasingly continued to use it. The voices of bank employees on the use of AI in their banks in fostering its responsible use are, therefore, included in this study, using empirical data gathered from three United Kingdom (UK) high street banks. Harnessing AI’s power and promise responsibly will help in managing FCRs. This chapter, as such, offers novel ways in which stakeholders of banks can use the transformative power of AI in FCRM sustainably and responsibly, while promoting the principles of Fairness, Education, Accountability, and Transparency (FEAT).
