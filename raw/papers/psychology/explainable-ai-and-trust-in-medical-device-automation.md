# Explainable AI and Trust in Medical Device Automation

**著者**: Alycia Sebastian, J. Anciline Jenifer
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 心理学

## Abstract

Medical device automation is increasingly dependent on artificial intelligence (AI) to carry out important clinical tasks; however, due to the lack of transparency in traditional AI models, safety, and clinician trust must be a concern. The problem that this chapter addresses is the characteristic “black-box” nature of AI, which hinders its adoption in healthcare environments where interpretability is necessary for ethical and operational reasons. The goal is to build an AI framework for medical device automation that is highly accurate and easy to explain so that clinicians are more likely to trust its use while maintaining patient safety. The methodology combines predictive modeling techniques, such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Convolution Neural Network (CNN), and XGBoost, with explainable AI (XAI) techniques, including SHAP, LIME, and attention mechanisms. The framework is evaluated through a clinicians-in-the-loop approach and simulation-based safety validation. Quantitative results have shown that the proposed framework achieves an accuracy rate of 95.2%, an F1-score of 94.5%, a fidelity rate of explanation of 92.1%, and a trust index of 90.7%, which outperforms several state-of-the-art methods. In conclusion, this approach demonstrates that explainability and trustworthiness can be optimized at the same time in AI-driven medical device automation, thus making a way for safer, interpretable, and clinically accepted AI deployment in healthcare environments.
