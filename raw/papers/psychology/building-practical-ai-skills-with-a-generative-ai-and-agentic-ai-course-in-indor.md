# Building Practical AI Skills with a Generative AI and Agentic AI Course in Indore

**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence is becoming increasingly connected to everyday business and technology workflows. From generating content and analysing documents to automating repetitive processes and developing intelligent applications, AI is being applied across a wide range of professional functions. As these applications evolve, learning AI involves more than understanding basic concepts or experimenting with individual AI tools. Professionals and aspiring technology learners increasingly need practical knowledge of Generative AI, retrieval systems, AI agents, automation, and deployment. Moving From AI Concepts to Practical Skills Understanding what artificial intelligence can do is only the beginning. Practical AI learning involves applying concepts to specific problems and understanding how different components work together. For example, a learner may start by understanding how a large language model generates responses. The next step could involve controlling those responses through prompt engineering, connecting the model to external information through RAG, and eventually creating an agent that can use tools to complete multiple tasks. This progression helps connect individual concepts into complete AI workflows. Generative AI as a Foundation Generative AI allows models to produce different types of content, including text, code, images, and other digital outputs. Large language models have also expanded the use of AI in research, communication, software development, customer support, and business processes. A structured learning program can introduce learners to areas such as: Generative AI fundamentals Large language models Prompt engineering Token and context management AI output evaluation AI application development Model limitations Responsible AI practices The AI Pro program published by upGrad includes foundations of applied GenAI systems, prompt engineering pipelines, output control, and evaluation pipelines as part of its curriculum. Understanding Retrieval-Augmented Generation One practical challenge with AI systems is providing models with relevant information for a specific task. Retrieval-Augmented Generation, commonly called RAG, is one approach used to connect AI models with external knowledge sources. A RAG workflow can involve: Documents → Data Processing → Embeddings → Vector Database → Retrieval → AI Model → Response Learners can explore how information is divided into usable sections, converted into embeddings, retrieved based on relevance, and provided to a language model as context. The published upGrad curriculum includes RAG pipelines, embeddings, vector databases, semantic search, context-aware question answering, chunking, retrieval strategies, and troubleshooting hallucinations. Exploring Agentic AI Workflows Agentic AI introduces another layer of functionality. Instead of limiting an AI application to a single response, an agentic workflow can involve multiple steps, tools, decisions, and actions. A simplified workflow could look like: Goal → Planning → Tool Selection → Action → Evaluation → Next Step → Final Output For example, an AI agent designed for business research could potentially identify a task, retrieve relevant information, process the information, and prepare a structured result.A Generative AI and Agentic AI Course in Indore can provide a structured environment for developing these skills through guided learning, technical exercises, and project-based application. Learning these concepts gives students an opportunity to understand how AI systems can move from simple question-answering toward multi-step workflows. The AI Pro curriculum includes multi-step agent workflows, tool calling, autonomous task execution, API integration, reasoning and decision flows, memory-based planning, and failure-handling concepts. Learning Through Hands-On Projects Practical projects can help transform technical concepts into usable skills. The published program information states that learners work on 15+ live projects, with examples covering RAG systems, AI agents, and deployed GenAI applications. Potential project areas include: 1. Enterprise Document Intelligence Learners can explore an AI system capable of searching, summarising, and answering questions from a collection of documents. 2. Autonomous Workflow Agent A project can demonstrate how an agent plans tasks, interacts with tools, and performs multiple operations. 3. AI Assistant With RAG Combining retrieval with an AI assistant can demonstrate how external knowledge can be incorporated into AI-generated responses. 4. AI Application Deployment Learners can explore how an AI solution can be exposed through APIs and prepared for deployment. These types of projects provide a practical context for understanding how different AI components interact. Developing Prompt Engineering Skills Prompt engineering remains an important part of Generative AI applications. A well-designed prompt can provide an AI model with: A defined objective Relevant context Clear instructions Output requirements Examples Constraints Learners can experiment with different prompting approaches and evaluate how changes in instructions affect model outputs. However, practical AI development goes beyond prompting. Once learners understand prompting, they can explore retrieval, tool calling, agents, APIs, evaluation, and deployment. Working With Modern AI Tools Practical AI development involves working with multiple tools and technologies. The published AI Pro curriculum lists technologies including Python, Pandas, NumPy, OpenAI, LangChain, LangGraph, FAISS, Hugging Face, Neo4j, Jupyter, Streamlit, GitHub, Power BI, and n8n.A Generative AI and Agentic AI Course in Indore can provide a structured pathway for learners to explore these technologies through instructor-led learning and practical projects. Exposure to these technologies can help learners understand different parts of an AI development workflow. For example: Technology Area Potential Application Python AI application development OpenAI Generative AI applications LangChain AI application and workflow development LangGraph Agentic workflow development FAISS Vector search Hugging Face AI and machine learning models Streamlit Application interfaces GitHub Version control and collaboration n8n Workflow automation The exact tools used in professional projects can vary, so understanding the underlying concepts is equally important. Developing AI Problem-Solving Skills Practical AI learning should focus on solving problems rather than simply learning tools. A useful process can involve: Identifying a real problem. Determining whether AI can contribute to the solution. Selecting appropriate data and models. Designing the workflow. Building a prototype. Testing the outputs. Identifying errors and limitations. Improving the system. Deploying or presenting the final solution. This approach can help learners develop a more structured understanding of AI application development. Building AI Skills Through Classroom Learning Learning complex AI technologies can involve several interconnected concepts. Classroom-based learning can provide learners with direct access to instructors and peers while they work through these concepts. The Indore program information describes structured batches, live instructor sessions, dedicated mentors, and offline classroom learning. This type of environment can support: Immediate doubt resolution Guided technical exercises Peer discussions Project feedback Instructor demonstrations Structured learning schedules For learners who prefer direct interaction while developing technical skills, this can provide an alternative to entirely self-paced learning. Creating a Portfolio of AI Projects Practical projects can also become part of a learner's professional portfolio. A project portfolio can document: The problem statement Technology used AI architecture Development approach Workflow Results Challenges Improvements For example, instead of simply mentioning "RAG" on a resume, a learner could demonstrate a project showing how documents were processed, indexed, retrieved, and used to generate contextual responses. This makes the learning experience more demonstrable and project-oriented. Understanding AI Deployment Building an AI prototype is different from deploying a functional application. Deployment introduces additional considerations such as: APIs System architecture Performance Cost Security Latency Monitoring Reliability Responsible AI The AI Pro curriculum includes API-based GenAI services, local and cloud-ready deployment, end-to-end architecture, and responsible AI practices. Exposure to these concepts can help learners understand the broader AI application lifecycle. Responsible AI and System Limitations Practical AI skills also require awareness of limitations. AI models can generate incorrect or incomplete information. Applications that use external data can also encounter retrieval errors, outdated information, or inappropriate outputs. Learners should therefore understand concepts such as: Output evaluation Hallucination management Human oversight Data privacy Security Safety boundaries Responsible AI implementation The published curriculum specifically includes failure handling, safety boundaries, and responsible AI practices. Career Applications of Practical AI Skills Generative AI and agentic AI skills can complement existing technical and professional backgrounds. Depending on their prior education and experience, learners can explore areas such as: Generative AI development AI engineering AI application development AI automation
