# General Education Teachers’ Lived Experiences of Using AI to Support Students with Learning Disabilities

**著者**: Leia Faith Davis
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 心理学

## Abstract

The purpose of this qualitative phenomenological study was to explore the experience of general education teachers in the First TN Core region of East Tennessee integrating AI into their instructional practice to support students with learning disabilities. This study applied Bandura’s (2001) Social-Cognitive Theory as a framework to examine how teachers’ beliefs, environment, and behaviors interacted as reciprocally causational factors. Data for this research were collected during 30–60-minute, one-on-one recorded zoom interviews with 13 general education teachers from districts in the First TN Core region. Participants’ teaching experience ranged from 4 to 25 years, and teachers represented a variety of subject areas across elementary, middle, and high school settings. Findings revealed that teachers were experiencing developing perspectives about AI because of its prevalence in the social and educational domains, and that despite their recognition of potential ethical liabilities, teachers leveraged their personal agency to experiment with and leverage AI for a variety of uses. All teachers used AI to differentiate learning, and several realized a positive impact on inclusion and success for students with learning disabilities. In their successful experiences, teachers also described professionally affirming outcomes in their professional practice. Teachers demonstrated strong self-efficacy in exploring the applications of AI in the classroom as they sought self-driven learning because of the lack of effective or relevant professional development. However, teachers were supported by trusting relationships with school leaders who endorsed experimentation and by peer social models who assisted in providing mastery experiences for teachers developing confidence. These environmental factors influenced teachers’ behavior to seek additional support and mastery experiences. Additionally, participants revealed concerns about the potential negative impact of the use of AI on critical thinking, but several teachers made strategic adjustments to their educational practices to preserve thinking opportunities for students, demonstrating the reciprocal relationship between teachers’ beliefs and behaviors. Further, teachers were motivated by student success and feedback, particularly with inclusion and success for students with learning disabilities (SWDs). Overall, the findings suggest that teachers with strong self-efficacy and trusting leadership are willing to experiment with AI guided by a personal sense of ethical practices to differentiate learning and improve outcomes for students, creating more inclusive and successful experiences for students with learning disabilities. This study contributes to the growing body of research about AI in education and may provide insight for educational leaders seeking to support students with disabilities and guide ethical practices for teachers.
