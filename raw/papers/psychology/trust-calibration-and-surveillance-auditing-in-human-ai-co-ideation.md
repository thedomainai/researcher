# Trust Calibration and Surveillance Auditing in Human-AI Co-Ideation

**著者**: Akram Bayat
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 心理学

## Abstract

As generative artificial intelligence becomes deeply embedded in computing and design education, instructors face the urgent challenge of teaching students to think critically about machine suggestions rather than trusting them uncritically. This paper evaluates a structured, classroom-ready Human-AI Co-Ideation Workshop designed to protect student creative agency and reduce automation bias. In the activity, students tackled a forward-looking design challenge focused on student wellbeing and academic success. We introduced a deliberate pedagogical delay by dividing the workshop into sequential stages: students first generated initial design ideas entirely on paper without computers, then used multiple commercial language models to expand their concepts, and finally completed a comparative gap analysis and reflection. Analyzing student design worksheets, conversational interaction logs, and reflective essays revealed four consistent patterns: students struggled to bridge personal lived experiences with broad institutional systems; articulate machine outputs created a "Smoothness Trap" that discouraged questioning; learners had to actively protect their design confidence against polished machine text; and students practiced "defensive curation" by identifying and stripping out invasive surveillance and behavioral tracking mechanisms introduced by automated models. This paper provides instructors with an adaptable workshop structure, an evaluation rubric, and practical teaching strategies to help students collaborate with AI safely and thoughtfully.
