# Understanding Organisational Citizenship Behaviour During AI-Enabled Organisational Change: a Mixed Method Case Study of AI-enabled Implementation in an Australian mining organisation.

**著者**: Natasha Hawkins, Xiaoshuang Lin
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 心理学

## Abstract

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is increasingly transforming workplaces across a wide range of industries, including the Australian mining sector. Mining organisations are investing in AI-enabled technologies such as machine learning systems, intelligent monitoring tools, decision-support platforms, autonomous equipment, and computer vision technologies to improve safety, productivity, and operational performance. While these technologies offer significant opportunities, their successful implementation depends not only on the technology itself but also on how employees experience and respond to the organisational changes that accompany them. This research project, Understanding Organisational Citizenship Behaviour During AI-Enabled Organisational Change, investigates the human factors that influence successful AI implementation within mining organisations. The purpose of this research is to better understand why employees exposed to similar AI-enabled organisational changes may respond differently. Some employees actively support implementation efforts by learning new skills, helping colleagues adapt, sharing knowledge, and contributing ideas for improvement. Others may engage only at a minimum level or become resistant to change. Understanding these differences is important because employee behaviour can significantly influence whether AI initiatives achieve their intended organisational benefits. The study draws on Self-Determination Theory (SDT), a widely recognised theory of human motivation. SDT proposes that individuals are more likely to demonstrate positive engagement and discretionary effort when their psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness are satisfied. This research examines whether employees who feel prepared for AI-enabled organisational change are more likely to experience these psychological needs being met, and whether this increases their willingness to engage in positive discretionary workplace behaviours. The research also investigates the influence of trust in AI and perceived risk of AI, which may shape how employees interpret and respond to organisational change initiatives. The project will be conducted as a mixed-methods case study within the Australian mining industry. Quantitative survey data will be collected from employees to examine relationships between preparedness, trust, risk perceptions, psychological need fulfilment, and behavioural outcomes. Qualitative interviews will then be undertaken to gain a deeper understanding of employees’ experiences and perspectives regarding AI-enabled organisational change. This sequential approach will provide both statistical evidence and rich contextual insights into employee experiences. The expected outcomes of the research include both academic and practical contributions. Academically, the study aims to extend existing knowledge of AI implementation by moving beyond traditional technology adoption models and focusing on employee behavioural responses during organisational change. Practically, the findings will provide organisations with evidence-based recommendations to improve the design and implementation of AI-enabled change initiatives. The research is expected to identify strategies that help organisations build trust, reduce perceived risks, increase employee preparedness, and create conditions that encourage positive discretionary behaviours. Ultimately, the project seeks to support more effective and sustainable AI implementation while contributing to positive employee experiences and organisational outcomes within the mining industry.
