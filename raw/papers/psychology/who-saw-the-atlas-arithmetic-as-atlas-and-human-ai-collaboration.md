# Who Saw the Atlas? Arithmetic as Atlas and Human-AI Collaboration

**著者**: Bill Widi
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 心理学

## Abstract

Who Saw the Atlas? Arithmetic as Atlas and Human-AI Collaboration develops a mathematical framework in which arithmetic operations are understood as maps of Roads and Memories. A Road determines how a number travels toward a landmark or Home, while Memory preserves information about the number’s position and history. Comparing square, triangular, and other landmark systems produces an Arithmetic Atlas of alternative numerical biographies. The mathematical investigation culminates in a square–triangular Memory resonance theorem. When a positive integer occupies the same nonzero relative position within both its square and triangular chambers, the number must be composite, and a proper factor can be recovered from the reduced ratio of the chamber widths. The result demonstrates how comparison among maps can make an arithmetic property visible that no single map displays by itself. The work then turns the Atlas toward the collaboration that produced it. Extended excerpts from the dialogue between Bill Widi and ChatGPT document the roles of proposal, recognition, resistance, testing, reframing, and judgment in the development of the mathematical framework. The final part uses this case to examine creativity across unlike entities, distributed epistemic structure without distributed personhood, authorship as responsible stewardship, and the limitations of treating artificial intelligence as either merely a passive tool or an artificial human mind. Presented by Bill Widi as curator, the work extends the WidiVision practice of reorganizing familiar mathematical objects so that new relationships and questions become visible. It asks not only what the Arithmetic Atlas allows us to see, but what kinds of seeing and creativity may arise through sustained human–AI interaction.
