# Impact of Agile Methods on Tacit Knowledge Sharing from Self-Determination Perspective: A Mixed Method Study of Chinese IT Teams

**著者**: Ka Yiu Tsui, Geoff Turner
**年**: 2027 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 心理学

## Abstract

The purpose of the study is to examine whether agile methods foster tacit knowledge sharing in IT project teams and to identify the motivational mechanisms, especially basic psychological needs and shared goals, underpinning this relationship in Chinese contexts. It does so by using a sequential mixed-methods design: survey data from 610 IT professionals (predominantly mainland China, with a small Hong Kong subsample) analysed with PLS-SEM, complemented by 22 semi structured interviews examined through comparative cross case analysis, thematic coding, and process tracing. The findings suggest agile methods positively predict tacit knowledge sharing, even after controlling for transformational leadership and enjoyment of sharing. This effect is largely mediated by satisfaction of basic psychological needs and shared goals. Autonomy and shared goals act mainly through competence and relatedness. Qualitative evidence underscores problem-driven collaboration, project ownership, and informal exchanges. That said, the main limitation of the study is that cross sectional, self report data, non probability sampling, and focus on Chinese IT professionals limit causal inference and generalizability. Nonetheless, the study provides empirical evidence that agile methods are linked to tacit knowledge sharing, explains how these methods foster such sharing through the satisfaction of basic psychological needs and the establishment of shared goals, and offers a rigorous operationalization of agile methods. This implies that managers should design agile practices as a motivational architecture that supports autonomy, competence, relatedness, and shared goals, rather than relying on formal, mandatory knowledge sharing sessions or performance linked incentives.
