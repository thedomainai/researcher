# Trust Talk Is Scoped and Unlexicalized: How People Express Trust in AI, and What Standard Instruments Cannot See

**著者**: Muhammad Ali Shahidy, Usha Lakshmanan
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 心理学

## Abstract

Trust-in-automation research has recognized since the mid-1990s that trust can attach to a system's particular functions rather than to the system as an undifferentiated whole, and Lee and See (2004) explicitly recommend maximizing this functional specificity to prevent misuse anddisuse. Widely used self-report trust instruments nonetheless ask respondents to rate "the system" as a single referent; no item in any instrument examined here lets a respondent name the task or capability their rating concerns. This paper reports Phase 1 of a project measuring how far naturalistic discourse about trust in AI diverges from that instrument design. A prevalence sample of 20,000 items was drawn without trust-relevance filtering from a corpus of 12,497,945 items across 17 AI-focused subreddits, December 2022 through January 2026. Among 2,924trust-relevant items, 96.97% (95% Wilson CI [96.27%, 97.52%]) expressed a trust or distrust evaluation without using trust-specific vocabulary. The same items were coded for whether their evaluation attached to AI as an undifferentiated category or to a specific object; as directly measured, 84% were object-scoped, a figure corrected to approximately 92% after hand verification identified a one-directional coding error in the global label. A keyword-based approximation of the explicitness codebook achieved only κ = 0.464 against the same gold standard, with roughly two-thirds of its positive calls false. The two findings are reported as separable: implicitness in trust vocabulary is documented directly, and scoping is offered as an explanation for it, not as something the data alone demonstrate. If trust evaluations are formed at the level of a specific object in roughly nine cases in ten, and conveyed using trust-specific vocabulary in barely three cases in a hundred, general single-item trust instruments are summarizing a considerably more differentiated attitude structure than the number they return.
