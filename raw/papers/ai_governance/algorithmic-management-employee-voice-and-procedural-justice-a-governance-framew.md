# Algorithmic Management, Employee Voice, and Procedural Justice: A Governance Framework for Accountable AI-Assisted Organizations

**著者**: Tuğhan TURAN
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: AIガバナンス

## Abstract

Algorithmic systems increasingly allocate tasks, rank performance, flag anomalies, and recommend decisions that affect employment conditions. This critical integrative review examines how algorithmic management may reshape employee voice and perceptions of procedural justice in AI-assisted organizations. It distinguishes automated recommendations from fully automated decisions, information disclosure from effective contestability, and worker participation from symbolic consultation. Established research on algorithmic control, organizational justice, algorithmic transparency, and human-AI decision structures provides the conceptual basis. The article develops an accountability chain linking data provenance, decision rights, reviewability, employee explanation, correction pathways, and evaluation of organizational outcomes. It argues that the mere presence of a human reviewer does not demonstrate meaningful oversight; review authority, adequate information, and the ability to change an outcome are separate requirements. Potential benefits of automation include consistency and early detection of harmful errors, while risks include opaque sanctions, strategic data manipulation, and the displacement of responsibility. No primary interviews, experimental observations, or quantitative effect estimates are reported. The proposed empirical agenda combines process tracing, employee surveys, decision-log audits, scenario-based experiments, and organizational comparisons. The framework can be supported only if its specified safeguards improve measurable fairness, accuracy, employee trust, and organizational performance beyond existing governance practices. algorithmic management; employee voice; procedural justice; AI governance; human oversight; organizational accountability
