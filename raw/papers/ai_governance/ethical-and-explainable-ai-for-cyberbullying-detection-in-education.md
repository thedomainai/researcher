# Ethical and Explainable AI for Cyberbullying Detection in Education

**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0

## Abstract

The significant and complex nature of cyberbullying in educational settings is further compounded by the onset of generative AI large language models capable of generating convincing yet harmful digital content. This chapter introduces a new opportunity and addresses the limitations of recent studies that rarely handle bias, privacy, and inclusivity as interconnected issues by developing an integrated ethical framework for cyberbullying detection. The framework is built on the three pillars of bias mitigation, data privacy protection, and student inclusivity. Employing a conceptual and interdisciplinary approach, this study synthesizes findings from educational research, AI ethics, and global policy, underpinned by comparative case studies in diverse schooling environments. The chapter presents a three-pillar model and practical road map intended to guide educators, policymakers, and AI developers in deploying responsible detection systems. It further stresses the importance of using XAI to provide greater transparency and trust, resulting in safety, especially relating to digital inequalities that make many students both vulnerable users and underrepresented contributors of valuable knowledge or data for AI. The chapter provides a forward-looking approach to how responsible and explainable AI can promote safer learning environments by integrating ethical principles with practical guidance that augments protection, fairness, and inclusivity for all students.
