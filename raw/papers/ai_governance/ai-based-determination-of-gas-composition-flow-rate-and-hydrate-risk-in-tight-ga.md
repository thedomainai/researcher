# AI-Based Determination of Gas Composition, Flow Rate, and Hydrate Risk in Tight Gas Reservoirs During Underbalanced Coiled Tubing Drilling

**著者**: Klemens Katterbauer, Saleh Komies, S. Abu Alsaud, Ahmed Alsmaeil
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: AIガバナンス

## Abstract

Abstract The accurate real-time determination of gas composition, flow rate, and hydrate formation risk during Underbalanced Coiled Tubing Drilling (UBCTD) in tight gas reservoirs represents a significant multidisciplinary challenge at the intersection of reservoir engineering, multiphase flow dynamics, and operational safety. Conventional logging-while-drilling (LWD) and surface sampling techniques are frequently compromised under the dynamic, non-equilibrium conditions characteristic of UBCTD, where rapid pressure and temperature changes, complex multiphase flow regimes, and sensor signal distortion due to phenomena like hydrate formation converge to create substantial uncertainty in critical parameters. This uncertainty directly impedes optimal reservoir evaluation, production forecasting, and the implementation of timely flow assurance strategies. To bridge this gap, this paper presents a novel, physics-informed artificial intelligence (AI) framework designed for the simultaneous, real-time estimation of gas compositional fractions (CH4, C2, C3+, CO2, N2), total and phase-specific flow rates, and a quantitative Hydrate Risk Index (HRI). The model is developed and validated on a large-scale, synthetically generated dataset comprising 1000 physically consistent samples that encapsulate the stochastic noise, sensor nonlinearities, and operational variabilities of field conditions. By integrating direct sensor measurements (Doppler velocity, capacitance, resistivity) with fundamental physical drivers (formation pressure, bottomhole temperature, underbalance pressure, water cut), and embedding a physics-derived HRI as both a prediction target and a contextual constraint, the proposed multi-task deep learning architecture moves beyond black-box data fitting. Results demonstrate robust predictive accuracy across all output variables, with feature attribution analysis (SHAP) confirming strong alignment with established thermodynamic and flow principles. The framework exhibits notable resilience to simulated sensor faults, thereby offering a transformative tool for proactive hydrate mitigation, enhanced compositional surveillance, and optimized flow management in tight gas UBCTD operations, ultimately contributing to improved recovery and operational safety.
