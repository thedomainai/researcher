# Knowledge and attitudes of the role of artificial intelligence in healthcare among undergraduate nursing students in Mardan, Pakistan: A cross-sectional study

**著者**: Abdur Rahman, Muhammad Tariq, Ismail Shahid, Khadija Khadija, Mahnoor Ali
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: AIガバナンス

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence (AI) is gradually emerging as a breakthrough in healthcare provision, enhancing clinical decision-making, patient safety, and efficiency. Nursing students must be adequately equipped to understand and leverage AI technologies as future healthcare specialists. Nevertheless, there is a lack of local data on nursing students' knowledge and attitudes toward AI in healthcare in Pakistan. It was a research study aimed at assessing the quality of knowledge and attitudes towards AI among undergraduate nursing students in the nursing colleges of Mardan, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan. An in-depth survey was used to conduct a cross-sectional study among undergraduate Generic Bachelor of Science in Nursing students of 11 nursing colleges located in Mardan over a period of four weeks. The sampling method was non-probability convenience sampling. The participants (n=310) were recruited to complete a structured, validated 2-rule questionnaire that included knowledge (10 questions) and attitude (10 questions) towards AI in health care. As the research results showed, nursing students exhibit considerable knowledge of AI (mean knowledge score: 4.02 [standard deviation (SD) = 0.58]). Most respondents agreed that AI had beneficial uses in the healthcare industry, could improve nursing practice, and, as such, should be featured in nursing education. The overall attitude toward AI was positive, with a mean score of 3.72 (SD=0.48). The majority of students viewed AI as useful for patient care and for advancing healthcare. Nevertheless, the problems concerning ethical concerns, privacy, legal duty, and job substitution were also communicated. Nursing undergraduates at nursing colleges in Mardan have positive experiences and a comprehensive understanding of AI in healthcare. Even with positive perceptions, the current issues suggest that there should be systematic education, ethical counseling, and curriculum alignment on AI to equip future nurses with appropriate, safe, and effective approaches to working with AI.
