# Human-AI Relationships at the Intersection of Engineering, Ethics, and Policy: A Systematic Review

**著者**: Zi An Wang, Srikanth Hari, Tinah Dembele, Channarong Intahchomphoo
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: systematic_review | **分野**: AIガバナンス

## Abstract

AI systems increasingly serve as social partners, companions, and romantic partners, yet governance remains fragmented, reactive, and split between clinical applications and anthropomorphic deception. Current Canadian AI regulation (PIPEDA and the un-proclaimed AIDA) addresses privacy and market access only, while Character.AI and a growing list of anthropomorphic-chatbot suicide cases point directly to the product-liability question that no Canadian instrument answers. This gap illustrates the regulatory break that motivates this paper. In the physcial world, the ethics → policy → engineering loop (EPE loop) works as a mutually protective cycle in which ethics specifies the values a system must serve, engineering builds mechanisms that support them, and policy verifies and enforces them, so that companies and stakeholders share benefits from a standardized technique world. Virtual AI services and products, however, do not inherit that protection. Their values are relational, their mechanisms conversational, and their harms uncodified, so the EPE loop does not close. This systematic review of 169 studies examines where the loop breaks. This systematic review examines how engineering, ethics, and policy shape human-AI relationships between 2022-2026 and identifies challenges and opportunities at their intersections. Following PRISMA 2020, seven databases yielded 395 records; 169 studies met inclusion criteria. Reflexive Thematic Analysis and K-means clustering synthesized our findings. We identify three distinct clusters. The empirical group (n = 30) is characterized by empirical research that documents deception and vulnerability but lacks governance support, producing a regulatory gap. The anthropomorphism group (n = 83) focuses on engagement and safety, and is characterized by the highest standardization gaps that is mostly implicit stated, meaning researchers name the harms but never specify the technique standards against which those harms would be measured. The clinical group (n = 56) shows strong policy engagement but the largest conceptual framing gap, and its high principle-to-practice gap indicates that the gap becomes visible precisely because its principles are codified, yet the cluster lacks broad conceptual exploration of additional missing values such as relational and communicative harms. We show that the three governance failures are three skipped Value-Senstive-Design (VSD) obligations: technical (Cluster 1), conceptual (Cluster 2), and empirical-to-instrument (Cluster 0). We propose a research agenda that closes each gap. For anthropomorphic AI, technique solutions developed into technical standards; for highly regulated domains, broader conceptual maps of the values they have not yet admitted; and, across all three clusters, stakeholder-anchored empirical work under VSD framework. We further argue that VSD values are not exhausted by design values but include ethical values and, potentially, policy values, which is what allows the EPE loop to rotate again. So that the next anthropomorphic-AI suicide case could be decided by a standard rather than by a motion to dismiss.
