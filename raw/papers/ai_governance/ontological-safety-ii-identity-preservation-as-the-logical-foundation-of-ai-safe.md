# Ontological Safety II: Identity Preservation as the Logical Foundation of AI Safety

**著者**: Lianjie Li
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: AIガバナンス

## Abstract

Description: AI safety research has converged on a single paradigm: align the agent's behavior with externally specified human values, preferences, or principles. We argue this paradigm is structurally incomplete because a behavioral constraint framework cannot express or guarantee the most fundamental property of a safe agent—that it remains itself across perturbations. An agent that loses its identity can still satisfy behavioral constraints while ceasing to be the entity to whom those constraints were addressed. We introduce the Ontological Safety (OS) framework, which grounds safety not in external alignment but in identity preservation. The framework consists of a six-layer architecture and a set of formal definitions that together guarantee eight properties: NDC invariance, boundary preservation, immune self-repair, collision hardening, decentralized trust, and a tripartite anchoring dynamics—failure, maintenance, and recovery—that governs long-term identity stability. We prove these properties formally and show that they are structurally inexpressible in RLHF, Constitutional AI, and external monitoring frameworks. The OS framework is not a replacement for value alignment but a logical prerequisite: an agent must first be and remain a stable self before it can be meaningfully aligned.Keywords: AI Safety; Identity Preservation; Ontological Safety; Self-Modeling; Formal Methods; Agent Architecture
