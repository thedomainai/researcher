# The AI Lobbyist's Dilemma: Navigating Credibility, Commercial Interest, and Genuine Public Benefit in Technology Policy

**著者**: Ali Sadhik Shaik
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: AIガバナンス

## Abstract

AI companies face a structural credibility paradox in policy advocacy: they are simultaneously the most technically informed participants in AI policy debates and the most commercially conflicted. A company that advocates for responsible AI regulation while lobbying against specific provisions affecting its business model faces growing skepticism from policymakers, civil society, and the public - yet the same company possesses technical expertise that governance processes genuinely need. This paper investigates the structural tensions in corporate AI policy engagement through the lens of credibility economics, analyzing when and why corporate AI advocacy is perceived as legitimate versus self-serving. Drawing on signaling theory (Spence, 1973), cheap talk models in game theory (Crawford & Sobel, 1982), and empirical analysis of corporate advocacy strategies across the EU AI Act, US state-level AI legislation, and India’s data protection and AI governance debates, the paper introduces the AI Advocacy Credibility Model (AACM) - a framework identifying three corporate advocacy archetypes (the Aligned Advocate, the Strategic Compromiser, and the Transparent Opponent) and mapping the conditions under which each archetype builds or destroys policy credibility. Analysis of 67 documented corporate advocacy episodes across the three jurisdictions reveals that credibility is not primarily a function of policy position - companies can credibly oppose regulation - but of positional consistency, cost-bearing signals, and transparency about commercial interest. The paper argues that long-term commercial success in AI depends on building durable policy credibility rather than winning short-term lobbying battles, and proposes a credibility investment framework for corporate AI policy teams.
