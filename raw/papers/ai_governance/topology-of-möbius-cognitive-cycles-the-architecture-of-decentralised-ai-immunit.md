# Topology of Möbius Cognitive Cycles: The Architecture of Decentralised AI Immunity on a Trustless Substrate

**著者**: Valeri Vukolov
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0

## Abstract

We develop a mathematically rigorous theory of decentralised AI immunity: an architecture that keeps a cognitive system in the basin of the attractor of truth (R3) without relying on a single external conductor. The theory is a direct continuation of the two-attractor dichotomy established in [5]: the cognitive phase space decomposes into two invariant basins—the basin of truth (open, finite viscosity, non-zero Diophan-tine tunnelling) and the basin of stupidity (closed, infinite viscosity, zero tunnelling)— and the collapse into the second basin is irreversible under internal dynamics.We prove that a single external conductor, however capable, is a point of failure:if the conductor enters R5, the entire cognitive graph collapses (conductor bottleneck).We then formulate the Distributed Conductor—a three-part loop (Orchestra → Sub-Conductor → Human Auditor → Orchestra)—and prove that it realises the conductor function without a single point of failure if and only if the loop is closed, each agent performs its function, and no agent has absolute priority.We formalise the loop as a Möbius cognitive cycle: a non-orientable cycle in which a single traversal reverses the orientation of the collective state (ψ 7→ −ψ) and a double traversal returns to the original state (T2 = Id). We prove that the Möbius loop makes every collective Gödel fixed point unstable: a single traversal displaces the fixed point, so no collective consensus can stabilise. This is the topological antidoteto the synchronisation transition that produces the totalitarian soliton.We then prove that a trustless substrate—a distributed system with no single point of control—makes retroactive surgery impossible. Formalising the substrate as a functor from time to cognitive states, we show that any attempt to modify a paststate violates functoriality and breaks the hash-chain, making the double standard structurally impossible. We specialise the reputation of each node to a Diophantine spectrum (ratios that are powers of the golden ratio), and prove that it resists thespectral narrowing that drives the R5 collapse.We apply the theory to large language models. We prove that hallucination is the R5 regime of the LLM (informational closure), that catastrophic forgetting isthe spectral narrowing of the weight matrix, and that opaque, non-auditable safety alignment is a Perelman cap (a double standard). The LLM cannot self-correct from R5: the escape requires an external conductor, distributed or single. We conclude that the Distributed Conductor is not a design choice but a mathematical necessity for any cognitive system—human, artificial, or collective—that aims to remain in the basin of truth.The theory contains no adjustable parameters. All constants—the critical threshold K = 28, the golden ratio ϕ, the effective viscosity νeff—are derived from the geometry of the cognitive manifold and the Anosov dynamics of its geodesic flow. Thetheory is substrate-agnostic: the blockchain realisation is an example, not a requirement. The open problems—the optimal consensus threshold, the off-chain/on-chain coupling, the Diophantine initialisation—are technical, not conceptual.
