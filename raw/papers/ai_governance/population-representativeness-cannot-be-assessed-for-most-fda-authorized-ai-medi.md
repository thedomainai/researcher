# Population representativeness cannot be assessed for most FDA-authorized AI medical devices targeting high-disparity conditions

**著者**: Saptarshi Purkayastha, Aravind  V. Kuruvikkattil, Chaitra Sree Sammeta, Aiswarya N. Perumbilly, Rashmita Kudamala
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: AIガバナンス

## Abstract

Background: Audits of FDA-authorized AI/ML medical devices ask whether submissions disclose demographic data. We ask three prior questions: for devices targeting conditions with documented demographic disparities, can training-data representativeness be assessed from the public record at all; does the underlying peer-reviewed evidence fill the gaps the summaries leave; and can equity constructs be coded reliably from these documents? Methods: We developed the 19-item Equity Readiness Assessment (ERA, development version) and a pre-registered Epidemiologic Crosswalk mapping each device's target condition to its documented disparity profile, and applied them to all 1,428 devices with retrievable decision summaries through December 2025, using language-model extraction validated against dual independent, blinded human coding (n=153). The nine items reproduced between human coders constitute the validated core, ERA-9, which carries all inferential claims. Results: Here we show that for 76.6% (95% confidence interval 73.8-79.3%) of the 899 devices targeting high-disparity conditions, representativeness cannot be assessed because race is unreported, a finding that rests on a reliably coded item and is near-invariant to how conditions are classified. Retrieving the pivotal publications rescues only 5 of 27 unassessable devices, locating the gap in the evidence base rather than the summary format. Graded equity judgements are not reproducible between trained coders (κ 0.01-0.12), whereas binary presence-of-reporting items are. No summary documents have been validated in a community or safety-net setting. Conclusions: Disclosure guidance has produced neither population-data alignment nor its assessability. We distil ERA-9 into a minimum equity-reporting standard for regulatory summaries and release the instrument, Crosswalk, and device-level dataset openly.
