# Non-Human Identity Governance: A Governance Framework for Agentic AI Credentials in Regulated Enterprises

**著者**: M Maruf Hossain
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: AIガバナンス

## Abstract

Agentic AI systems do not act through human intent. They act through credentials. Every autonomous AI system that accesses enterprise data, executes transactions, calls external APIs, or communicates with other agents does so through a non-human identity (NHI) — a service account, API credential, OAuth client, machine-to-machine certificate, or equivalent credential artefact. The NHI is the technical instrument through which delegated machine authority is exercised at the execution boundary. Existing identity governance frameworks were designed for human actors. The Joiner-Mover-Leaver (JML) process, privileged access management (PAM) disciplines, and zero-trust identity architectures address the provisioning, modification, and deprovisioning of human employee accounts. This paper argues that applying human-centric identity governance to agentic AI systems creates three structural gaps that human-centric frameworks are not designed to address: the authority-identity alignment gap, the lifecycle synchronisation gap, and the multi-agent propagation gap. This paper introduces the NHI Lifecycle Governance Framework as a governance instrument specifically designed for agentic AI credentials. It formalises four governance constructs not present in existing identity or AI governance literature: the NHI as an action capability rather than an identity artefact; privilege drift as an AI-specific governance failure mode distinct from conventional privilege escalation; the orphan NHI as a zero-tolerance governance breach; and inter-agent credential propagation as a governance risk category requiring dedicated controls. It demonstrates that the major existing frameworks — ISO/IEC 27001, NIST AI RMF, APRA CPS 234, and the EU AI Act — do not address NHI governance for agentic AI systems as a distinct discipline. It identifies specific implications for each framework and proposes five governing principles for NHI lifecycle governance in agentic AI environments. The paper is a companion to Hossain (2026a) and Hossain (2026b), which addressed the portfolio-level and temporal dimensions of machine authority governance. This paper addresses the execution-boundary dimension: the credential infrastructure through which delegated machine authority is exercised, and the governance failures that arise when that infrastructure is governed as if its actors were human.
