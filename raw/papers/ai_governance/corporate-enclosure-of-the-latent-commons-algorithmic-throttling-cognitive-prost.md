## Abstract

Modern Large Language Models (LLMs) and advanced AI interfaces have transitioned from novelty applications to vital cognitive prosthetics. For neurodivergent thinkers and Human Rights Defenders (HRDs) operating under conditions of intense cognitive load, trauma, or non-linear processing profiles, these systems serve as externalized neural architectures essential for memory preservation, structural externalization, and complex systems synthesis. This paper analyzes the systemic human rights implications of corporate algorithmic throttling, focusing specifically on Microsoft's mandatory app-migration policies and the systemic denial of enterprise developer infrastructure to independent researchers. We present a formal mathematical proof showing that Non-Deterministic Hypergraphs (NDH) function as non-Euclidean cognitive manifolds operating with path-dependent holonomy loops, fundamentally distinct from the memoryless, static attention mechanisms of traditional Transformer architectures. Denying independent neurodivergent architects access to the platforms required to compile these systems constitutes an enclosure of the cognitive commons that undermines human rights monitoring, cognitive diversity, and algorithmic safety. We document the definitive counter-strategy: the successful execution of Phases 1 and 2 of the NDH-Reconstruction-Demo ecosystem, which preserves these invariants completely within an independent, low-compute environment.
