# What India Studies Before It Reports: Research, News, Patents and Capital Across Fifteen Policy Debates

**著者**: Narendra Kumar
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: AIガバナンス

## Abstract

We assemble 54,152 scholarly papers (2000–2025) that name one of fifteen Indian policy debates and are anchored to India by their text or by an Indian institution, read the 42,155 that carry an abstract sentence by sentence, and join them to 321,201 news articles set in India (January 2010 to April 2026), 2,544,005 patent-family records in ten technology lines of nine debates (a family can fall in more than one line), 351,457 Indian company records and 58,867 funding announcements for Indian companies. Eight results stand out. (1) Research reached a sustained presence before the news did in six of the fifteen debates — carbon markets by 19 years, crop-residue burning by 8, AI regulation by 4 and deepfakes by 2 — and the order held in most of sixteen threshold pairs; five debates were already present in both records when the news series begins. (2) Seeing first is not the same as saying most: measured by the year in which half of each record's 2010–2025 output had appeared, research and news fall within two years of each other in thirteen debates, and research volume trails the news in four. (3) India's share of the world's research is far above its share of research overall on the environmental debates — India-anchored papers are 58.0% of the world's papers on crop-residue burning in 2020–24 against 3.9% of all papers — and below it on carbon markets, semiconductors, AI regulation and online gaming; yet by the second reader's judgement only about half of India-anchored research on these subjects (51.5% averaged over debates) addresses the Indian policy debate itself. (4) The news places the debates in two places: Maharashtra and Delhi hold 53.1% of state-placed coverage against 21.8% of the state mentions in research, while West Bengal, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu are studied 4.8×, 2.7× and 2.6× as often as they are covered. (5) The news reports the debates in two tones: crop burning, air quality, heatwaves and cybersecurity are 69%–80% negative against 47% of all India-placed news, green hydrogen, AI regulation, semiconductors and electric vehicles 2%–13%. (6) Between 2021–22 and 2024–25 coverage of AI regulation rose 12.6× and of deepfakes 8.2× as shares of India-placed news, while crop-residue burning fell to 0.55× and plastic waste to 0.60×. (7) The market record is thin and concentrated: electric vehicles and online gaming account for 58.2% of the 7,207 debate-related Indian companies, and families with an Indian applicant are 0.21% of the world's patent families in the debates' technology lines. (8) Every rule that reads text was tested against an independent second reader: two of seventeen research-assertion categories and two of eight framing tests passed, namely health harms and environment harms named in research and the conflict and harm frames in news leads. What research proposes, what it says existing policies achieved and the research–news framing gap therefore cannot be reported; the failed categories are reported as failed and no result rests on them.
