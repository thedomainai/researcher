# A Scanning-Based Model of Perceptual Time: The Half-Time Hypothesis (HMT), Angular Integration, and Environmental Pedagogy

**著者**: JOAO NETO
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: AIガバナンス

## Abstract

This preprint introduces a novel physical framework for understanding perceptual time in biological and artificial systems. Unlike classical mechanics where time is absolute, perceptual systems manufacture time through the integration of spatial variation via scanning. We formalize perceptual time as T_inf = ΔΩ/⟨Ω̇⟩, where ΔΩ is angular variation (solid angle) and ⟨Ω̇⟩ is effective scanning rate. From this foundation, we derive the Half-Time Hypothesis (HMT): stable perception requires integrating environmental variation within approximately half the time before physical consequences impose correction. Key contributions:- A quantitative law connecting geometry, scanning, and temporal perception- The concept of Free Temporal Margin (FTM) as temporal surplus for meta-processing- Explanation of light invariance vs. manufactured time through movement- Demonstration that environmental factors (gravity, medium resistance) modulate temporal margins without violating core scanning laws- Unified framework applicable to biology (bird head-bobbing, human vision), robotics, and AI consciousness The model generates testable predictions across neuroscience, comparative biology, robotics, and artificial intelligence. It explains why perceptual time is fundamentally relational—emerging from the coupling between sensory integration capacity and environmental constraints, rather than being an intrinsic property of neural processing alone. This work bridges physics, perception science, and AI safety, offering insights into temporal alignment of systems whose processing rates exceed environmental dynamics.
