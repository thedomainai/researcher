# Can Regulation Curb AI Adoption Narrative–Practice Decoupling?An Agent-Based Model of the A-Share Market

**著者**: 孙明
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: AIガバナンス

## Abstract

Firms increasingly communicate their adoption of artificial intelligence (AI), but the intensity of such narratives may not correspond to the extent of AI implementation in their existing business operations. This paper examines how the gap between firms’ public AI narratives and observable AI-related activities interacts with investor behaviour and regulatory design. We build an agent-based model of firms, retail and institutional investors, and a regulator, informed by firm-level data on 3,133 A-share listed firms (2021–2024). AI narrative intensity (Talking) is measured from annual-report text, while observable AI implementation (Doing) is proxied by AI-related hiring and patenting; their standardised difference measures narrative–practice decoupling, not proof of deceptive intent. Under a common AI-hype shock, we compare nine stylised regulatory mechanisms with a no-regulation baseline across four intensity levels (200 ensemble simulations). Four results emerge. The market’s own pricing discipline is weak (β D ≈ − 0.37 at λ = 0). Effects stratify sharply: repeat-offender escalation (− 2.07) and targeted enforcement (− 1.80) are strongest, while short selling and label-only disclosure coincide exactly with the no-regulation baseline (zero effect under this implementation). Ablation shows that structural design—escalation rather than raw penalty size—drives effectiveness. A Pareto search identifies repeat-offender escalation plus targeted enforcement as a recurring core pair (85.2% of non-dominated solutions) and the best tested combination (β D = − 2.125), with deterrence non-monotonic in the number of mechanisms. These findings are conditional on the model’s behavioural assumptions; policy implications are illustrative and require validation against real-market outcomes.
