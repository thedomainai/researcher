# Tech-Enabled Recruitment Architecture for the Digital Energy Workforce

**著者**: Adeel Riaz
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: meta_analysis | **分野**: AIガバナンス

## Abstract

Abstract Energy sector organizations face persistent speed, accuracy, and fairness challenges in recruiting digitally skilled professionals, placing them at a disadvantage against technology firms competing for the same talent pool. At nuclear power generation organizations, this challenge is compounded by workforce scale and heterogeneity: recruitment must simultaneously serve high-volume outage staffing, safety-critical license-gated roles, specialized engineering and maintenance functions, and a large managed-service contractor population, often within a single hiring cycle. This paper proposes the architecture and implementation roadmap for a tech-enabled recruitment platform designed to accelerate identification, assessment, and selection of digital and specialized talent at a UAE nuclear energy organization. The proposed platform comprises four integrated components: a natural language processing (NLP) pipeline for semantic skills extraction using domain-specific entity recognition trained on energy sector role taxonomies, including verification screening for regulatory-gated roles; adaptive technical assessments calibrated to demonstrated capability across architecture, cloud, data engineering, and AI/ML competency areas; a structured interview scoring model using SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) attribution designed to make cross-evaluator inconsistencies detectable and auditable; and end-to-end workflow automation integrated with the organization's HRMS. Candidate data governance is designed around a privacy-by-design framework with consent management, data minimization, and a full audit trail. Industry-wide benchmarks indicate the scale of efficiency gain such platforms can plausibly deliver: recent survey data from SHRM and LinkedIn indicate that organizations using AI in recruiting report material time and workload reductions in screening and administrative tasks, while independent academic literature on explainable AI demonstrates that SHAP-based attribution is a validated method for detecting bias in automated hiring decisions. This paper uses these external benchmarks, together with the organization's recruitment volumes and role diversity, to establish anticipated outcome ranges and a proposed evaluation framework for the platform, rather than reporting outcomes from a completed deployment. The paper concludes that tech-enabled recruitment is a strategic operational capability for nuclear and broader energy organizations, and that designing governance transparency, including algorithmic explainability and candidate data rights, into the platform from the outset positions transparency as a talent attraction differentiator rather than a compliance cost.
