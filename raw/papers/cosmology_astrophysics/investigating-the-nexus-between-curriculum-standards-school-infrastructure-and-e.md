# Investigating the nexus between curriculum standards, school infrastructure, and equitable education: the mediating role of teachers’ quality

**著者**: Mohammad Faruque Sarker, Md Shamimul Islam
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 宇宙論・宇宙物理

## Abstract

Purpose Achieving equitable education is central to sustainable development, yet empirical models explaining its determinants remain limited. The purpose of this study is to develop a research model of equitable education and to test an integrated framework examining how curriculum standards, school infrastructure and teachers’ quality influence equitable education outcomes in secondary schools. Design/methodology/approach A quantitative research design was adopted. Data were collected from multilevel respondents: students and teachers via multi-stage cluster sampling. This study used partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) technique and analyzed data using Smart PLS software. Findings The findings confirm that curriculum standards and teachers’ quality have significant and positive direct relationships with equitable education outcomes. Importantly, teachers’ quality partially mediates the relationship between curriculum standards and equitable education, and fully mediates the relationship between school infrastructure and equitable education. School infrastructure has no direct effect on equitable education. Originality/value This study contributes to the educational equity literature in three ways. First, it develops and validates a measurement model for equitable education, providing a useful instrument for future empirical research. Second, it proposes and tests an integrated framework linking curriculum standards, school infrastructure, teachers’ quality, and equitable education. Third, it demonstrates the pivotal mediating role of teachers’ quality in converting institutional resources into equitable educational outcomes. The findings provide evidence-based guidance for policymakers and school administrators seeking to achieve equitable and sustainable educational development. Future studies should validate this predictive model in diverse socioeconomic and cultural contexts to enhance its generalizability.
