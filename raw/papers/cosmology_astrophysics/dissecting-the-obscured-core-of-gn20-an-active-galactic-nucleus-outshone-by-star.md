# Dissecting the obscured core of GN20: An active galactic nucleus outshone by star formation

**著者**: M. Hamed, Luis Colina, Pablo G. Pérez‐González, J. Álvarez Márquez, Alejandro Crespo Gómez
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 2
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 宇宙論・宇宙物理

## Abstract

We investigated the relative contributions of star formation and active galactic nucleus (AGN) activity to the total energy budget of GN20, one of the most luminous dusty star-forming galaxies known at z>4, through spatially resolved spectral energy distribution (SED) decomposition. We performed Bayesian SED fitting with CIGALE on two spatially distinct apertures --- the nuclear core (r=0.14 ∼1 kpc physical) and the full galaxy (r=1.4 9.9 kpc) --- combining JWST/NIRCam and MIRI broadband imaging, JWST/NIRSpec PRISM integral field unit pseudo-continuum photometry spanning 42 wavelength bins across rest-frame 0.12--1.05 μm, and archival HST and millimeter interferometry data from NOEMA and PdBI. The integrated SED is dominated by star-heated dust, with only a marginal AGN contribution at galaxy-wide scales (f_ ^ =0.09 AGN int The nuclear core, however, requires a significant AGN component (f_ =0.34 AGN ) to account for a mid-infrared excess at rest-frame wavelengths of ∼2.5--3.6 μm characteristic of AGN-heated torus dust. The AGN accounts for sim34% of the nuclear infrared luminosity but only sim9% of the total integrated L_ spectroscopy. The inferred black hole mass places GN20 within the local M_ while its aperture-corrected luminosity is the same in the two apertures, explaining its weak signature in integrated diagnostics and its consistency with existing upper limits from Spitzer relation at the Eddington limit, and in the over-massive regime at sub-Eddington accretion rates, suggesting early and rapid black hole assembly concurrent with the dominant starburst. GN20 exemplifies a class of systems in which nuclear-scale SED decomposition, enabled by the angular resolution and infrared sensitivity of JWST, is the only means to uncover a buried AGN overwhelmed by galaxy-wide star formation.
