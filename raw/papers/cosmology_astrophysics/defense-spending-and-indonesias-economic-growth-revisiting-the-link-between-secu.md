# Defense Spending and Indonesia's Economic Growth: Revisiting the Link Between Security Maritime and Development

**著者**: Windy Widya Putri, Sri Murtiana, Endang Purwaningsih
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 宇宙論・宇宙物理

## Abstract

The relationship between defense expenditure and economic growth remains unsettled because strategic security, domestic production, technological learning, and fiscal opportunity costs operate through channels. This article reexamines Indonesian military spending during 2021–2025 using exploratory secondary data and a structured, non-systematic narrative synthesis of ten comparative empirical studies. Evidence comes from SIPRI, Indonesian budget documentation, official economic statistics, and published research available through November 2026. Internationally defined Indonesian military expenditure reached approximately US$15.03 billion, or 1.04% of gross domestic product, in 2025. Real economic growth was 5.11% that year, although their simultaneous movement does not demonstrate causation. The analysis separates operational security benefits from possible domestic industrial effects and alternative public spending forgone. A Schumpeterian perspective identifies conditional innovation pathways involving research, supplier learning, technology transfer, and civilian applications, without presuming that these outcomes occurred. Available public data cannot reliably quantify Indonesian defense research spillovers, domestic procurement value added, or sector-specific expenditure displacement. An illustrative allocation equivalent to 1.5% of unchanged 2025 GDP would imply roughly US$21.7 billion, but this calculation is neither a verified government target nor a forecast. Comparative studies report heterogeneous associations across countries, periods, and empirical methods. The study therefore offers an evidence-bounded interpretation rather than an estimated national growth multiplier. It recommends consistent expenditure classifications, transparent procurement evaluation, measurable maritime capability outputs, and explicit assessment of long-term financing and fiscal trade-offs for policy deliberation in Indonesia.
