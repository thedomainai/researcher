# Job quality in developed economies: a systematic literature review

**著者**: Angela J. Knox, Jo Ingold, Huong Le, Qian Yi Lee
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: systematic_review | **分野**: 宇宙論・宇宙物理

## Abstract

Purpose This paper systematically reviews empirical literature on job quality (JQ) in developed economies. Based on 40 articles spanning over 25 years, we analyse the key definitions, theories, methods, measures and outcomes associated with JQ in developed economies. Design/methodology/approach In conducting the review, we followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. Our initial search yielded 9262 published records. Based on inclusion criteria, 40 articles were included in this review. These papers were published between 1999 and 2025, across 31 journals. Most studies were published after 2010 in management, industrial relations and health-related journals. Findings We reveal inconsistencies and problems with existing approaches, and make conceptual, theoretical and methodological contributions. Finally, we develop a research agenda to inform and improve future studies related to JQ, and advance the literature and policymaking. Research limitations/implications In terms of possible limitations regarding these findings, restricting the review to Q1 journal could potentially bias the results and the inclusion of only English-language studies could inadvertently exclude valuable research published in other languages. Practical implications The findings will enable researchers to better plan and design future research. This research will usefully inform policymaking directed towards improving JQ and subsequently benefiting workers, organisations, economies and societies. Social implications Advancing theoretical and empirical knowledge regarding JQ will enable the creation and maintenance of better jobs and thereby improve social and economic outcomes, including health and wellbeing and social mobility. Originality/value Extending beyond the narrow foci of previous JQ reviews, this review identifies gaps and inconsistencies in JQ research and generates conceptual, theoretical and methodological contributions. Subsequently, it develops a future research agenda designed to enable researchers to better plan and design research, and inform policymaking.
