# Adaptive Reduction Theory (ART): Editors of Trajectories

**著者**: Oleksii Grakov
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

Adaptive Reduction Theory (ART) extends from system description to system intervention by analyzing how trajectories are edited under incomplete representations, hidden remainder, bounded action, and viability constraints. Rather than assuming neutral control or frictionless optimization, ART studies how agents and institutions redirect evolving systems using partial models of reality while attempting to preserve operational viability under disturbance and change. This volume develops the concept of editors of trajectories: actors that modify system evolution through selection, suppression, amplification, timing, coordination, repair, and structural intervention. Editors include governments, firms, hospitals, educational systems, scientific institutions, media platforms, legal systems, and artificial intelligence systems. Using the canonical ART kernel, 𝐾∗=(Ω,Φ,Ξ,𝑇,Λ,𝑉,Π,𝐴) the manuscript analyzes how interventions preserve viability, generate hidden burdens, distort objectives, transfer fragility, or stabilize adaptive order. It further examines editors themselves—including regulators, evaluators, scientific institutions, and other systems of falsification—as objects subject to calibration failure, capture, delay, and structural drift. For organized activities that persistently modify future states under constraints, ART proposes that many domain differences may be represented as projections of a common kernel, where apparent distinctions arise through coordinate choices, metrics, and local constraints rather than wholly separate structures. ART is proposed as a general language for governance, management, policy, organizational control, scientific self-audit, and machine-assisted decision systems.
