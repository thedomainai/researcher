# The tension between artificial intelligence and knowledge: Power relations in the policies of universities and publishing organisations

**著者**: Xin Zhou
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

Maintaining academic integrity is a serious issue in higher education, affecting the paradigms governing the production and dissemination of human knowledge. Although a significant body of research has explored the ethics of AI tool usage in higher education settings, these studies have neglected to examine the power relations inherent within them. In response to this issue, this study employs Bourdieu’s theories of symbolic violence and actor-network theory to review the AI usage policies of Australian higher education institutions and mainstream academic journal publishers. Specifically, actor-network theory is first applied to identify the governance networks surrounding AI usage within the technological adoption process, thereby establishing that AI tools act as mediating agents that actively facilitate communication between institutions and users. Furthermore, symbolic violence provides a comprehensive explanation of the means by which power-holding institutions legitimise such governance. In summary, the aim of this inquiry is to expose and challenge the “false pretences” of AI ethics regulations that merely adjust, reinforce and gloss over the status quo, thereby channelling the potential of AI towards justice and combating latent human bias.
