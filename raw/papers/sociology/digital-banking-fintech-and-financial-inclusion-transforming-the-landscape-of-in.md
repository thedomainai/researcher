# Digital Banking, FinTech, and Financial Inclusion: Transforming the Landscape of Inclusive Finance

**著者**: Kiran Badiger, RAJAKUMAR UKKUND
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

The rapid convergence of digital banking and financial technology (FinTech) has emerged as one of the most consequential forces reshaping the global financial ecosystem, with profound implications for the pursuit of universal financial inclusion. This paper examines the multifaceted relationship between digital banking innovations, FinTech disruption, and inclusive finance, with particular emphasis on developing economies and historically underserved populations. Drawing upon theoretical frameworks, empirical evidence from diverse national contexts, and policy analyses, the study investigates how mobile banking platforms, digital payment systems, blockchain technology, artificial intelligence, peer-to-peer lending mechanisms, and Central Bank Digital Currencies are collectively dismantling traditional barriers to formal financial participation. Landmark cases such as Kenya's M-Pesa revolution, India's Unified Payments Interface (UPI) ecosystem, and China's digital payments infrastructure illustrate what is achievable when technology, institutional will, and coherent policy frameworks converge around shared inclusion objectives. The article further explores the persistent structural challenges that continue to impede inclusive financial progress, including the digital divide, in adequate rural connectivity, digital and financial literacy deficits, gender disparities in technology access, cybersecurity vulnerabilities, algorithmic bias in AI-driven financial systems, and the inherent tensions in regulating rapidly evolving FinTech ecosystems without stifling beneficial innovation. The findings suggest that while digital banking and FinTech hold immense transformative potential for democratizing access to finance, realizing this potential in a sustainable and equitable manner requires deliberate policy interventions, innovation-friendly yet consumer-protective regulatory frameworks, meaningful public-private partnerships, and sustained investments in digital literacy and infrastructure development. The paper concludes by advancing actionable strategic recommendations for governments, financial regulators, banking institutions, FinTech firms, and civil society organizations to collectively harness digital innovation as a genuine and durable catalyst for equitable financial development across all segments of society.
