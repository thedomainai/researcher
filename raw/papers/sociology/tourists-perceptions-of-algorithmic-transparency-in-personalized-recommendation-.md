# Tourists’ Perceptions of Algorithmic Transparency in Personalized Recommendation Systems: A Content Analysis in Smart Tourism Destinations

**著者**: Yunyoung Kim Yunyoung Kim, Wooje Cho
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

Purpose: This study examines how tourists perceive the transparency of personalized recommendation algorithms in smart tourism destinations. Methodology: Using a content analysis approach, this study analyzed 145 user-generated texts collected between 2022 and 2025 from Google Maps, TripAdvisor, social media platforms, and online communities to identify interpretive patterns in tourists’ responses to AI-based recommendation systems. Findings: Algorithmic recommendation systems shape user perceptions through multiple interrelated dimensions, encompassing transparency and explainability in how recommendations are generated, privacy vulnerability associated with data collection and perceived surveillance, system competence and usefulness reflected in accuracy and contextual relevance, and emotional-cognitive reactions such as trust, discomfort, and perceived loss of autonomy. Research limitations/implications: A limitation of this study is that it relies on user-generated text data, which constrains the examination of cognitive mechanisms and causal relationships underlying perceptions of algorithmic transparency. In addition, the temporal scope of the dataset, limited to the period from 2022 to 2025, may not fully capture rapid advancements in AI-driven recommendation systems and evolving regulatory environments. Moreover, the lack of differentiation across platform types, recommendation contexts, and cultural backgrounds restricts the generalizability of the findings. These limitations should be considered when interpreting the results and suggest opportunities for future research. Originality/value: The findings show that transparency is interpreted as an experiential concept shaped by system performance, data governance, and emotional comfort. These insights highlight the need for user-centered transparency strategies and ethical algorithmic practices in smart tourism. The study contributes to broader discussions on responsible AI by clarifying how tourists construct meaning around algorithmic processes in digital travel environments.
