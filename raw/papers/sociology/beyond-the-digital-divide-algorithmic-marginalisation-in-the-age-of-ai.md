# Beyond the digital divide: algorithmic marginalisation in the age of AI

**著者**: Azmit Begum, Khusbu Thakur, Saleeq Ahmad Dar
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

Purpose The purpose of this paper is to examine how the advent of artificial intelligence (AI) may exacerbate the existing digital divide and create new forms of inequality, particularly among marginalised communities. Drawing on the Matthew effect, the paper explores how existing advantages in digital access, AI literacy and knowledge resources may accumulate over time, enabling privileged groups to benefit disproportionately from AI while further disadvantaging those already excluded. Design/methodology/approach This paper adopts a qualitative, conceptual methodology based on a critical review of the literature on the digital divide, digital equity, AI literacy and the Matthew effect. It also uses a conceptual figure to illustrate how AI-driven decisions, shaped by information available in online data sets, may be imposed on marginalised communities whose experiences and perspectives remain underrepresented in these data sets. Findings AI may deepen existing inequalities when digital data sets underrepresent the knowledge and experiences of marginalised communities. The Gujjar and Bakerwal communities of Jammu and Kashmir illustrate how such omissions can exclude them from AI-driven decision-making. Digital equity must therefore ensure not only access to technology but also meaningful representation in the knowledge systems shaping otherwise AI risks reinforcing the very inequalities it promises to overcome. Originality/value This paper is original and represents the authors’ own work. It offers a distinct perspective on the relationship between AI, the digital divide and the marginalisation of underrepresented communities.
