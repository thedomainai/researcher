# Public trust in the adoption of AI for cancer diagnostics: developing a human-AI trust and ethics model

**著者**: Petra Cattanach
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

As the UK Government and NHS seek to move from trials to ‘scaling up’ AI programmes for the early detection of cancers, this research emphasises the involvement of the UK public as a primary stakeholder in a human-centric approach to adoption. Furthermore, this research claims that whilst these systems offer significant benefits to Computer-Aided Diagnostics (CAD), they are coupled with possible risks, such as the lack of transparency and accountability. Additionally, these risks have the potential to impact human rights and undermine individual and societal ethical principles, ultimately eroding public trust in Artificial Intelligence as a Medical Device (AIaMD) and negatively impacting their adoption in clinical workflows. Through the lens of Human-Computer Trust theory, the invisible factors of Cognitive and Affect-based Trust, and the novel inclusion of Ethical Principles that contribute to public trust in the adoption of AI cancer diagnostics are explored. Prompted by the literature, a novel conceptual model is developed that considers both ethical principles alongside trust to acknowledge the social and ethical impact of the adoption of AI in healthcare. Moreover, the Human Healthcare Practitioner (human-in-the-loop) is considered as a mediating factor. Quantitative data is collected from a representative sample (n = 311), and descriptive and inferential statistics are applied to analyse the data to identify patterns and test the hypotheses and relationships. The findings from the study indicate that Cognitive-based Trust is a contributing factor to trust, showing a growing confidence in AIaMDs; however, Affect-based Trust is low, demonstrating that the respondents are not willing to completely trust the output of these systems at this time. While Ethical Principles are important factors to the respondents and contribute to trust, they are less significant in the model than Cognitive-based Trust. Finally, the human-in-the-loop factor is important but does not contribute to trust in AIaMDs. This research contributes to current academic theory, methodology and practice through the identification of the research gap, the development of a theoretical framework drawn from the resulting literature, that in turn contributes to a conceptual model that is tested using novel empirical data collected from the UK public. Finally, this research promotes ‘plain English’ communication to the public of AI's benefits and risks at an individual and societal level to support successful adoption within NHS oncology.
