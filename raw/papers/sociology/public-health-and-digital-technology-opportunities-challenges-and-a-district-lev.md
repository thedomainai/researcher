# Public Health and Digital Technology: Opportunities, Challenges and A District-Level Framework for Parbhani, Maharashtra

**著者**: Virendra M Chavhan, Miss Sonal Tuljaram Kame
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

Abstract Digital technology has become an important component of contemporary public-health systems. Telemedicine, mobile health applications, electronic health records, digital identity, health information systems, remote monitoring, artificial intelligence and data analytics can support prevention, early detection, referral, continuity of care and population-level surveillance. India has developed a substantial digital-health ecosystem, including the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM) and eSanjeevani. WHO describes digital health as an area with major potential to improve access, decision-making and health-system performance, while also emphasizing equity, interoperability and responsible implementation. [1–5] This paper examines the relationship between public health and digital technology with special reference to Parbhani district of Maharashtra. It uses a narrative review and policy-analysis approach based on WHO documents, Government of India sources, Census material and recent peer-reviewed literature. The paper discusses potential applications in primary healthcare, maternal and child health, nutrition, noncommunicable disease management, infectious-disease surveillance, health education and referral services. Particular attention is given to the rural–urban digital divide, digital literacy, connectivity, privacy, cybersecurity, interoperability and workforce readiness. A district-level framework is proposed to connect community-level workers with digital registration, teleconsultation, electronic records and public-health analytics. The paper argues that technology should complement rather than replace human-centred public-health services. For Parbhani, a phased approach combining infrastructure, training, assisted digital access, data governance and local evaluation can support more equitable digital health development.
