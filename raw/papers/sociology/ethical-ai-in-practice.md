# Ethical AI in Practice

**著者**: Pradhyuman Singh Lakhawat, Rashmi Singhai, Poonam Singh, Kenneth Amoah Binfoh
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

The swift incorporation of artificial intelligence (AI) into science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) education has generated revolutionary prospects for customized learning, intelligent tutoring, predictive analytics, and automated evaluation. But with these benefits come important ethical issues that have to do with governance, openness, responsibility, prejudice, privacy, and responsible innovation. This chapter looks at how Ethical AI is changing in STEM education by looking at real-world case studies that show both good and bad uses of AI-powered educational technology. The chapter starts by putting AI in the context of modern STEM education systems. It talks about how data-driven decision-making, adaptive learning platforms, and AI-powered analytics are changing the way teachers and students learn. These technologies may lead to better learning and more efficient use of resources, but they also make people worry about things like algorithmic bias, unclear decision-making processes, monitoring of student data, and unfair access to AI tools. The chapter examines how educational institutions, technology creators, and legislators are dealing with the ethical issues that come up when AI is used in schools by looking at a few case studies that focus on institutions, technology, and policy. There is a lot of focus on governance structures that make sure AI-enabled classrooms are accountable and follow the rules. The chapter also analyzes transparency mechanisms such as explainable AI models, open algorithms, and participatory oversight systems that allow educators and students to understand and question automated decisions. The report also emphasizes responsible innovation strategies that incorporate ethical design principles, stakeholder involvement, and fairness concerns along the entire AI development process. The chapter presents a structured framework for the implementation of ethical AI practices in STEM education by integrating insights from several case settings. The results show that ethical governance, clear algorithms, and socially responsible innovation are all necessary to develop trust in AI systems and make sure that everyone has equal access to education. In the end, this chapter adds to ongoing academic debates about AI ethics in education and gives useful advice to politicians, teachers, and tech developers who want to use AI responsibly in STEM learning environments.
