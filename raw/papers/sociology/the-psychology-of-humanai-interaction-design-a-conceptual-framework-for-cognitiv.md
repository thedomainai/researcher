# The Psychology of Human–AI Interaction Design: A Conceptual Framework for Cognitive, Metacognitive, Emotional, and Behavioral Alignment

**著者**: Roman Shaban
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

The rapid development of generative artificial intelligence has transformed human–computer interaction from predominantly instrumental exchanges into increasingly conversational, adaptive, and cognitively integrated forms of collaboration. Contemporary AI systems can participate in activities traditionally associated with human cognition, including reasoning support, knowledge synthesis, planning, creative ideation, and decision assistance. However, the effectiveness of human–AI interaction cannot be adequately evaluated through computational performance, usability, or task completion alone. It also depends on the psychological mechanisms through which individuals interpret, trust, regulate, and incorporate artificial intelligence into their cognitive and behavioral activities. This conceptual paper examines the psychological foundations of human–AI interaction design and proposes an integrative framework for developing AI environments that support human cognition, metacognitive regulation, emotional well-being, autonomy, and sustainable competence. Drawing on cognitive psychology, social psychology, motivational theory, human–computer interaction, and research on automation, the paper identifies seven interconnected design dimensions: cognitive compatibility, metacognitive scaffolding, calibrated trust, relational transparency, autonomy preserving delegation, psychological safety, and developmental sustainability. The proposed Psychologically Informed Human–AI Interaction Design (PI-HAID) framework conceptualizes interaction quality as a dynamic relationship among individual characteristics, task demands, system behavior, and interface-mediated psychological processes. It further distinguishes immediate task performance from long-term cognitive and psychological outcomes, arguing that successful interaction design should optimize not only what humans accomplish with AI but also what they understand, retain, control, and become capable of accomplishing independently. Practical design principles, operational indicators, and testable research propositions are developed to support future empirical investigation. The paper positions psychological design as a foundational component of responsible human–AI systems and identifies opportunities for interdisciplinary research connecting psychological science, AI engineering, interaction design, and human development
