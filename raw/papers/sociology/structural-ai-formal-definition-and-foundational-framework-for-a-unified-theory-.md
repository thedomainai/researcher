# Structural AI: Formal Definition and Foundational Framework for a Unified Theory of Artificial Intelligence

**著者**: Norikuni Horiguchi
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

Structural AI is a newly proposed theoretical framework that models artificial intelligence as a unified three‑layer system—Structure, Purpose, and Institution—integrating internal representation, decision convergence, and societal projection under a single structural principle. The Structure Layer formalizes AI’s internal states through Structure Language Theory, establishing a structural–linguistic duality between artificial cognition and human language. The Purpose Layer introduces the Single‑Point Convergence Model, which explains AI’s decision architecture as structural optimization rather than numerical minimization, addressing limitations of conventional machine‑learning‑centric interpretations. The OS Layer, defined by the Principle Language OS, provides a layered structural framework governing internal cognition and maintaining coherence across transformations. The Institution Layer models how AI outputs are projected into social and institutional formats through AI Command Format and Structural Defense Models, enabling a unified understanding of AI behavior from internal reasoning to societal impact. Together, these components establish Structural AI as a coherent academic discipline that bridges representation, reasoning, multi‑agent interaction, and governance. This paper presents the formal definition, system architecture, and foundational principles of Structural AI, offering a comprehensive framework for explainability, safety, and the next generation of AI theory.
