# Institutional archetypes of resistance to artificial intelligence

**著者**: Michal Kubovics
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: meta_analysis | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

Introduction What is the underlying structure of resistance to the adoption of artificial intelligence in media organisations? This study is the first to combine Ising network analysis with latent class analysis (LCA) to map the architecture of barriers to the adoption of artificial intelligence and to identify individual-level archetypes of resistance, interpreted through the lens of institutional logic. Methods Data were collected in March 2026 from 151 media and marketing professionals from the UK and the US via the Prolific platform. Seven barriers were measured as binary indicators. The analysis combined Ising network analysis (IsingFit, with a comparative EBICglasso estimation and bootstrapped stability checks), latent class analysis (poLCA, K = 2–5), Kruskal-Wallis tests with effect sizes, and binary logistic regression supplemented with IRT composites. Results The Ising model reveals a sparse 'hub-and-spoke' network of barriers (2 out of 21 possible edges), in which mistrust acts as a central node linking legal risk (w = 2.832) and quality concerns (w = 3.431), whilst the five remaining barriers function as conditionally independent dimensions. LCA (K = 2, BIC = 1063.6, entropy = 1.000, bootstrap stable in 99.5% of 200 resamples) identifies two qualitatively distinct archetypes: T1 Practical Opponents (42.4%, n = 64), characterised by structural and capacity barriers in the complete absence of distrust [ P = 0.000 (95% CI: 0.000, 0.000)]; and T2 Distrustful Sceptics (57.6%, n = 87), defined by absolute distrust [ P = 1.000 (0.934, 1.000)] and saturated legal ( P = 1.000) and qualitative barriers ( P = 1.000). A sensitivity LCA without distrust confirms the two-class structure (BIC = 972.2, entropy = 1.000), thereby establishing robustness beyond a simple division based on distrust. Membership of an archetype is not predicted by organisation type, frequency of AI use, or perceived risk/benefit scores (binary logistic regression, all p &amp;gt; 0.24; Nagelkerke's pseudo-R2 = 0.040). T2 sceptics demand significantly greater transparency regarding AI content (M = 4.44 vs. 3.97; H = 9.401, p = 0.002, r = 0.250), whilst exhibiting comparable preferences for human oversight to T1. Discussion Validation of the theory of resistance to innovation confirms that T1 are passive resisters (functional/psychological ratio F/ P = 1.16) and T2 are active resisters (F/ P = 0.61; H = 70.324, p &amp;lt; 0.001, r = 0.682). The findings are consistent with an interpretation of archetypes as epistemological rather than structural constructs, although the absence of significant predictors does not by itself exclude measurement limitations or limited statistical power.
