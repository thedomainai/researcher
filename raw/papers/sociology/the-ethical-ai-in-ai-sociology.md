# The Ethical AI in AI Sociology

**著者**: Prem Misir
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

This chapter underscores the need for an ethical AI, grounded not solely in computer science, but in a perceptive and philosophical understanding of how social forces influence technology. Employing a Bourdieusian lens, among others, to the codes linking our digital society may demonstrate how redesigning ethical standards can challenge structural racism and systemic inequities, which existing AI models repeatedly typify. This chapter includes several theoretical frameworks (ethical AI as a sociopolitical phenomenon, inclusive of Social Construction of Technology, Actor-Network Theory, and technological determinism; Critical Theory, inclusive of power relations, reproduction of inequality, and ethics washing; and a shift from prescriptive to critical/reflexive task), intending to present an understanding of the multi-layered relationship between AI and society. These sociological tools are essential for any inquiry into when and how inequalities are made stronger by AI systems because AI sociology has shifted the center from targeting technical errors to targeting the unequal distribution of power and resources grounded within society&s;s institutions and systems.
