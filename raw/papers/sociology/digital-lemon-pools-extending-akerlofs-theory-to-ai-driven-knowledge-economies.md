# Digital Lemon Pools: Extending Akerlof's Theory to AI-Driven Knowledge Economies

**著者**: Ramakant Kulkarni, Bharati Badiger
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

This paper revisits the seminal contribution of George A. Akerlof in The Market for Lemons and extends the concept of the “lemon pool” to contemporary digital and AI-driven knowledge economies. Akerlof’s theory of information asymmetry explains how quality uncertainty leads to adverse selection, resulting in the dominance of low-quality goods in markets. In the present digital era, characterized by the widespread adoption of artificial intelligence tools such as ChatGPT and large-scale digital platforms, similar asymmetries have intensified in content, research, and service markets. Using a conceptual and exploratory methodology based on secondary data, this study identifies the emergence of “digital lemon pools” in AI-generated content ecosystems, academic publishing, e-commerce, and gig economy platforms. The findings suggest that the inability to effectively distinguish between high- and low-quality outputs leads to declining trust, reduced incentives for quality production, and potential systemic inefficiencies. The paper further discusses the self-reinforcing cycle of quality degradation and evaluates existing corrective mechanisms such as signalling, certification, and reputation systems. The study concludes that while technological advancements have democratized production and access, they have simultaneously exacerbated information asymmetry, making Akerlof’s insights more relevant than ever. The paper calls for strengthened institutional and technological interventions to preserve trust and quality in digital knowledge markets.
