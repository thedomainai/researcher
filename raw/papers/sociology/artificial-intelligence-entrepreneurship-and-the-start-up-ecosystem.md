# Artificial Intelligence, Entrepreneurship and the Start-up Ecosystem

**著者**: Dr. ANUJ KUMAR Sharma
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

India's start-up ecosystem has, within roughly a decade, grown from a policy aspiration into one of the world's largest by volume: 1,97,692 entities carried formal Startup India recognition as of October 2025, built atop a national venture-funding base that raised approximately USD 10.5 billion across 2025 alone. Artificial intelligence now occupies an unusual dual position within this ecosystem, simultaneously a tool available to founders across every sector and the defining characteristic of an increasingly dominant sub-sector of new ventures in its own right. This chapter examines that dual position directly, organised around three interdependent questions: how AI is being used as a tool within the entrepreneurial process itself, how AI-focused ventures are performing as a distinct and increasingly concentrated sector of India's start-up base, and how AI is reshaping the broader ecosystem - the policy instruments, capital pools, and institutional infrastructure - within which both kinds of venture operate. It develops a conceptual framework distinguishing these tool, sector, and ecosystem dimensions, situated within Entrepreneurship Theory and Practice's recent call for a dedicated scholarly research agenda on AI and entrepreneurship and within NITI Aayog's Responsible AI governance principles. It then reviews the rapidly expanding global academic literature on AI's role across the entrepreneurial process - opportunity identification, decision-making, venture performance, and entrepreneurship education - before synthesising recent India-specific evidence: a national AI start-up funding trajectory that reached USD 676 million across fifty-seven deals in the first half of 2026 alone, a deep-tech investment base in which artificial intelligence now accounts for 84 per cent of start-ups and 91 per cent of funding, and two India-based artificial-intelligence unicorns reached within roughly two years of each other. Set against a global venture-capital market in which AI captured an unprecedented 80 per cent of all funding in the first quarter of 2026, concentrated overwhelmingly in a handful of foundation-model companies, the chapter argues that India's AI start-up ecosystem is expanding briskly by its own historical standard while remaining a comparatively minor participant in the global capital concentration defining the current moment - a distinction with direct implications for how the ecosystem's policy architecture should be read.
