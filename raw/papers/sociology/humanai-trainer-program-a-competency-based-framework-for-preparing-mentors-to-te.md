# Human–AI Trainer Program: A Competency Based Framework for Preparing Mentors to Teach Effective, Critical, and Responsible Artificial Intelligence Use

**著者**: Roman Shaban
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

The increasing adoption of generative artificial intelligence (AI) across educational, professional, and everyday environments creates a growing need for individuals capable not only of using AI systems effectively but also of teaching others to interact with these technologies critically, responsibly, and independently. Although AI literacy initiatives increasingly address foundational knowledge and practical skills, the systematic preparation of mentors who can facilitate sustainable human–AI competence remains an important educational challenge. This article introduces the Human–AI Trainer Program (HATP), a conceptual, competency-based framework for preparing AI mentors, educators, workplace trainers, and professional facilitators. The proposed program integrates technological knowledge, instructional methodology, cognitive and metacognitive regulation, critical evaluation of AI-generated information, ethical responsibility, and learner-centered professional development. Drawing on AI literacy research, self-regulated learning theory, technological pedagogical content knowledge, and international AI competency frameworks, the article distinguishes effective AI use from the ability to teach others how to use AI. It argues that technological proficiency alone is insufficient for successful AI mentorship. The article presents a multidimensional trainer competency model, an eight-module educational curriculum, the TRAIN-AI instructional cycle, assessment criteria, supervised teaching requirements, and a proposed evaluation methodology. Particular attention is given to preventing uncritical AI dependence, adapting instruction to learners with different capabilities, maintaining human agency, and supporting the transfer of learning into independent professional practice. The proposed framework is intended for universities, professional development organizations, corporate learning environments, educational institutions, and independent AI training initiatives. The central argument is that the effectiveness of AI education should be evaluated not by how many technological functions learners can reproduce, but by their ability to use artificial intelligence purposefully, critically, ethically, and independently across changing contexts
