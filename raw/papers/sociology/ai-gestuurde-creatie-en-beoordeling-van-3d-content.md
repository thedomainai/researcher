# AI-gestuurde creatie en beoordeling van 3D-content

**著者**: Jit Chatterjee
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

The demand for 3D content keeps rising across games, movies, online retail, industrial digital twins, and eXtended Reality (XR). The supply, however, depends on a complex manual process. A hand-built model passes through modeling, sculpting, unwrapping, texturing, rigging, and animation. Every stage requires a different specialist, and completing one specific model can take weeks. On the other hand, capture based alternatives, such as multi-view camera setups, require hardware and expertise that most users do not have. Artificial Intelligence (AI), and in particular Generative AI (GenAI), offers a path towards reducing these costs, since it can learn to generate new content from data rather than relying on manual effort or dedicated capture equipment. Therefore, there is a need for AI-assisted methods that operate on an input already available to everyone. Deep Generative Networks have reached impressive quality in 2D image generation. Tools such as DALL-E and Stable Diffusion can produce a photorealistic image from a short text prompt. Moreover, their ease of use has allowed for a general adoption by society as non-experts can employ these tools without dedicated training. However, extending them to 3D content raises two difficulties related to their generation and evaluation. First, a 3D model must remain consistent from every viewing direction, which typically requires many input views. Therefore, a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF), for instance, needs dozens of images of a scene to reconstruct it photorealistically. Second, the web-scale image collections that enabled 2D generation have no counterpart in 3D. Moreover, generated 3D content is difficult to evaluate. Quality is usually measured either through subjective studies or through Full-Reference (FR) metrics that compare the content against a pristine original. Neither is applicable here, since generated content is never captured and therefore has no pristine counterpart to compare against. This thesis addresses generation and quality assessment as two parts of the same problem. Therefore, this thesis hypothesizes that photorealistic 3D content can be generated from a single RGB image. Furthermore, its visual quality can be assessed without a reference. Towards this dual objective, the RGB image is first extended into a panorama. Second, it is converted into 3D and textures are added. Third, dynamic human avatars must be included in a photorealistic manner to enable interaction. The work in this thesis is organized around these three research topics, and for each one, challenges related to generation and assessment are tackled. The first topic extends a narrow Field-of-View (nFoV) image into a panoramic image. The extrapolated region must remain coherent with the observed input, and the result must respect the spherical geometry of a 360-degree image. Three contributions are presented, two on generation and one on assessment. First, 360-GAN addresses both requirements, spherical consistency and visual fidelity, by combining a cycle consistent adversarial framework with a structural similarity loss, and reaches a Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) of 46.59, nearly 6 points better than the state-of-the-art. Second, PanoLoRA builds the guidance into the architecture instead. Circular padding removes the discontinuity where the panorama wraps, and Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) adapts a pretrained diffusion model to the panoramic domain by tuning just 0.8% of its parameters, improving FID by 20 points over the state-of-the-art method. Third, on the topic of assessment, QAPIC-VR introduces a framework for performing subjective evaluation of generated RGBD panoramic indoor scenes in Virtual Reality (VR). To validate it, thirty participants evaluated the visual quality and realism of indoor scenes generated using state-of-the-art models, including PanoLoRA. A Friedman test confirmed significant differences between the generators, and PanoLoRA showed the smallest gap to ground truth on every measure. The second topic reconstructs 3D content from a single 2D image, where the geometry and the appearance are recovered separately. Three contributions are presented, two on generation, 3D-Scene-Former and GGD-Tex, and one on assessment, MHyNet-PC. First, 3D-Scene-Former estimates the geometry, refining its initial 3D predictions with a transformer, and outperforms the state-of-the-art in 3D object detection and 3D pose estimation by 11.37%. Second, GGD-Tex synthesizes the texture. It maps the image onto the recovered geometry through predicted masks and depth, and drives a diffusion model conditioned jointly on geometric and visual cues through multi-modal cross-attention, so that the appearance remains consistent with the shape. It improves the FID score by 9.38 points over the leading method. Third, MHyNet-PC introduces a No-Reference (NR) quality assessment framework for point clouds and meshes. Statistical descriptors of the raw geometry are combined with learned features of its rendered projections, raising prediction accuracy by 10% over the state-of-the-art while cutting inference time by 40%. The third topic sets the content in motion and narrows the target to the human. Two contributions are presented, one on generation, PhysAvatar3D, and one on assessment, GAMQ. First, PhysAvatar3D addresses a limitation of pixel-level supervision. Such supervision does not constrain what the input image does not show, so an avatar can satisfy the loss while remaining physically implausible from other viewpoints. PhysAvatar3D overcomes this by consulting a frozen Vision Language Model (VLM) during training. The model inspects the current rendering for physically impossible body regions, and its verdicts become a penalty that removes the offending geometry. The resulting avatar, built as 3D Gaussian splats, outperforms the state-of-the-art methods HumanLift, IDOL, and LHM by 4.090, 5.579, and 2.343 dB in Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), respectively. Second, GAMQ introduces an NR quality assessment model for dynamic 3D human avatars. It extracts geometry, appearance, and motion features with dedicated encoders, fuses them through cross-attention, and achieves a Pearson Linear Correlation Coefficient (PLCC) of 0.8399 on the DDH-QA dataset, outperforming both FR and NR state-of-the-art methods. Beyond these technical contributions, the methods developed in this thesis have practical value across several application domains. In cultural heritage, a museum can build an immersive XR experience of a room or an artwork from 2D image, rather than from a costly multi-view capture system. In retail, a single 2D image of a product can be turned into a 3D model for an online store. In real estate and interior planning, a room can be reconstructed from one image for virtual staging and redesign. In telepresence, a person can be represented by a dynamic 3D avatar built from a single 2D image. In each of these domains, 3D content is needed, but the equipment and expertise to capture it are not available, which is precisely the gap that a single-image approach fills. In conclusion, this thesis shows that photorealistic 3D content can be generated from a single RGB image, provided that the model is guided towards a plausible completion of what the image does not show, and that its visual quality can be assessed without a reference, provided that the assessment combines several modalities and it is aligned with human judgment. Thus, a single photograph becomes a sufficient starting point for assisting 3D content creation. This lowers the barrier for small teams and individual creators, and it serves applications such as XR, digital twins, and telepresence.
