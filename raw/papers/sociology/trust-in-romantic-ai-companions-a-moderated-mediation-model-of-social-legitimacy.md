# Trust in romantic AI companions: a moderated mediation model of social legitimacy, relational safety, and AI shared memory

**著者**: Muhammad Salman Latif, Gang Li, Mohsin Shahzad
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

Purpose Romantic artificial intelligence (AI) companions represent a socially contested and emotionally sensitive form of memory-enabled AI platform, reshaping how users experience emotional connection, engagement, and trust in data-driven human-AI services. However, how users reduce uncertainty and develop trust in such systems remains underexplored. Drawing on uncertainty reduction theory, this study examines how social legitimacy and perceived relational safety shape trust, and how AI shared memory perception changes this trust-building process. Design/methodology/approach This study proposes a moderated mediation model and analyzes survey data from 356 active users of romantic AI companions in China using partial least squares structural equation modeling and necessary condition analysis. Findings Social legitimacy is positively associated with trust, both directly and indirectly through perceived relational safety. AI shared memory perception strengthens perceived relational safety but weakens the relationship between social legitimacy and perceived relational safety, indicating a substitution effect. The indirect effect of social legitimacy on trust via perceived relational safety is stronger when AI shared memory perception is low and weaker when it is high. Originality/value This study extends uncertainty reduction theory to memory-enabled human–AI relationships by showing how external social validation, perceived relational safety, and AI shared memory jointly shape trust. It identifies AI shared memory perception as a mechanism that can partially substitute for external social validation, shifting uncertainty reduction from socially anchored acceptance toward platform-enabled continuity. By examining romantic AI companionship as a context involving stored interaction histories, personalization, and emotional disclosure, the study offers implications for memory transparency, user control, and responsible data governance in AI services.
