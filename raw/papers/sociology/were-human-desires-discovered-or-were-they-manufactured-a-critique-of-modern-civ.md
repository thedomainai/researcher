# Were Human Desires "Discovered," or Were They "Manufactured"? A Critique of Modern Civilization's Self-Fulfilling Ontology from the Perspective of E = ΦR

**著者**: Kazunori Ohumi
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 社会学

## Abstract

What if modern civilization did not merely discover human desire—but manufactured it? This paper argues that economics, education, institutions, and AI increasingly crystallize dynamic human becoming into fixed motivational structures, demanding a civilizational shift from desire optimization toward generative resonance. Modern civilization is built upon a deceptively simple assumption: human beings possess stable desires, and social systems exist to satisfy them efficiently. This assumption underlies economics, consumer capitalism, educational competition, institutional governance, and increasingly AI-mediated personalization. Yet this paper argues that such a view mistakes historically produced motivational patterns for intrinsic human nature. Using Universal Phase Crystallization Theory (UPCT), the paper proposes a radically different existential ontology formalized as E = ΦR, where existence is not static structural identity, but dynamic generative-relational becoming. Desire is therefore not a fixed anthropological input, but an emergent process shaped through institutional, symbolic, and relational environments. The paper introduces the concept of self-fulfilling ontology, showing how ontological assumptions about human nature become institutional designs, institutional designs shape behavior, and repeated adaptation becomes misrecognized as empirical confirmation of “human nature.” Competition, recognition dependence, and consumer desire are analyzed as paradigmatic examples. Artificial intelligence is positioned not merely as a productivity technology, but as a hyper-structural reinforcement system that accelerates desire fixation through adaptive optimization, personalized reinforcement, and frictionless motivational closure. AI thereby risks intensifying the deepest civilizational pathology of modernity: existential over-crystallization. The paper reconstructs freedom as generative participation rather than desire satisfaction, and proposes a new existential ethical criterion: d(ΦR)/dt≥0 This reframes ethics around the preservation and expansion of generative potential and relational resonance, calling for a transition from E = S civilization (structural existence) toward E = ΦR civilization (generative-relational existence). Academic Contributions 1: Ontological Reversal of Modern Anthropology This paper challenges the foundational modern assumption that human desires are stable pre-existing properties. Instead, it argues that institutional systems actively participate in producing the motivational structures later interpreted as human nature. This constitutes a reversal of dominant anthropological assumptions across economics, political theory, and social philosophy. 2: Introduction of Self-Fulfilling Ontology The paper introduces the formal concept of self-fulfilling ontology, extending beyond self-fulfilling prophecy models by demonstrating how ontological assumptions become institutional architectures that recursively generate empirical confirmation. This offers a new conceptual framework for analyzing civilization-level anthropological construction. 3: UPCT-Based Desire Theory By applying Universal Phase Crystallization Theory, the paper reconceptualizes desire as a generative-relational emergent process rather than a stable utility-bearing property. This creates a novel bridge between process ontology, institutional theory, motivation theory, and existential philosophy. 4: AI as Ontological Accelerator The paper advances a fundamentally new AI critique by interpreting artificial intelligence not merely as an automation or epistemic system, but as a structural accelerator of desire fixation and existential closure. This reframes AI ethics at the ontological and civilizational level. 5: Reconstruction of Freedom and Ethics The paper reconstructs freedom as generative participation and introduces a formal existential ethical criterion: d(ΦR)/dt≥0 This offers a normative framework for evaluating institutions and intelligent systems according to whether they preserve or suppress human generative becoming.
