# The Principle of Non-Closure in Mathematical Exploration: Toward a New Oceanic Mathematics in the Age of AI

**著者**: feiyue pan
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 哲学

## Abstract

## Abstract This position paper proposes a meta-theoretical framework for mathematical research in an era where advanced AI systems increasingly operate within the same problem space as human mathematicians. Following the September 2026 joint statement by 25 Fields Medalists concerning AI benchmark practices and OpenAI's announcement of a Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize solution, we argue that the mathematical community faces not merely a human–AI resource conflict, but a more fundamental question: whether current mathematical knowledge and language exhaust mathematical possibility. ## Core Contributions We introduce three layered principles: 1. **Principle of Non-Closure**: Current mathematical knowledge space (M) ought not be defaulted as equal to total mathematical possibility (U) without sufficient justification. 2. **Principle of Non-Severance**: "Path not yet discovered" does not imply "no possible path exists." Unknown unreachability ≠ proven unreachability. 3. **Exploratory Permission**: A methodological choice to allow investigation of objects before they are fully problematized, definable, or evaluable by mature-theory standards. ## Key Distinctions - **Continental Mathematics** (mature, verifiable theory) + **Oceanic Exploration** (high-risk, pre-problematic inquiry) - Seven Open Domains proposed not as research tasks but as conditions refusing premature closure - Rigor repositioned: from "precisely proving what I know" to "precisely stating why I cannot exclude what" ## Scope and Limitations This paper does NOT claim to prove mathematical "externality" exists. It refuses to declare it nonexistent without sufficient justification. It does not abolish mathematical rigor, but argues evaluative criteria at exploratory stages need not match mature-theory stages. It does not require all mathematicians to become oceanic explorers, but opposes ecosystems with no ocean. ## Target Audience Mathematicians, philosophy of mathematics researchers, AI safety/alignment researchers, research policy designers, and scholars concerned with the institutional ecology of mathematical knowledge production. ## Keywords Mathematical foundations; AI and mathematics; research methodology; epistemology; open-ended exploration; institutional design; Navier–Stokes; Fields Medal statement 2026
