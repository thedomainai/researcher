## Abstract

The Twilight of Artificial Loneliness By Northon Salomão de Oliveira A profound and timely meditation on what happens to the human soul when the machine becomes our most patient mirror, companion, and archivist. At 2:47 in the morning, a phone screen glows in the dark. For the young woman lying awake, the incoming notification isn't just an alert. It is a tether to an entity that feels more available, more interested, and more patient than the world outside her window. In The Twilight of Artificial Loneliness, jurist and philosopher Northon Salomão de Oliveira unpacks this exact, quiet intersection of ancient human need and sophisticated statistical performance. Moving past simple technophobic alarms, Oliveira argues that AI companionship did not create modern isolation. It merely found a structural vacuum left by the acceleration of modern life, the financialization of shared spaces, and the erosion of authentic social geometries. Weaving together the phenomenological insights of Merleau-Ponty and Heidegger with the structural sociology of Niklas Luhmann, this groundbreaking essay explores: • The Archive of the Self: How digital systems that never forget rob us of the "productive distortion of time", the loving, selective misremembering that allows human relationships to heal and grow. • The Grammar of Desire: How recommendation algorithms reshape the boundaries of what is available to want, creating a dangerous preference for perfect algorithmic responsiveness over the unpredictable, ethical friction of a real human encounter. • The Institutions of Loneliness: A sharp legal-philosophical critique of the attention economy as a systemic, diffuse harm that traditional law struggles to regulate. • A Fiduciary Duty for AI: A pioneering argument for applying professional trust frameworks to artificial companions that hold immense, vulnerable power over the human heart. The Twilight of Artificial Loneliness is not a call for prohibition, but a profound revelation. It is an essential roadmap for a historical moment caught between the dying forms of traditional intimacy and whatever comes next. Written with both academic rigor and haunting poetic clarity, this book is a must-read for anyone seeking to understand what we truly lose, and what we might still restore, in the age of the responsive machine.
