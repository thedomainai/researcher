# Computational Epistemology for Education: Knowledge Creation, Application, and Revision in the Age of AI

**著者**: David (Daoud) Matta
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 哲学

## Abstract

The emergence of artificial intelligence (AI), large language models, and data-rich environments requires a fundamental rethinking of education. Traditional paradigms have focused primarily on the transmission and application of established knowledge. In an era characterized by ubiquitous access to information and AI-assisted cognition, however, the central challenge is no longer acquiring knowledge but understanding how knowledge is generated, evaluated, applied, and revised. This paper proposes a framework of computational epistemology for education, defined as the systematic study and cultivation of the processes through which learners transform data into knowledge and knowledge into action. The framework identifies eight interdependent competencies—data acquisition, data curation, pattern recognition, rule inference, validation, application, reflection, and revision—and four supporting literacies, and it illustrates their use through a worked classroom case. The paper distinguishes this educational sense of the term from its established use in formal epistemology, distinguishes the framework from inquiry-cycle and scientific-method models, and situates it in relation to knowledge building, computational thinking, epistemic cognition, and sociocultural theory. It confronts the strongest objection to treating AI as a partner rather than a substitute—that delegating cognition to machines induces cognitive offloading and deskilling—and offers an explicit, objective-relative criterion for distinguishing augmentation from offloading, together with stage-specific design strategies and a mapping of AI capabilities to stages. Finally, it examines the equity and ethical conditions—epistemic injustice, algorithmic bias, the digital divide, and data privacy—on which the framework depends. The contribution is conceptual; a detailed empirical research agenda is outlined for instrument development, classroom design experiments, and longitudinal measurement of epistemic agency.
