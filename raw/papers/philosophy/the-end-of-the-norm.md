# The End of the Norm

**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0

## Abstract

What if the greatest crisis of law is not that people break the rules, but that the rules themselves have quietly stopped meaning anything? In The End of the Norm, jurist and philosopher Northon Salomão de Oliveira invites readers on a profound journey through one of the most unsettling questions of our time: What remains of law when its authority survives only as habit, ritual, or institutional inertia? Moving effortlessly between legal philosophy, political theory, psychology, artificial intelligence, memory, and ethics, this thought-provoking work challenges some of the deepest assumptions of modern jurisprudence. Through elegant prose, original concepts, and compelling reflections, the book explores the widening gap between the written law and the lived experience of those it claims to govern. Threaded throughout the narrative is the unforgettable figure of Miriam, a veteran courthouse clerk whose stubborn filing cabinet becomes a powerful metaphor for the fragile architecture upon which legal systems ultimately depend. Her quiet presence reminds us that institutions are sustained not only by constitutions, statutes, and courts, but by ordinary people who continue believing in them long after certainty has begun to fade. Far from offering easy answers, The End of the Norm proposes a bold rethinking of legal authority in an age shaped by artificial intelligence, digital evidence, climate change, algorithmic governance, and declining institutional trust. It is an invitation to reconsider not only what law is, but why humanity continues to seek order in a world that owes us none. Written for readers of legal theory, philosophy, political thought, and anyone fascinated by the future of institutions, this book offers an original and intellectually ambitious contribution to one of the defining debates of the twenty-first century.
