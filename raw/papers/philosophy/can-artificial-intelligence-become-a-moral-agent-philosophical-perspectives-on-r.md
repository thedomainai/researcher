# CAN ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE BECOME A MORAL AGENT? PHILOSOPHICAL PERSPECTIVES ON RESPONSIBILITY, ETHICS AND MACHINE AUTONOMY

**著者**: Dr. Ananya Iyer Assistant Professor, Department of Philosophy University of Hyderabad, Hyderabad, India
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 哲学

## Abstract

The rapid development of artificial intelligence has renewed philosophical debate about whether machines can become moral agents rather than merely instruments used by human beings. Artificial intelligence systems increasingly recommend medical interventions, evaluate financial risks, assist recruitment, generate educational content, operate autonomous machines, and influence decisions with significant consequences for individuals and society. These developments raise a fundamental question: if an artificial system can make autonomous decisions that have morally relevant consequences, can it also bear moral responsibility for those decisions? This article examines the philosophical conditions of moral agency and evaluates whether contemporary artificial intelligence satisfies them. The study employs qualitative conceptual analysis and draws on literature in moral philosophy, philosophy of mind, machine ethics, and AI governance. Particular attention is paid to autonomy, intentionality, consciousness, normative understanding, accountability, and responsibility. The analysis suggests that contemporary AI can demonstrate forms of operational or functional autonomy without possessing the richer characteristics normally associated with human moral agency. AI systems may produce ethically appropriate outcomes and follow normative constraints, yet this does not necessarily imply that they understand moral reasons, experience obligation, possess conscience, or can be held responsible in the human sense. The article argues that describing present-day AI as a full moral agent risks creating a responsibility gap in which developers, institutions, and users evade accountability by attributing decisions to algorithms. A more defensible approach is to understand AI as a morally significant technological actor whose behaviour can affect ethical outcomes while ultimate responsibility remains distributed among human and institutional agents. The future development of increasingly autonomous systems may require reconsideration of traditional categories of moral agency, but technological sophistication alone is insufficient to establish moral personhood.
