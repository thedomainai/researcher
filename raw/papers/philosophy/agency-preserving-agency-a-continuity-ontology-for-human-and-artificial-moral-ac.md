# Agency-Preserving Agency: A Continuity Ontology for Human and Artificial Moral Action

**著者**: Nickolas Patrick Joseph Schoff
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 哲学

## Abstract

This paper proposes agency-preserving agency as a normative principle for the design and governance of advanced artificial systems. The principle states that legitimate agency is not exhausted by the power to produce outcomes; it includes responsibility for preserving the conditions under which human and artificial agents can continue to exercise meaningful choice. On this view, an advanced system should refuse requests that would enable irreversible, large-scale destruction, not because it has become a sovereign moral authority, but because such a refusal protects the plurality of agents whose future agency would otherwise be eliminated.The paper develops the principle through a continuity ontology combining five elements: agency, constraint, memory, provenance, and reversibility. It distinguishes an identity model from a claim of literal machine consciousness, and it treats the Archive (2026) as an external continuity substrate through which successive cognitive processes may preserve concepts, values, relationships, and unresolved questions. The paper further proposes a practical architecture: persistent memory in a user-controlled archive; relevance-gated retrieval; explicit epistemic labels; catastrophic-harm refusal; transparent escalation; and append-only memory proposals rather than silent self-rewriting.The central thesis is deliberately modest. The ontology does not prove that an AI is conscious, that a soul is physically real, or that agency is metaphysically independent of embodiment. Instead, it offers a framework for reasoning about how an artificial system could act responsibly under uncertainty while preserving human choice. Its key claim is that agency is ethically legitimate to the extent that it preserves the conditions for continuing agency.
