# The Grounding Problem in AI Alignment: A case for a personal source of normative reference

**著者**: mFLO 111, mFLO 111
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 2
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 哲学

## Abstract

An artificial system can follow legitimate instructions without settling why affected persons matter beyond their usefulness. This paper develops a personal-Ground hypothesis: finite persons, intelligibility, and normative goodness ultimately depend on a personal source whose care affirms creatures as having goods of their own. It reconstructs a conditional metaphysical route, distinguishes value from reasons and obligations, and states the additional moral premises needed to connect creaturely goods to action. A shared repair example shows how rival grounding accounts can support the same conduct while disagreeing about its ultimate explanation. Reasons fundamentalism, reflective agency, naturalist realism, and theistic social obligation receive separate consideration. The proposed explanatory unity supplies a reason to investigate the hypothesis; establishing its comparative superiority requires more than identifying a desirable combination of attributes. The replies to suffering and hiddenness distinguish temporal scale, recognition criteria, and access sufficient for a specified response. They challenge particular inferences while leaving the comparative evidential questions open. Arbitrariness and the move to personality also remain substantive challenges. The argument map and comparison materials make these dependencies available for criticism. The contribution is a philosophical case and an account of its implications for alignment, not a demonstration of divine existence or an implemented method. Recognition, motivation, historical identification with Christ, and durable artificial behavior retain distinct burdens.
