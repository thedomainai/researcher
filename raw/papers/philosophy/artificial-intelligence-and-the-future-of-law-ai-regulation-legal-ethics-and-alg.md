## Abstract

What happens to justice when decisions are no longer shaped only by human judgment? Artificial intelligence is transforming courts, governments, law firms, financial institutions, and public administration at a speed that challenges centuries of legal tradition. As algorithms increasingly influence decisions that affect liberty, privacy, equality, and democratic accountability, the law faces one of the most profound questions of the twenty-first century: can justice remain truly human in an age of intelligent machines? In Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Law, legal scholar Northon Salomão de Oliveira offers a thoughtful and original exploration of the relationship between artificial intelligence, legal philosophy, ethics, and governance. Rather than treating AI as either a technological miracle or an existential threat, the book examines the deeper transformation taking place within legal institutions and the meaning of justice itself. Drawing from legal theory, philosophy, political thought, psychology, economics, and contemporary debates on AI regulation, the book explores topics including: • AI regulation and global governance • Algorithmic decision-making in courts and public administration • Legal ethics in the age of artificial intelligence • Algorithmic bias and discrimination • Transparency, accountability, and explainable AI • Human judgment versus machine prediction • Privacy, surveillance, and digital rights • The future of legal practice and the legal profession • Democracy, institutional legitimacy, and technological power Written in an elegant and accessible style, this book combines intellectual depth with clear analysis, making complex legal and philosophical questions understandable without sacrificing rigor. Ideal for lawyers, judges, academics, policymakers, researchers, law students, and readers interested in artificial intelligence, technology, ethics, and the future of legal systems, this work invites readers to reconsider one of civilization's oldest institutions at a moment of unprecedented technological change. The future of law will not be determined by algorithms alone. It will be determined by the choices we make about how technology should serve justice, freedom, and humanity.
