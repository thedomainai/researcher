# The Generative Architecture of Reality: Introducing the Theory

**著者**: David Michael Doy
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 哲学

## Abstract

This book introduces the Generative Architecture: a unified theory of reality that begins with a single claim—possibility is the fundamental substance of reality, not a metaphor or logical modality, but a real, conserved substrate existing in two modes: latent (unexpressed) and realised (structured). Everything else—energy, mass, geometry, time, mind—emerges from the organisation of possibility. Written for non-specialists with no background in physics, mathematics, or philosophy required, this gentle introduction walks through the entire architecture: the generative loop (imbalance → action → feedback → pattern → tendency → development → renewed imbalance), collapse and development as universal operators, emergent geometry (including the Einstein limit), Predictive Memory Compression, the thermodynamics of possibility (generative entropy, the generalised second law, Landauer's principle, black hole thermodynamics), the Resonance Engine (a new class of engine that extracts work from structured resonance), and implications for physics, artificial intelligence, cognitive science, and philosophy. The book dissolves foundational paradoxes—the fragmentation of modern thought, the incompatibility between quantum theory and general relativity, the mind-body problem, and free will—by replacing incompatible primitives with a single generative substrate. The result is a unified framework: one ontology, one loop, one law, one mind, one cosmology.
