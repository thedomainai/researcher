# Tracing uncertainty in mind perception: Examining the role of affirming versus denying mental capacities in robots.

**著者**: Yasmina Giebeler, Nueud Kassaye, Basil Wahn, Eva Wiese
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 哲学

## Abstract

Mind perception, the capacity to infer others’ intentions and affective states, is central to socialcognition. Its two dimensions, agency (act and plan) and experience (sense and feel), shapeperceived responsibility and empathy, structure moral behavior, and determine perceived socialrelevance. Robots are less readily perceived as “having a mind”, which can negatively impacthuman-robot interaction. Although it is well established that an agent’s appearance impacts mindperception, it remains unclear how body and face cues selectively promote perceptions of agencyand experience. Moreover, prior research strongly relies on explicit ratings, offering limitedinsight into decision-making processes and potential uncertainties associated with judging arobot’s mind. Mouse-tracking provides a promising approach capturing both explicit mindperception judgments and associated uncertainties. As uncertainty is linked to negative affectiveresponses and cognitive conflict, the present study uses mouse tracking to examine whetherexplicit agency and experience ratings, associated with robotic face and body features variations,trigger uncertainty. Explicit ratings showed distinct effects of appearance: body features wereassociated with variations in agency perception, whereas facial features explained variations inboth agency and experience. Mouse-tracking parameters did not differ as a function of physicalfeatures (body vs. face) or mind perception dimensions (agency vs. experience). Interestinglythough, overall greater uncertainty was observed when participants affirmed versus deniedmental capacities, which was particularly pronounced for agency assessments. These findingsdemonstrate that face and body cues reliably shape mind perception, while also being linked tointrinsic uncertainty associated with affirming mental capacities in artificial agents.
