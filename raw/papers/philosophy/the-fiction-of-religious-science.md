# The Fiction of Religious Science

**著者**: Northon Salomao de Oliveira
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0

## Abstract

The Fiction of Religious Science lies a question that has quietly shaped civilization for centuries: what happens when faith borrows the authority of science, and science begins to speak with the certainty of faith? In this bold philosophical essay, Northon Salomão de Oliveira dismantles one of the most persistent intellectual confusions of the modern world: the tendency to cloak metaphysical beliefs in scientific language and to transform empirical knowledge into a new form of unquestionable dogma. Rather than attacking either religion or science, he exposes the subtle transaction through which each occasionally appropriates the credibility of the other. Drawing on philosophy of science, legal theory, epistemology, psychology, behavioral economics, artificial intelligence, and ethics, the book introduces an original concept: the "custody of belief." Borrowing the legal notion of chain of custody, Oliveira argues that every claim, whether religious, scientific, political, or technological, must be judged not only by its conclusions but also by the integrity of the path that produced them. From creationism and scientific reductionism to predictive models, algorithmic decision-making, medical ethics, climate debates, and the philosophy of law, each chapter reveals how institutions gain authority, how certainty is manufactured, and why genuine intellectual honesty requires acknowledging the limits of every method of knowing. Rich with memorable metaphors, rigorous philosophical argument, and elegant prose, The Fiction of Religious Science refuses easy polarizations. It does not ask readers to abandon either faith or science. Instead, it invites them to distinguish evidence from interpretation, observation from meaning, and certainty from wisdom. Provocative, interdisciplinary, and deeply human, this is a book for philosophers, scientists, jurists, theologians, scholars, and every reader who has ever wondered where knowledge ends and belief begins. Some instruments reveal the universe. Others reveal ourselves. The greatest mistake is believing they always do the same thing.
