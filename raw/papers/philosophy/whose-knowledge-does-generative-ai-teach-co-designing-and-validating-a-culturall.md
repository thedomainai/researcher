# WHOSE KNOWLEDGE DOES GENERATIVE AI TEACH? CO-DESIGNING AND VALIDATING A CULTURALLY RESPONSIVE FRAMEWORK FOR INDIGENOUS KNOWLEDGE IN (KALASHA) EDUCATION, PAKISTAN

**著者**: Nadia Iftikhar, Muhammad Umar Mehmood, Um E Rubab
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 哲学

## Abstract

The use of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) in the creation of educational content is also emerging as a field with a growing impact, but it can pose challenges in terms of cultural accuracy, linguistic marginalisation, epistemological authority and Indigenous data sovereignty as it relates to Indigenous knowledge. Whose Knowledge Does Generative AI Teach? Co-Designing and Validating a Culturally Responsive Framework for Indigenous Knowledge in Kalasha Education, Pakistan, explored the nature of how existing GenAI systems portray Kalasha indigenous knowledge and how community members who hold knowledge and teachers can participate in culturally responsible education AI practices. This study took an interpretivist and pragmatic perspective, and used a four-phase participatory co-design, qualitative-dominant approach in Bumburet, Rumbur and Birir valleys, Lower Chitral, Pakistan. The data collection comprised an audit of 108 responses produced by three different GenAI systems, 28 semi-structured interviews conducted with 14 community knowledge holders and 14 teachers, two participatory co-design workshops, and two rounds of framework validation which included 16 reviewers. The results showed that there was a significant lack of cultural and linguistic competence in the representations produced by AI, as 62.0 percent of the outputs were characterized as having moderate to severe factual or cultural inaccuracies and 70.4 percent had unwarranted confidence. The problems were especially evident for the Romanised Kalasha prompts, where there were linguistic inaccuracies. Thematic analysis revealed five main concerns: epistemic distortion, linguistic erosion, pedagogical mediation, pedagogical boundaries of sacred knowledge and community authority. Thereafter, participants co-designed a Green–Amber–Red knowledge classification system and a 5-pillar Culturally Responsive GenAI Governance Framework in response. The final composite score for validation of the framework was 4.58 out of 5 and 96.2% of the reviewers agreed. The report concludes that for culturally responsive educational AI, community involvement, language responsiveness, and teacher mediation and Indigenous data sovereignty are necessary, not sufficient. It provides a validated framework to support the ethical representation and educational use of Kalasha knowledge without creating and launching a new AI application.
