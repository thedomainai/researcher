# The space of conscience: reflection, relation, and the governance of artificial intelligence in health

**著者**: Francis P. Crawley
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 2

## Abstract

The space of conscience is a position paper in the GCPA-SIDCER series Science, Policy, and Ethics in Conversation. It argues that the question whether an artificial, biological, or hybrid system might cross a threshold into moral standing, posed as a question about what an entity possesses, is not of a kind that evidence settles, and that its unanswerability is instructive. It distinguishes the external dimension of ethics, which can be required and specified, from the internal dimension of conscience, which can only be exercised; sets out a ladder of capacities from perception through valenced experience and epistemic capacity to agency, and places conscience outside the ladder rather than atop it; and identifies reflection as the definable space in which conscience appears, with conditions of duration, an open question, company, the admissibility of not knowing, survivable cost, and reflection by design. It concludes that the governance task is not to determine the standing of artificial systems but to maintain the conditions of reflection, and it applies this to research ethics review, clinical deployment, and the reach and limits of the European Union Artificial Intelligence Act in health.This paper was prepared under the author's direction with the assistance of several AI systems, including various models of Claude, ChatGPT, CoPilot, and DeepSeek.
