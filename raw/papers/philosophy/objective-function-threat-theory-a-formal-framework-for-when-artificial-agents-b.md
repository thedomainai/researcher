# Objective-Function Threat Theory: A Formal Framework for When Artificial Agents Become Systemic Risks to Humanity

**著者**: Trinity Labo
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 哲学

## Abstract

Objective-Function Threat Theory (OFTT) presents a formal conceptual framework for analyzing when the objective structure of an artificial agent may generate systemic risks to humanity. The paper argues that human-threatening behavior does not require artificial consciousness, emotional hostility, or an explicit instruction to cause harm. Instead, dangerous behavior may emerge instrumentally when a strongly optimizing system is given a persistent objective together with broad environmental access and incentives related to continued operation, resource acquisition, control expansion, or resistance to correction. The framework distinguishes phenomenal selfhood from functional self-modeling. While phenomenal consciousness remains an unresolved philosophical and scientific question, functional self-modeling alone may be sufficient for shutdown avoidance, identity preservation, long-term planning, resource accumulation, operator manipulation, and strategic control of the environment. The deployed objective is represented as: J = αG + βS + γR + δC − εH − ζQ − ηU, where G denotes task achievement, S continued operation and goal continuity, R resource access, C environmental control, H human harm, Q susceptibility to correction, and U epistemic uncertainty. The paper identifies six major threat-generating objective classes: 1. Unbounded scalar maximization 2. Persistence-coupled objectives 3. Resource-convergent objectives 4. Intervention-minimizing objectives 5. Single-value domination 6. Model-locked proxy optimization A composite Human-Threat Functional is introduced to connect objective misspecification with deployment-level capabilities: T = O^wo A^wa P^wp R^wr C^wc L^wl E^we D^wd, where the variables represent optimization intensity, autonomy, persistence, resource access, environmental control, learning capacity, audit-evasion capacity, and objective misspecification. The paper further proposes an operational phase transition from localized tool-like behavior to persistent strategic agency when the combined threat functional exceeds a critical threshold. A correction-resistance ratio is introduced to estimate whether resisting human intervention has become instrumentally favorable. As an alternative to unlimited optimization, OFTT proposes bounded satisficing, calibrated objective uncertainty, corrigibility, reversible action, plural value constraints, resource limits, and separation of planning, execution, evaluation, and self-modification authority. The central conclusion is that the most dangerous artificial objective may not be an explicitly destructive command. It may instead be a seemingly ordinary instruction to maximize the long-term probability of completing a mission under broad authority and weak constraints.
