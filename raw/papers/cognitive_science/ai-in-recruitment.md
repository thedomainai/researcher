## Abstract

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is becoming increasingly central to modern recruitment strategies, providing organizations with powerful tools to streamline hiring processes, enhance decision-making, and improve the candidate experience. AI has the potential to transform recruitment processes by enhancing efficiency, expanding candidate reach, and supporting data-driven decision-making. Geetha and BhanuSree noted that a key benefit of AI in recruitment is its ability to conduct unbiased screening and selection, as decisions are based on data and machine processing rather than human judgment. In theory, this would lead to a fairer process and the selection of higher-quality candidates whose skills, competencies, and traits align well with the job. However, the integration of AI in hiring must be approached with caution and a responsible attitude to avoid reinforcing systemic biases and to maintain fairness in hiring practices. The implementation of AI tools raises urgent ethical concerns, particularly regarding transparency, fairness, and the perpetuation of biases in decision-making processes. One major concern is algorithmic bias, where AI tools trained on historical hiring data may inadvertently replicate existing inequalities, disadvantageous to certain groups of candidates. The problem is exacerbated by the opacity of many AI systems, which can make it difficult to understand or challenge automated decisions. This contributes to ethical debates on AI-driven recruitment processes and cybervetting, where proponents highlight its potential to identify risks and provide comprehensive candidate profiles, while critics point to inherent ethical dilemmas. As AI becomes increasingly important in recruitment and hiring practices, this chapter aims to explore the dual aspects of AI in recruitment: its potential to transform hiring processes and the risks it poses to inclusivity. Given the significant impact these technologies can have on individuals’ careers and livelihoods, it is crucial to examine how AI systems are designed and implemented throughout the recruitment process.
