# Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning in Anesthesia

**著者**: Pooyan Ahanrobai, Zahra Jafarzadeh Jahromi, Arian Yousefi
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 認知科学

## Abstract

This chapter reviews the application of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) in intraoperative anesthesia, focusing on three main domains: prediction of adverse events, clinical decision support, and closed-loop automation. The most extensively studied application is short-horizon prediction of intraoperative hypotension (IOH), with commercial tools such as the Hypotension Prediction Index (HPI) demonstrating the ability to anticipate hypotensive events minutes before onset. Prospective trials and meta-analyses indicate that HPI-guided protocols can reduce hypotension exposure, although consistent improvements in major postoperative outcomes remain unproven. Hypoxemia prediction with real-time, clinician-facing explanations has shown promise in enhancing situational awareness and enabling earlier intervention. Closed-loop anesthesia delivery systems—particularly for depth-of-hypnosis control—can improve time-in-target compared with manual titration, but safety engineering, multi-loop interactions, and regulatory constraints limit widespread adoption. Methodological critiques emphasize that evaluation practices, including selection bias and unrealistic negative sampling, can inflate apparent model performance. The chapter also addresses ethical and governance challenges, including algorithmic bias, accountability, alarm fatigue, and the need for model lifecycle management. The authors conclude that future progress depends on clinically grounded evaluation, pragmatic outcome-focused trials, and integration of interpretability and safety engineering into deployed systems, rather than pursuing incremental gains in retrospective discrimination alone.
