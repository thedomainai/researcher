# COGNITIVE WORKFLOW DESIGN

**著者**: Roman Shaban
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 認知科学

## Abstract

The integration of generative artificial intelligence (AI) into intellectual work is transforming not only how information is processed but also how cognitive activities can be organized, sequenced, distributed, and evaluated. Conventional approaches to AI assistance frequently emphasize prompt formulation, individual task automation, or the quality of generated outputs. However, complex intellectual activities involve multiple interdependent cognitive operations whose sequence, allocation, and coordination may substantially influence the quality of the resulting work. This paper introduces Cognitive Workflow Design (CWD) as a conceptual and methodological approach to constructing structured sequences of cognitive operations involving human participants and AI systems. The proposed framework conceptualizes a cognitive workflow as an adaptive arrangement of goal formulation, task decomposition, information acquisition, analytical processing, knowledge generation, critical evaluation, synthesis, and reflective revision. Building upon distributed cognition, cognitive offloading, metacognitive regulation, cognitive load theory, and human–AI collaboration research, the paper develops the Human–AI Cognitive Workflow Design Framework (HA-CWDF). The framework specifies nine interconnected stages: goal clarification, cognitive task decomposition, capability-based allocation, workflow sequencing, coordinated execution, quality verification, knowledge integration, metacognitive reflection, and workflow adaptation. Particular attention is devoted to distinguishing the automation of individual tasks from the deliberate design of complete cognitive processes. The paper proposes a workflow representation model, principles for constructing effective cognitive sequences, evaluation criteria, and illustrative applications in research, learning, and professional knowledge work. The framework is presented as a conceptual proposal requiring empirical validation rather than an established method of cognitive optimization. The central argument is that the benefits of generative AI depend not only on the capabilities of the technology but also on how cognitive activities are organized around it. The deliberate design of human–AI cognitive workflows may provide a foundation for more systematic, transparent, adaptive, and intellectually responsible knowledge work
