# Outcome-aware Evaluation of Recommendation Rankings in Art Therapy Using Multimodal Physiological Utility

**著者**: Saravanakumar Duraisamy, Bereket Abera Yilma, Parvin Emami, Luis A. Leiva
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 認知科学

## Abstract

Recommender systems are often evaluated using observable behavioral signals as surrogate relevance measures of user experience.However, in well-being and mental health support applications, user experience is inherently non-observable.To address this limitation, we introduce a multimodal physiology-driven framework for evaluating recommendation rankings in art therapy using functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) and eye-gaze signals.We define a physiological utility scalar that captures item-level internal response by combining prefrontal hemodynamic responses with attention and engagement dynamics derived from gaze behavior, enabling direct comparison between system-generated rankings and rankings induced by internal user responses.We show that fNIRS-derived utility exhibits moderate alignment with system rankings (Spearman's 𝜌 ≈ 0.40, pairwise accuracy ≈ 0.69), with inter-hemispheric functional connectivity showing the strongest alignment among the evaluated neural descriptors.Gaze-derived utility yields stronger observed alignment (𝜌 ≈ 0.55, pairwise accuracy ≈ 0.75), particularly for attention continuity and engagementrelated representations.Multimodal integration yielded the highest observed agreement between recommendation rankings and physiology-derived utility (𝜌 ≈ 0.61, pairwise accuracy ≈ 0.77).Our findings provide exploratory evidence that multimodal physiological signals may offer complementary information for evaluating recommendation rankings in outcome-sensitive art therapy.
