# Hybrid Intelligence Leadership: Managing Teams Through Human–AI Complementarity

**著者**: Roman Shaban
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 認知科学

## Abstract

The integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into organizational processes is transforming not only how work is performed but also how teams are structured, coordinated, and led. As generative AI systems and increasingly autonomous agents assume analytical, creative, informational, and operational functions, leadership must evolve beyond the conventional management of exclusively human contributors. However, the introduction of AI does not automatically improve collective performance. Effective hybrid intelligence requires deliberate coordination of complementary capabilities, transparent allocation of decision rights, appropriate reliance, continuous evaluation, and the preservation of human professional autonomy. This conceptual paper examines Hybrid Intelligence Leadership (HIL) as an emerging approach to managing teams in which human professionals and AI systems perform interdependent functions toward shared organizational objectives. Drawing on theories of human–AI complementarity, distributed cognition, organizational learning, psychological safety, and sociotechnical systems, the paper introduces the Hybrid Intelligence Leadership Architecture (HILA). The proposed architecture comprises six interdependent leadership functions: strategic alignment, capability-based task allocation, collaborative coordination, epistemic and operational assurance, human capability development, and adaptive governance. The model emphasizes that leadership effectiveness depends not simply on technological sophistication but on how human and artificial contributions are organized, evaluated, and continuously reconfigured. Practical applications, implementation principles, organizational risks, and testable research propositions are presented. Particular attention is given to avoiding automation bias, preventing the erosion of human expertise, maintaining accountability, and ensuring that AI adoption strengthens rather than marginalizes human contributors. The paper argues that the central responsibility of hybrid intelligence leadership is to create organizational conditions in which human judgment and artificial capabilities generate sustainable collective value without compromising autonomy, responsibility, or professional development.
