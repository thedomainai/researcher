# Toward a Socratic Scaffolding design paradigm for diagnostic AI: A conceptual middleware framework

**著者**: Savvas A. Chatzichristofis, Eleftheria Hatzimichael, Elena Kakoulli, Angelos Amanatiadis
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 認知科学

## Abstract

This paper introduces a conceptual middleware framework that reconfigures the epistemic dynamics of clinician-AI interaction in diagnostic contexts. Departing from automation-first paradigms, the proposed system delays the disclosure of AI-generated outputs until the clinician has articulated an initial diagnostic hypothesis. This inversion reinstates the human agent as the initiating epistemic authority and preserves interpretive sovereignty in the clinical reasoning process. Grounded in dialogic pedagogy and situated cognition, the framework scaffolds reflective inquiry through Socratic prompts, contextual cues, and guided deliberation. Drawing on Vygotsky’s Zone of Proximal Development, Schön’s model of the reflective practitioner, and Suchman’s theory of situated action, it conceptualizes diagnosis as an interpretive and socially embedded activity, not reducible to computational prediction. Rather than reporting clinical outcome evidence or diagnostic performance results, the paper offers a philosophical counter-design that challenges the normative legitimacy of prediction-led interaction models. It engages critically with Dreyfus’s critique of disembodied expertise and Fricker’s notion of epistemic injustice, arguing that prevailing system architectures risk displacing clinicians’ epistemic agency and undermining responsibility attribution. To probe workflow fit and surface practical constraints, we also conducted a small formative Wizard-of-Oz study using hematology morphology vignettes, in which clinicians experienced both an AI-first baseline and the proposed delayed-disclosure flow. We use the resulting qualitative feedback to derive mode-sensitive design requirements (for example, prompt budgets, bypass and escalation logic, and logging boundaries), rather than to make diagnostic performance claims. While aligned with ethical frameworks from UNESCO, WHO, and the OECD, the contribution lies in embedding these normative commitments into the temporal and interactional structure of AI use. The proposal thus reframes diagnostic AI not as an autonomous oracle, but as a dialogic partner in co-reasoning. By integrating epistemological theory, design philosophy, and ethical reflection, the paper contributes to current debates in the philosophy of AI and social epistemology, advancing a model of human-machine interaction grounded in interpretive integrity and professional judgment.
