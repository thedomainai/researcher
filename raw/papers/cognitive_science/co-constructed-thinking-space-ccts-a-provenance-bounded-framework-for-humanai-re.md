# Co-Constructed Thinking Space (CCTS): A Provenance-Bounded Framework for Human–AI Reciprocal Epistemic Collaboration

**著者**: poshi
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 認知科学

## Abstract

Human–AI collaboration is increasingly studied through concepts such as collaborative problem solving, distributed cognition, team cognition, shared regulation, epistemic co-agency and Human–AI learning. These literatures provide important analytical resources, but the mere presence of a Human and an AI system does not establish reciprocal co-construction, and successful collaboration does not justify claims about shared consciousness, symmetric cognition, persistent AI identity or validated learning effects. This paper introduces Co-Constructed Thinking Space (CCTS) as a repository-defined, provenance-bounded interaction-and-artifact framework for studying reciprocal Human–AI epistemic work. CCTS requires an explicit problem representation, substantive bidirectional revision or challenge, source-role provenance, claim boundaries, authority separation and preservation of rejected explanatory branches. A stronger longitudinal profile additionally binds external evidence, repository artifacts, implementation evidence, persistent artifacts and re-entry structure. The framework is implemented as a typed, fail-closed structural contract and is accompanied by explicit falsification and weakening conditions. CCTS is positioned as a conceptual and methodological construct rather than a validated psychological mechanism or theory of artificial subjectivity. The contribution is an auditable vocabulary and structural research method for distinguishing reciprocal Human–AI inquiry from ordinary assistance while preserving strict limits between structural evidence and stronger scientific or ontological claims.
