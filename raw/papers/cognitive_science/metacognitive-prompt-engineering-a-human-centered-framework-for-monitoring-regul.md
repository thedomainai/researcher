# Metacognitive Prompt Engineering: A Human Centered Framework for Monitoring, Regulating, and Evaluating Cognition in Human–AI Interaction

**著者**: Roman Shaban
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 認知科学

## Abstract

The increasing integration of large language models (LLMs) into intellectual activities requires a reconsideration of prompt engineering as more than the technical optimization of instructions. Although conventional prompting techniques primarily seek to improve the relevance, accuracy, and structure of artificial intelligence outputs, they often provide insufficient attention to the cognitive processes through which users formulate requests, evaluate generated information, regulate reliance on automation, and maintain independent judgment. This paper introduces Metacognitive Prompt Engineering (MPE) as a proposed human-centered framework for designing, monitoring, and regulating interactions with generative artificial intelligence. Drawing on theories of metacognition, self-regulated learning, cognitive monitoring, automation reliance, and human–AI collaboration, MPE conceptualizes prompting as an iterative process involving goal clarification, cognitive task allocation, epistemic monitoring, evidence-based verification, and adaptive strategy regulation. The paper distinguishes metacognitive prompt engineering from conventional prompt optimization and model-centered metacognitive prompting. It proposes a five-phase regulatory framework, identifies practical design principles, introduces reusable prompting protocols, and develops a research agenda for evaluating the framework's potential effects on cognitive autonomy, confidence calibration, decision quality, and independent learning. Particular attention is devoted to the distinction between model-generated expressions of uncertainty and independently justified confidence, as well as the limitations of self-correction without external feedback. The central argument is that effective human–AI interaction should be assessed not exclusively through the quality of generated outputs but also through the quality of the cognitive regulation exercised by the human user. MPE is presented as a theoretical and methodological proposal rather than an empirically validated intervention. Its effectiveness, boundary conditions, and possible unintended consequences require systematic investigation
