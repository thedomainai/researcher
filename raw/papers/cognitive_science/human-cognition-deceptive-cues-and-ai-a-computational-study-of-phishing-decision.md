# Human Cognition, Deceptive Cues, and AI: A Computational Study of Phishing Decision Signals

**著者**: Sujay Oggu
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 認知科学

## Abstract

This independent research preprint examines phishing detection at the intersection of cognitive science, artificial intelligence, and cybersecurity. The study analyzes expert-coded deceptive cues in a public defensive phishing corpus and evaluates how patterns such as urgency, impersonation, social engineering, and technical indicators are distributed across phishing emails. The computational analysis includes cue prevalence, pairwise co-occurrence testing, hierarchical clustering, robustness checks, and a machine-learning diagnostic designed to measure dataset-source artifacts rather than phishing classification performance. Results show that urgency was the most common cue in the heterogeneous phishing subset, while the overall cue structure did not form strongly separated phishing categories. The study also found that a simple model could distinguish between heterogeneous and templated phishing examples with relatively high accuracy, highlighting the risk of dataset bias and shortcut learning in phishing-detection research. The paper connects these computational findings to prior research on human attention, cue utilization, persuasion, and phishing susceptibility while clearly separating observed machine behavior from claims about human cognition. The broader goal is to show why future phishing research should compare the cues used by humans and machine-learning systems while carefully controlling for dataset provenance and source leakage. This preprint includes original computational analysis performed on publicly available defensive cybersecurity data. Reproducibility materials and analysis outputs were maintained separately during the research process.
