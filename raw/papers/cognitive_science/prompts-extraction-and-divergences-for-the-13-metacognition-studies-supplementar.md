# Prompts, extraction, and divergences for the 13 metacognition studies: supplementary material for "Delegating Extraction, Not Synthesis"

**著者**: Heloise Acco Tives, Edna Dias Canedo, Patrícia A. Jaques
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 認知科学

## Abstract

This deposit documents one analytical step of a systematic review, the AI-assisted extraction of 13 studies that treat metacognition as a moderator of generative AI effectiveness in programming learning, and the human comparative synthesis that followed it. The 13 studies are a subset of a mapping of 101 primary studies, delimited by the explicit presence of the moderator in the extracted data. Extraction was carried out with Google NotebookLM under a three-pillar protocol (preparation and calibration, AI-assisted processing, human validation), one document at a time, with the categories communicated in the prompt text and full human verification against the source. The comparative synthesis was conducted by the researchers without the tool, because extraction recall, the measure that makes delegation verifiable at the other steps, has no referent at that step. The workbook records, per study, the prompts applied and the raw outputs, the occurrence of each learner profile with participant counts and anchor excerpts from the primary studies, the predominant profile after human verification, and a field-by-field comparison between what the tool recorded and what the synthesis established. Twenty-seven attributions were examined and fourteen diverge, classified as under-attribution, granularity loss, over-attribution, or unsupported corroboration. Aggregate counts are computed by formula and recalculate with any change to the underlying sheets. Prompt texts and raw tool outputs are kept verbatim in Portuguese, the language in which they were submitted. Anchor excerpts are quoted from the primary studies, which are in English. This is a supplementary deposit. The materials of the underlying mapping, including the search strategy, selection decisions, methodological rigor assessment, extraction from the 101 studies, and the configurational coding, are deposited separately. The relationships documented here are co-occurrences between study characteristics and reported outcomes. No causal claim is made or supported by these files.
