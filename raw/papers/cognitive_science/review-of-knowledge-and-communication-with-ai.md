# Review of: "Knowledge and Communication with AI"

**著者**: Diego R. Faria
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 認知科学

## Abstract

This is an ambitious, intellectually wide-ranging, and at several points genuinely provocative commentary.The manuscript attempts something more substantial than another discussion of whether contemporary large language models are "intelligent".Instead, it constructs an idealised future AI and asks what ontological status such an entity might possess if it became an integrated, knowledgegenerating, and communicative partner of human beings.The central proposal is that such an AI could appropriately be considered a Subject -or cognitive singularity -within a human-AI knowledgecommunication continuum.I nd this direction potentially valuable.In particular, the manuscript productively connects philosophy of language, ontology, epistemology, moral philosophy, social power, AI agency, and human-AI relations.Its attempt to move beyond the simple dichotomy of "AI as tool" versus "AI replacing humanity" towards AI as an epistemic partner is worth developing.The paper also contains an important distinction that should be preserved: the author is not claiming that present AI is conscious.Rather, the proposed subjecthood is primarily epistemic and communicative, grounded in knowledge generation rather than phenomenal consciousness.This is one of the manuscript's stronger conceptual moves.However, in its present form, I do not think the argument is suf ciently rigorous to sustain several of its strongest conclusions.The main problem is not the speculative nature of the article.Speculative philosophy of AI is entirely legitimate.The problem is that the manuscript frequently moves from an explicitly idealised AI to claims about what intelligence, rationality, morality, cognition, communication, and future AI necessarily entail.At several crucial points, normative propositions, empirical propositions, metaphysical propositions, and predictions about future AI are insuf ciently distinguished.
