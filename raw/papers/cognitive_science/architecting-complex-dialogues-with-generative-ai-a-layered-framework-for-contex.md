# Architecting Complex Dialogues with Generative AI: A Layered Framework for Context Management, Cognitive Coordination, and Human–AI Collaboration

**著者**: Roman Shaban
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 認知科学

## Abstract

The increasing integration of generative artificial intelligence into research, education, professional decision-making, and knowledge-intensive activities has transformed human–AI interaction from isolated question-answer exchanges into extended, multidimensional dialogues. Conventional prompting approaches, however, often treat interactions as sequences of independent instructions rather than as structured cognitive processes requiring continuity, contextual coherence, adaptive planning, and systematic evaluation. This conceptual paper examines the architecture of complex dialogues with generative AI and proposes a layered framework for organizing sustained, goal oriented human–AI collaboration. The proposed Complex Dialogue Architecture (CDA) conceptualizes dialogue as a dynamically regulated system consisting of eight interconnected layers: intentional, contextual, structural, operational, epistemic, metacognitive, mnemonic, and governance. Together, these layers support goal specification, context preservation, task decomposition, interaction orchestration, evidence assessment, reflective monitoring, memory management, and human oversight. The paper further introduces a dialogue-state representation, a recursive coordination protocol, and a multidimensional evaluation model for assessing the effectiveness and reliability of extended AI assisted interactions. Drawing on cognitive science, human–computer interaction, distributed cognition, and contemporary research on language agents, the framework distinguishes conversational fluency from cognitive effectiveness and emphasizes the preservation of human agency throughout increasingly complex AI-mediated workflows. Practical applications are discussed in scientific research, professional problem-solving, education, and collaborative knowledge production. The paper offers a theoretical foundation for designing dialogue systems that function not merely as conversational interfaces but as structured environments for distributed cognitive activity. Its propositions and evaluation criteria are presented as candidates for future empirical investigation rather than experimentally established findings
