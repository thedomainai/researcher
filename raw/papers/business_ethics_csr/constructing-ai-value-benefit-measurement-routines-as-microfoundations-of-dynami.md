# Constructing AI value: benefit measurement routines as microfoundations of dynamic capabilities

**著者**: Flávio Costa Romão
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経営倫理・CSR

## Abstract

Purpose This paper aims to explore how organisations determine and implement the value of artificial intelligence (AI). It seeks to understand how AI value is built through organisational practices, focusing on benefit measurement routines (BMRs) and their influence on decision-making and value creation. Design/methodology/approach This study uses a canonical action research approach conducted over three iterative cycles within a large European insurance company. Data were gathered through workshops, interviews, internal documents and field observations involving cross-functional stakeholders. The longitudinal design allows analysis of how benefit measurement practices develop and how they influence organisational understanding of AI value over time. Findings The findings indicate that AI value is not merely determined through measurement but is actively constructed via iterative, negotiated and socio-technical processes. BMRs influence perceptions of value by defining categories, aligning stakeholders and integrating measurement outputs into governance. Three main mechanisms are identified: shaping visibility by defining what qualifies as AI value, facilitating coordination through shared yet partial representations of value and aiding organisational adaptation by periodically revising measurement approaches and value interpretations. These mechanisms are linked to inherent tensions related to codification, stakeholder alignment and governance integration. Research limitations/implications This study is conducted within a single organisational context, which might restrict the extent to which findings can be applied broadly. Nevertheless, the results provide insights that are analytically generalisable regarding the role of measurement practices in AI value realisation. Future research could explore similar practices across different industries and varying levels of AI maturity. Practical implications This paper emphasises that organisations should view AI value measurement as an evolving organisational capability rather than just a technical task. Managers need to develop measurement practices that are iterative, involve multiple stakeholders and are closely connected to decision-making. Recognising the inherent tensions in measurement can help organisations avoid depending too much on simplified metrics and enhance the value gained from AI initiatives. Originality/value This study advances information systems research on information technology value realisation by demonstrating that benefit measurement practices are constitutive rather than merely representative of organisational value. It extends the microfoundations agenda by showing that dynamic capabilities are enacted on organisationally constructed representations of reality, and that BMRs are integral to the creation of those representations. It also reinterprets the benefits realisation gap as a knowledge-construction challenge rather than a measurement-accuracy problem.
