# From Principles to Capability: Building Capacity for Responsible AI Adoption

**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence (AI) governance is entering a new phase. The challenge is no longer simply to establish principles for responsible AI, but to build the human, organisational and institutional capability required to implement those principles in practice. Discussions at the United Nations Global Dialogue on AI Governance[1] in Geneva highlighted the importance of human-centred governance, economic resilience, information integrity, child safety and more inclusive access to AI capability. For policymakers, this creates a shift in priorities: alongside regulation and standards, greater attention is needed on capability-building. For businesses, particularly SMEs, responsible AI needs to become part of everyday leadership, workforce development and innovation practice rather than an additional compliance activity. Universities and business-support organisations can play an important role by translating emerging AI knowledge into practical guidance, skills and trusted support. This Insight proposes an AI Governance Capability Framework comprising five interconnected capabilities: technical, institutional, organisational, human and international. It argues that these capabilities need to develop together if AI is to generate broad-based productivity, innovation and sustainable economic value. The policy priority is therefore clear: move from principles to capability by embedding responsible AI into mainstream economic, skills, innovation and business support. [1] United Nations (2026).
