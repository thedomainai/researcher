# The ESG Banking Model

**著者**: Gulzar Singh
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経営倫理・CSR

## Abstract

Environmental, social, and governance (ESG) principles have moved from peripheral commitments to fundamental design constraints that now shape how retail banks operate, compete, and serve society. The transition from voluntary sustainability reporting to binding regulatory expectation has redefined ESG as an institutional architecture rather than a communications exercise. Retail banks today are required to integrate climate-adjusted credit assessment, emissions-conscious facility design, fair and ethical technology deployment, transparent governance, and socially aligned customer engagement into the core of their business models. This chapter examines how hybrid institutions must absorb ESG principles into product structures, risk frameworks, branch networks, digital systems, and leadership behaviour, reflecting the shift from policy aspiration to operational necessity. It explains why ESG is now inseparable from resilience, long-term value creation, community trust, and regulatory credibility. The chapter also highlights that ESG is no longer limited to environmental pledges or compliance metrics; it is a multidimensional capability that determines how institutions earn legitimacy in a world of rising climate volatility, tightening regulatory scrutiny, expanding digital interfaces, and widening economic inequality. Customers now expect banks to demonstrate responsible conduct in pricing, lending, data handling, and technology deployment. Regulators demand alignment with global sustainability frameworks and verifiable disclosures. Communities rely on banks as anchors of local resilience. By connecting climate intelligence, green infrastructure, ethical AI, financial literacy, and transparent reporting, this chapter positions ESG as a strategic operating system for modern retail banking. It argues that hybrid institutions that embed ESG deeply into organisational architecture will outperform peers in trust, resilience, competitiveness, and institutional longevity. ESG becomes tangible only when it is experienced by customers in their everyday interactions with the bank. Clear pricing, fair decisions, reliable service, and responsible data use are the points at which sustainability and governance translate into trust. Where these are inconsistent, ESG remains theoretical despite strong reporting.
