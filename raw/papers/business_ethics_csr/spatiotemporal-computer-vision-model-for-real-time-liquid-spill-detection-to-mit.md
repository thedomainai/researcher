# Spatiotemporal Computer Vision Model for Real-Time Liquid Spill Detection to Mitigate Financial and Environmental Risk in Oil and Gas Facilities

**著者**: Jae Yoon Chung, Mehdi Korjani
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経営倫理・CSR

## Abstract

Abstract Uncontrolled liquid spills in upstream and midstream facilities represent a significant threat to safety, operational profitability and environmental compliance. Traditional detection methods often suffer from delayed response times, inability to pinpoint the exact leak source, and high rates of false alarms that lead to operator desensitization. This paper quantifies the operational benefits and demonstrates the environmental sustainability value of a real-time Deep Learning and video analytics framework designed to provide immediate spill detection and precise localization across critical areas including wellheads, separators, and storage tank farms. The study evaluates a computer vision AI model capable of processing thermal and optical camera streams continuously. The model detects liquid presence and contours, tracking the region's size across consecutive frames. If the area is growing, the spill is classified as active and an alert is triggered. This temporal tracking approach eliminates false alarms caused by stationary residues or pre-existing stains. By capturing where the spill is growing and its exact onset time, the system constructs an automated forensic timeline, enabling operators to act with precision. The model was validated on a real-world dataset including diverse appearance conditions (White Hot, Black Cold, and optical RGB liquid presentations), seasonal background changes, and varying camera-to-source distances. Field data demonstrates a median time-to-alert of 34.7 seconds across confirmed events, with high-flow events alerted within seconds and incipient weeps detected within minutes, an alert precision of ~90% in a blind field evaluation against confirmed leak events and a labeled evaluation recall of 86.7%, directly addressing alarm fatigue and increasing operator confidence in alert credibility. The system sends video evidence to the control room so operators can verify the event instantly, eliminating the travel time previously required for manual confirmation. This rapid response directly reduces spilled volume, limits secondary damage, and lowers remediation costs. The system's ability to provide an exact "leak start time" transforms Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) reporting from estimated figures to data-driven metrics, supporting zero-spill targets. The novel contribution of this work is the application of spatiotemporal contour tracking to facility-level liquid spill detection, rather than classifying individual frames. The system tracks the spatial growth of candidate liquid regions across consecutive frames, enabling discrimination between active spills and stationary artifacts that are indistinguishable in any single frame. The approach generalizes across thermal infrared and optical RGB modalities without separate models or site-specific retraining.
