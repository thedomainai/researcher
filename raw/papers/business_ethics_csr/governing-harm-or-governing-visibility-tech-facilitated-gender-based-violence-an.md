# Governing Harm or Governing Visibility? Tech-Facilitated Gender-Based Violence and the Limits of AI Governance in the UK

**著者**: Georgia Vallis
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経営倫理・CSR

## Abstract

Tech-facilitated gender-based violence (TFGBV) is frequently framed as a series of novel harms emerging from rapid technological innovation. This paper challenges that assumption, arguing that TFGBV is better understood as a digitally intensified manifestation of longstanding patriarchal violence, embedded within the political economy of digital platforms and contemporary AI governance. Focusing on the UK, the paper offers a critical, theoretically informed analysis of regulatory responses to AI-enabled abuse, including non-consensual intimate deepfakes (NCID) and harmful pornographic content such as strangulation. The paper is primarily theoretical and socio-legal in approach, combining feminist theories of the continuum of violence with political-economic perspectives on platform governance. It critically evaluates the Online Safety Act 2023 (OSA) as a central case study, analysing its conceptual framing, implementation mechanisms, and subsequent amendments, including the 2026 prohibition of strangulation imagery in pornography. While the OSA represents a significant institutional shift, I argue that it remains constrained by a technocratic and gender-neutral model of governance that prioritises downstream content moderationover upstream structural intervention. The analysis demonstrates that cultural normalisation of misogynistic content, engagement-driven platform business models, and weak international enforcement mechanisms collectively sustain a governance framework that manages, rather than prevents, harm. In this context, AI operates simultaneously as both a tool of abuse and a limited regulatory solution, reinforcing existing power inconsistencies. By situating TFGBV within a broader continuum of gendered violence, this paper contributes to debates on online safety by exposing the structural limits of current policy approaches. I recognise that meaningful intervention requires a shift towards feminist-focused, upstream governance strategies that address the socio-technical conditions enabling digital violence, rather than treating such harms as isolated or exceptional.
