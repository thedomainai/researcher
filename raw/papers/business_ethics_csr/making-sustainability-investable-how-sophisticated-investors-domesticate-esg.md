# Making Sustainability Investable: How Sophisticated Investors Domesticate ESG

**著者**: Margot Heyman, Laetitia Pozniak
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経営倫理・CSR

## Abstract

Socially responsible investing (SRI) has expanded considerably over the last decade, yet the integration of environmental, social, and governance (ESG) considerations into individual investment decisions remains uneven. Existing research frequently attributes this gap to insufficient financial or sustainable-finance literacy. However, such explanations struggle to account for the behaviour of financially sophisticated investors who possess advanced financial knowledge, substantial investment experience, and familiarity with ESG-related issues. This study examines how these investors reconcile sustainability considerations with conventional portfolio objectives. Drawing on 22 semi-structured interviews with financially sophisticated investors in French-speaking Belgium and adopting a Gioia-inspired inductive approach, we investigate how investors navigate the sustainability-profitability tension in practice. Our findings reveal a coherent process that we conceptualize as ESG domestication, through which sustainability is rendered compatible with conventional portfolio rationality. This process operates through three interrelated mechanisms. First, rational ESG filtering subjects sustainability to financial requirements while preserving individualized ethical red lines, making ESG acceptable when compatible with profitability, diversification, risk management, and long-term financial security. Second, hybrid ESG assessment combines delegation to financial intermediaries with selective verification through personal research, controversies, and AI-assisted information gathering. Third, temporal displacement relocates unresolved sustainability-profitability tensions to an anticipated future characterized by greater regulatory harmonization, market maturity, and ESG standardization. By moving beyond knowledge-deficit explanations, this study contributes to sustainable finance research in three ways: it challenges the conventional opposition between finance and sustainable finance, highlights the subjective and evolving nature of sustainability, and develops ESG domestication a process through which sophisticated investors make sustainability cognitively manageable, morally acceptable, and financially legitimate within portfolio decision-making.
