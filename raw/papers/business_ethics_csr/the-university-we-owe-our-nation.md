# THE UNIVERSITY WE OWE OUR NATION

**著者**: Olusegun Stephen Albert
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0

## Abstract

Preface & Systemic Diagnosis The Teleological Gap: Aligning Institutional Output with National Destiny The foundational premise of this monograph — a systemic documentary consolidation of policy, institutional, and scholarly texts on the Nigerian public university, anchored in the regional empirical pilot reported in the author's IELPN conference paper, Realigning Nigerian Higher Education for AI, Employability, and Ethics: An Aristotelian–Ubuntu Framework for Educational Leadership (Obi, 2026) — is that the public university is failing to drive national development because it suffers from an acute state of teleological dislocation. The pilot's full design, instruments, reliability statistics, and inferential analysis are recorded in that paper; the monograph cites the record and does not reproduce it. The empirical corridor is deliberately regional; the documentary corridor is deliberately national. Every "systemic" claim in this book is therefore an inference from the convergence of documentary evidence with a regional empirical anchor — not a census of all 200+ Nigerian universities. That distinction is an instrument of intellectual honesty, and it is the methodological spine of the entire volume (McCowan, 2016). In classical philosophical traditions, an entity's telos defines its ultimate purpose, its optimised final state, and its primary reason for being. The legal and structural telos of the Nigerian Public University System was engineered to be the primary engine room for national self-reliance, intellectual sovereignty, sustainable wealth creation, and local problem-solving (Nnadozie et al., 2023). The university was never meant to be an end in itself: its degrees, its research, and its community engagements were all constituted as means to a national end, and its right to public investment rests on that constitutional bargain. Instead, a profound, systemic Gap has emerged. The university system has transformed into a bureaucratic mechanism preoccupied with inputs (admissions data, infrastructural expansions, regulatory compliance checklists) and proximal outputs (the mass production of credentials and rote graduates). Meanwhile, the distal outcomes — national development, industrial innovation, socio-ethical leadership, and economic resilience — remain unfulfilled. The system has grown efficient at producing the very things that were never its purpose, and negligent of the things that were. [Institutional Output: Certificates / Rote Data] ≠ (THE SYSTEMIC GAP) [National Development: Innovation / Ethical Wealth] This volume treats that Gap not merely as an administrative or financial shortfall, but as a profound philosophical failure occurring across three critical dimensions: The Epistemic Mismatch. Our classrooms continue to rely on rigid, colonial-era instructional paradigms that teach students what to think rather than how to network, innovate, and piece together knowledge within a highly connected, indigenous digital ecosystem. The epistemology of the classroom has not been reconceptualised; it has merely been inherited. The Administrative Deficit. University leadership lacks Administrative Phronesis (practical wisdom). Trapped in reactive crisis management — handling industrial disputes, funding shortfalls, and political disruptions — administrators lack the Temporal Intelligence required to proactively align institutional policy with shifting cultural, economic, and technological seasons. Contemporary governance research confirms that universities strategise time — deploying multiple temporal horizons in their self-governance — yet largely in the service of administration and planning rather than of educational purpose (Tuunainen et al., 2023; Vettori & Warm, 2025); the kairos-aware leadership this monograph prescribes is the corrective (elaborated in Chapter 6) (Andersen & Gatti, 2015; NWARU, 2026). The Technological-Ethical Void. As artificial intelligence fundamentally alters the global workforce, the NUS risks producing graduates who are technically obsolete and ethically adrift, lacking the communal anchoring of Ubuntu necessary to rebuild a fractured national social fabric (Oviawe, 2016). Ubuntu has indeed been advanced as a normative resource for correcting the exclusionary trajectories of global AI ethics — argued to be of immense value for inclusive implementation of universal AI ethics principles (Gwagwa et al., 2022), and for grounding AI governance in community, mutual care, and collective well-being rather than individual utility (Akpah, 2026). The promise, however, is not self-executing: recent analysis finds neither Ubuntu's normative structure nor Africa's emergent AI governance initiatives yet offers a coherent, practicable "African AI ethics" (Yilma, 2025), and UNESCO's own guidelines process has been shown to lean on Western "universal" principles with African ethics playing only a small role (Norren, 2022). That gap is precisely the void this monograph's Chapter 9 framework is built to close. The Political Economy of the Teleological Gap. A teleological diagnosis can silently abstract from material constraint, and this monograph refuses that abstraction. The gap between proximal outputs and distal outcomes is not only a failure of ends; it is a failure of means, reproduced under chronic underfunding and the recurrent suspension of the academic calendar. Industrial action by the Academic Staff Union of Universities has been shown to disrupt academic calendars and programmes, erode the culture and tradition of the university system, and damage town–gown relationships, slowing community exchange and development programmes (Oyebode & Obaka, 2022). The costs are not confined to campuses: evidence from the University of Lagos community indicates that over 90% of surveyed local businesses experienced staff retrenchment during strike periods, with 323 jobs lost and total revenue falling from ₦58,570,000.00 before a strike to ₦34,440,000.00 during it (Ogundipe et al., 2025). The consequence for this book's architecture is decisive: every mechanism proposed herein — readiness-based phasing, assessment redesign, AI governance, metadata discipline — is designed to operate under sustained resource constraint and calendar fracturing, not under ideal conditions. A reform that requires a stable semester is not a reform for the Nigerian system; it is a wish. The implementation-under-constraint principle is therefore elevated to a first-order design requirement of the Consolidated Policy Matrix. Beyond Critique: The Purpose of This Monograph. This book moves decisively beyond mere critique. By consolidating seven foundational research papers into a unified, actionable policy design, this monograph serves as an Evidence Framework for 21st Century Skills Integration. Each chapter systematically dismantles a specific component of the institutional gap, offering concrete administrative interventions. The ultimate goal is to provide the National Universities Commission, the Federal Ministry of Education, University Governing Councils, and Vice-Chancellors with the conceptual and practical tools needed to close the Gap permanently, transforming the Nigerian public university from a factory of mere credentials into the supreme crucible for national development. Scope, Evidence, and the Validation Agenda. The monograph's claims are of three kinds, and each carries its own epistemic warrant: (i) philosophical claims (teleological dislocation, hylomorphic failure), warranted by argument; (ii) documentary claims (institutional and policy patterns), warranted by the consolidated corpus and its protocol (see Appendix A); and (iii) empirical claims (pilot findings), warranted by the Rivers State pilot as reported in the IELPN empirical paper, with limits explicitly stated. The validation agenda is published in advance: multi-state replication, corroboration against NUC documents and statistics, and stakeholder validation workshops with the National Universities Commission, the Federal Ministry of Education, senates, and governing councils before any benchmark is elevated from proposal to national standard. Strategic Horizon: How This Monograph Serves the Higher Education Ecosystem. To close the teleological gap between institutional output and national destiny, this monograph is architected to serve three distinct, critical tiers of the educational ecosystem simultaneously: The Regulatory Tier: Serving the National Universities Commission. For national regulators and policymakers, this volume serves as an operational validation framework and implementation compass for the new Core Curriculum and Minimum Academic Standards. Rather than treating curriculum reform as a mere administrative checklist, Part I (Chapters 4 & 5) provides the NUC with precise, evaluative thought metrics and connectivist-indigenous learning models. This enables the regulatory body to transition national compliance metrics away from passive, rote certification toward active, networked cognitive innovation — shifting what is measured from the transmission of content to the production of thinking. The Frontline Tier: Serving Educational Leadership Practitioners. For educational practitioners, deans, and faculty members (including bodies such as the IELPN and PEAN), this book functions as a pedagogical and mentorship manual. Through Part II (Chapters 7 & 8) and Chapter 10, practitioners are equipped with the logical tools of the Analytic Standpoint to defend classrooms against modern misinformation — the first pedagogical defense against an age of algorithmic falsehood. Furthermore, it delivers actionable counselling frameworks to re-engineer university career centers into proactive hubs for long-term human capital resilience, directly addressing the psychosocial realities of student underemployment that no certificate can cure.
