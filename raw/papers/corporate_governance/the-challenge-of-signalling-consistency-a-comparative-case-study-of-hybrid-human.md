# The challenge of signalling consistency: a comparative case study of hybrid human–AI coaching implementation

**著者**: Silja Christina Kotte, Gil Bozer
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: コーポレートガバナンス

## Abstract

Purpose AI is transforming organisational HRD practices, including workplace coaching. Hybrid human–AI coaching, in which employees are coached by both a human and an AI coach, introduces interpretive ambiguity about the nature of coaching and the roles human and AI agents play. Using the Consolidated Framework of Implementation Research (CFIR) and HR System Strength Theory (HR SST), we explored barriers and facilitators to implementing hybrid human–AI coaching in organisations and how stakeholders make sense of it. Design/methodology/approach Our qualitative comparative case study drew from 17 semi-structured interviews with multiple stakeholders (HR professionals, the AIC provider, human coaches and coachees) from two organisations that implemented hybrid coaching. Hybrid deductive-inductive thematic analysis identified key barriers and facilitators within the CFIR domains, condensed into the three determinants of HR SST. Findings Factors influencing implementation include strategic positioning, human–AI alignment, cultural fit and stakeholder engagement and co-design. Differences in the HR SST determinants distinctiveness (i.e. strategic vs. ad-hoc HRD practice) and stakeholder consensus (e.g. management engagement) shaped diverging implementation trajectories between the two cases. Consistency, especially regarding human–AI coaching alignment, was a critical shared challenge. Originality/value We show that implementing hybrid human–AI coaching involves a complex process of organisational sensemaking that must be managed carefully with the HR SST determinants in mind. Our study extends HR SST by demonstrating that consistency is a challenge not only across HR practices but also within dual-agent HRD practices such as hybrid coaching. It also shows how hybrid human–AI coaching appears to lead to task reallocation rather than alleviation in coaching.
