# The Chokepoint Is Not the Chain: Ownership, Board Control, Infrastructure Power, and the Authority to Stop a Frontier Model

**著者**: Peter Bell
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: コーポレートガバナンス

## Abstract

This working paper examines how a frontier-model release may be delayed, conditioned, redirected, or prevented through corporate control, cloud and compute contracts, semiconductor export controls, voluntary testing arrangements, and formal public law. These mechanisms can sometimes produce the same immediate outcome—a model does not ship—but they are not institutionally equivalent. The paper distinguishes practical stopping power from legally constituted authority. It compares each mechanism by its source of power, controlling actor, target, trigger, reach, instrument, immediate consequence, notice, reasons, supervision, cure, remedy, review, reversibility, durability, and opportunities for substitution or exit. It evaluates publicly documented ownership and board structures, public-benefit-corporation arrangements, standard cloud-provider suspension provisions, current United States export controls, and voluntary frontier-AI safety commitments. It does not infer company-specific release vetoes, confidential contractual dependencies, or demonstrated effectiveness from the existence of published instruments. The analysis argues that corporate and infrastructure chokepoints may substitute for or complement public regulation in identified circumstances, but they do not reproduce a general public-law authority chain. Public law may add defined coverage, compulsory reach, procedure, reasons, records, supervision, review, and remedy; it does not thereby guarantee technical competence or practical effectiveness. The paper offers a bounded institutional-design synthesis connecting corporate governance, infrastructure power, and frontier-model release authority.
