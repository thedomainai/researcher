# Transforming Incident Investigations Through Causal Reasoning and Human Factor Implementation

**著者**: A. Al Ghafri, D. S. Mahrezi
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: コーポレートガバナンス

## Abstract

Abstract This paper presents Petroleum Development Oman's (PDO) innovative transformation of its incident investigation process, shifting from a compliance driven, reactive model to a systems-thinking, learning-oriented framework. The scope includes redesigning investigation workflows to adopt causal reasoning, embedding reflective learnings, standardizing human error classification, and deploying AI-enabled tools to enhance accountability, reduce manhours, and increase the strategic value of investigations. The initiative aligns with PDO's internal HSE policies and SPE's emphasis on safety culture and operational excellence The redesign followed a structured, evidence-based approach: literature review, benchmarking against global best practices, and one to one stakeholder interviews across PDO's operations (further details in section 2.1). A twotiered investigation model (Standard vs. Detailed) was developed with clear classification criteria, RASCI (Responsible, Accountable, Supportive, Consulted, and Informed) accountability matrices, and escalation paths to for high impact events. The process was piloted across 54 operational units, refined through iterative feedback, and integrated with a corporate dashboard and AI tool for real-time tracking, classification, and action logging. Code 10 (uncontrolled release from subsurface equipment) integrity investigation framework was introduced for the first time, and embedding Human Factor specialist into the investigation workflow to deepen root cause analysis The redesigned process delivered measurable improvements: 63% reduction in investigation manhours, 30% detailed vs. 70% standard investigations to optimize resource allocation, and stronger line ownership through clear accountability structures. The AI tool enabled faster classification (reducing delays more than 63%) and improved compliance via the corporate dashboard. Repeat incidents dropped significantly, and the first-ever Code 10 integrity investigations were successfully implemented. Human error classification was standardized across PDO, enhancing consistency and learning. The process also drove a cultural shift investigations are now viewed as strategic learning opportunities rather than administrative tasks. Key outcomes include improved prevention of recurrence, higher quality corrective actions, and stronger integration of HSE into operational decision making. This paper introduces a scalable, systems-thinking framework for incident investigations in upstream oil and gas, combining behavioral science, process redesign, and AI, that moves beyond traditional root cause analysis. It offers a replicable model for other operators seeking to transform investigations from administrative tasks into strategic learning engines. The integration of Human factor specialists, Code 10 investigations, and AI-enabled classification represents a novel approach not widely documented. The paper contributes to the state of knowledge by demonstrating how cultural, procedural, and technological levers can be aligned to drive sustainable safety improvement in complex operational environments
