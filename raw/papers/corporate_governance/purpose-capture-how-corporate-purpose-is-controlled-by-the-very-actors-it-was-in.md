# Purpose Capture: How Corporate Purpose Is Controlled by the Very Actors It Was Intended to Constrain

**著者**: Hamzeh Adel Al Amosh
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: コーポレートガバナンス

## Abstract

Corporate purpose is commonly presented as a means of broadening organizational responsibility beyond short-term shareholder value. Yet purpose does not enter practice intact. It must be interpreted, translated into routines, converted into indicators, and assigned to organizational functions. This paper develops a process theory of purpose capture to explain how the actors charged with operationalizing a broad purpose can acquire authority to redefine it around their own priorities, measures, and constraints. Purpose capture is defined as the progressive concentration of semantic, operational, and evaluative authority through which a morally plural purpose is transformed into a narrower organizational program while retaining the legitimacy of the original aspiration. The process unfolds through moral articulation, interpretive competition, operational translation, metric ownership, stakeholder narrowing, and purpose inversion. Six generative mechanisms explain movement across these phases: moral underspecification, jurisdictional advantage, tractability selection, metric enclosure, representational filtration, and justificatory reversal. The theory distinguishes purpose capture from decoupling, purpose-washing, mission drift, goal displacement, and hybrid-organizing tensions. It also identifies four implementation configurations and develops propositions concerning purpose specificity, functional power, measurement concentration, stakeholder voice, board custodianship, and temporal pressure. The resulting framework recasts purpose implementation as a contested governance process over who may define organizational value, whose claims remain visible, and which forms of evidence determine whether purpose has been realized.
