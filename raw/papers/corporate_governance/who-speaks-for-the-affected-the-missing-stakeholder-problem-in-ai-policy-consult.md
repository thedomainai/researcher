# Who Speaks for the Affected? The Missing Stakeholder Problem in AI Policy Consultations

**著者**: Ali Sadhik Shaik
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: コーポレートガバナンス

## Abstract

AI policy consultations - public comment periods, expert panels, multi-stakeholder forums, and parliamentary hearings - systematically underrepresent the populations most directly affected by algorithmic deployment. This paper documents the structural exclusion of affected populations from AI governance processes across the United States, European Union, and India by identifying four mechanisms of exclusion: (1) technical jargon barriers that render participation inaccessible to non-expert publics; (2) comment period architectures that privilege organized industry and civil society actors over individual affected parties; (3) geographic and economic barriers to in-person consultation in national capitals; and (4) fundamental information asymmetries between AI deployers who understand system design and affected populations who experience consequences without comprehending causes. Drawing on democratic theory, procedural justice scholarship, and empirical analysis of consultation submissions across 14 regulatory proceedings, the paper introduces the Consultation Exclusion Framework (CEF) - a four-mechanism model explaining why affected populations are structurally absent from AI governance - and proposes the Participatory Remedy Architecture (PRA), a five-component structural reform package. The paper argues that without structural reform, AI governance risks becoming a professionally mediated conversation about populations rather than with them, undermining both democratic legitimacy and regulatory effectiveness.
