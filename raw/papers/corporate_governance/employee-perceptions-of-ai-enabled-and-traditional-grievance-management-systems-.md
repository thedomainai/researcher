# EMPLOYEE PERCEPTIONS OF AI-ENABLED AND TRADITIONAL GRIEVANCE MANAGEMENT SYSTEMS IN PAKISTAN’S CORPORATE SECTOR: A COMPARATIVE STUDY

**著者**: Syeda Sumblah Bukhari, Zaeem Yasin
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: コーポレートガバナンス

## Abstract

The use of artificial intelligence (AI) in organizational systems is growing, such as for handling employee grievances. While the systems with AI can be expected to increase consistency, reduce the time needed for clarity and increase the efficiency of the resolution, this doesn't always lead to better employee perception or organizational results. The study aims to contrast the employee perceptions of AI powered and conventional manual grievance management systems in corporate sector of Pakistan. The data presented in this study were gathered by survey from 500 employees, with 250 employees using AI-enabled systems and 250 employees using traditional/manual arrangements. The respondents included banking, telecom, energy/petroleum, textile/manufacturing and e-commerce organizations. Seven constructs were explored: procedural justice, algorithmic trust, ethical transparency, procedural inclusivity, resolution effectiveness, grievance fatalism and retention intentions. No statistically significant overall difference was found between the two system groups (Pillai's Trace = .011, F (7, 492) = 0.796, p = .591) using multivariate analysis of variance. There were also no significant univariate follow-up comparisons. No statistically significant indirect effects were found in the parallel mediation analysis, through procedural justice, algorithmic trust or algorithmic resolution effectiveness. Exploratory industry comparisons at the industry level showed some differences in the e-commerce and telecommunications industry. The results indicate that simply implementing an AI-powered grievance tool is not enough to change the mindset of employees and the performance of organizations. Implementation also needs to be thought about in terms of organizational processes, access to staff, accountability and the institutional environment in which grievances are handled.
