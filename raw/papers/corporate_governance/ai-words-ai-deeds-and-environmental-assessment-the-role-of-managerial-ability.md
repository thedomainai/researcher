# AI words, AI deeds, and environmental assessment: the role of managerial ability

**著者**: Yuchen Zhang, Jeya Santhini Appannan, Zarina Zakaria
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: コーポレートガバナンス

## Abstract

Purpose This study aims to examine how managerial ability (MA), AI-related disclosure (AI Words) and AI-related investment intensity (AI Deeds), a CSMAR-based proxy covering enabling digital assets, relate to composite environmental performance (EP) and external environmental ratings and whether the two AI measures are assessed differently. Design/methodology/approach Using data from Chinese non-financial A-share listed firms for 2010–2023, the study estimates models with firm and year fixed effects to examine the associations of MA, AI Words and AI Deeds with EP and external environmental ratings. Findings MA has a modest positive association with EP and a consistent negative association with AI Deeds, although the corresponding indirect association remains exploratory. In the contemporaneous model for the full rated sample, AI Words is associated with higher external ratings and AI Deeds with lower ratings, while neither is associated with rating-excluded EP. This pattern indicates a potential environmental assessment gap. Practical implications Regulators, rating providers and boards may benefit from distinguishing visible AI narratives from verifiable implementation and outcomes. Assessment and oversight frameworks for climate-transition governance could consider disclosure, resource allocation and energy implications rather than treating AI adoption as uniformly beneficial. Such alignment may also strengthen the credibility of sustainability information, stakeholder trust and corporate accountability. Originality/value Building on words–deeds, symbolic–substantive and AI-washing research, this study extends the words–deeds distinction to environmental assessment and positions MA as a governance-related resource-orchestration factor while recognising the measurement sensitivity of the MA–EP evidence.
