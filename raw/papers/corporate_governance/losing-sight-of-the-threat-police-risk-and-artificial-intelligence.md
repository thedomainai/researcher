# Losing sight of the threat: police, risk, and artificial intelligence

**著者**: Cole Heffren, Lorna Ferguson, Laura Huey
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: コーポレートガバナンス

## Abstract

Purpose Artificial Intelligence (AI) is rapidly reshaping society, presenting both opportunities and challenges for law enforcement. Police organisations are cautiously adopting these technologies to improve operational efficiency. However, significant risks also emerge as criminals exploit AI for harmful activities. Threats such as deepfakes, advanced phishing, and AI-driven malware endanger communities. Understanding how police leaders evaluate the various risks and prepare for AI-related threats is crucial for public safety. This study examines the perspectives of Canadian police personnel on their organisations’ readiness to address the challenges posed by malicious AI use.Design/methodology/approach We conducted thematic analysis of in-depth qualitative interviews (n = 41) with Canadian police who have knowledge of, or involvement in, organisational decision-making related to AI (both sworn and civilian).Originality/value This study found that, given pre-existing reputational risk factors, police personnel use risk-averse decision-making when implementing AI technology. Within the Social Construction of Technology (SCOT) theory, participants tended to focus on attenuating public concerns, to the detriment of planning and preparing for the criminal use of AI technologies. Given the rapid advances in AI technologies used by criminals that threaten to overwhelm police organisations, several strategies are discussed herein to mitigate these threats.
