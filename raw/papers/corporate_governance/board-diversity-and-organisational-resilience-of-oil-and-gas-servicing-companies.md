# Board Diversity and Organisational Resilience of Oil and Gas Servicing Companies in Nigeria: A Theoretical Perspective

**著者**: Charles Igoinfama, Simeon G. Nenbee, Waribugo Sylva, Thomas Chinye Okoisama
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0

## Abstract

In the volatile landscape of Nigeria's oil and gas servicing sector, board diversity has emerged as a critical factor influencing organizational resilience. This theoretical review explored the interplay between board diversity, proxied by gender and age diversity, and organizational resilience, measured through adaptability and robustness. Drawing on institutional theory and dynamic capabilities theory, the paper posits that diverse boards enhance decision-making processes, foster innovation, and enable firms to navigate regulatory pressures and market uncertainties prevalent in Nigeria. By synthesizing existing literature, this review highlights how gender and age diversity contribute to resilient organizational structures in a resource-dependent economy like Nigeria's, where oil and gas servicing companies face unique challenges such as environmental risks, geopolitical tensions, and economic fluctuations. The discussion underscores the need for theoretical advancements that incorporate contextual factors specific to developing markets, offering implications for policy and future empirical research. The study concluded that that appointing diverse board members should be pursued with strategic intentionality, focusing on the quality of discourse and integration of perspectives, rather than tokenism. It was recommended that Oil and gas servicing companies in Nigeria should prioritize the strategic recruitment of gender-diverse board members, ensuring at least a balanced representation of women to leverage their contributions to ethical decision-making and stakeholder engagement, as evidenced by studies showing reduced opportunistic behaviors and enhanced corporate social responsibility in volatile sectors. This approach would foster adaptability by incorporating diverse ethical lenses and bolster robustness through improved risk management in high-risk environments like the Niger Delta.
