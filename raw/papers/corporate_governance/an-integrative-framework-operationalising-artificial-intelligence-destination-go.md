# An integrative framework operationalising artificial intelligence destination governance and geopolitical risk moderation in sustainable tourism

**著者**: Javed Ali, Mohd Amran Mohd Daril, Siti Hajar Hussein, Nohman Khan
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: コーポレートガバナンス

## Abstract

The nexus between sustainable tourism, artificial intelligence (AI), and geopolitical fragility has attracted growing scholarly attention, yet no study has systematically synthesised how maritime security disruptions — specifically a Strait of Hormuz blockage — may intersect with tourism flows and sustainability transitions in the world. This paper addresses that gap through a PRISMA 2020-compliant systematic literature review of 24 peer-reviewed studies published between 2018 and 2026. Drawing on the Triple Bottom Line (TBL) framework, Stakeholder Theory, and Smart Destination Theory, we examine: (i) the typology and relative sustainability of tourism forms; (ii) the success and failure determinants of sustainable destination management; (iii) the structural role of AI and smart technologies in tourism governance; and (iv) the plausible but under-theorised impact of Hormuz disruptions on tourism. Our synthesis reveals that heritage-based and ecotourism modalities — particularly when augmented by AI-enabled governance — consistently outperform other tourism types on environmental, social, and economic sustainability dimensions. AI emerges not merely as a technological adjunct but as a structural catalyst for destination resilience. Geopolitical shock variables such as maritime blockages remain almost entirely absent from tourism sustainability models despite their demonstrated macroeconomic significance. A comprehensive multi-dimensional research framework is proposed to guide future empirical and mixed-methods investigations.
