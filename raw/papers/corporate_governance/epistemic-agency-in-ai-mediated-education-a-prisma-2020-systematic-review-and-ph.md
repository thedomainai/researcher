# Epistemic Agency in AI-Mediated Education: A PRISMA 2020 Systematic Review and Philosophical Synthesis of Autonomy, Understanding, and Educational Authority

**著者**: Connor Nitchals
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: systematic_review | **分野**: コーポレートガバナンス

## Abstract

Generative artificial intelligence (AI) has moved from an auxiliary educational technology to an epistemic intermediary that can formulate explanations, generate arguments, summarize sources, simulate dialogue, and produce assessable work. This transformation raises a philosophical question prior to questions of effectiveness: what happens to learning when cognitive and justificatory labor can be delegated to systems that produce knowledge-like outputs without human understanding or responsibility? This systematic review synthesizes peer-reviewed philosophical and theoretically oriented scholarship on AI-mediated education published between 2023 and 13 August 2026. Following PRISMA 2020, structured searches were conducted across ERIC-indexed records and publisher platforms covering educational philosophy, philosophy of education, educational theory, and adjacent interdisciplinary scholarship. Records were screened against explicit eligibility criteria focused on epistemic agency, autonomy, understanding, educational authority, teacher roles, and normative conditions of AI use. Twenty articles were included in the final synthesis. Conceptual quality was appraised using an adapted textand-opinion framework emphasizing source authority, argumentative transparency, conceptual coherence, engagement with counterpositions, and educational relevance. Six recurring themes were identified: (1) epistemic outsourcing and agency attenuation; (2) the distinction between performance and understanding; (3) algorithmic authority, trust, and testimonial dependence; (4) the irreducibility of teachers as ethical and relational agents; (5) epistemic justice, infrastructural power, and personalization; and (6) design principles for agency-preserving AI use. Across otherwise divergent perspectives, a consistent normative pattern emerged: educationally defensible AI use requires learners to retain responsibility for questioning, warranting, interpreting, and owning claims. The review develops a Human Epistemic Stewardship (HES) framework specifying five conditions for legitimate AI-mediated learning: contestability, justificatory ownership, productive friction, plural authority, and relational oversight. The synthesis reframes the central educational question from whether AI improves output to whether AI use preserves the practices through which persons become capable of knowing, judging, and acting for themselves.
