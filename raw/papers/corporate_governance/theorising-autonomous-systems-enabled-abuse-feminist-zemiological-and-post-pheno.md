# Theorising Autonomous Systems-Enabled Abuse: Feminist, Zemiological and Post Phenomenological Paths to Legal Change

**著者**: Arwa Bukhtiar
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: コーポレートガバナンス

## Abstract

AI-driven smart homes re-engineer the home into an even more intricate machinery of coercive control, raising fundamental questions about how law conceptualises harm, agency and responsibility. This research presents theoretical socio‐legal work and develops Autonomous Systems‐Enabled Abuse (ASEA) to name forms of domestic abuse co‐produced by abusers, designers, platforms and devices, rather than simply enacted through human ‘misuse’ of neutral tools. Building on feminist critiques of incident‐based, gender‐neutral law and continuum‐based accounts of violence, it argues that domestic abuse must be understood as a socio‐technical regime of governance over women’s lives, in which AI-driven smart home infrastructures autonomously monitor, restrict and destabilise everyday routines, generating ambient, anticipatory and infrastructural harms that current legal frameworks struggle to see as abuse. Focusing on England and Wales, this research undertakes a doctrinal and theoretical analysis of domestic abuse law and the UK’s international human rights obligations to ask how domestic abuse law should be reconceptualised to recognise and regulate ASEA against women. Methodologically, it synthesises case law, statutory developments and emerging policy with feminist, zemiological and post‐phenomenological theory to develop a relational account of harm, intent and causation that can track responsibility along socio‐technical chains, drawing analogies from corporate liability, product liability and doctrines of continuing causation. In doing so, the research re‐imagines core legal concepts, particularly mens rea, harm and the human machine boundary, as historically contingent constructs that must be reshaped to confront ‘technological patriarchy’ in AI‐driven homes. This research speaks directly to the Centre for Protecting Women Online (CPWO) conference theme by showing how technologies such as connected infrastructures and platforms enable domestic abuse, how this extends from online platforms to AI-driven smart home systems that expose women to violence inside the home, and how legal frameworks might be reshaped to better protect women in digitally saturated domestic environments.
