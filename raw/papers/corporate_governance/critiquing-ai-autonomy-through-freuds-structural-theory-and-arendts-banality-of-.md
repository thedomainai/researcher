# Critiquing AI Autonomy through Freud’s Structural Theory and Arendt’s Banality of Evil

**著者**: B.Y. Hughes Graduate Student
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: コーポレートガバナンス

## Abstract

This project examines contemporary claims about “AI autonomy” and “recursive self‑improvement” through a rigorous interdisciplinary framework combining psychoanalytic theory, political philosophy, and modern control engineering. The central argument is that current large language models (LLMs) do not possess autonomy in any substantive psychological, moral, or political sense. Using Freud’s structural theory, the study shows that LLMs lack endogenous drives, mediating ego structures, and internalized norms. Through Arendt’s concept of the banality of evil, it identifies the real risk not as self‑directed rebellion but as large‑scale, unreflective execution of externally defined objectives. Modern control theory further clarifies that so‑called “AI runaway behavior” corresponds to mathematical instability caused by human design choices rather than emergent agency. Overall, the project reframes AI risk as a structural and institutional problem: the danger arises when human actors delegate judgment to systems incapable of reflection, allowing banal, procedural harm to scale. The work provides conceptual foundations for rethinking AI governance, responsibility, and the misuse of autonomy‑related narratives in public and corporate discourse.
