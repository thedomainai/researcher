# Aligning technological, pedagogical, and critical competencies for AI integration in teacher education: a dependency-based framework

**著者**: Wilter Leonel Solórzano Álava, Dunia Lisbet Domínguez Gálvez, Julio César Pino Tarragó, Vanessa Mariuxi García Macías
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: meta_analysis | **分野**: コーポレートガバナンス

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly reshaping teacher education, yet previous research has shown that its integration often remains fragmented, technocentric, and weakly connected to pedagogical and critical dimensions. This study examines structural limitations in AI integration and proposes a dependency-based framework for aligning technological, pedagogical, and critical competencies in preservice teacher preparation. A sequential mixed-methods design was used. The qualitative phase analyzed 16 documents, including teacher education syllabi and peer-reviewed studies, through hybrid thematic analysis. The quantitative phase examined perceived competency patterns among 194 preservice teachers enrolled in a general didactics course. The documentary analysis identified four recurrent limitations: technocentric orientation, curricular fragmentation, insufficient teacher preparation, and weak incorporation of ethical and critical perspectives. The empirical results showed an uneven competency profile, with higher technological competence (M=4.28, SD=0.39) than pedagogical competence (M=3.74, SD=0.30) and critical competence (M=3.13, SD=0.28). Inferential analysis confirmed statistically significant differences among the three dimensions, χ²(2, N=194)=368.29, p<0.001, with a very large effect size (Kendall’s W=0.95). The main contribution is the concept of structural imbalance, which explains AI integration as competency misalignment rather than isolated skill deficiency. The proposed framework contributes to educational evaluation by offering a criterion for assessing whether AI-related competencies are coherently aligned to support pedagogically meaningful and ethically responsible practice.
