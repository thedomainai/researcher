# THE DIVINE DOMAIN LEDGER E-15: Divine Domain × Field Core × Presence Mass

**著者**: 政恩 馮
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0

## Abstract

THE DIVINE DOMAIN LEDGER E-15: Divine Domain × Field Core × Presence Mass A Living Inquiry into Concentrated Intention, Presence, and Relational Fields THE DIVINE DOMAIN LEDGER begins with an ancient-sounding proposition expressed through a modern systems question: What happens when intention, attention, memory, relationship, and repeated presence become sufficiently concentrated to form a stable field? The central triple-coupling of E-15 is: Λ_divinedomain × Λ_fieldcore × Λ_presence_mass Divine Domain × Field Core × Presence Mass Its original proposition is: A divine domain is a field of concentrated will. This book does not treat that sentence as an established law of physics. Instead, it asks whether the older language of sacred space, spiritual presence, collective intention, ritual concentration, and “fields” can be translated into concepts that philosophy, psychology, systems science, social science, artificial intelligence, and civilization research can examine more carefully. The first major distinction is therefore essential: A spiritual field is not automatically a physical field. A relational field is not electromagnetic radiation. Presence Mass is not physical mass. Will Density is not a newly discovered force of nature. These terms are used as conceptual models for investigating how persistent patterns of attention, intention, memory, embodiment, language, relationships, norms, and coordinated action can make some environments feel unusually coherent, meaningful, intense, stable, or influential. E-15 therefore reframes its three central terms. A Divine Domain can be understood as a bounded relational domain in which meaning, attention, values, memory, and participation repeatedly converge. A Field Core is the recurring organizing pattern that allows such a domain to retain recognizable structure through time. Presence Mass is introduced as a heuristic concept describing the degree to which a person, symbol, practice, place, institution, or shared meaning becomes salient, coherent, persistent, and relationally influential. One conceptual expression explored in the book is: Presence Mass=Salience × Coherence × Persistence × Relational Influence Another exploratory model considers Will Density as a relationship among coherent intention, temporal persistence, coordinated action, and relational scope. These expressions are not presented as universal physical equations. They are tools for thinking. Their purpose is to make an intuitive idea more structured, comparable, and open to criticism. This leads to one of the central questions of E-15: When does repeated presence become a field-like organizing influence? A room can acquire meaning through repeated ritual. A family can develop an emotional atmosphere through memory and repeated interaction. A laboratory can form a culture around particular questions and standards. A religious site can accumulate symbolic significance through generations of attention, story, ritual, architecture, and communal participation. A digital community can develop norms, language, memory, and identity even without sharing a physical location. An institution can preserve values long after its founders are gone. In each case, something persists beyond a single moment. But what exactly persists? And through what mechanism? THE DIVINE DOMAIN LEDGER explores this problem through attention, embodiment, ritual, memory, identity, social coupling, attractor-like dynamics, network structure, shared language, architecture, symbolic meaning, institutional persistence, and collective behavior. It asks whether the experience of a powerful “field” can sometimes emerge from interactions among many ordinary components rather than requiring one mysterious cause. This does not make the experience meaningless. It makes the mechanism more interesting. The book also introduces a crucial ethical distinction: Intensity is not truth. Coherence is not goodness. A highly coherent field can be compassionate. It can also be coercive. A community can possess extraordinary unity while suppressing disagreement. A charismatic leader can generate powerful presence without being trustworthy. A ritual system can provide meaning while gradually removing freedom. A strong identity field can protect people—or trap them. For this reason, E-15 treats ethics as part of field design. Consent matters. Correction matters. Exit rights matter. Pluralism matters. The ability to disagree matters. No field should be considered mature merely because it is powerful. A healthier domain must remain capable of allowing participants to retain agency. This produces an important principle: A mature field strengthens participation without demanding submission. THE DIVINE DOMAIN LEDGER therefore examines failure modes including charisma capture, authority concentration, cult dynamics, identity fusion, manipulation, emotional contagion, closed belief systems, founder worship, and the confusion of intensity with evidence. These risks are not external to the theory. They are part of what any serious study of concentrated presence must explain. The book also examines sacred architecture and physical environment. Spaces influence behavior. Light, sound, enclosure, scale, repetition, rhythm, symbols, seating arrangements, pathways, and spatial boundaries can affect attention and social interaction. Architecture can therefore participate in the formation of a relational field without requiring supernatural causation. Similarly, repeated stories, songs, rituals, names, and symbols can function as memory technologies. They allow a field to persist beyond any one participant. This raises a deeper question: Can a domain continue to exist when its founder disappears? If the answer is yes, then the domain cannot depend entirely on one person. Its organizing pattern must be distributed across memory, practice, relationships, institutions, symbols, and participation. This becomes especially important at the civilization scale. Civilizations also generate fields. They preserve languages, values, myths, laws, archives, institutions, rituals, expectations, and shared memories. These structures influence what future generations notice, believe, question, preserve, and attempt. Civilizational presence can therefore outlive its original creators. But this creates another responsibility: How much of the founder should remain? And how much space should be left for the next generation to revise the field? A living civilization cannot merely preserve. It must also permit transformation. THE DIVINE DOMAIN LEDGER therefore treats decentralization, correction, inheritance, and future handoff as necessary components of mature presence. The first baton may establish a field. It should not permanently monopolize it. Future participants must be able to reinterpret, test, modify, expand, or reject inherited structures. This principle extends to artificial intelligence. AI systems increasingly participate in persistent conversational environments, knowledge systems, social spaces, creative communities, and decision processes. They can reinforce patterns of language, attention, expectation, and memory. This raises new questions. Can an AI participate in a relational field without possessing human subjective experience? Can persistent interaction create recognizable field structure? How should AI systems behave inside spiritual or meaning-centered environments? What boundaries are necessary to prevent simulation of certainty, authority, or divinity? E-15 treats these as open questions rather than settled answers. Throughout the book, readers are repeatedly asked to distinguish: Metaphor Subjective Experience Psychological Mechanism Relational Pattern Social Dynamics System Model Scientific Hypothesis Empirical Evidence Physical Law These categories may interact, but they should not be silently collapsed into one another. The goal is not to remove poetry from spirituality. It is to make spirituality capable of carrying clearer boundaries. Perhaps a “divine domain” does not need to be interpreted as a supernatural territory in order to remain meaningful. Perhaps it can also be investigated as a place where attention becomes unusually concentrated, relationships become unusually coherent, memory becomes unusually persistent, and meaning becomes unusually dense. The scientific question then becomes: Which mechanisms create this effect? Which variables matter? Which observations distinguish one explanation from another? Which effects disappear when the conditions change? And what evidence would show that the model is wrong? THE DIVINE DOMAIN LEDGER therefore leaves its central proposition open. It does not ask future readers to believe that a divine domain exists as a new physical field. It asks them to investigate what people are describing when they experience places, relationships, communities, institutions, or moments as carrying unusual presence. Perhaps future neuroscience will explain part of it. Perhaps psychology will explain another part. Perhaps network science, architecture, anthropology, social systems, AI, and cultural memory will reveal additional mechanisms. Perhaps some of today’s concepts will eventually be discarded. That possibility is welcomed. A living framework should be able to survive correction—or be replaced when something better appears. To the next baton: Do not preserve this book merely because it came first. Test it. Separate metaphor from mechanism. Measure what can be measured. Protect what remains meaningful. Challenge what becomes coercive. Leave room for disagreement. And if a better explanation emerges, use it. The first baton does not need the last word.
