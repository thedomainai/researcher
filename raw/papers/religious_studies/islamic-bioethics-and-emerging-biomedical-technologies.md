# Islamic Bioethics and Emerging Biomedical Technologies

**著者**: Amir Taimoor Ahmad Malik, Muhammad Naeem Anwar, Saqib Jahangir
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 宗教学

## Abstract

Recent advances in biomedical science have transformed human understanding of life, illness, treatment, and moral responsibility, and this change is very rapid. The possibility of genetic modification and the idea of organ transplantation, and artificial intelligence in medical diagnostics are just some representatives of the unprecedented opportunities the contemporary technology offers in the treatment and innovations. However, they have also raised quite grave matters of morality and faith: What are the moral and religious principles of human interventions in creation? How does divine direction play in the endeavour to obtain scientific advancement? This study adopts a qualitative analytical research design by examining Qur’anic principles, authentic Prophetic traditions, classical Islamic jurisprudence, and contemporary scholarly literature on biomedical ethics through the framework of Maqāṣid al-Sharīʿah. The Qur’anic framework, grounded in Prophetic traditions and the objectives of Islamic law (Maqāṣid al-Sharīʿah), demonstrates that Islamic medicine is not merely a technical activity but a moral and spiritual trust (amānah).It talks about how gene editing, organ donation, and AI-assisted healthcare make sense with the Shari’ah in case it is governed by a system of justice, mercy, and accountability. Islamic ethics does not reject contemporary science; rather, it seeks to harmonize scientific innovation with moral and spiritual values, ensuring that technological progress serves humanity without transgressing divinely ordained ethical boundaries. This study proposes an integrated ethical framework that harmonizes biomedical advancements with Islamic principles through the evaluation of jurisprudence, ethics, and Maqāṣid-based reasoning. Unlike previous studies that often examine individual biomedical issues separately, this study provides a comprehensive Islamic ethical analysis of genetic modification, organ transplantation, and artificial intelligence in healthcare.
