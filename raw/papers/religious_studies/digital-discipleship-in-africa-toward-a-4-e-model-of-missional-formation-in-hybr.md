# DIGITAL DISCIPLESHIP IN AFRICA TOWARD A 4 E MODEL OF MISSIONAL FORMATION IN HYBRID RELIGIOUS LIFE

**著者**: Christopher Kabwe Mukuka
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 宗教学

## Abstract

African churches now inhabit a digital religious ecology in which unprecedented reach can coexist with a serious formation deficit: people may encounter abundant Christian content without entering durable processes of biblical learning, accountable community, service, and reproductive witness. This conceptual missiological study develops a critical conceptual synthesis of digital religion, media ecology, African religion-and-media scholarship, Ubuntu-oriented relational thought, and biblical-missiological theology. It argues that the distinctive African contribution is not a technological technique but a relational grammar: digitally mediated ministry should be evaluated by whether it deepens reciprocal belonging, communal discernment, shared agency, embodied ecclesial participation, and responsibility for others. The synthesis yields the 4-E Digital Discipleship Framework—Engage, Educate, Empower, and Evangelise—conceived as an iterative pathway rather than a rigid sequence. The framework also addresses accessibility, sacramental limits, digital authority, and artificial intelligence (AI), proposing that AI remain assistive and accountable to human persons, ecclesial communities, and truthful theological judgment. Zambia provides a bounded illustration of layered hybridity across high- and low-bandwidth media. The model is offered as a theoretically grounded heuristic requiring empirical testing. Its original contribution is to move African digital missiology from metrics of digital presence toward formed presence: disciples whose online and embodied practices display biblical literacy, relational accountability, ministerial agency, and the capacity to accompany others into mature Christian participation.
