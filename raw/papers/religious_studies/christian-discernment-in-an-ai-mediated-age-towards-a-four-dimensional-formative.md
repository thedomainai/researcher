# Christian Discernment in an AI-Mediated Age: Towards a Four-Dimensional Formative Ecology

**著者**: Arleen Luo
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 宗教学

## Abstract

Christian engagement with generative artificial intelligence has understandably focused on ethics, accuracy, and the theological adequacy of what systems produce. Yet a prior question concerns formation: what happens to the persons and communities who increasingly interpret, learn, deliberate, and seek guidance through AI-mediated practices? This paper approaches that question through theological discernment, understood as a Spirit-formed capacity to perceive and respond faithfully to God's action. It proposes that the formation of such discernment can be examined through a four-dimensional ecology: environmental, epistemic, anthropological, and relational. These dimensions are analytically distinguishable but formatively interdependent; their significance lies in the configurations produced among them rather than in independent effects. Drawing together theological accounts of discernment with interdisciplinary work on formation, digital religion, and human–AI interaction, the paper develops the framework as a general theoretical account rather than a population-specific model. Generative AI makes the need for such an ecological account particularly acute because it can participate across several dimensions at once: expanding access while synthesising knowledge, redistributing cognitive work, acquiring relational salience, and altering the conditions under which authority is recognised. The paper uses provenance, cognitive governance, and conversational mediation to demonstrate the framework's analytical value and argues that human responsibility must be understood as differentiated participation within, rather than autonomous control over, this ecology. The framework remains provisional and open to empirical testing. It forms the conceptual foundation of an ongoing research programme whose first empirical field is Chinese Christian emerging adults in diasporic settings.
