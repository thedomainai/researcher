# Bamiyan: The Lost Paradise – A Scientific Investigation into the Garden of Eden, the Avestan Sacred Geography, and the Āzrah People ## Extended Description This research paper presents a comprehensive comparative analysis of sacred texts and historical sources from multiple civilizations—Avestan, Jewish, Christian, Islamic, Persian, and Chinese—to investigate the thesis that the Bamiyan Valley in central Afghanistan is the original location of the Garden of Eden, the Avestan Airyanem Vaejah (Iranwij), and the sacred geography of the world's major religions. The study integrates primary sources including the Torah (Genesis 2:8-14), the Avesta (Vendidad, Mihr Yasht, Zamyād Yasht), the Qur'an (Surah Al-Hijr, Surah Al-Kahf), the Persian chronicle Majmal al-Tawarikh, and the pioneering research of Captain Francis Wilford (1799). It demonstrates that the Bamiyan Valley is the cradle of humanity's sacred imagination, where foundational concepts of paradise, hell, the bridge of judgment (Chinvat Bridge / As-Sirat), angels, and the primordial human originated. ## Key Findings - The Torah places the Garden of Eden "in the east," and early Jewish and Christian scholars associated it with the mountains of Khorasan (Bamiyan).- The Avesta names Airyanem Vaejah as the first land created by Ahura Mazda and identifies Arəzahī as the eastern clime of first sunrise—the linguistic origin of "Āzrah" (the self-designation of the Hazara people).- The Avestan sacred rivers (Haētumant, Xᵛâstrâ, Hvaspâ, Fradaθâ, and others) originate from the Hindu Kush mountains in Bamiyan.- The Qur'an uses the term "Salsal" for Adam's creation, a name given to the largest Buddha statue in Bamiyan.- The Persian chronicle Majmal al-Tawarikh explicitly states that Adam descended to Earth in Bamiyan.- Captain Francis Wilford's 1799 article identified Bamiyan as the Garden of Eden, shaping the Western imagination for two centuries.- Arab scholars and rulers after the Islamic conquests systematically appropriated this sacred geography, relocating Eden from Bamiyan to an abstract eschatological concept (Jannāt ʿAdn) to facilitate religious and commercial expansion.- The Āzrah (Hazara) people are the biological and cultural heirs of this sacred geography, with haplogroup J2a1-Page55 (13%) and G2-M201 (8.7%) linking them to the Neolithic farmers of the Iranian plateau, and 25% of Hazaragi vocabulary derived directly from Avestan. ## Academic Value This paper provides a critical re-evaluation of Zoroastrian, Jewish, Christian, and Islamic sacred geography, demonstrating that Bamiyan is not merely a historical site but the original Paradise, the cradle of humanity's sacred imagination, and the homeland of the Āzrah people.
