# MODAL-ORGANIZATIONAL THEORY OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

**著者**: Roman Shaban
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 組織科学

## Abstract

One of the fundamental problems in contemporary artificial intelligence research concerns the relationship between the computational structures of intelligent systems and the cognitive capabilities they exhibit. Although artificial neural networks are typically described in terms of computational units, parameters, architectures, internal representations, and information-processing mechanisms, these descriptions do not fully resolve the conceptual distinction between operations currently performed by a system, operations that remain accessible under specified conditions, and transformations that may alter the system's repertoire of possible cognitive activities.This treatise develops a modal-organizational framework for analyzing artificial intelligence through the structure of its functional possibilities, the constraints governing their realization, and the transformations through which these possibilities may change.The central argument is that the organization of an intelligent artificial system cannot be adequately characterized solely by its currently realized computations. A broader analysis must also account for the relations between available operations, the conditions governing their composition, the resources required for their realization, and the permissible transformations of the organizational structures themselves.The proposed framework distinguishes five analytical levels: physical implementation, computational structure, causal-functional organization, compositional organization, and the modal structure of cognitive capabilities.A formal model of cognitive organization is introduced in terms of states, operators, composition rules, organizational constraints, and resource costs. On this basis, three categories of cognitive possibility are distinguished: actualized, operationally accessible, and transformationally accessible capabilities.The treatise further differentiates four types of organizational change: behavioral variation, compositional expansion, organizational-rule reconfiguration, and representational-space transformation.Four elementary formal propositions are developed concerning the invariance of capabilities under structure-preserving recoding, the insufficiency of restricted behavioral equivalence for establishing modal equivalence, the conservativity of adding an already definable operator, and the dependence of resource-bounded capabilities on implementation costs.The framework is examined in relation to causal abstraction, mechanistic explanation, functionalism, multiple realizability, computational mechanics, category-theoretic compositionality, dynamical systems, and theories of transformational creativity.A central conclusion is that novelty of output, novelty of capability, novelty of mechanism, and novelty of organizational principle must be treated as conceptually distinct phenomena.The proposed theory does not claim to establish a new physical constituent of intelligence or an empirically verified universal mechanism. Its contribution is a systematic philosophical and formal framework for examining the relationship between computational realization, organizational structure, functional possibility, and transformations of the space of accessible cognitive operations.
