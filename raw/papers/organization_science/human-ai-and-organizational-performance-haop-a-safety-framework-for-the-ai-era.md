# Human, AI, and Organizational Performance (HAOP): A Safety Framework for the AI Era

**著者**: Jaina Ko
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 組織科学

## Abstract

Human, AI, and Organizational Performance (HAOP) is a safety framework extending Human and Organizational Performance (HOP) to work systems in which AI performs consequential functions. The framework recognizes three performers in one work system — the human who adapts, the AI that optimizes within its representation and constraints, and the organization that authors the conditions both perform inside — and provides the governance architecture that follows from seeing them together: characteristic failure and contribution signatures; a five-domain hazard register for AI-enabled work; an Accountability by Control discipline that keeps responsibility attached to the roles holding relevant control; and the True Function Test, an initial diagnostic that can be applied now. Revision 4.0 is a major revision. New and substantially expanded in this version: Verification Gates: a designed control boundary at consequential transitions, extending the hold-point discipline from physical conditions to representations, with the anchor taxonomy formalized as source, state, and dynamics anchors producing three forms of grounding. Five hazard domains for AI-enabled work, framed as hazard conditions identifiable prospectively — including Verification Overrun as the bridge between the human-performance and work-system-design domains. Failure and contribution signatures for all three performers, and the coupled conditions Compound Drift and Verification Overrun. Accountability rebuilt around control, duty, and capacity: the three alignment failures (false signature, hidden owner, fragmented control), the vendor boundary with Imposed Offloading, and the human spine. Governance and standards alignment: EU AI Act Articles 14 and 26(2) read as a Verification Capacity requirement; positioning relative to Rasmussen and STAMP/STPA; the organizational performer's hazard domain; and hazard-prevalence metrics aligned with ISO 45001, ISO/IEC 42001, and ASTM E2920-26. The proposed ten-step HAOP work-design sequence and the Designed AI Reassessment Pause. Closed informational loops and the collapse of correction integrated into the information-integrity hazard domain.
