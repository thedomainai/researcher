# Detection Without Speed Isn’t Defense: Human-AI Coordination Against Cognitive Cyberattackers

**著者**: Ayla Santos, Stephan Carney, Rob Groome, Nikolos Gurney
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 組織科学

## Abstract

Accurate attacker identification improves defense only if defenders can act in time. Because an attacker’s cognitive profile can dictate their network maneuvering, we evaluated whether an AI defender that profiles attacker cognition outperforms a human-inspired defender, and if pairing them improves defense. Using an agent-based simulation of an enterprise network, we tested two cognitively distinct attackers (aggressive vs. cautious) against three defender configurations (human-analog heuristic, AI, and human-machine team) under partial observability in a 2×3 factorial design. Counterintuitively, the human-machine team underperformed both individual defenders. The AI defender performed best, yet its identification accuracy remained at chance, indicating its success stemmed from strong default actions rather than accurate profiling. Improving the AI’s observability raised identification accuracy across three interventions but failed to improve defense. Only a structural intervention trapping attackers without identification reduced breaches, leveraging a compact topology with centralized hubs. These findings show that rapidly stopping a threat is distinct from identifying it. In many instances, effective human-AI cyber defense may rely less on profiling accuracy than on response speed and seamless coordination
