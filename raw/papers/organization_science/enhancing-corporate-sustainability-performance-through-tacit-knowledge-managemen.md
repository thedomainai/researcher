# Enhancing corporate sustainability performance through tacit knowledge management: the dual mediation of green business strategy and generative AI with top management support

**著者**: 邓诗霞, Tejas R. Shah, Salwa Saleh Almasabi, Omar Abdeljaber, Sándor Remsei
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 組織科学

## Abstract

Purpose Amid escalating global environmental and social pressures, corporate sustainability performance (CSP) has become a critical strategic imperative, particularly for highly polluting sectors. Drawing on the natural resource-based view and dynamic capabilities theory, this study aims to examine the impact of tacit knowledge management process (TKMP), particularly knowledge acquisition, sharing and application on the CSP of steel industry in China. Furthermore, it explores the dual mediating mechanisms of green business strategy (GBS) and generative artificial intelligence (GAI) in translating TKMP into CSP, while examining top management support (TMS) as a boundary condition moderating the relationship between the TKMP dimensions and GBS. Design/methodology/approach The study gathered data from 357 employees of steel manufacturing companies in China using survey questionnaires. The study used smart-PLS to examine both measurement and structural models. Findings The results showed that all three dimensions of TKMP are positively associated with CSP. GBS and GAI significantly mediate the relationships between the TKMP dimensions and CSP (complementary partial mediation). In addition, TMS positively moderates (strengthens) the relationship between tacit knowledge application and GBS, but it negatively moderates (weakens) the relationship between tacit knowledge acquisition and GBS and it does not significantly moderate the relationship for knowledge sharing. Originality/value These findings provide theoretical insights into how intangible knowledge resources and advanced artificial intelligence can jointly support sustainability, offering practical guidance for policymakers and managers seeking to leverage TKMP and GAI to achieve environmental and social objectives in heavy manufacturing contexts.
