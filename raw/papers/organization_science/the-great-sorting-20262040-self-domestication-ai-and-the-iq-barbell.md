# The Great Sorting, 2026–2040 Self-Domestication, AI, and the IQ Barbell

**著者**: Joel Alcarez
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 組織科学

## Abstract

Human self-domestication — the multi-millennial selection for tameness, cooperation, and reduced reactive aggression — plateaued around 2010, the point at which institutional, digital, and educational compliance systems reached saturation in developed economies. Generative AI does not continue this trend uniformly. It reverses it, but only for the undomesticated tail of the distribution: individuals with low attachment to compliance norms who convert AI capacity into iteration speed rather than output substitution. This paper models both populations — the compliant 80% (“Offloaders”) whose cognition atrophies as AI absorbs judgment, and the undomesticated 20% (the “tail”) who use AI as a sparring partner and compound raw capability — across 2026–2040. A Monte Carlo simulation (N = 10,000 agents) shows population-mean cognitive scores dipping slightly through 2030–2033 as Offloaders dominate headcount, before recovering by 2040 as the tail’s compounding gains pull the mean back up. Variance nearly doubles the top-to-bottom spread. A companion leverage-gap model shows one undomesticated individual with AI matching a 50-person compliant team by the mid-2030s. Institutions, which hire and promote for domestication, are structurally blind to this shift. The result is not a dumber species — it is a feral distribution.
