# The role of artificial intelligence in change management: ethical and organizational culture implications

**著者**: David Edgar, Roberto Biloslavo, Matteo Rizzo
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 組織科学

## Abstract

Purpose This paper examines the accelerating integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into organizational change management (OCM). It explores AI's dual role as both a powerful facilitator of change and a continuous disruptor itself, focusing specifically on the ethical and cultural challenges it introduces to traditional operational and decision-making frameworks. Design/methodology/approach Employing a narrative literature review approach, this study synthesizes 47 peer-reviewed journal articles, conference proceedings, relevant practitioner papers and book chapters published since 2010. The review systematically analyzes the literature on the integration, impact and long-term consequences of applying AI in OCM contexts. Findings The review underscores AI's transformative capacity to reshape OCM from a traditional, linear process into a dynamic, continuous “meta-change” formalized here as an integrative process linking AI's technological properties to three generative mechanisms, two governing elements and OCM outcomes expressed as four theoretical propositions. While AI enhances change efficiency, it also introduces significant cultural and ethical hurdles, including algorithmic bias, identity threat among management and the unintended reshaping of workplace culture and norms. The findings indicate that successfully navigating this change landscape requires a strategic shift toward staged implementation, organizational agility, cultural alignment, effective ethical policies and, most importantly, human-AI symbiosis, underpinned by Explainable AI (XAI) to foster workforce trust. Research limitations/implications The research advances the conversation around AI in managing the change process and emphasizes the need for dynamic and human-oriented interventions to work in parallel with AI. Future research should explore long-term implications of AI adoption in OCM, focusing on the interplay between technological advancements and organizational culture. Investigating sector-specific nuances and developing strategies to address workforce adaptation, resistance and continuous learning are critical areas for further study. Practical implications Organizations can utilize AI to personalize change strategies, foster agility and support data-driven decisions. Leaders should emphasize ethical AI implementation and workforce upskilling to ensure successful integration and maintain trust. Collaborative frameworks that combine human intuition with AI insights are essential for achieving sustainable outcomes. Social implications Social implications including understanding the workforce displacement, upskilling and changes in organizational culture aligned to any integration of AI in the management of organizational change. While AI enhances efficiency, it may fuel inequalities, automate routine tasks and challenge human–AI collaboration dynamics. Ethical concerns like algorithmic bias and accountability impact trust and fairness, while rapid transitions can cause employee stress and resistance. A broader societal effect could be economic polarization and ethical use of AI. Originality/value This paper bridges the critical gap between theoretical discourse on AI-driven OCM and practical, ethical implications and implementation. While AI's potential to optimize change is widely recognized, existing research remains fragmented regarding the ethical governance and cultural frictions associated with its adoption. By identifying emergent themes from the narrative literature review, this study offers a theoretically grounded agenda for “meta-change,” reconciling technological agility with human-centric governance in AI-enabled organizational change.
