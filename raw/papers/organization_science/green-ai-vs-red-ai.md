## Abstract

As artificial intelligence systems scale rapidly, the environmental consequences of training large language models remain critically underexamined. This chapter, per the authors, investigates the carbon cost differential between computationally intensive “Red AI” approaches and environmentally conscious “Green AI” methodologies prioritizing efficiency and sustainable design. Through systematic benchmarking across diverse hardware configurations and model architectures, a comparative framework is established for quantifying carbon expenditure throughout the LLM lifecycle. Key dimensions examined include data center energy sourcing, hardware accelerator efficiency, training duration optimization, and carbon offsetting strategies. Architectural innovations such as sparse attention, parameter sharing, and pruning are analyzed for measurable contributions to emissions reduction without sacrificing model performance. Findings illuminate actionable pathways for researchers and organizations seeking to align LLM development with global climate accountability standards.
