# Artificial intelligence as recurring organizational change: accountability and management control challenges across AI waves

**著者**: Paula Cristina de Almeida Marques, Marcelo de Santana Porte
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 組織科学

## Abstract

Purpose Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly embedded in organizational decision-making, raising questions about how decision authority, accountability and management control are allocated and maintained. This paper aims to examine why accountability and management control challenges may recur across different technological configurations. It proposes that such challenges can arise when changes in effective decision authority are not accompanied by corresponding clarification of organizational responsibility and opportunities for oversight and intervention. Design/methodology/approach The study adopts a conceptual and interpretive research design based on a historically informed analysis of organizational change. Drawing on literature from management control, governance and organizational studies, it compares four waves of AI-related organizational change: computational decision support as a precursor to organizational AI adoption, followed by expert systems, data-driven and algorithmic management and contemporary AI-enabled environments. The analysis develops a conceptual synthesis of recurring accountability questions across these configurations. Findings The analysis identifies four recurring areas of concern: potential misalignment between effective decision authority and formal responsibility; limitations in decision visibility and traceability; insufficient integration of decision technologies with strategic management control; and governance arrangements that may require adaptation as systems and organizational uses change. These patterns are not presented as inevitable outcomes of AI adoption. Instead, the study proposes authority–responsibility decoupling as a mechanism through which accountability and management control gaps may persist or reemerge under particular organizational conditions. Originality/value The paper contributes to research on accounting and organizational change by connecting contemporary AI governance questions to a longer history of organizational decision technologies. Its wave-based framework distinguishes a precursor phase of computational decision support from subsequent AI adoption and links recurring accountability challenges to decision rights, responsibility, oversight and management control. The resulting conceptual proposition provides a basis for future empirical research and for examining how governance arrangements can be adapted to AI-enabled decision processes.
