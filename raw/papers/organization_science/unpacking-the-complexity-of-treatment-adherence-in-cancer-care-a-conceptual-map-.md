# Unpacking The Complexity Of Treatment Adherence In Cancer Care: A Conceptual Map For Ai Intervention Design

**著者**: Ranjan Dissanayake, Nalika Ulapane, Stephen Vaughan, Ahmad Salehi Shahraki, Nilmini Wickramasinghe
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 組織科学

## Abstract

Treatment adherence in cancer care remains a persistent, high-impact challenge that resists conventional intervention due to its deeply embedded sociotechnical complexity.This study reconceptualises adherence as a dyadic socio-technical coordination problem, rather than an individual behavioural deficit.We present a co-designed conceptual map developed as Phase 1 of a Design Science Research Methodology (DSRM) inquiry to rigorously structure this complexity and identify actionable loci for AI-enabled intervention.Grounded in the World Health Organization's Five Dimensions of Adherence and extended through a People-Process-Technology framework lens and Task-Technology Fit theory, the map decomposes adherence into interacting patient, practitioner, and system-level factors.A key structural innovation is the explicit disaggregation of the "health system" dimension into practitioner-level and organizational-level constructs, enabling finer-grained identification of intervention points.The analysis identifies the patient-practitioner dyad as the primary locus of adherence formation, reframing adherence as an emergent property of relational coordination.Within this reframing, AI is positioned as an active coordination agent capable of mediating communication, aligning decision timing, and augmenting clinical and behavioural synchronisation through mechanisms such as predictive adherence risk modelling, real-time decision support, and AI-mediated patient engagement.This work contributes (1) a novel IS conceptualisation of adherence as a dyadic coordination system, (2) a co-designed mapping approach for structuring wicked problem domains, and (3) a theoretically grounded identification of AI intervention boundaries aligned with value-based healthcare.The study establishes a rigorous foundation for subsequent DSRM phases focused on designing and evaluating AI-enabled adherence artefacts.
