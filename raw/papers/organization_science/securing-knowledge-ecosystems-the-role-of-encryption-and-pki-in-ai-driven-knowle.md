# Securing Knowledge Ecosystems: The Role of Encryption and PKI in AI-Driven Knowledge Management Systems

**著者**: Vincent Lozupone
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 組織科学

## Abstract

As organizations adopt AI-powered knowledge management at scale, the trust problem shifts from simple data storage to the integrity, provenance, and controlled use of knowledge across retrieval, summarization, and agentic workflows. Encryption and public key infrastructure (PKI) provide the cryptographic foundation for protecting knowledge assets, binding identities to actions, and ensuring that AI systems operate on data that is confidential, authentic, and auditable. The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) frames cryptography as the mathematical protection of information and notes that modern cryptographic standards now include postquantum approaches that can be implemented today to protect current communications against future quantum threats. The NIST also positions its AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) as a way to incorporate trustworthiness into the design, development, use, and evaluation of AI systems. This paper synthesizes current guidance from NIST, industry practitioners, and emerging AI security research to produce a comprehensive examination of how encryption and PKI can be applied across the full AI knowledge pipeline – from document ingestion to agentic output. It further addresses governance, regulatory alignment, post-quantum readiness, and zero-trust architecture principles relevant to enterprise-scale deployments.
