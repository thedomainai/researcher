# Synthetic Inertia: Restoring the Shock Absorber That AI Agents Remove from Organizations

**著者**: Hemanjan Padhy
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 組織科学

## Abstract

Human latency has always protected organizations without anyone designing it: work that passes through people moves slowly enough that mistakes are often caught before they spread. AI agents remove this latency. Borrowing from power systems, where grids that lose the natural inertia of spinning generators now engineer synthetic inertia back in, I argue that organizations need the same layer. I define organizational inertia time as the time before a disturbance spreads past tolerance, and I state a design rule: an organization's systems should never be able to change faster than it can notice. I then propose the inertia governor, which limits how fast the combined state of the business can move rather than how fast any single actor can act. A simulation of a 40-step organization shows that replacing 80% of steps with agents cuts inertia time from 312 minutes to 4, and raises contamination at detection from 3.5% to 52%. Per-agent rate limits recover little (47%). The inertia governor holds contamination to 13% and cuts exposure before detection by 76%, without slowing normal work at its calibrated setting. Across nine failure modes, seven have design fixes that recover much or most of the protection. Two limits remain: drift and shocks that look like normal business.
