# Beyond usefulness: cognitive and emotional correlates of AI use intentions among HR professionals

**著者**: René Arseneault, Houssem Eddine Ben Messaoud
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 組織科学

## Abstract

Purpose Artificial intelligence (AI) is transforming human resource management (HRM), yet the cognitive and psychological factors associated with human resource (HR) professionals’ intentions to use AI remain incompletely understood. Drawing on the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and appraisal perspectives, this study examines the relative associations of Cognitive Evaluation, AI-Use Anxiety, AI-Related Job Insecurity, organizational AI use across HR functions, and AI Knowledge with behavioral intention to use AI. It also explores whether AI Knowledge moderates these relationships. Design/methodology/approach Survey data were collected from 285 HR professionals. Confirmatory factor analyses assessed the measurement structure of the study constructs, followed by hierarchical moderated regression analyses examining their unique associations with behavioral intention and the exploratory moderating role of AI Knowledge. Findings Cognitive Evaluation, reflecting perceived usefulness and ease of use, showed the strongest unique association with behavioral intention to use AI. AI-Use Anxiety demonstrated a smaller negative association, whereas AI-Related Job Insecurity, AI Use Across HR Functions, and AI Knowledge did not explain significant unique variance once the competing predictors were considered simultaneously. The comprehensive model explained 66.8% of the variance in behavioral intention. The exploratory interaction analyses provided no clear evidence that AI Knowledge systematically moderated the relationships examined. Practical implications Organizations implementing AI in HR should address both employees’ evaluations of the technology and apprehension surrounding its use. Implementation strategies should demonstrate the practical usefulness and usability of AI through role-specific examples, hands-on training, and opportunities to experiment with AI tools in realistic HR contexts. Organizations should also recognize AI-use anxiety as a distinct potential barrier by providing accessible support, low-risk opportunities for familiarization, and clear communication throughout implementation. These strategies may help create conditions that support employees’ willingness to use AI. Originality/value This study extends research on AI use intentions by examining cognitive, emotional, knowledge-related, and organizational-exposure factors simultaneously rather than in isolation. The findings show that HR professionals’ intentions to use AI are most strongly associated with evaluations of its usefulness and usability, while AI-use anxiety represents a smaller but distinct negative correlate. By distinguishing AI-use anxiety from AI-related job insecurity and individual AI knowledge from organizational AI exposure, the study provides a more differentiated account of the factors associated with HR professionals’ intentions to use AI.
