# Understanding the potential role of GenAI and knowledge governance in the higher education sector through knowledge governance theory

**著者**: Xie Xiaojuan, Muhammad Azfar anwar, Aljawharah Fahad Aljubilah, Ebtesam Abdullah Alzeiby
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 組織科学

## Abstract

Purpose This study aims to investigate how generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) knowledge workflow capabilities influence community knowledge governance (CKG) and community knowledge sharing (CKS) within higher education communities, through the mediating mechanisms of knowledge digitization and knowledge legitimacy. Design/methodology/approach Drawing on a quantitative cross-sectional survey design, data were collected from 357 faculty members, postgraduate researchers and academic staff operating within higher education institutions actively integrating GenAI tools into their academic knowledge workflows. WarpPLS structural equation modeling was used for model analysis, enabling simultaneous assessment of direct and indirect effects across the proposed framework. Scales were adapted from validated instruments in prior knowledge management, AI capability and academic behavior research, ensuring reliability and construct validity across all measured variables. Findings Results confirm that GenAI knowledge workflow capability positively and significantly influences both CKG and CKS in higher education settings. Furthermore, knowledge digitization and knowledge legitimacy each partially mediate these relationships, amplifying the contribution of GenAI capabilities to community knowledge outcomes. Originality/value This research makes a novel theoretical contribution by integrating knowledge governance theory, the knowledge-based view and legitimacy theory into a unified framework that explains how GenAI capabilities generate community-level knowledge performance outcomes in academic settings. Practically, the findings offer actionable guidance to university administrators, academic community managers, learning technology designers and higher education policymakers on leveraging AI-driven tools to build effective, legitimate and well-governed digital knowledge ecosystems within higher education communities.
