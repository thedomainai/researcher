# HUMAN–AI SYSTEMS DESIGN

**著者**: Roman Shaban
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 組織科学

## Abstract

The increasing integration of generative artificial intelligence (AI) into professional, educational, scientific, and organizational environments requires a transition from isolated tool use toward systematically designed human–AI systems. Although contemporary AI technologies can support information processing, reasoning, content generation, and decision-making, their practical effectiveness depends on how human and artificial capabilities are organized into coherent structures of joint activity. This paper introduces Human–AI Systems Design (HAISD) as a conceptual and methodological approach to developing, coordinating, and evaluating integrated systems of human–artificial intelligence collaboration. The approach considers human–AI interaction not merely as a sequence of prompts and responses, but as an organized system of complementary cognitive functions, distributed responsibilities, information exchanges, decision rights, feedback mechanisms, and adaptive control processes. Building upon research in human–AI collaboration, automation, human-centered artificial intelligence, and collective intelligence, the paper proposes a seven-layer architecture for designing human–AI systems. The architecture incorporates goal specification, capability assessment, cognitive task allocation, interaction coordination, epistemic verification, governance, and adaptive development. Five operational models of joint activity are distinguished: AI-assisted execution, complementary cognitive collaboration, human-led AI delegation, adversarial cognitive collaboration, and adaptive human–AI orchestration. A six-stage design methodology is developed to guide implementation across professional and research contexts. The paper further proposes criteria for assessing system performance, including task effectiveness, coordination efficiency, decision quality, human autonomy, reliability, and long-term competence preservation. The central argument is that the effectiveness of human–AI collaboration depends not only on the capabilities of individual participants but also on the architecture of their interaction. Consequently, Human–AI Systems Design is presented as an emerging interdisciplinary problem involving cognitive science, psychology, human-computer interaction, systems engineering, organizational research, and AI governance
