# Open Innovation and the management bias: The critique towards the management perspective

**著者**: Tiago Brandão
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: アントレプレナーシップ

## Abstract

Since its formalisation in 2003, open innovation has become one of the most influential paradigms in innovation studies and management discourse. The concept has been widely adopted across academic, industrial and policy domains and is often presented as a novel and inclusive response to the challenges of contemporary knowledge production. Using a historiographical and discourse-analytical approach, this article shows how the discourse of innovation and openness has been shaped by a persistent managerial bias. Rather than assessing empirical applications, the paper critically examines how open innovation is still tied to traditional models of innovation, while reinforcing firm-centred, market-driven and managerial logics. It analyzes the appropriation of ‘openness’ in different domains—from high-tech industries to EU policy frameworks—and highlights the performative role of management literatures in legitimising entrepreneurial and organisational change. In doing so, the study problematises the ideological assumptions embedded in open innovation narratives and questions their normative neutrality. The article concludes with implications for innovation scholars, policymakers and practitioners, arguing for more pluralistic and historically grounded approaches to innovation beyond the managerial mainstream.
