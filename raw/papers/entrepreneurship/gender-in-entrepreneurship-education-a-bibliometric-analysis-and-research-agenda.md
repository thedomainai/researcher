# Gender in entrepreneurship education: a bibliometric analysis and research agenda

**著者**: Andika Andika, Sri Palupi Prabandari
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: アントレプレナーシップ

## Abstract

Purpose This study maps the intellectual structure and thematic evolution of research on gender in entrepreneurship education (EE) and examines how existing studies report and explain gender differences in EE effectiveness, identifying implications for programme design and future research. Design/methodology/approach A bibliometric analysis of 590 Scopus-indexed documents (2005–2026) was conducted using VOSviewer and Biblioshiny to examine keyword co-occurrence, citation networks and thematic evolution. Additionally, 20 highly cited studies reporting gender differences in EE effects were coded according to the direction of these differences and the explanations provided. A broader dataset of 6,030 EE publications served as a comparative baseline. Findings The literature predominantly focuses on entrepreneurial intention and self-efficacy, while structural and contextual perspectives remain less developed. Among the 20 studies, EE effects were larger for women in seven, larger for men in six, no significant gender differences were found in two and mixed results were reported in five. Nine studies provided no explanation, while five offered individual-level explanations, five contextual explanations and only one integrated both perspectives. Digital and AI-related research also remains limited, representing approximately half the relative share of the broader EE literature. Originality/value This study reveals a disconnect between established individual and structural perspectives and their limited integration in explaining gender differences in EE effectiveness. It highlights the need for gender-disaggregated evaluations and theoretically grounded explanations of how and for whom EE works.
