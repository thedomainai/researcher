# Memories of the Future: Cyborgs, Hypomnēsis, and Cerebral Events Beyond the Brain

**著者**: Universidad Autónoma de Madrid, Alexander Hope
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 脳科学

## Abstract

This article takes up Catherine Malabou’s concept of ‘cerebrality’ in The New Wounded: traumatic cerebral events whose senselessness cannot be incorporated into psychic life, but which the subject nonetheless survives. While Malabou emphasises the materiality of such events and their capacity to alter the psyche, I argue that her account remains too focused on the brain in isolation and insufficiently attentive to originary technicity. Using Anne McCaffrey’s Brainship stories and contemporary discussions of brain-computer interfaces (BCIs), this article explores how embodiment and technologically extended memory complicates the relation between cerebrality and hypomnēsis. Specifically, the self-inflicted destruction of a cyborg brainship’s memory banks in McCaffrey and Ball’s Partnership provides a way to rethink how neuronal plasticity, technics and subjectivity fit together as a complex assemblage. In this analysis, damage to technologically integrated forms of extended cognition can constitute a form of destructive plasticity capable of fundamentally altering the course of psychic life.
