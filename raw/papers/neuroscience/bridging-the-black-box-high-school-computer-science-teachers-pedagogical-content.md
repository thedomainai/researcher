# Bridging the “Black Box”: High School Computer Science Teachers’ Pedagogical Content Knowledge and Self-Efficacy in Foundational Data Science and Artificial Intelligence

**著者**: Jacqueline Corricelli
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 脳科学

## Abstract

As Artificial Intelligence (AI) expands to secondary computer science (CS), CS educators need to change our approach to CS pedagogy from deterministic, traceable algorithms toward understanding "black boxes" or probabilistic analysis. This requires that we modify CS curricula to refocus to include conceptual abstraction, data critique, and ethics. However, a disconnect currently exists between teachers' perceived requirements and the actual skills needed to effectively teach AI. To explore this gap, this study draws on survey data from high school CS educators (N = 55), investigating the relationships between Pedagogical Content Knowledge (PCK-A), Teacher Self-Efficacy (AI-TSE), and beliefs about rigor in AI instruction.Descriptive findings show variation in pedagogical content knowledge (67.3% to 98.2% accuracy), along with a ‘mathematical dishonesty’ paradox. While 69.1% (n = 38) of educators do not believe advanced math is required for students to understand AI, 41.8% (n = 23) report feeling 'dishonest as a teacher' when teaching algorithms without it. Exploratory regression modeling indicated that instructional confidence trended positively with PCK-A (B = 0.101, p = 0.080), while beliefs about math rigor showed no predictive association (p = 0.357; model R2 = 0.062, p = 0.189). Confidence is more related to that teacher’s strategies for abstraction, rather than mastery of advanced math. Educators reported their lowest confidence evaluating student projects based on model choice rather than code complexity (M = 2.75, SD = 1.31). Based on these findings, educators are categorized into a 2x2 Readiness Grid. This paper seeks to bridge the "black box" of AI education, suggesting a 4-pillar professional learning model. Moreover, a qualitative phase is currently underway to explore the lived experiences of CS educators as we navigate this pedagogical shift.
