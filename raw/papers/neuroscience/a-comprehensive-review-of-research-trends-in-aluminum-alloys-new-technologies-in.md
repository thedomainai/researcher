# A Comprehensive Review of Research Trends in Aluminum Alloys: New Technologies in Microstructure Investigation and Mechanical Properties Improvement

**著者**: V. Yu. Bazhin, N. A. Gordevnin, S. S. Demidov
**年**: 2027 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 脳科学

## Abstract

Aluminum alloys are widely used in aerospace, automotive, and electronics due to their light weight, strength, and formability. Recent advances in electron microscopy and numerical simulation have deepened the understanding of how microstructure governs tensile strength. This study combines a bibliometric mapping of the literature with a metallurgical interpretation of the field: an initial 3,831 records (1990-2025) retrieved from Scopus were screened to 3,101 and refined to a final dataset of 2,916 documents analyzed with R-Studio (Bibliometrix) and VOSviewer. The most frequent keywords are aluminum alloys, microstructure, and tensile strength, and they are shown to map directly onto the core strengthening mechanisms of precipitation hardening, grain refinement, and recrystallization control. The average citation per article is 20.35 and the average number of authors per article is 4.67, indicating strong research collaboration. Although the annual growth rate of articles is -4.99%, research quality has increased significantly through advanced imaging such as scanning and transmission electron microscopy (SEM, TEM) and atom-probe tomography. Particular attention is given to coherent L12-ordered Al3RE precipitates (Al3Sc, Al3Er, Al3Yb), whose lattice misfit with the aluminum matrix controls strengthening, creep, and recrystallization resistance, with ytterbium highlighted as a low-cost alternative to scandium. International collaborations account for 16.44% of published articles. The findings confirm that coupling experimental and computational methods is essential for developing aluminum alloys with optimized mechanical properties.
