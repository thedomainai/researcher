# Universal Living-System Consent, Personhood, Identity, Restitution & Recoverability: Terminal Mechanism and Architecture Closure

**著者**: Interval Studio, Noelia Sofia Sanchez
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 脳科学

## Abstract

This publication develops a universal living-system architecture for protecting personhood, consent, identity, evidence, responsibility, recoverability, and future possibility across materially consequential transitions. The framework treats the human person as a multidimensional living continuity system spanning physical and biological state, brain and nervous-system activity, cognition, consciousness-associated states, identity, agency, emotion, relationships, family continuity, information, digital representations, derived models, economic dependencies, technological interfaces, infrastructure, and successor states. Consent is modeled as a living, time-indexed and purpose-bounded state rather than a permanent binary authorization. Observation, inference, prediction, intervention, experimentation, commercialization, model training, transfer, automation, publication, physical actuation, and successor use remain distinguishable transitions. Permission for one does not silently authorize another. A major extension addresses identity under technological integration. Technologies introduced into, attached to, or functionally coupled with the human body may alter embodiment, body schema, agency, cognition, emotion, capability, dependency, social relationships, autobiographical continuity, and experienced identity. The framework therefore distinguishes identity transformation from identity loss and rejects any inference that technological integration transfers ownership, authority, authorship, personhood, or retrospective consent to a device manufacturer, operator, institution, model, or successor. The architecture introduces interval-based governance spanning baseline state, disclosure, comprehension, consent or lawful authority, intervention, adaptation, monitoring, updates, drift, adverse events, challenge, correction, removal, substitution, recovery, identity integration, succession, and decommissioning. Rights and safeguards are evaluated against time-to-irreversibility so that nominal remedies arriving after recovery paths have closed are not treated as equivalent to effective protection. The publication integrates bioethics, human-subject research, neuroethics, medicine, psychology, neuroscience, prosthetics, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, digital identity, genomics, family systems, child rights, economics, infrastructure, control theory, safety engineering, evidence science, law, information governance, quantum-applicable technologies, future-generation ethics, and Recoverability-Constrained Systems. It develops cross-domain mechanisms including consent integrity vectors, consent-drift detection, identity-delta ledgers, person-representation firewalls, embodied-technology boundary maps, co-agency attribution graphs, derivative-persistence graphs, provenance and benefit-lineage structures, independent reconstruction reserves, successor-obligation capsules, evidence/privacy dual custody, recovery-latency budgets, unknown-path interlocks, identity-safe update gates, and recursively governed safeguard architectures. The framework is adversarially closed against consent laundering, purpose laundering, authority laundering, data and inference laundering, identity substitution, evidence capture, responsibility fragmentation, time laundering, jurisdiction shopping, emergency ratchets, model-authority laundering, audit theater, vendor dependency capture, synthetic-derivative laundering, update laundering, recovery theater, quantum prestige laundering, and successor-obligation erasure. Information closure requires preservation of provenance, semantic state, inference boundaries, uncertainty, transformations, recipients, derivatives, model lineage, evidence state, and correction paths. Technology closure requires materially equivalent safeguards regardless of whether an implementation is biological, digital, analog, software, hardware, firmware, cloud, edge, distributed, autonomous, robotic, neurotechnological, cyber-physical, quantum where scientifically applicable, hybrid, or future-developed. The framework is self-recursive: validators, auditors, models, consent mechanisms, recovery systems, exceptions, standards, regulators, and the framework itself remain subject to provenance, evidence, authority, challenge, correction, succession, and recoverability requirements. Its terminal rule is generative rather than dependent on a finite list of technologies. Any newly distinguishable actor, state, relation, mechanism, dependency, technology, representation, intervention, successor, harm, or recovery path must enter the same operational kernel whenever its separation could materially alter truth, identity, consent, agency, authority, safety, evidence, responsibility, causation, dependency, remedy, continuity, recoverability, or future possibility. The formal reduction terminates at distinction: PERSONHOOD → IDENTITY → AGENCY → CONSENT → AUTHORITY → ACTION → EFFECT → SUCCESSOR STATE → EVIDENCE → RESPONSIBILITY → REMEDY → RECOVERABILITY → POSSIBILITY → CONTINUATION → RELATION → DISTINCTION. Local silence is reached only when no additional presently distinguishable factor changes the protected decision. New evidence, technologies, actors, dependencies, consequences, or scientific understanding automatically reopen the architecture. The publication deliberately separates documented evidence, testimony, inference, models, hypotheses, beliefs, disputed propositions, and unknown states. It does not establish the truth of any particular alleged undisclosed intervention, does not treat spiritual or metaphysical concepts as experimentally demonstrated mechanisms, and does not use quantum terminology as evidence without a defined, measurable and falsifiable quantum mechanism.
