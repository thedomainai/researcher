# Emotional abuse, emotional neglect, and psychological wellbeing among Chinese college students: the mediating role of self-compassion and the moderating role of social support

**著者**: Lanlan Yang, Kangling Ye, Yan Qin, Liang Huang, Xiaoqing Lin
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 脳科学

## Abstract

Emotional neglect and emotional abuse are two common forms of adverse childhood experiences. To examine their associations with university students' psychological wellbeing, the indirect association through self-compassion, and the moderating role of social support, the present study conducted a cross-sectional survey using convenience sampling. A total of 705 undergraduate students from six universities in Fujian Province, China, completed the Short Form of the Childhood Trauma Questionnaire (CTQ-SF), the Self-Compassion Scale (SCS), the Perceived Social Support Scale (PSSS), and the Scales of Psychological Well-Being (SPWB). Results showed that: (1) both emotional neglect and emotional abuse were significantly negatively associated with university students' psychological wellbeing; (2) self-compassion showed a significant indirect association in the relation of both forms of maltreatment to psychological wellbeing; when the other form of maltreatment was simultaneously included, the indirect associations via self-compassion remained significant for both, whereas the direct association for emotional abuse was not significant; (3) the direction of the moderating effect of social support was opposite to that predicted by the stress-buffering hypothesis, and the individual interaction terms were not robust when both forms of maltreatment were entered simultaneously; the moderating effect may primarily operate on the arithmetic average of the standardized neglect and abuse scores ( S ), rather than on the difference between them ( D ). Given the cross-sectional design and convenience sampling, these findings do not support causal interpretation. Future studies could adopt longitudinal or experimental designs to examine whether interventions designed to enhance self-compassion can improve psychological wellbeing among university students.
