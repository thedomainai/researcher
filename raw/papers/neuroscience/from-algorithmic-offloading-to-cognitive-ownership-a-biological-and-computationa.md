# From Algorithmic Offloading to Cognitive Ownership: A Biological and Computational Framework for Student-AI Interaction in Mathematics Education

**著者**: Katharine Clemmer, Lily Lawrence
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 脳科学

## Abstract

Mathematical cognition is an active process in which students build and revise mental models, not simply absorb procedures. As Artificial Intelligence (AI) becomes ubiquitous in K-16 education, it frequently supplies instant procedural steps. This triggers algorithmic offloading, bypassing the hypothesis generation and error detection essential for durable learning, which ultimately risks the dimensional collapse of neural maps. Despite this, the field lacks a systematic, student-facing protocol to govern these interactions. This paper argues that when AI is structurally reconfigured from an answer-generator into a "constraint engine," it allows students to self-engineer the neuro-cognitive frameworks required for mathematical reasoning. We propose a five-component interaction architecture that empowers learners to: (1) constrain AI from answering prematurely, (2) engineer high-diagnosticity error signals to pierce the neural noise floor, (3) scale practice deliberately, (4) enforce mandatory systems consolidation intervals, and (5) isolate overlapping mathematical structures through contrastive discrimination. Crucially, this framework dismantles the educational 'accommodation fallacy': the reliance on external modifications that view cognitive variance through a deficit lens, rather than redesigning core curriculum tasks to support a student's inherent neurosignature. By embedding strategic working memory offloads and strict strategy boundaries directly into the interaction loop, the protocol actively leverages a student’s unique neurosignature to stabilize distinct topological deviations. Grounded in predictive coding and Cognitive Load Theory, this architecture establishes the baseline biological requirement for optimizing any human brain.
