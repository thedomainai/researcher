# EthicaL Issues Arising from Neurotechnology

**著者**: Hongkun Chen
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 脳科学

## Abstract

Rapid advances in neuroscience are expanding the capacity to measure, interpret and modulate neural activity. Brain–computer interfaces, neuroimaging, neuromodulation and AI-based brain-data analysis may improve treatment, communication support and scientific understanding. However, their growing use also creates ethical challenges that cannot be resolved by technical safety alone. This article examines the ethical implications of neurotechnology through privacy, justice and inequality because it is motivated by the concern that public awareness, practical safeguards and enforceable governance have not kept pace with innovation. Based on a qualitative review and normative analysis of recent neuroethics literature, the article considers the sensitivity of brain data, the need for transparent and fair decision-making, and the risk that unequal access and biased algorithms may create new forms of disadvantage. The analysis suggests that current approaches often focus on safety and informed consent, while offering limited practical guidance on secondary data use, institutional decision-making and remedies for harm. The article argues that governance should be anticipatory, rights-based and participatory, combining meaningful consent, independent oversight, inclusive research, bias assessment and fair access. These measures are essential if neurotechnology is to advance human well-being rather than deepen exclusion as it moves into wider clinical, commercial and social settings.
