# S-AI-WorldModel: Regulated Canonicalization of the World Model

**著者**: Said Slaoui
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 脳科学

## Abstract

S-AI-WorldModel introduces a formally structured approach to world modeling within the Sparse Artificial Intelligence (S-AI) paradigm. The article addresses a central limitation of contemporary AI systems: although large language models, reinforcement learning systems, latent world models, and neuro-symbolic architectures achieve strong empirical performance, they generally do not maintain a unified world representation that is simultaneously explicit, dynamically stabilized, causally interpretable, formally certifiable, and parsimonious. The proposed architecture represents the world through a canonical cognitive structure composed of entities, relations, predicates, events, causal dependencies, goals, temporal organization, and working memory. This representation is progressively refined through a regulated recursive process designed to reduce ambiguity, inconsistency, and unnecessary complexity until a stable canonical representation can be committed. S-AI-WorldModel combines several mechanisms from the broader S-AI corpus: recursive canonicalization, hormonal metacognitive regulation, stability analysis, formal certification, explicit stopping conditions, and cognitive parsimony. Its hormonal subsystem extends the previously introduced S-AI regulatory field with three world-model-specific hormones dedicated to causal coherence, temporal ordering, and contextual anchoring. The article distinguishes the stability of the hormonal regulation mechanism from the convergence of the world-model dynamics itself and formulates separate conditions for each. It also introduces a dedicated world-model stability criterion integrating convergence toward a canonical representation, reduction of representational entropy, improvement of coherence, and control of representational complexity. The resulting framework is organized as a multi-layer cognitive architecture with specialized agents responsible for perception integration, predicate construction, causal analysis, temporal organization, memory management, verification, and metacognitive regulation. The overall objective is not merely to predict future states, but to determine when an evolving representation of the world has become sufficiently stable, coherent, certifiable, and parsimonious to serve as a reliable cognitive substrate for reasoning, planning, and action.
