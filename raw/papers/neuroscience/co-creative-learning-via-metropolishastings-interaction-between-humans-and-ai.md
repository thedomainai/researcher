# Co-creative learning via Metropolis–Hastings interaction between humans and AI

**著者**: Ryota Okumura, Tadahiro Taniguchi, Akira Taniguchi, Yoshinobu Hagiwara
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 3
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 脳科学

## Abstract

We propose co-creative learning as a novel paradigm where biological and artificial agents (e.g. humans and robots) mutually integrate their partial perceptual information and knowledge to construct shared external representations, a process we interpret as symbol emergence. Unlike traditional AI teaching based on unilateral knowledge transfer, this addresses the challenge of integrating information from inherently different modalities. We empirically test this framework using a Human–AI interaction model based on the Metropolis-Hastings naming game (MHNG), a decentralized Bayesian inference mechanism. In an online experiment, 69 participants played a joint attention naming game (JA-NG) with one of three computer agent types (MH-based, always-accept, or always-reject) under partial observability. Results show that Human–AI pairs improved their categorization accuracy through interaction, and that an MH-based agent converged on a shared sign system aligned with the two agents' joint observations significantly more closely than an agent learning from its own observations alone, a process underpinned by the collaborative minimization of their collective free energy. No statistically significant difference between the MH and Always Accept conditions was detected after Holm correction. Unlike unconditional acceptance under Always Accept, however, the MH-based agent uses an evidence-sensitive acceptance rule based on its local generative model. Furthermore, human acceptance behavior aligned closely with the MH-derived acceptance probability. These findings provide the first empirical evidence for co-creative learning emerging in Human–AI dyads via MHNG-based interaction. This suggests a promising path toward symbiotic AI systems that learn with humans, rather than from them, by dynamically aligning perceptual experiences, opening a new venue for symbiotic and organic AI alignment.
