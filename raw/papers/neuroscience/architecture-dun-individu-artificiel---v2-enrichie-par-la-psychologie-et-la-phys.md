# Architecture d'un individu artificiel - v2 enrichie par la psychologie et la physiologie humaines.

**著者**: Josselin Orta
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 脳科学

## Abstract

This document describes, building block by building block, how one might design an "artificial individual": a machine that does more than answer questions, with a body, needs, emotions, memories, and a personality that develops over time. The architecture draws on current knowledge from human neuroscience, psychology, and physiology.What it coversThe document starts from an initial schema of 37 building blocks and examines it against research in neuroscience, psychology, and physiology. The resulting architecture contains 51 building blocks covering functions such as perception, bodily regulation, emotion, memory, decision-making, learning, sleep, and self-modeling.For each building block, the document describes what scientific research currently supports, the level of confidence in that knowledge, and how the underlying principle might be translated into a computational or robotic system.An appendix proposes an implementation plan consisting of 62 software modules, including their relationships, approximate operating timescales, and possible testing approaches.What it can be used for• Design: a starting map for researchers and engineers working on autonomous agents, embodied AI, or robotics.• Understanding: a structured overview of major functions performed by the human brain and body, decomposed into functional components for learning, teaching, or discussion.• Debate: an identification of potentially sensitive components, including pain, isolation, attachment, and other mechanisms that may raise ethical questions if implemented in artificial systems.• Sharing: the document is publicly timestamped and released for broad reuse. Its publication also creates a public record of the concepts and architectural elements described here, which may be relevant as prior art in future intellectual-property assessments.This is not a working system. It is an architectural and conceptual design proposal that has not yet been experimentally validated or peer reviewed.
