# Cognitive Science in Prompt Engineering: A Human-Centered Framework for Effective Interaction with Large Language Models

**著者**: Roman Shaban
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 脳科学

## Abstract

Prompt engineering has emerged as an important method for shaping the behavior and outputs of large language models (LLMs). However, conventional approaches frequently emphasize linguistic instructions, technical configurations, and model performance while paying comparatively limited attention to the cognitive processes through which humans formulate objectives, communicate intentions, evaluate information, and regulate their reliance on artificial intelligence. This paper examines prompt engineering from the perspective of cognitive science, integrating theoretical insights from working memory research, cognitive load theory, metacognition, cognitive flexibility, cognitive offloading, distributed cognition, and human–computer interaction. The central argument is that effective prompt engineering should be conceptualized not merely as the optimization of textual instructions but as the design and regulation of a human–AI cognitive interaction system. Within this system, prompts function as external representations of human goals, assumptions, constraints, and evaluation criteria. Their quality depends both on how appropriately they guide model behavior and on how effectively they support human understanding, critical judgment, and intellectual autonomy. The paper proposes a Cognitive–Metacognitive Prompt Engineering Framework (CMPEF), integrating six stages: cognitive orientation, task representation, strategic delegation, output evaluation, adaptive refinement, and cognitive integration. It additionally introduces a distinction between immediate AI-assisted task performance and the preservation or development of independent human competence. The proposed framework generates testable research hypotheses concerning cognitive load, metacognitive calibration, prompt quality, cognitive flexibility, and learning transfer. Practical applications are considered in education, scientific research, professional decision-making, and AI assisted knowledge work. The paper concludes that cognitive science provides a foundation for transforming prompt engineering from an instruction-centered practice into a human-centered discipline concerned with the quality, sustainability, and epistemic reliability of human–AI collaboration
