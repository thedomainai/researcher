# Physical AI Bridging the Digital Mind and the Material World

**著者**: A. Shaji George
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 脳科学

## Abstract

Physical AI is a pivotal moment in the interaction of machines with the physical world. It refers to a family of AI systems that interact with the physical world by integrating high-level models with sensors, actuators and control systems to perceive, reason, decide and act in the physical world. Physical AI, unlike generative or digital-only AI, that creates text, code or images within the confines of software, exists where what matters is measurable in motion, force and matter. This article discusses the field in six dimensions historical background in robotics and embodied cognition, underlying architecture of perception, planning and action, industries being reshaped, barriers that continue to limit the field, and engineering and policy reactions that are beginning to take shape, and paths that are likely to shape the next decade. The analysis is based on the latest research in the industry, market forecasts and peer-reviewed studies to ensure a balance between caution and enthusiasm. Results indicate that as Physical AI is making strides in sectors such as transportation, manufacturing, healthcare, and retail, there are still hurdles, including challenges in simulation to reality, energy consumption, ethical considerations, and workforce adaptation. The article ends by stating that there is a need for organizations and policy makers to get ready for a future where intelligent machines are moving, manipulating and working in collaboration with humans in all the areas of economic life.
