# The Software Development Life Cycle in the Age of Artificial Intelligence

**著者**: sohaila mohamed
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 脳科学

## Abstract

This preprint examines two linked questions about how software gets built: why the earliest phases of the Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) — analysis and requirements — decide so much of a project's outcome, and how modern artificial intelligence is changing the way each phase is carried out. The first half draws on the economics of defect escalation and on decades of project-failure research to argue that rigorous software analysis is still the strongest lever on whether a project succeeds: problems caught while they are still text cost a fraction of what they cost once they reach production, and the factors most often behind failed projects are analytical ones, chiefly unclear and changing requirements. The second half walks the lifecycle phase by phase — planning, requirements, design, implementation, testing, deployment, and operations — and reviews what AI actually does in each, from requirements refinement and architecture recommendation to code generation, self-healing tests, adaptive CI/CD pipelines, and predictive operations (AIOps). It closes on the emerging shift toward agentic, specification-driven development and the governance practices that keep that acceleration from compounding risk instead of quality. The paper includes supporting figures and tables covering defect-cost escalation, project-outcome and failure-factor data, and AI-adoption indicators, along with a full reference list. This is an unpublished preprint, made openly available for reading and citation.
