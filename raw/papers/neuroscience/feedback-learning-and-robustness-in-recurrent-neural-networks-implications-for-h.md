# Feedback, Learning, and Robustness in Recurrent Neural Networks: Implications for Human and Artificial Intelligence

**著者**: James S. Magnuson
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 脳科学

## Abstract

This project investigates the computational mechanisms of top-down and predictive processing in spoken word recognition, asking what kind of feedback a recurrent architecture needs in order to reproduce the behavioral and neural signatures commonly attributed to prediction. Interactive-activation models, predictive-coding models, and recurrent neural networks all rely on feedback, but they differ in what is fed back and in whether prediction error is explicitly represented. To separate these factors, the project trains and compares a series of recurrent models that vary in two controlled respects, where feedback originates and how internal state is updated, on a common incremental recognition task with localist phoneme input, a 1,000-word lexicon, and joint lexical and phonemic targets. Aim 1 contrasts Elman and Jordan simple recurrent networks, varying feedback locus and backpropagation depth. Aim 2 tests gated (LSTM) networks with and without output-level feedback. Aim 3 compares an explicit recurrent predictive-coding model against the interactive-activation model TRACE. Models are evaluated against behavioral, neural, and information-theoretic benchmarks from human studies, with output compared quantitatively to archival human data; simulation and analysis plans will be preregistered. Broader impacts include technical training for students, and development of classroom-ready lessons on AI for elementary and middle school students. All model code, simulation and analysis pipelines, and derived results will be released openly. The findings constrain which architectures support predictive processing in perception and inform the design of more interpretable AI.
