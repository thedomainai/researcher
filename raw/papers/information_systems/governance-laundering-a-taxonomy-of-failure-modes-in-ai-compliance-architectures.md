# Governance Laundering: A Taxonomy of Failure Modes in AI Compliance Architectures

**著者**: Edward Meyman
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 3
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 情報システム

## Abstract

AI governance tools are often purchased as if they provide authorization-layer assurance, yet many produce governance artifacts (logs, dashboards, attestations, certificates) that cannot support independent verification of a specific AI decision. This paper names the resulting condition governance laundering: a governance claim whose assurance exceeds the evidence or enforcement properties supporting it. Policy theater and six related failure-mode families describe its structural forms. The paper defines seven evidence-grade governance requirements (R1 through R7), classifies each failure-mode family by the requirement it violates, and formalizes the Expanded Anti-Laundering Protocol (EALP): seven diagnostic tests, extending the Anti-Laundering Tests of A Taxonomy of AI Governance Approaches: Distinguishing Visibility, Alignment, and Authorization (Zenodo DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.18275969), with explicit procedures, pass criteria, and graded outcomes. The paper contributes: A definitional boundary between governance artifacts (bare activity records) and governance evidence sufficient for independent verdict reconstruction A seven-family structural taxonomy of failure modes, from policy theater to semantic drift The EALP: seven tests covering export, offline replay, state completeness, fail-closed enforcement, mutation and drift, pre-execution dependency, and custody of the authorized action A three-grade outcome model (Evidence-Grade, Monitoring-Grade, Artifact-Only), with a withheld-grade rule for incomplete evaluations and stated limits on what each grade establishes Supplemental assessments, recorded separately from the grade, for path coverage (non-bypassability), authority and delegation, and input provenance An a priori susceptibility map across eight categories of governance products, with implications for procurement, audit, and regulatory evaluation Scope. The framework is vendor-neutral and architecture-focused. It evaluates claims of per-decision authorization assurance; it does not propose a governance architecture or treat organizational controls as deficient for serving a different function. Susceptibility, an observed test failure, and a governance-laundering finding are distinct: a laundering finding requires an identifiable representation whose claimed assurance materially exceeds what was established for the same scope.
