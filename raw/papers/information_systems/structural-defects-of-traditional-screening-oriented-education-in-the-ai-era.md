# Structural Defects of Traditional Screening Oriented Education in the AI Era

**著者**: YI LIANG
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 情報システム

## Abstract

Before the advent of intelligent tools, human civilization established an educational‑selection mechanism centered on knowledge memorization, manual calculation and standardized assessment. This mechanism has long prioritized individuals with Type‑A thinking who excel at memorization, logical deduction and standardized computation, while continuously squeezing and marginalizing people with Type‑B creative thinking who are good at intuitive association, paradigm‑breaking innovation and cross‑dimensional holistic conceptualization. Traditional tracking‑based schooling and diploma‑based recruitment criteria of enterprises form a dual‑selection closed loop, restraining the upper limit of human‑oriented innovation. The popularization of generative AI should in theory unlock the potential of creative talents. Nevertheless, unregulated over‑use of electronic devices and direct copying of AI‑generated homework answers are spreading globally among primary and secondary‑school students. Multiple countries including France and Norway have enacted laws and regulations to impose graded restrictions on minors’ AI usage. These regulatory practices confirm that unrestricted access to AI carries risks of irreversible degradation of independent logical reasoning ability among young populations. Cognitive‑science research has long distinguished convergent‑versus‑divergent thinking and reproductive‑versus‑productive thinking. Nevertheless, existing theories generally lack a continuous‑gradient model and have not been systematically integrated with educational‑selection mechanisms. Built upon established cognitive theories, this paper argues that Type‑A and Type‑B thinking are not dichotomous, mutually‑exclusive categories, but rather form a continuous‑spectrum cognitive spectrum. Purely Type‑A or purely Type‑B individuals do not exist. Multiple typical cognitive profiles can be defined according to the relative strengths of the two cognitive dispositions. Based on this continuous‑spectrum model, this paper constructs a practical implementation framework featuring differentiated AI governance across educational stages and reformed teaching‑assessment systems. Generative‑AI usage shall be rigidly restricted in primary and secondary education to consolidate foundational skills of independent computation and scientific reasoning. Senior high‑school education shall de‑emphasize score‑only evaluation and add practical‑innovation courses and diversified creative assessment. In higher education, AI shall be fully adopted as a research‑and‑engineering assistant, while purely paper‑based assignments shall be phased out, with deliverable practical projects serving as core assessment carriers. This hierarchical governance framework seeks to safeguard foundational independent‑thinking training in basic education and meet the demands of human‑AI collaborative innovation in higher‑education, so as to mitigate two major educational dilemmas: the marginalization of innovative talents under examination‑oriented systems and cognitive decline caused by under‑regulated AI exposure among young learners.
