# Embodied AI Agents in Urban Healthcare Systems: An Organisational Transformation Framework and Narrative Synthesis for Sustainable Implementation

**著者**: Ali Asadollahi
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 情報システム

## Abstract

Background As cities worldwide face escalating demographic pressures, urban healthcare ecosystems are increasingly transitioning from disembodied algorithmic prediction toward embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) agents—ranging from autonomous diagnostic devices and clinical service robots to conversational avatars and ambient urban sensing systems. Despite technical accuracy, municipal and hospital-based deployments frequently fail to translate into sustained operational value, stalling at pilot stages or encountering professional resistance. Conventional implementation paradigms rooted in narrow 'adoption' models obscure the complex sociotechnical reconfiguration required for lasting integration across urban clinical workflows. Objective To synthesize evidence across implementation science, urban sociotechnical systems, dynamic capabilities, organisational learning, and embodied AI governance into a coherent framework explaining how physically and digitally embodied AI transitions from standalone pilots into enduring institutional capabilities. Methods We conducted a structured narrative synthesis adhering to the SANRA (Scale for the Assessment of Narrative Review Articles) guidelines. The review triangulates five canonical foundation works, eight real-world implementation and external-validation cases of clinical and autonomous AI, and four benchmark regulatory/governance frameworks (including WHO, NIST AI RMF, and the EU AI Act). Deductive coding against established implementation models (CFIR and NASSS) was combined with inductive thematic extraction of operational governance, spatial-workflow dynamics, and institutional learning. Results Sustainable implementation is governed by five interdependent transformation domains: (1) Strategic Alignment, (2) Workflow & Spatial Embedding, (3) Governance & Lifecycle Accountability, (4) Learning & Adaptive Redesign, and (5) Value Realisation & Institutional Normalisation. We operationalise institutional capability across six measurable dimensions—Governance Maturity, Workflow & Physical Integration, Organisational Learning, Professional Trust & Human Agency, Continuous Monitoring & Drift Feedback, and Strategic Adaptation—and structure these into a Capability Transformation Ladder. Six inductive propositions outline an actionable research agenda for urban health strategists, clinical directors, robotics engineers, and municipal policymakers. Conclusions Moving beyond technology adoption toward organizational and spatial transformation reorients urban health leaders to the structural conditions under which embodied AI succeeds. Rather than treating robotic and algorithmic agents as plug-and-play software procurements, sustainable integration requires continuous capability building, explicit accountability architectures, and recursive sociotechnical adaptation.
