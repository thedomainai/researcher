# Technical Superiority Without Institutional Readiness: The War of the Currents as a Historical Precedent for AI Governance

**著者**: Alfredo Merlet
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 2
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 情報システム

## Abstract

This working paper argues that technical superiority is neither a necessary nor a sufficient condition for the diffusion of a new technology, and that the gap between technical capability and institutional readiness — the organizational, financial, and legal infrastructure required to convert an invention into a durable system — is a more reliable predictor of which technologies succeed. The argument is developed through a single historical case: the "War of the Currents" between Nikola Tesla's and George Westinghouse's alternating-current (AC) system and Thomas Edison's direct-current (DC) system, and the later collapse of Tesla's Wardenclyffe wireless-transmission project. Alternating current prevailed commercially despite Edison's better-funded opposition campaign, because Westinghouse supplied the institutional infrastructure — patent consolidation, standardization, capital access, and legal defense — that Tesla's invention alone could not provide. Wardenclyffe failed for the mirror-image reason: Tesla could not translate technical vision into a financing structure that J. P. Morgan and other investors could underwrite. The paper reframes this historical episode as an early instance of the institutional-readiness gap that the author's ongoing research program documents empirically in contemporary artificial-intelligence governance, and situates it explicitly alongside the author's related working papers on adaptation and human-capital readiness. The paper is conceptual and illustrative rather than causal: it draws on a single, well-documented historical case to motivate a framework, not to test it statistically.
