# Who becomes replaceable? Generative AI and the discursive stratification of work

**著者**: Hana Kim
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 人間-AI協働

## Abstract

Debates about artificial intelligence and employment usually treat replaceability as an attribute of technologies, tasks, or occupations. This study instead examines replaceability as a public classification: how news texts select some workers as targets of AI-related change and assign them different occupational futures. The analysis covers 151,957 occupation-centred passages extracted from 446,417 news articles. A human-coded validation set was used to fine-tune a four-way RoBERTa classifier distinguishing neutral, augmentation, task-automation, and occupational-replacement frames. Estimates use cluster-balanced occupation-text units. After the release of ChatGPT, occupational AI-impact framing in news rose by 4.0 percentage points. The conditional increase in replacement-oriented news framing was smaller and was assembled mainly through a shift in attention towards occupations that already carried high replacement-oriented rates. The central result is vertical stratification within occupations. The post-2022 activation gap between junior and senior mentions widened by 20.0 percentage points after controlling for occupation and year. Once activated, junior mentions were structurally more likely to be framed as replacement-oriented (+ 40.4 points) and replacement-only (+ 56.5 points), whereas senior mentions were more often represented as augmented. The pattern survives career-stage cue masking, occupation-by-year fixed effects, alternative treatment of 2022, and separation from routine-task and prestige language. A blinded human replication of 204 matched junior-senior pairs produced the same contrast: 76.4% of activated junior mentions were replacement-oriented, compared with 28.8% of senior mentions, with 113 discordant pairs in the junior-replacement direction and seven in the reverse direction. These findings reframe the labour politics of AI as discursive stratification. Public discourse does not simply forecast technological exposure; it distributes vulnerability, retention, and developmental opportunity across career hierarchies.
