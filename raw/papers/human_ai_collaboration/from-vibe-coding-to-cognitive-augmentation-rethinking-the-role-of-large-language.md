# From Vibe Coding to Cognitive Augmentation: Rethinking the Role of Large Language Models in Software Development and Developer Learning

**著者**: Glorious Babalola
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 人間-AI協働

## Abstract

Overview This publication presents Version 1.0 of an ongoing research project examining the changing relationship between large language models (LLMs), AI-assisted programming, software development, programming education, and human problem-solving. The rapid adoption of generative AI coding tools has introduced a significant shift in how software is learned and produced. Developers and students can increasingly delegate activities such as code generation, debugging, explanation, documentation, and problem decomposition to AI systems. While existing research has identified substantial benefits in areas such as programming productivity, learning support, and reduction of cognitive workload, growing research also raises questions about over-reliance, cognitive offloading, critical thinking, verification, and the development of independent programming ability. This paper begins from that tension. Rather than asking whether AI is simply "good" or "bad" for programmers, the research explores a more fundamental question: Under what conditions can AI-assisted programming improve productivity and learning while preserving and developing the independent problem-solving abilities of software developers and programming learners? Purpose of Version 1.0 This first version is not presented as a completed empirical study. Instead, it establishes the literature and conceptual foundation for a broader research program. The paper reviews and synthesizes existing literature concerning: Large language models and AI-assisted programming Generative AI in programming education Developer productivity and AI coding assistants Cognitive offloading and human–AI interaction Critical thinking and generative AI AI-assisted learning versus AI-assisted production The experiences and practices of developers using AI coding tools Risks associated with excessive dependence on AI-generated solutions Based on this literature, the paper introduces a preliminary conceptual framework for cognitive-preserving AI use in software development and developer learning. Proposed Research Direction The paper proposes that the central challenge is not whether developers should use AI, but how AI should be integrated into the development and learning process. The proposed approach distinguishes between AI use that substitutes for human reasoning and AI use that augments it. The preliminary framework, referred to as the Cognitive-Preserving AI Development Framework (CPADF), emphasizes the following cycle: Think → Attempt → Ask → Evaluate → Implement → Explain → Reproduce The framework is intended to encourage developers and learners to retain meaningful engagement with problem formulation, reasoning, debugging, evaluation, explanation, and independent reproduction, while still benefiting from the productivity and informational capabilities of AI systems. The framework is conceptual at this stage and should therefore be regarded as a research proposition requiring further investigation, rather than as a validated intervention or established methodology. Position Within the Broader Research Project This paper represents the first contribution to an evolving research project. Future stages of the project may investigate the proposed framework through additional literature review, expert evaluation, surveys, interviews, controlled experiments, observational studies, and other empirical methods. Potential areas for future investigation include: The relationship between AI-assisted programming and independent debugging ability. Differences between unrestricted AI use and structured, cognitive-preserving AI use. The effects of AI assistance on programming learning and long-term retention. Differences in AI use between novice, intermediate, and experienced developers. The relationship between confidence in AI-generated code and critical evaluation. The effects of repeated AI dependence on opportunities to practice programming problem-solving. Whether structured AI-use frameworks can preserve learning while maintaining productivity benefits. How these effects may differ across educational and professional contexts. Future versions may incorporate additional evidence, expert feedback, empirical findings, methodological refinements, and collaboration with researchers, educators, and software engineering practitioners. Intended Contribution The objective of this work is not to argue that AI-assisted programming is inherently harmful, nor to reject the use of AI coding tools. Instead, it seeks to contribute to an emerging discussion about human–AI collaboration in software development by proposing a distinction between cognitive substitution and cognitive augmentation. The broader goal is to explore how developers and programming learners can use increasingly capable AI systems without unnecessarily delegating the very cognitive processes that they need to develop and maintain. This work is therefore intended as an open research contribution and starting point for discussion, criticism, replication, and future empirical investigation. Researchers, educators, software engineering practitioners, and other interested contributors are invited to review, challenge, extend, or collaborate on subsequent stages of the research.
