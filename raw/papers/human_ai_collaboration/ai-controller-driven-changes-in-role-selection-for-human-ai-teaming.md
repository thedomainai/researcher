# AI Controller-Driven Changes in Role Selection for Human-AI Teaming

**著者**: Liz Digioia, Yazied Hasan, Robert McDaniels, Torin Adamson, Evan Carter
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 人間-AI協働

## Abstract

Much of the current research on human–AI teams focuses on which role humans vs. AI should undertake. However, the impact a given AI has on the human has not been explored as thoroughly. We investigate (1) how changes in AI controller behavior affect human choice in role, (2) how those choices shift over time, and (3) how those choices affect performance. Our data comes from a mobile game of a navigation task where a teammate with a valuable payload travels through a crowded space while an escorting teammate helps clear the crowd to aid safe progression to a goal. During a six-month daily behavioral study with 83 participants, 55 of whom completed the study, humans played in the role of either the payload or the escort and were paired with an AI agent in the alternate role using a controller, Evasion AI or Goal AI. Nearly half of the players consistently selected roles independent of controller, while the others exhibited readily classifiable behaviors in response to controller. Of those, the dominant behaviors were either to escort Evasion AI and take control away from Goal AI or to fluctuate between roles. These selections often evolved over time, revealing that players migrated between similar behaviors rather than extremes. Additionally, analysis of performance revealed that often choosing the navigation role is associated with degraded performance.
