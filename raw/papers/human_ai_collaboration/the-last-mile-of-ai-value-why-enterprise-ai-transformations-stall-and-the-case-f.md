# THE LAST MILE OF AI VALUE: Why enterprise AI transformations stall, and the case for the AI business value expert

**著者**: T. El Kaissi
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 人間-AI協働

## Abstract

Enterprise adoption of artificial intelligence is now near-universal, yet measurable financial value remains rare: roughly nine in ten organizations use AI, 37 percent report any earnings impact, and 6 percent report material impact. This paper synthesizes large-sample survey evidence from 2024 to 2026 (McKinsey, BCG, IBM, S&P Global, Gartner, Deloitte, Accenture, RAND and others), economic theory on complementary intangible investment, and documented enterprise cases to test two claims: that the binding constraint on AI value is organizational rather than technological, and that a business-anchored "AI business value expert" capability, one that selects problems, translates them into solutions, redesigns workflows and owns the financial result, is the most consistent marker of success. The first claim is strongly supported by convergent evidence, including BCG's finding that roughly 70 percent of implementation challenges arise from people and process and McKinsey's finding that workflow redesign is the strongest differentiator of earnings impact. The paper joins this survey evidence to randomized field experiments showing double-digit task-level productivity gains (14 percent among 5,179 support agents; 12 to 25 percent among 758 consultants), locating the loss of value precisely between task and enterprise, and constructs a harmonized value ladder that reconciles conflicting headline statistics by aligning definitions of success. It proposes a last-mile model grounded in the economics of complementary investment, absorptive capacity and boundary spanning, and states four falsifiable propositions. The second claim is supported but correlational: Chief AI Officer coverage rose from 26 to 76 percent of large organizations in one year, and firms with AI-first leadership designs scale more, but no causal study yet isolates the role. Documented failures (MD Anderson, Zillow Offers) are analyzed alongside vendor-reported successes. The paper concludes the capability is necessary but not sufficient, and a capability rather than a title. A dedicated section applies the findings to the GCC, where adoption (84 percent) has outrun value realization (11 percent) even more sharply than globally, and to Oman, where national policy runs ahead of enterprise practice. A practical, falsifiable operating model closes the paper.
