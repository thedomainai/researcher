# Perspective Taking Shapes Creative Performance in Human–AI Collaboration

**著者**: Yajie Wang, Yang Peng, Huiqing Hu, Qingbai Zhao, Man Zhang
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 人間-AI協働

## Abstract

This study compared human–human and human–AI collaboration in creative performance and perspective-taking behavior and further examined whether the relationship between perspective-taking and creative performance differed between the two forms of collaboration. According to the actor–partner interdependence model, an individual’s creative performance is influenced not only by their own perspective-taking behavior (actor effect) but also by their partner’s perspective-taking behavior (partner effect). The results showed that: (1) In both the Alternative Uses Task (AUT) and Product Improvement Task (PIT), human–AI collaboration produced significantly higher mean idea novelty and more frequent perspective-taking behavior than human–human collaboration. (2) The effects of perspective-taking on creative performance differed between the two forms of collaboration. In human–human collaboration, both actor and partner effects of perspective-taking significantly and positively predicted idea fluency in both tasks; in the PIT, partner perspective-taking also significantly and positively predicted idea flexibility. In human–AI collaboration, in the AUT, AI’s immediate perspective-taking significantly and positively predicted both its own and its human partner’s idea fluency, whereas humans’ immediate perspective-taking significantly and negatively predicted both their own and AI’s idea flexibility. In the PIT, humans’ delayed perspective-taking significantly and positively predicted both their own and their AI partner’s idea fluency and flexibility, whereas AI’s delayed perspective-taking significantly and negatively predicted both its own and its human partner’s idea novelty. These findings indicate that perspective-taking operates differently in human–AI collaboration than in human–human collaboration and that its effects vary by collaborator role for specific tasks and creativity indicators, providing new evidence for understanding the mechanisms of creative interaction in human–AI collaboration.
