# Climate modulates plant diversity effects on productivity in global plant mixture experiments

**著者**: Guoyong Yan
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 人間-AI協働

## Abstract

Biodiversity is a key driver of ecosystem productivity, yet whether climatic context modifies biodiversity effects in plant mixture experiments remains poorly resolved. Here, we compiled a global dataset of 4,629 observations from 185 forest and grassland diversity experiments to test whether mean annual temperature (MAT) and soil water availability modulate the relationship between plant species richness and productivity overyielding. Across all experiments, we find that plant species richness significantly increased productivity overyielding, and this pattern was robust across both response metrics. The species-richness effect became stronger with increasing MAT but weaker with increasing aridity index (AI), indicating stronger biodiversity effects in warmer and relatively drier climates. Simple-slope analyses confirmed that the species-richness effect was positive across all climate levels, but increased from low to high temperature and declined from dry to wet conditions. The stronger diversity effects in warmer climates were linked to enhanced complementarity among species, whereas selection effects showed no significant climate-dependent response. The AI-dependent pattern in productivity overyielding was not fully explained by complementarity or selection effects, suggesting that water-related modulation may involve additional mechanisms. These findings demonstrate that climatic context not only influences productivity directly, but also determines the strength and potential mechanisms of biodiversity effects on ecosystem functioning. Our results highlight the importance of maintaining and restoring plant diversity to sustain productivity under changing climatic conditions, especially in warmer and water-limited environments.
