# Artificial intelligence and HR work transformation: a systematic review and theoretical framework of intra-occupational polarisation

**著者**: Fernando Arístegui Monge, Mónica Villanueva-Villar, Elena Rivo-López
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: systematic_review | **分野**: 経済学

## Abstract

Purpose This study aims to examine how artificial intelligence (AI) transforms human resource (HR) work, arguing that the narrative of homogeneous professional enrichment conceals a distinct pattern: intra-occupational polarisation. AI may split the HR occupational category into a high-value interpretive pole and a residual procedural pole exposed to skill erosion. Design/methodology/approach This study combines a focused PRISMA review of articles from Scopus and Web of Science, a VOSviewer co-occurrence analysis of author keywords, a temporal overlay and a bibliographic coupling analysis of documents, and a directed content analysis of 13 articles selected by citation influence and thematic relevance. Findings The bibliometric map distinguishes strategic-analytical and automation-oriented research streams. The content analysis shows that the literature has implicitly described polarisation without naming it: capability frameworks articulate the interpretive pole but often presuppose a universal transition, while evidence on heterogeneous professional responses and skill erosion explains why the split may widen without organisational intervention. The studies point in the same direction, but none establishes AI as the cause. Research limitations/implications The pattern is inferred from studies across human resource management and adjacent fields rather than observed directly in HR. Longitudinal research following HR professionals through AI adoption is needed to test it. Practical implications Organisations adopting AI without preserving interpretive competencies risk a competence trap in which short-term efficiency erodes the capabilities needed to govern automation. For practitioners, the contribution is a change of focus: AI adoption is also a job-design decision, and the relevant question is not only what the system does but also who in HR retains the judgement to interpret and override it. Originality/value This study specifies intra-occupational polarisation as an analytical category for HR work, distinguished from task polarisation, algorithmic management and digital Taylorism by its focus on divergence within a single occupation. It connects labour process theory with the strategic capabilities perspective and specifies skill erosion and organisational design as mechanisms and moderators.
