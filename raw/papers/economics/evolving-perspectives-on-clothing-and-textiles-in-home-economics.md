# Evolving Perspectives on Clothing and Textiles in Home Economics

**著者**: Isha Nilesh Gawande
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経済学

## Abstract

This article examines the conceptual evolution of clothing and textiles within Home Economics, now recognized as Family and Consumer Sciences (FCS). Historically framed around early 20th-century domestic skills, garment construction, and frugal household management, textile education has transformed into a multifaceted, interdisciplinary research discipline. Driven by mid-century industrialization, post-war consumer behavior, and the formal institutional rebranding codified at the 1993 Scottsdale Conference, modern FCS leverages a human ecology framework to address critical global challenges. Grounded in this paradigm, contemporary textile pedagogy intersects material science, circular economy models, digital technologies, and socio-cultural identity frameworks. The field challenges persistent public misconceptions that reduce textile education to mere manual needlework. Instead, modern FCS curricula incorporate STEAM concepts-including fiber chemistry and 3D digital design-alongside fast fashion critiques, textile recycling, adaptive apparel, and supply chain transparency. Furthermore, emerging research directions embrace artificial intelligence, bio-fabricated textiles, and policy-driven circular economy directives, establishing FCS as a vital platform for environmental stewardship, consumer empowerment, and technological adaptation.
