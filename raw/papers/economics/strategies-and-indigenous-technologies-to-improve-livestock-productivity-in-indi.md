# Strategies and indigenous technologies to improve livestock productivity in India

## Abstract

Abstract India is the world's largest milk producer; however, the average milk productivity per dairy animal remains considerably lower than that of developed dairy-producing nations. Major constraints include limited availability of genetically superior breeding bulls, low artificial insemination (AI) coverage, cryopreservation-induced sperm damage, poor field-level semen quality assessment, and the high cost of advanced reproductive technologies such as semen sexing. Developing affordable indigenous technologies is therefore essential to enhance reproductive efficiency and accelerate genetic improvement. Over the past decade, ICAR–Central Institute for Research on Buffaloes (ICAR-CIRB), Hisar has developed a suite of indigenous reproductive technologies that strengthen the entire AI chain. These include a ready-to-use semen extender to improve post-thaw sperm quality, the CIRB Artificial Vagina for increasing semen dose production through optimized semen collection, the portable Spermoscope for on-site evaluation of sperm motility under field conditions, the CIRB AI Gun for minimizing thermal damage during insemination, and a low-cost semen sexing technology for increasing the production of female calves. Together, these complementary technologies improve semen production, preservation, evaluation, and utilization while reducing dependence on imported equipment. This paper reviews the scientific basis, development, commercialization, and potential impact of these technologies on dairy production. Their integration into breeding programmes can improve conception rates, accelerate dissemination of elite genetics, increase the availability of replacement females, and enhance milk productivity. The widespread adoption of these indigenous innovations will support sustainable dairy development, strengthen India's self-reliance in reproductive biotechnology, and contribute to national initiatives such as Atmanirbhar Bharat, Make in India, and Viksit Bharat 2047. Introduction The livestock sector plays a vital role in food security, rural livelihoods, and economic development, with dairy farming serving as a major source of income for millions of smallholder farmers in India. Although India is the world's largest milk producer, the average milk productivity per dairy animal remains considerably lower than the global average. Buffaloes, which contribute nearly half of the country's milk production, represent a valuable genetic resource; however, only about 30% of breedable bovines are covered under artificial insemination (AI), while the majority are still bred through scrub bulls of unknown genetic merit. This highlights the need for rapid genetic improvement through efficient reproductive technologies. Artificial insemination is the most effective tool for disseminating superior germplasm and enhancing milk production. However, its potential is constrained by a limited number of elite breeding bulls, inadequate semen dose production, cryopreservation-induced sperm damage, and poor field-level semen quality assessment. Frozen-thawed sperm often undergo structural and functional deterioration, reducing conception rates, while the absence of portable semen evaluation tools and temperature fluctuations during semen handling further compromise AI success under field conditions. To address these challenges, ICAR–Central Institute for Research on Buffaloes (ICAR-CIRB), Hisar has developed a series of indigenous reproductive technologies that strengthen every stage of the AI chain. These include a ready-to-use semen extender for improved cryopreservation, the CIRB Artificial Vagina for enhanced semen collection, the portable Spermoscope for field evaluation of sperm motility, the CIRB AI Gun for minimizing thermal damage during insemination, and an emerging low-cost semen sexing technology to increase the production of female dairy calves. This lead paper reviews the scientific basis, development, commercialization, and potential impact of these technologies on improving reproductive efficiency, accelerating genetic gain, increasing milk productivity, and promoting sustainable dairy development in India. 2. Dairy Productivity Scenario in India India is the world's largest milk producer, and the dairy sector is a major contributor to agricultural growth, rural employment, and nutritional security. Dairy farming supports millions of smallholder farmers, who typically own two to five animals, making improvements in reproductive efficiency crucial for enhancing milk production and farm income. Buffaloes, owing to their high milk fat content, adaptability, and longer productive life, contribute nearly half of the country's total milk production and remain the backbone of India's dairy industry. Despite its leadership in milk production, India's average milk productivity per animal remains considerably lower than the global average (1,777 vs. 2,699 kg/year). This productivity gap is primarily attributed to limited availability of genetically superior breeding stock, low artificial insemination (AI) coverage, inadequate reproductive management, and poor access to advanced breeding technologies. Furthermore, only about 1,825 elite buffalo breeding bulls were available in semen stations during 2020–21, producing 41.37 million frozen semen doses, which are insufficient to meet the breeding requirements of the country's buffalo population. Consequently, improving semen production and reproductive efficiency is essential for accelerating genetic gain. The Government of India has launched several programmes, including the Rashtriya Gokul Mission, National Programme for Dairy Development, genomic selection, and expansion of AI services, to improve livestock productivity. However, sustainable improvement requires affordable technologies that enhance every stage of the reproductive process, including semen collection, cryopreservation, semen quality assessment, insemination, and female calf production. Indigenous reproductive technologies such as improved semen extenders, the CIRB Artificial Vagina, Spermoscope, CIRB AI Gun, and low-cost semen sexing technology offer practical solutions for strengthening AI programmes while reducing dependence on imported technologies. Their large-scale adoption can accelerate genetic improvement, increase milk productivity, improve farmers' income, and support the sustainable growth of India's dairy sector. 3. Constraints to Reproductive Efficiency in Indian Dairy Production Reproductive efficiency is a key determinant of dairy productivity, influencing genetic improvement, conception rate, calving interval, replacement rate, and lifetime milk yield. Despite significant advances in breeding programmes, several biological, technical, and infrastructural constraints continue to limit reproductive performance in India. Major challenges include the limited availability of genetically superior breeding bulls, inadequate artificial insemination (AI) coverage, poor semen quality following cryopreservation, and lack of affordable advanced reproductive technologies. Cryopreservation remains one of the major bottlenecks in AI programmes because freezing and thawing induce membrane damage, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and premature capacitation-like changes, reducing sperm viability and fertility. Conventional egg-yolk-based semen extenders may further contribute to these changes due to the presence of progesterone, calcium, and high-density lipoproteins. In addition, improper semen collection practices, particularly non-standardized artificial vagina temperatures, adversely affect semen volume, sperm concentration, and semen dose production. Field conditions present additional challenges, as AI technicians generally lack portable facilities to assess post-thaw sperm motility before insemination, while temperature fluctuations during semen handling and AI further compromise sperm survival. The limited accessibility of advanced reproductive technologies such as computer-assisted semen analysis, embryo transfer, genomic selection, and flow cytometry-based semen sexing also restricts rapid genetic improvement because of their high cost and dependence on imported equipment. Although semen sexing offers enormous potential for increasing female calf production and accelerating genetic gain, its widespread adoption remains constrained by cost and technical complexity. Addressing these limitations requires affordable, indigenous, and field-oriented technologies that improve semen production, cryopreservation, semen evaluation, insemination efficiency, and female calf production. The integration of such innovations with effective technology transfer, farmer awareness, and capacity building will be critical for enhancing reproductive efficiency, increasing milk productivity, and ensuring the sustainable growth of India's dairy sector. 4. Indigenous Reproductive Technologies for Enhancing Dairy Productivity in India Improving reproductive efficiency is one of the most cost-effective approaches for increasing dairy productivity because it accelerates genetic improvement, enhances conception rate, shortens calving interval, and increases the availability of genetically superior replacement animals. While several advanced reproductive technologies have been developed worldwide, their adoption in developing countries remains limited due to high capital investment, expensive consumables, dependence on imported equipment, and the need for highly skilled manpower.
