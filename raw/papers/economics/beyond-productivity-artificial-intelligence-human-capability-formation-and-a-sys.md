# BEYOND PRODUCTIVITY Artificial Intelligence, Human Capability Formation, and a Systemic Framework for Evaluating Automation As a Conceptual Framework Grounded in Eudaimonia

**著者**: Ardi Nurcahya
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経済学

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence is increasingly evaluated in terms of productivity, accuracy, speed, and cost. These measures matter. But they can overlook another function of human activity: some activities do not only produce an output; they also build and sustain the capabilities that people need to understand, judge, adapt, supervise, and take over a function when conditions change. This paper proposes the Capability–Practice–Capacity (CPC) Framework to examine how automation may affect human capabilities through changes in practice opportunities. Its central chain runs from AI task capability to task substitution, practice opportunity, capability formation and maintenance, human capacity, and systemic consequences. The paper makes three contributions. It places practice opportunity as a conceptual mediator between task substitution and capability change. It extends the analysis from individuals to organisational and intergenerational capability pipelines. And it introduces Human Capability Reserve (HCR) as an evaluative concept for asking whether a system still retains relevant human capacity when automation fails, must be challenged, or needs to be taken over. The framework is conceptual. It does not claim to have resolved the question of what automation does to human capability. It proposes a way of asking the question more precisely — and a set of hypotheses that can be tested.
