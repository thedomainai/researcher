# New Technology, New Problems: How AI Could Strain China’s Social Contract

**著者**: Xingchen Billy Yang
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経済学

## Abstract

China’s government is making artificial intelligence (AI) a national development priority and is promoting its adoption across the country’s economy and society. This essay discusses how AI may affect China’s economy, specifically how it may challenge the performance-based legitimacy China’s government has depended upon over the last several decades. In particular, this essay shows how AI affects workers in different industries, workers at different stages of their careers, different age groups, and different geographic locations. Evidence suggests AI is driving down demand for routine tasks and certain forms of creative work and that wealthier, more economically diversified regions are better able to cope with the disruption. It also shows that feelings of insecurity are on the rise, as demand for entry-level workers may be decreasing and senior workers now confront a situation in which their junior counterparts can do some of their jobs. This essay also discusses opportunities arising from AI in the public sector, specifically how AI could help China’s economy by creating new industries and raising productivity and can help its government by improving the delivery of public services. Finally, this essay discusses how China’s government is trying to help people who have been directly and indirectly affected by AI through initiatives like employment programs, court rulings protecting employees, and monitoring of how the economic gains from AI are distributed. Although the evidence shows AI results in these labor market changes, whether they will undermine public trust remains a projection rather than an established fact.
