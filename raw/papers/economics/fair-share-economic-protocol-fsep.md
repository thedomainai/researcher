# Fair-Share Economic Protocol (FSEP)

**著者**: Joshua Tanner
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経済学

## Abstract

AI-driven automation accelerates the rate at which market value is generated while decoupling productivity from human labor hours. Under current legal frameworks, this dynamic heavily concentrates capital into corporate monopolies. The Fair-Share Economic Protocol (FSEP) is a concrete, opt-in legal and economic framework designed to alter that trajectory by establishing a decentralized, self-sustaining public wealth pool. FSEP operates as a dual-layer legal instrument. The foundational layer utilizes restrictive copyleft licensing (e.g., Polyform Noncommercial) to ensure the underlying code, models, and architectures remain completely free for personal, academic, and non-commercial use. The second layer is a commercial exception contract. This contract permits commercial monetization strictly on the condition that the individual human beneficiaries agree to a cap on pool-derived income, with all excess revenue routed into a Universal Basic Income (UBI) pool distributed equally to registered participants. Crucially, this income cap applies exclusively to commercial revenues derived directly from the FSEP intellectual property pool. It does not audit or cap an individual's general personal income, net worth, or unrelated business ventures. To prevent the centralization of administrative power, FSEP is structured on a maturity ladder: • Phase A (Current): Relies on existing intellectual property litigation to enforce the commercial exception, utilizing the growing defensive IP pool as a structural incentive for compliance without requiring invasive surveillance. • Phase B (Scaling): Introduces AI-assisted human juries, fair market value accounting for non-monetary assets, and incentivized whistleblower bounties. • Phase C (Post-Scarcity): Transitions to dynamic income cap compression (scaling from 10x global median GDP down to 2x-5x) and decentralized democratic governance as the network approaches global population parity. This protocol is published as a live structural blueprint rather than a theoretical pitch. The author's foundational projects, including the sDAG routing architecture and Portal Server, are bound by this framework from day one, seeding the initial UBI pool and establishing the first defensive IP safe harbor. Refinement of specific legal parameters and contract clauses is explicitly invited from experts in open-source law, software licensing, and macroeconomic policy.
