# Signatures of selection underlying adaptation and production traits in tropical Bos indicus cattle: A systematic review

**著者**: Dyah Maharani, Pita Sudrajad, Sigit Bintara, Tri Satya Mastuti Widi, Mulumebet Worku
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: systematic_review | **分野**: 経済学

## Abstract

Abstract Tropical Bos indicus (zebu) cattle are essential for livestock production in tropical regions because of their adaptation to heat, parasites, and low-quality feed. Natural and artificial selection leave detectable genomic footprints, yet evidence from signatures of selection studies remains fragmented across breeds, geographies, and analytical methods. This systematic review synthesizes genome-wide selection-signature evidence in tropical Bos indicus to identify consistently reported candidate genes associated with adaptation and production traits and assess methodological trends across genome-wide selection-signature studies. Following PRISMA 2020 guidance, Scopus was searched via Watase Uake (completed April 2026) using predefined keyword combinations related to selection signatures, selective sweeps, and zebu/indicine cattle. After focusing on publications from 2012–2026 and applying eligibility criteria requiring validated selection-signature methods and trait-linked candidate genes in tropical Bos indicus , 58 original studies were retained for qualitative synthesis. Whole-genome sequencing was the predominant platform (22/58), and multi-statistic designs were common (39/58). Adaptation-related signatures yielded 512 gene–trait entries (429 unique genes) across 54 studies, dominated by immunity/parasite resistance and thermotolerance annotations. The most recurrent adaptation candidates included DNAJC18 , KIT , BoLA/MHC genes, HOXC13 , HSPA4 , HSPA9 , PDGFRA , and SOD1 , with HELB recurring as a DNA-maintenance locus under tropical selection. Production-related signatures yielded 497 entries (444 unique genes) across 46 studies, mainly for milk, reproduction, and growth/carcass traits; HMGA2 was the most repeatedly reported production gene, followed by SLC4A4 , GHR , KCNIP4 , NCAPG , NCKAP5 , RXFP2 , and SLC24A3 . Thirty-three genes overlapped both domains, nominating targets at the adaptation–productivity interface. These recurrent loci consistently point to biological pathways related to heat stress response, immune regulation, and tissue development. Selection signatures in tropical zebu are highly polygenic, yet a reproducible core of stress-response, integument, immune, growth, dairy, and fertility genes emerges across studies. These consensus loci provide priority targets for functional validation, genomic selection, and conservation strategies aimed at improving climate resilience without compromising productivity.
