# AI Workflow Engineering: Architectural Principles, Orchestration Models, and the Design of Reliable Multi-Stage Artificial Intelligence Processes

**著者**: Roman Shaban
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経済学

## Abstract

The increasing integration of generative artificial intelligence into professional, scientific, and organizational environments has created a growing need for systematic methods of designing, coordinating, and evaluating complex AI-enabled processes. While prompt engineering primarily concerns the formulation of instructions for individual model interactions, AI Workflow Engineering addresses the broader architecture through which multiple computational operations, language models, external tools, information resources, and human decisions are integrated into coherent and reproducible processes. This paper introduces AI Workflow Engineering as an applied interdisciplinary framework for constructing multi-stage artificial intelligence systems. It conceptualizes an AI workflow as a structured network of operations governed by explicit dependencies, information-transfer mechanisms, execution conditions, validation procedures, and accountability constraints. The proposed framework distinguishes task decomposition, workflow orchestration, tool integration, state management, contextual information architecture, quality assurance, exception handling, and human oversight as interconnected design dimensions. It also develops a reference architecture for combining deterministic computational procedures with probabilistic language model operations. Particular attention is devoted to the relationship between automation and human cognitive control. The paper argues that effective AI workflows should not be evaluated exclusively through computational speed or operational efficiency, but also through reliability, traceability, adaptability, epistemic quality, and the preservation of human decision-making authority. A practical reference workflow demonstrates how information acquisition, automated processing, generative analysis, verification, human approval, and final artifact production can be organized into a unified system. The paper contributes a conceptual vocabulary, a formal architectural model, a proposed engineering methodology, and a set of evaluation criteria intended to support the development of reliable, modular, and human-supervised AI workflows
