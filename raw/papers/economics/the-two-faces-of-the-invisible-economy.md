# The Two Faces of the Invisible Economy

**著者**: James Felton Keith, Doucouré Tidiane
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経済学

## Abstract

The productive structure of the AI economy contains two economically significant quantities that are largely absent from conventional factor accounting. On the input side, personal data, expert judgment, behavioral traces, creative works, and other human-generated signals enter machine learning and automation systems as informational inputs that improve prediction, classification, matching, reasoning, and autonomous production. These inputs are generated by individuals and institutions but are commonly priced as zero, priced as ordinary hourly labor, or absorbed into capital returns after aggregation. On the output side, AI agents and physical automation systems perform labor-equivalent tasks at scale, but this machine labor is not recorded as labor in payroll systems, workforce reports, or national accounts. This paper integrates the informational factor of production framework with the Human-Equivalent Work Unit (HEWU) framework to construct a unified input-output model of the AI economy. We define informational stock as the reduction in uncertainty produced by human-generated informational inputs, formalized through mutual information and performance-attribution measures. We define machine labor output as the labor-equivalent value of AI or automation tasks relative to human baseline productivity, adjusted for task complexity and quality. We show that omitting informational stock from the production function biases measured capital shares upward when informational stock is positively correlated with capital. We further show that omitting machine labor from labor accounts creates a parallel output-side distortion: economic work is performed but not classified as labor-equivalent production. The paper develops a four-layer value-chain model: personal data input, informational stock, machine production, and machine labor output. We introduce formal attribution equations linking downstream machine labor value to upstream informational contributors through marginal contribution, Shapley-style allocation, and performance-delta methods. We then propose a two-tier measurement architecture consisting of an Informational Stock Layer and a HEWU Output Layer. The combined architecture supports enterprise auditing, AI governance, insurance underwriting, legal damages estimation, labor-market analysis, and national statistical modernization.
