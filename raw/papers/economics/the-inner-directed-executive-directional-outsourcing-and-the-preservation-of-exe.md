# The Inner-Directed Executive: Directional Outsourcing and the Preservation of Executive Authorship in the Age of Artificial Intelligence

**著者**: David (Daoud) Matta
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経済学

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence is rapidly increasing the amount, speed, and apparent authority of the external intelligence available to organisational leaders, and scholarship has responded by theorising automation, augmentation, and appropriate reliance. This paper identifies a second problem generated by the same development: as external systems become more capable of contributing to executive cognition, executives require a stronger capacity to determine direction from within. It proposes an External Intelligence–Inner Direction Paradox: the greater the capability and ubiquity of external intelligence, the more consequential internally grounded direction becomes. It further introduces directional outsourcing as a fourth and distinct level of delegation beyond task, cognitive, and judgment outsourcing, occurring when external systems come to shape problem framing, evaluative criteria, and purposes rather than analysis alone. Inner direction is defined not as intuition or resistance to evidence but as reflective self-governance, distinguished on discriminant grounds from adjacent leadership constructs and argued to be formed through exercised judgment rather than possessed. Three falsifiable propositions are advanced, with a six-movement Inner-Direction Cycle, a preliminary operationalisation, boundary conditions, and an account of the organisational structures that produce directional outsourcing independently of individual disposition. The paper argues that the human-in-the-loop principle is insufficient, because procedural presence does not establish authorship, and concludes that the distinctive leadership capability of the artificial intelligence era may be the capacity to remain the author of direction.
