# When Is the Evidence Enough? Youth Labor Markets, AI, and the Cost of Waiting for Certainty

**著者**: Ewan Simpson
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経済学

## Abstract

Something is going wrong at the point where young people enter the labor market. That proposition is becoming easier to defend as evidence accumulates. The claim that artificial intelligence is causing a youth employment crisis remains unresolved. Slower growth, tighter monetary conditions, post-pandemic adjustment, health-related inactivity, employer caution, skills mismatches, demographic pressures, and technological change can all contribute to weak youth outcomes. Their relative importance differs across countries and occupations. The accumulation of evidence nevertheless matters. Payroll records, vacancy data, unemployment and underemployment statistics, employer surveys, surveys of students and early-career workers, and analyses by major international institutions increasingly point toward strain at the first stage of working life. AI repeatedly appears within that deterioration, especially where junior work is highly exposed to automation or rapid task change, but the causal contribution remains unresolved. This paper argues that the youth labor market has crossed an evidentiary threshold for action even though it has not crossed the threshold for attributing its deterioration primarily to AI. That distinction matters especially because entry-level work is not only a source of employment. It is one of the mechanisms through which inexperienced workers become experienced workers. If labor-market entry weakens while AI simultaneously removes or redesigns the tasks through which novices learn, the longer-term problem extends from employment into capability formation.
