# The Legal Philosophy of Inequality: Justice, Capital, and Redistribution in Global Society

**著者**: Northon Salomao de Oliveira
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0

## Abstract

What is the value of a legal right if it cannot be exercised? The Legal Philosophy of Inequality: Justice, Capital, and Redistribution in Global Society advances a bold and original thesis: the deepest injustice in modern legal systems is not merely the unequal distribution of rights, but the unequal capacity to transform formally recognized rights into practical remedies. Introducing the concept of legal liquidity, Northon Salomão de Oliveira argues that a right exists in full only when its holder possesses the real economic, institutional, and social means to enforce it. Beginning with the haunting image of an unredeemed pawn ticket discovered in an old drawer, the book develops a powerful philosophical framework that connects private law, constitutional law, access to justice, inheritance, financial inequality, actuarial risk, artificial intelligence, and global climate governance into a single theory of legal inequality. Across these diverse fields, one question persists: why do formally equal rights produce profoundly unequal outcomes? Engaging critically with the ideas of John Rawls, Friedrich Hayek, Amartya Sen, Hans Kelsen, H. L. A. Hart, Ronald Dworkin, Lon Fuller, Thomas Piketty, Marc Galanter, and many others, the book challenges one of the most enduring assumptions of modern jurisprudence: that formal equality before the law is sufficient to achieve justice. Instead, it argues that the true measure of a legal order lies in the distance between rights declared and rights redeemed. Bringing together legal philosophy, political theory, economics, sociology, and empirical legal studies, this work offers a new vocabulary for understanding inequality in the twenty-first century. Rather than advocating the abolition of markets or private property, it proposes a more precise and achievable objective: treating the practical enforceability of legal rights as a public good that every just society has a duty to provide. Provocative, interdisciplinary, and internationally oriented, The Legal Philosophy of Inequality invites scholars, jurists, policymakers, and readers of political philosophy to reconsider one of the foundational questions of law: When does a right become real?
