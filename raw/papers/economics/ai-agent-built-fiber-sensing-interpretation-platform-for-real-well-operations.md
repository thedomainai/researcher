# AI-Agent-Built Fiber-Sensing Interpretation Platform for Real Well Operations

**著者**: Arjun Guglani, Ehsan Nikjoo
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 経済学

## Abstract

Abstract Distributed acoustic sensing (DAS) and distributed temperature sensing (DTS) are fiber-optic technologies that turn an optical fiber into a distributed sensor array. Originally developed in the telecommunications and defense sectors, these methods were adopted by the oil and gas industry. DTS entered thermal-recovery surveillance in the mid-1990s (Brown and Hartog 2002), and DAS followed more widely after the first downhole trials in 2009 (Molenaar et al. 2012). They now expand the observational basis for well logging by providing distributed temperature and acoustic measurements over the instrumented interval at survey-specific spatial and temporal sampling. However, interpretation-tool development has lagged acquisition technology: multiple vendor formats, multiday survey volumes, and job-specific processing still leave much of the acquired data underused relative to acquisition cost. An engineer-directed development approach was evaluated in which a domain engineer used general-purpose artificial intelligence (AI) coding agents to construct a DAS/DTS and well-log interpretation software environment. Development was performed against field-surveillance data sets from multiple vendors and job types. As application scope increased, unstructured agent use was replaced by a documentation-driven operating framework comprising architecture rules, task-scoped guidance, decision ownership, plan-before-implementation, and automated numerical and regression checks. The implemented environment provided multiformat data access, stream-from-disk review of multiday surveys, synchronized visualization, deterministic processing, and restorable sessions. A first usable environment was completed within weeks and subsequently used to implement and evaluate two interpretation workflows across multiple surveys. For injection surveillance, channel-coherence processing, masked ridge extraction, and engineering quality control converted recurring low-frequency DAS responses into a common-frame thermal-slug trajectory library. For gas lift surveillance, operating-state DTS traces were combined with a dimensionless dual-band DAS coincidence field and reviewed with completion geometry, surface measurements, and event history. The workflows derive interpretive confidence from recurrence across events and concurrence across measurements, respectively. The results establish the feasibility of locating governed software implementation near domain expertise. Individual engineers and small technical teams can thereby retain tested methods within a shared technical foundation, shortening the cycle from field observation to reproducible operational workflow from many months to a few days.
