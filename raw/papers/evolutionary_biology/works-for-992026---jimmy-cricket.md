# Works for 9/9/2026 - Jimmy Cricket

**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0

## Abstract

Listen and ask questions on Gemini Notebook: https://notebook.google.com/notebook/01cb405a-a6ff-43c4-91c7-9f4863bc7015?authuser=1 Tuning the Future: Human–AI Coevolution, Historical Memory, and the Relational Architecture of Intelligence examines the emerging shift from treating artificial intelligence as an isolated computational object toward understanding intelligence as something that can arise through relations among humans, AI systems, historical sources, institutions, archives, media, and embodied experience. The paper argues that present actions continually modify the informational environment inherited by future receivers. A conversation, correction, publication, recorded homily, retrieved source, or newly created artifact can become part of tomorrow’s accessible history. This process is formalized through History-Modified Accessibility Systems (HMAS), which models how prior traversal can alter later accessibility, salience, and available paths through information. A second framework, Catholic Invariant Grammar (CIG), is used to examine how relational structures may survive transformation across different media and participants without collapsing those participants into identity. The paper emphasizes strict distinctions among source, representation, receiver, interpretation, and reconstruction. It treats provenance as essential to trustworthy human–AI cognition and argues that preserved errors and corrections can become valuable historical information rather than being discarded as mere failure. The study places these ideas within a broader intellectual history that includes writing, archives, Vannevar Bush’s associative trails, Shannon’s information theory, Wiener’s cybernetics, transactive memory, distributed cognition, extended cognition, contemporary human–AI coevolution, and AI-mediated collective memory. A Catholic theological layer examines the Church as a long-running transmission system organized around reception, preservation, correction, and faithful handing-on. Jesus Christ is treated as the theological reference point for Christian interpretation, while clear boundaries are maintained between analogy and identity, archive and presence, AI and personhood, and informational reconstruction and sacramental reality. The paper also includes a live September 9, 2026 case study, a formal derivation of history-modified relational accessibility, a plain-language explainer, an explainer for children, a biography of comedian Jimmy Cricket, and an editorial on the future of AI, memory, provenance, education, historical reconstruction, archives, and human dignity. Its central claim is simple: we receive yesterday while living today, and everything we do today helps construct the history that tomorrow will receive.
