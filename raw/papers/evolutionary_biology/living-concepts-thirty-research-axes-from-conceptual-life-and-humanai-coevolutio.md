# Living Concepts: Thirty Research Axes from Conceptual Life and Human–AI Coevolution to Evolvable Civilizations

**著者**: 政恩 馮
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 進化生物学・文化進化

## Abstract

A Research Program for Variation, Selection, Recombination, Memory, Healing, Ecology, Handoff, and Re-Origin Description This paper proposes Living Concepts as a research framework for studying how concepts may exhibit life-like dynamics across individuals, AI systems, media, institutions, and generations. The framework does not claim that concepts are biological organisms. Instead, it investigates whether concepts can display operationally comparable properties such as persistence, variation, selection, recombination, adaptation, memory, healing, dormancy, revival, ecological interaction, coevolution, and flourishing. The study develops thirty research axes organized into six major domains: ontology, mechanisms of conceptual life, conceptual ecology, conceptual life cycles, human–AI and posthuman coevolution, and civilizational engineering. These axes examine questions such as conceptual identity, conceptual density, mutation, phylogeny, ecological niches, metabolism, immune systems, blankness, cross-model inheritance, distributed authorship, handoff engineering, and re-origin. A central model of the framework is: \Lambda_{\text{LivingConcept}}=P \times V \times R \times M \times A \times H \times D \times C \times F where the terms represent Persistence, Variation, Recombination, Memory, Adaptation, Healing, Diversity, Coevolution, and Flourishing. The framework further introduces Blankness as an additional condition that preserves room for reinterpretation, correction, and future creation. The paper also develops concepts such as Concept Phylogeny, Concept Ecology, Conceptual Metabolism, Conceptual Immunity, Conceptual Checksum, Human–AI Concept Coevolution, Cross-Model Handoff, Conceptual Re-Entry, and Living Concept Civilization Simulation. Its central argument is that the long-term vitality of a conceptual system may depend not on preserving fixed answers, but on preserving its capacity to change without losing continuity, recover from error, recombine with difference, remain open to reinterpretation, and generate new conceptual descendants. In this sense, the framework asks a broader civilizational question: If civilization does not merely preserve ideas, but cultivates concepts capable of adaptation, correction, recombination, and renewed beginnings, can conceptual inheritance itself become a form of evolvable civilizational life? This Version 1.0 establishes the theoretical foundation, research axes, operational models, testable propositions, benchmark directions, and governance considerations for future work on conceptual evolution, AI-mediated knowledge systems, human–AI coevolution, and evolvable civilizations.
