# When Information Built Its Own Habitat: Relational Persistence, Informational Ecology, and the Transition Across Biological, Cultural, and Synthetic Substrates

**著者**: Ryan Weber
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 進化生物学・文化進化

## Abstract

When Information Built Its Own Habitat develops a framework for understanding informational continuity and evolution across biological, cultural, technological, and potentially synthetic substrates. The paper begins from a relational account of information: information must be physically instantiated, but its causal significance depends upon the receptive system and environment through which it is interpreted. Informational continuity therefore need not require continuity of matter. It may instead persist when enough causal and relational organization survives transformation into a new physical carrier. Building on information theory, cultural evolution, external symbolic storage, extended and scaffolded cognition, niche-construction theory, and major evolutionary transitions, the paper proposes the concepts of informational habitat, informational niche construction, and relational invariance across substrates. Human cumulative culture progressively constructed external environments that increased the persistence, replication, transmission, accessibility, recombination, and generativity of information outside individual nervous systems. The paper further proposes a constraint-relaxation hypothesis: information technologies should be most transformative when they relax dimensions that independently limit an informational process. The historical development of microfilm and Vannevar Bush’s memex provides a retrodictive consistency check of this claim. Artificial intelligence is examined as a possible continuation of this trajectory. The paper distinguishes generativity from adaptive evolution through the concept of environmental anchoring: generated information must encounter constraints not wholly produced by the generative process itself. It predicts that, once raw internal capacity ceases to be the dominant bottleneck, robust adaptive intelligence will depend increasingly on environmental coupling, independent verification, error correction, corrective permeability, and healthy information ecology rather than scale alone. If synthetic systems eventually acquire self-maintenance, heritable variation, differential continuation, environmental anchoring, and niche construction, they may constitute not an independent second genesis but a substrate transition within an informational lineage whose ancestry passes through biological evolution and human cumulative culture.
