## Abstract

The rapid development of generative artificial intelligence is transforming the ways in which cultural heritage is documented, preserved, reconstructed, interpreted and communicated.Museums, archives, libraries, galleries, research institutions and heritage-management organizations increasingly use artificial intelligence for image recognition, automated cataloguing, transcription of historical documents, translation, restoration modelling, reconstruction of damaged objects, generation of educational content and creation of immersive cultural experiences.Generative AI extends these possibilities by enabling the production of new images, texts, sounds, audiovisual representations and virtual environments derived from large volumes of existing cultural data.At the same time, its capacity to imitate artistic styles, reproduce voices and appearances, reconstruct missing elements and generate highly plausible representations of the past creates a complex group of legal problems that cannot be resolved exclusively through conventional copyright doctrine.Cultural heritage occupies a distinctive position in this transformation.It simultaneously represents historical memory, an object of scientific research, a source of cultural identity, an economic resource and an important component of the global information environment.Cultural heritage may consist of physical objects, architectural sites, manuscripts, artworks, photographs, audiovisual materials, traditional knowledge, languages, rituals, music and other forms of
