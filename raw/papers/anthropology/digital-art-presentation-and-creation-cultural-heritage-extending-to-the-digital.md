# Digital Art Presentation and Creation: Cultural Heritage Extending to the Digital Realm

**著者**: Aleksandra Łukaszewicz
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 人類学

## Abstract

Digital Art is a broad term that historically stretches from experiments since the 60s of the twentieth century with computer art, electronic art, and multimedia art until the current virtual, augmented, holographic, and other technologies used in art creation in the 20s of the twenty-first century. Therefore, its presentation and creation are the processes that have developed in contemporary global culture for more than six decades resulting in the vast and dispersed Cultural Heritage in the digital layer of reality, in which digitized art is also included. This situation is already recognized and so we must speak about the Digital Heritage, introducing this term and concept after the historical introduction of other forms of conceptualization of the heritage—the Common Heritage of Humanity, that we continue reshaping in order to pass this what humans created of the high value to the future generations, although the forms and objects of creation continue transforming. Digital Heritage is a concept that embraces the digitized Cultural Heritage previously existing and identified as valuable for protection, and digital-born Cultural Heritage like the virtual artworks created for and possible only in virtual reality. The first case is mapped quite well by different international bodies, like the European Union or the United Nations Organization, elaborating specific guidelines for the protection, accessibility, and re-usability of the digitized Cultural Heritage. The second case is much more problematic, both on the conceptual and the legal level. Conceptually speaking, Digital Heritage cannot be easily categorized as intangible and should be considered diversely in different cases, for example, Net.art can be described as intangible, but a virtual artwork experienced in a headset and with sensors should be defined as tangible. This shows that the existing categories are not sufficient to describe the ontological status of digital objects and legally it is still difficult to decide on the basic rules allowing for the inclusion of digital-born objects among Cultural Heritage, which is now being reconsidered.
