# Artificial Intelligence in Christian Religious Education: Reconstructing Teachers' Roles in Developing Students' Digital Literacy and Critical Thinking

**著者**: Yunardi Kristian Zega, Andrea Elfata Ratulangi, Ryanto Adilang, Stefanus Meo Nekin, Sharon Long Saging
**年**: 2027 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 人類学

## Abstract

The rapid development of artificial intelligence, particularly generative artificial intelligence, is transforming how students access information and learn, including in Christian Religious Education. While artificial intelligence offers opportunities for personalized learning and richer engagement with biblical and theological content, it also raises concerns about inaccurate or biased religious content, over-reliance, weakened critical thinking, ethical risks, and the erosion of the relational and spiritual dimensions of faith formation. This study aims to reconstruct the roles of Christian religious education teachers in artificial intelligence-based learning so that it strengthens students' digital literacy and critical thinking within Christ-centered formation. A qualitative design using library research and conceptual analysis was employed. A corpus of 52 sources, comprising peer-reviewed studies published between 2018 and 2026 together with seminal works and policy documents regardless of publication year, was selected through explicit criteria and analyzed using reflexive thematic analysis and conceptual synthesis. The synthesis indicates that artificial intelligence shifts Christian Religious Education from content transmission toward guided inquiry; that its opportunities are realized only when its risks are mediated pedagogically and ethically; that teachers' roles must be reconstructed as learning facilitators, spiritual mentors, and digital ethics guides; and that digital literacy and critical thinking can be integrated through the evaluation and theological discernment of machine-generated content. The study proposes the Christ-Centered EEDE Framework (exploring, evaluating, discerning, and embodying), which integrates these elements into a teacher-guided learning cycle and offers a testable foundation for ensuring that artificial intelligence serves, rather than replaces, faith formation.
