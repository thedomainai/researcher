# Jurisdictional Settlements in LLM Mediated Analytic Regimes

**著者**: Aron Tharp, Hippolyte Lefebvre
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 人類学

## Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) promise to democratize organizational data work by enabling business managers to frame their analyses beyond preconfigured dashboards. However, the implications of this shift for both managers and data professionals’ authority and accountability remain under-theorized. Drawing on Abbott’s System of Professions, we examine how the rollout of a conversational LLM-mediated analytics interface in a health-technology unicorn reconfigured the jurisdiction of data work. We find that the interface redistributed rather than replaced legitimacy in the analytics chain: managers gained bounded autonomy to locate data, narrow questions, and generate provisional analyses, while data professionals’ jurisdiction shifted from routine analytic production toward ex-post certification, reviewing executive-facing and financially material outputs, and maintaining the conditions for reliable inference. We contribute to Information Systems research on data democratization with a theorization of three jurisdictional settlements between managers and data professionals around an LLM-mediated analytics regime: delegated inference, notarial legitimation, and infrastructure stewardship.
