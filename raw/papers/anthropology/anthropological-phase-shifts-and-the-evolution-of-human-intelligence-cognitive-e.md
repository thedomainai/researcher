# Anthropological Phase Shifts and the Evolution of Human Intelligence: Cognitive Externalization from Embodied Mind to Artificial Intelligence

**著者**: David Matta
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 人類学

## Abstract

Human history may be interpreted as a succession of cognitive externalizations — technological transformations that relocate capacities previously understood as constitutive of humanity into external systems. This paper develops the Externalization Principle as a general theoretical law governing anthropological phase shifts: the claim that technologies become culturally and anthropologically transformative precisely when they assume functions that a civilization had previously treated as definitional of the human. Drawing on intellectual history, philosophy of mind, cognitive science, and philosophy of technology, the paper traces five stages in the evolution of human intelligence: embodied intelligence (pre-literate cognition), symbolic intelligence (writing and the externalization of memory), distributed intelligence (printing and the redistribution of epistemic authority), computational intelligence (digital computing and the externalization of calculation), and cognitive ecology (artificial intelligence and the externalization of reasoning). Each stage is analyzed with respect to the anthropological crisis it provoked, the reconsolidation of human identity it eventually produced, and the distinction it forced between what is contingently human and what is constitutively human. The paper argues that AI represents the most disruptive instance of this pattern because it externalizes reasoning — the faculty upon which post-Enlightenment civilization founded its entire self-understanding. The concept of cognitive ecology is introduced to describe the emerging condition in which human intelligence is irreducibly distributed across biological, social, and computational systems. Implications for philosophy of mind, epistemology, and the ethics of AI design are developed throughout.
