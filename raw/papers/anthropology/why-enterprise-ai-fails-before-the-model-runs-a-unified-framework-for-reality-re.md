# Why Enterprise AI Fails Before the Model Runs: A Unified Framework for Reality, Representation, and Governance

**著者**: RAKTIM SINGH
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 人類学

## Abstract

Enterprise AI initiatives continue to struggle despite rapid advances in foundation models, data platforms, automation technologies, and agentic systems. While most discussions focus on models, algorithms, governance controls, and deployment architectures, many enterprise AI failures originate before model execution—in the gap between organizational reality and the machine-legible representations upon which AI systems depend.This paper presents a unified framework integrating the Representation Economy, Digital Anthropology for Enterprise AI, the Human Reality Gap, Representation Integrity, Representational Readiness, and the SENSE–CORE–DRIVER architecture. The central argument is that AI systems do not operate directly on reality; they operate on representations of reality. Consequently, the quality, completeness, contextual fidelity, and legitimacy of those representations become foundational determinants of downstream reasoning, governance, trust, and enterprise transformation outcomes.The paper introduces several interconnected concepts, including the Enterprise Reality Stack, the Representation Chain, the Enterprise Reality Audit, and the Representation Debt Lifecycle. Together, these frameworks explain how reality becomes observed, represented, encoded, reasoned over, governed, and acted upon within machine-mediated organizations. The analysis further argues that Digital Anthropology should be understood as a strategic enterprise capability for discovering organizational reality, while Representational Readiness should be treated as a prerequisite for large-scale AI deployment.By synthesizing previously published work across multiple papers, this article provides a comprehensive theoretical foundation for understanding why enterprise AI systems succeed or fail. It proposes that the next stage of AI maturity will depend less on model sophistication and more on an organization's ability to continuously discover, represent, govern, and renew the realities upon which intelligent systems depend.
