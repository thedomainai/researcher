# Human–AI Collaboration in Supply Chains: A Framework for Resilient Decision Making Under Disruption

**著者**: Md Asfaqur Rahman, Shakila Azad, Numair Bin Sharif, MD Hasibur Rahman Murad, Razia Sultana
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 行動経済学

## Abstract

Supply chain inventory management under disruption is increasingly complex, requiring planners to integrate fluctuating demand, delivery delays, and inventory shortages across multiple periods. Standard evaluations of AI decision support systems evaluate accuracy within single-trial settings, overlooking how algorithmic errors propagate into persistent, multi-period operational disruptions. To solve these issues, this article introduces a humancentered AI decision-support framework and inventory simulation system designed to aid planners during dynamic disruptions. Built on an OR-Gym single-echelon formulation and calibrated using three real-world datasets—Walmart M5 for demand, Olist for lead times, and DataCo for disruption frequencies—the system supports realistic stress-testing and state tracking. The system supports decision workflows by pairing automated order recommendations with real-time stockout risk predictions from a calibrated LightGBM classifier across three visual bands (Low, Moderate, High). These functions are combined into a two-stage ordering interface where planners lock in an initial independent order before viewing AI recommendations, preserving human decision authority and minimizing cognitive bias. A usability and human performance evaluation was conducted through a 30-week pilot demonstration to assess advice adoption, operational costs, and cognitive workload (NASATLX). With high classification performance and results showing that propagating AI errors cause 3.45 times more extra stockout weeks than absorbed errors, the platform demonstrated excellent usability and behavioral engagement. The findings demonstrate that the proposed decision-support framework effectively facilitates cognitive ergonomics, appropriate reliance, and human–AI collaboration in supply chain inventory management, underscoring the promise of human-centered AI systems to improve managerial performance and operational resilience.
