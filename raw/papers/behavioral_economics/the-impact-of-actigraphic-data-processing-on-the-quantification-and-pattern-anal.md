# The Impact of Actigraphic Data Processing on the Quantification and Pattern Analysis of Human Locomotor Activity

**著者**: Bálint Maczák
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 行動経済学

## Abstract

Actigraphy is a multidisciplinary method of measuring human locomotor activity using wrist-worn, accelerometer-based devices. A major limitation of this method is that devices from different manufacturers often compress raw acceleration data into activity values using different and proprietary algorithms, which significantly hinders the reproducibility and comparability of studies. In this work, I comprehensively explored the activity determination methods available in the literature, and we synthesized them into a two-stage, generalized data processing pipeline. Then, I systematically compared these methods by applying them to real-world, wrist-measured acceleration signals, thereby emulating the operation of various commercial actigraphs. The impact of these signal processing steps was quantified through correlation analysis, as well as by comparing circadian rhythm indicators and sleep-wake classifications extracted from the resulting activity signals. I showed that different actigraphs can quantify the exact same motion in vastly different ways, potentially inducing deviations in clinical indicators that exceed those typically associated with pathological conditions. Rather than employing the widely used approach based on probability distribution functions, I investigated the scale-invariant properties of locomotor activity in the spectral domain. Based on an extensive analysis, we showed that both multi-day raw acceleration signals and activity signals derived from them exhibit universal, scale-free spectral characteristics, including 1/f noise, which closely aligns with our observations regarding the spectral patterns of spatial displacement data. To achieve this, I analyzed the power spectral density (PSD) and the fluctuation function of the multiday signals, applying a Daniell method-based variance reduction technique to the PSD estimates. We found that this spectral approach is considerably less methodology-dependent than traditional statistical-distribution-based evaluations. However, my analyses also revealed that when separating actigraphic time series into sleep and wake periods, the fluctuation analyses widely used in clinical research can be heavily biased by device-specific signal processing and the presence of measurement errors.
