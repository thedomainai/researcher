# Human-AI Interaction in Personnel Selection: Job Applicant Reactions to Human, Hybrid, and AI Evaluations

**著者**: Vinicius Gracio Michelon
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 行動経済学

## Abstract

This PhD thesis consists of four studies on job applicant reactions to human, Artificial Intelligence (AI), and hybrid (human-AI) hiring processes. The research focuses on rejected applicants’ perceptions of fairness, trust, organisational evaluations, and behavioural intentions. Study 1 reports a systematic and meta-analytic review of 28 experiments (N = 6,338). Results indicate that job applicants perceive AI evaluations as less fair and less trustworthy than traditional human decision-making. Fairness emerged as a key mechanism that influenced organisational perceptions and behavioural intentions. Study 2 employed a between-subjects experiment (N = 125) on reactions to rejection decisions. Justice perceptions did not significantly differ across evaluator types. However, cognitive evaluations and affective discomfort influenced responses. Serial mediation analyses showed that these perceptions affected interactional justice, which in turn shaped organisational perceptions and behavioural intentions. Study 3 used an inductive qualitative design (N = 147) to explore why AI is perceived less favourably than human evaluators. Participants acknowledged AI’s efficiency and objectivity but raised concerns about contextualisation, humanness, and transparency. Anthropocentric bias and mind perception contributed to lower trust and fairness. Study 4 (N = 384) examined post-hoc explainability in a pre-registered experiment that manipulated evaluator type and rejection message format. Counterfactual explanations improved fairness and trust perceptions, alongside cognitive evaluations and affective discomfort, particularly for hybrid and AI evaluators. Still, residual algorithm aversion persisted. Overall, applicants evaluate AI-enabled selection less favourably than traditional human hiring. Nevertheless, explainability and human involvement can reduce algorithm aversion and support more acceptable integration of AI into personnel selection.
