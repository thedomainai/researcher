# Conversational AI and the Temporal Ecology of Self-Reflection

**著者**: Dana Kvietkute
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 行動経済学

## Abstract

Self-reflection has historically depended on temporal transformations that no one had to arrange: memory faded and reworked what it returned, the standpoint from which the past was met shifted, and other people changed alongside the person reflecting. Conversational AI separates these transformations from the person's own. Transcripts and selective system memory may change technically, yet they persist across the interval without undergoing it as lived time, and a responsive interlocutor answers without the relational time of a history shared with the person. Drawing on cultural psychology's premise that psychological processes unfold in irreversible time and on the phenomenology of time-consciousness, the paper describes the conditions of self-reflection as a temporal ecology of memory, standpoint, other people, and artefacts, related through their differing movement across time. Externalising part of one's past into conversational systems converts regulation that once proceeded tacitly into time work: deciding whether, when, in what order, and how often to return to earlier formulations. Such work draws on attention and judgement; where it is left to defaults, it is performed by those who design and govern the systems. A system that remembers and answers may also lend earlier formulations a witness's authority. The freedom to change, once protected by the coupling of fading and inaccessibility, thus comes to depend on regulatory work distributed between persons and infrastructures, and agency over one's own past, as experienced and as exercised, becomes partly a matter of governance. The paper derives empirical questions and implications for society, wellbeing, education, and personal development.
