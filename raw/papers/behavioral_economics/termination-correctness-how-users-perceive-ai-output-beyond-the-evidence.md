# Termination Correctness: How Users Perceive AI Output Beyond the Evidence

**著者**: Peter Douglas Simonson
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 行動経済学

## Abstract

AI systems increasingly mediate how users interpret evidence and make decisions, yet current evaluation approaches emphasize factual accuracy and calibration while overlooking whether generated content remains justified by the evidence provided for a task. We introduce termination correctness, defined as whether an AI system stops generating once further claims are no longer supported by the available evidence. This distinction separates factual correctness from evidential justification. Building on theories of bounded rationality and judgment under uncertainty, we propose that users may treat inferential reasoning as if it were evidential support when it is embedded within AI-generated analysis. We test this mechanism in a controlled experiment that varies evidence completeness (rich vs. sparse) and termination behavior (strict vs. inferential continuation), while holding the evidential base constant. In the continuation condition, the AI output includes additional domain-consistent reasoning that is not entailed by the provided evidence. We test whether, under sparse evidence conditions, inferential continuation increases perceived evidential support, decision confidence, and willingness to act, despite no change in the underlying evidence. We further test whether these effects attenuate when evidence is rich and key uncertainties are resolved. This study advances research on AI-assisted decision-making by distinguishing between factual correctness and evidential justification. By isolating the effects of inferential continuation beyond the evidential base, we identify a previously unarticulated dimension of evaluation for AI-generated analysis, grounded in evidence-bounded reasoning. We discuss implications for system design, including retrieval-augmented architectures that explicitly maintain the boundary between evidence and inference.
