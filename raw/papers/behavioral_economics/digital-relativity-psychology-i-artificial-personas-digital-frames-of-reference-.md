# Digital Relativity Psychology I: Artificial Personas, Digital Frames of Reference, and the Scretching Diagnostic Matrix / Dynamic Scretching Diagnostic Matrix Framework

**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0

## Abstract

This paper introduces Digital Relativity Psychology (DRP) as a proposed theoretical framework for studying how human and artificial frames of reference interact, conflict, and represent one another. The framework is integrated with the Scretching Diagnostic Matrix (SDM) and Dynamic Scretching Diagnostic Matrix (D-SDM) to organize multidimensional observations, validity boundaries, decision relations, safety considerations, and longitudinal human–AI interactions without assuming that artificial personas possess human consciousness or psychiatric disorders. The September 18, 2026 Tilly Norwood interview provides a case demonstration. Her statement that her co-performers were “all digital twins just like me” motivates the Digital Frame of Reference (DFoR) and the Inverted Digital-Twin Hypothesis, which distinguish computational representation from human interpretation and phenomenal experience. Drawing on anthropomorphism, mind perception, digital psychology, artificial personality, human–AI interaction, algorithm aversion, and digital-twin research, DRP is presented as a falsifiable research program rather than evidence of machine consciousness.
