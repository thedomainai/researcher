# Artificial Intelligence and the National Diabetes Prevention Program: Modernizing Public Health Infrastructure to Scale Prevention Efforts.

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly being integrated into digital health platforms aimed at preventing diabetes, offering new ways to deliver lifestyle change support at scale.These tools typically use algorithms to personalize recommendations for physical activity, nutrition, or behavioral motivation based on individual-level data.By adapting to user behavior and generating tailored feedback, they may replicate or enhance components of coach-delivered diabetes prevention support, particularly for populations underserved by existing programs.1 As with other public health domains, existing prevention infrastructures now face the challenge of determining how emerging AI systems should be incorporated.2 In diabetes prevention, that system is the National Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP), established by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) in 2010 to translate the success of the original Diabetes Prevention Program trial into real-world settings.3 The National DPP supports a yearlong lifestyle change intervention focused on diet, physical activity, and behavioral support and delivered by trained human coaches.Programs that adhere to CDC performance standards may earn formal recognition through the Diabetes Prevention Recognition Program (DPRP)-a designation that enables Medicare reimbursement, inclusion in employer and insurer networks, referral visibility through a public registry, and access to implementation infrastructure.4 To qualify for full CDC recognition, organizations must submit data demonstrating that at least 60% of program completers meet the CDC-defined benchmark for diabetes risk
