# AI Behavioral Design: A Psychological Framework for Designing Human–AI Interaction Around User Behavior, Cognitive Processes, and Individual Differences

**著者**: Roman Shaban
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 行動経済学

## Abstract

The growing integration of artificial intelligence into everyday life, education, professional activity, and decision-making creates a need for interaction systems that account not only for technological functionality but also for human psychological characteristics. Conventional approaches to AI design frequently emphasize model performance, usability, accuracy, efficiency, and user satisfaction. However, successful human–AI interaction also depends on motivation, cognitive limitations, behavioral habits, emotional responses, individual differences, trust calibration, and perceived autonomy. This article introduces AI Behavioral Design as an interdisciplinary approach to developing artificial intelligence interactions that are psychologically informed, behaviorally adaptive, and ethically constrained. It examines how principles from behavioral psychology, cognitive science, self-determination theory, human–computer interaction, and automation research can inform the design of AI systems responsive to differences in user capabilities, goals, expectations, and interaction patterns. The article proposes the Behaviorally Adaptive Human–AI Interaction Design (BAHAID) framework, comprising seven interconnected components: user-goal understanding, cognitive and behavioral context, motivational alignment, adaptive interaction, trust calibration, agency preservation, and longitudinal evaluation. The framework distinguishes behaviorally informed assistance from manipulative behavioral optimization and emphasizes that personalization should enhance users' understanding, competence, and decision-making capacity rather than exploit psychological vulnerabilities or maximize engagement as an end in itself. Practical design implications are examined across education, professional work, decision support, and everyday AI assistance. The article also presents empirically testable propositions, methodological considerations, and ethical safeguards for future research. The central argument is that effective AI behavioral design should optimize the quality of human AI cooperation while preserving the user's autonomy, psychological well-being, capacity for independent judgment, and ability to disengage
