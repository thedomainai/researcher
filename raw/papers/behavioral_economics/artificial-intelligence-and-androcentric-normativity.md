# Artificial Intelligence and Androcentric Normativity:

**著者**: Joshua Alexander González-Martín
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 行動経済学

## Abstract

This article analyzes how artificial intelligence reproduces and consolidates androcentric normativity through the affordances that emerge in the design, training, and use of algorithmic systems. Drawing on affordance theory, which understands affordances as invitations to action, we argue that technological artifacts not only enable practices but also embody implicit norms, shaping culturally situated modes of action. Although the literature on algorithmic bias has documented expressions of gender inequality, it often lacks a framework explaining how such biases become established as norms through user-system interaction. To address this gap, we propose a typology of androcentric normative affordances at three levels: (i) training data and corpora, (ii) design architectures and objectives, and (iii) interface configurations and usage practices. This typology is illustrated with mini-cases, such as Google’s machine translation, which has historically assigned caregiving professions to women. By conceptualizing androcentric bias as a set of normative affordances, the article offers a critical framework for diagnosing how ai systems normalize gender hierarchies and for rethinking their design toward more democratic sociotechnical practices.
