# Operationalising Generative AI in Subsurface Operations: A Low-Code Well Passport Skill Built by the End User

**著者**: M. Mohamed Al Hammadi, M. Ahmad Nazlan, D. Voilet, A. Al Raeesi, M. H. Hammadi
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 人的資源管理

## Abstract

Abstract Subsurface teams in mature, high-well-count fields routinely spend 30 to 60 minutes per well gathering data before any technical evaluation can start. Across an inventory of more than 2,000 wells, that custodial work pulls scarce petrophysical and engineering time away from interpretation and decision-making. This paper describes the deployment and early field validation of a suite of AI-driven workflow automations on a mature onshore carbonate field. A domain petrophysicist authored them, not a central digital team, and they run on an enterprise generative-AI platform that the organisation had already deployed. The automations are grouped into five operational domains: (1) well intelligence, covering automated retrieval of formation tops, casing configuration, H₂S and CO₂ classification, saturation history and pressure records, together with on-request assembly of well documents; (2) cement integrity, covering automated bond-log interpretation across all casing strings with isolation-risk flagging; (3) petrophysical quick-look, covering real-time logging-while-drilling (LWD) processing from any service provider on the asset, with composite-log and interpretation-software-ready output; (4) logging-program generation for saturation and production programs, annotated with live-condition safety information; and (5) intervention reporting, which consolidates multi-contractor performance into a single management view. In field use, well-data retrieval time fell by more than 90 percent. Logging-program preparation dropped from one to three hours per well to under five minutes. Real-time petrophysical evaluation is now produced automatically at each drilling cycle to support landing decisions. Every output follows the corporate documentation standard, which removes the variation between engineers, and every value can be traced back to the source file it came from. The framework recovers an estimated four or more engineering hours per well event, needs no new infrastructure or software licences, and works with any logging service provider active on the field. It offers a practical and immediately replicable route to AI adoption for operations-intensive subsurface teams.
