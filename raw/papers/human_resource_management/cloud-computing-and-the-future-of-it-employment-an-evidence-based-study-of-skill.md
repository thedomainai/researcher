# Cloud Computing and the Future of IT Employment: An Evidence-Based Study of Skills and Occupational Changes

**著者**: Km Sapna, Sushil Kumar Sharma
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 人的資源管理

## Abstract

Cloud platforms now host most new enterprise software, and this shift is altering both the tasks IT professionals perform and the skills employers look for. This paper triangulates four public sources: the Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025 (24,258 respondents who answered the cloud-development question), the Flexera State of the Cloud surveys (2021, 2023, 2025 and the 2026 release), the World Economic Forum Future of Jobs Report 2025 and the NASSCOM Strategic Review 2025. Two-proportion z-tests, a Wilcoxon signed-rank test and Spearman rank correlation were applied to 16 cloud, container, infrastructure-as-code and observability technologies. Professional developers reported higher use than learners for 12 of the 16 technologies (Wilcoxon W = 15, p = 0.011) and 9 of these differences survive a Bonferroni correction; learners led for Google Cloud and IBM Cloud, and Cloudflare and Heroku showed no difference. Professional-to-learner ratios were highest for Prometheus (3.49×), Terraform (3.14×), Datadog (2.79×) and Kubernetes (2.68×), which suggests that these skills are acquired mainly at work. In the Flexera editions examined, 75–78% of organisations named lack of resources or expertise as a challenge, 81% now use generative AI, and 63% of employers surveyed by the World Economic Forum see skill gaps as the main barrier to transformation. A role-transition framework that maps seven traditional IT roles to cloud-era roles is proposed, with implications for professionals, employers and academic institutions.
