# BOTS Unleashed: Exploring the Future of Autonomous System in Wells

**著者**: Pankaj L. Bhavnani, Gargi Chatterjee
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 人的資源管理

## Abstract

Abstract Wells operations are increasingly dependent on high-volume, cross-system, evidence-heavy workflows across planning, execution, reporting, cost control, integrity, and closeout. Many of these workflows remain manually intensive, requiring engineers and support teams to retrieve data, validate records, update registers, prepare reports, route exceptions, and retain evidence across multiple enterprise applications. This paper presents a practical automation model for Wells operations in which robotic process automation (RPA) BOTs perform deterministic, rules-based execution while artificial intelligence (AI) supports interpretation, pattern recognition, exception triage, and decision support. Beyond localized operational impact, RPA has evolved from a niche productivity tool into a core pillar of enterprise-wide digital transformation. Industry research indicates widespread adoption across enterprises and strong projected growth, with RPA increasingly embedded within hybrid automation ecosystems that combine Artificial Intelligence, Process mining, and Low-code platforms. This paper explores the deployment of BOTs in Wells operations as a practical manifestation of these broader trends, highlighting both operational value and the future trajectory toward hybrid automation-driven enterprise ecosystems. Industry evidence shows that RPA continues to grow strongly as an enterprise automation capability, with one public market forecast valuing the global RPA market at USD 10.13 billion in 2024 and projecting USD 53.39 billion by 2030. Public upstream case studies also demonstrate that AI and automation can deliver measurable performance improvements in drilling, seismic, production Optimization, and engineering cycle-time workflows. For Wells organizations, the implication is clear: BOTs and AI should be treated not as isolated digital tools but as governed components of the Wells management system that improve speed, consistency, assurance, and engineering focus when deployed against well-selected workflows.
