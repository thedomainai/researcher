# Bridging Reservoir Characterization and Production Performance Using AI-Driven Permeability in Carbonate Systems

**著者**: Sajith Girinathan, Ali Jibran, Mahmoud Ebeid, Ravi Verma, Rajiv Mani Tripathi
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: meta_analysis | **分野**: 人的資源管理

## Abstract

Abstract This paper presents field-based examples from structurally complex Cretaceous carbonate reservoirs in an onshore Abu Dhabi marginal field, illustrating how AI-enabled borehole image interpretation can deliver value beyond interpretation speed and automation. The study aims to demonstrate that high-resolution, AI-derived permeability provides a more realistic representation of reservoir flow capacity than conventional petrophysical permeability. By exhibiting stronger agreement with measured production and injection responses, the AI-derived permeability output offers a more reliable input for dynamic reservoir modelling, production forecasting, and history matching. The workflow combines borehole image logs with triple-combo data, drilling and mud logs, core information, and dynamic datasets from horizontal wells completed in heterogeneous, structurally complex Cretaceous carbonate intervals. Deep learning and computer vision techniques are used to segment borehole images, characterize diagenetic heterogeneity, detect fractures and faults, and generate a continuous, high-resolution permeability index. Finite element modelling and Darcy-based simulation are then applied to translate image-derived heterogeneity into permeability behaviour. The resulting AI-derived rock-property outputs are integrated with production and injection performance data to evaluate their applicability in completion design, flow interpretation, and reservoir modelling workflows. The case examples indicate that the higher-resolution AI-derived permeability index demonstrates a stronger and more consistent relationship with actual production and injection behaviour than conventional petrophysical permeability, which typically follows porosity trends and tends to average out fine-scale reservoir heterogeneity. Its positive correlation with PLT and injectivity profiles confirms its relevance as a more representative indicator of effective flow contribution. From a reservoir engineering standpoint, improved permeability definition is fundamental because dynamic models are highly sensitive to the spatial distribution of flow capacity. A high-resolution and geologically consistent permeability input reduces uncertainty, strengthens the physical basis for model calibration, and provides a more robust foundation for production forecasting and history matching compared with conventional lower-resolution permeability logs. The AI-derived permeability product also supports enhanced completion segmentation, stimulation planning, and water-control strategy by resolving heterogeneity that may otherwise remain obscured in conventional interpretations. In parallel, the workflow shortens interpretation turnaround from days or weeks to hours, enabling faster and better-informed decisions in structurally complex marginal-field developments. Overall, this study presents a field-validated AI-driven workflow that establishes a direct link between high-resolution, image-based permeability estimation and observed production performance in structurally complex carbonate reservoirs. The approach moves beyond conventional interpretation by capturing fine-scale geological heterogeneity and integrating it with dynamic flow response. The resulting permeability output offers a scalable and more dependable input for production forecasting, history matching, and reservoir development planning, representing an important advancement in the characterization and management of marginal carbonate fields.
