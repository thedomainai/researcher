## Abstract

Although the present study focused on a population-based sample of US military veterans, its implications extend to people and societies worldwide.The pathways through which neighborhood socioeconomic disadvantage may influence mental health -such as chronic stress, social disconnection, limited access to care -are universal and observed across high-, middle-and low-in come countries 8 .Identifying and addressing these contextual fac tors could help inform global strategies aimed at reducing the populationbased burden of common mental disorders.Incorporating neighborhood-level metrics such as the ADI into epidemiological research and clinical risk assessment represents a critical step toward advancing equitable, context-informed models of health care worldwide.Because neighborhood disadvantage is at least partly modifiable 9 , strategic investments in affordable housing, community safety, accessible evidence-based health care, and robust social support systems have potential to reduce mental health disparities and improve outcomes for individuals living in highly disadvantaged communities.
