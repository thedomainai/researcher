# Perspective Chapter: From Ledgers to Algorithms – A Historical and Contemporary Guide to Financial Statement Analysis

**著者**: Gideon Els
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 技術史

## Abstract

Financial statements are the primary language through which organizations account for their activities, yet the capacity to read them critically remains poorly distributed across all levels of economic life. This chapter provides a comprehensive, historically grounded guide to financial statement analysis, tracing the evolution of financial record-keeping from the clay tablets of ancient Mesopotamia and Luca Pacioli’s codification of double-entry bookkeeping in 1494, through the regulatory revolutions prompted by the Industrial Revolution and the 1929 Wall Street Crash, to the globally harmonized reporting frameworks, artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted analysis, and integrated sustainability disclosure that define contemporary practice. The chapter explains the structure, logic, and interrelationships of the three core financial statements using an original illustrative case (Meridian Trading Ltd) to anchor concepts in worked numerical examples. It then introduces the principal analytical techniques: ratio analysis, DuPont decomposition, trend and common-size analysis, industry benchmarking, and earnings quality assessment. Two contrasting case studies – the Enron collapse and Toyota’s decades-long financial discipline – demonstrate the real consequences of financial literacy and illiteracy at the organizational level, with explicit lessons for small businesses and individual readers. The chapter closes by examining the emerging landscape of integrated reporting, sustainability disclosure, blockchain-based record-keeping, and AI-driven financial analysis, arguing that greater automation demands greater, not lesser, human financial literacy. Employing a mixed qualitative-historical and illustrative quantitative approach, the chapter contributes to financial literacy education by demonstrating that understanding financial statements is not a technical specialism but a form of practical intelligence accessible to any motivated reader.
