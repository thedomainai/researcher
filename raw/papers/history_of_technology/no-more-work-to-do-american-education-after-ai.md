# No More Work To Do: American Education After AI

**著者**: Talbot S. Hook
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 技術史

## Abstract

For decades, American education has been motivated by training students for jobs, workplace productivity, and individual competition in a global economy. However, recent advances in artificial intelligence and robotics are beginning to destabilize these longstanding goals. As we outsource more of our labor, problem-solving, and cognition to these technologies, our justification for education as preparation for the workplace becomes less necessary and less believable. While many have written about employability in an era of AI, we as a society have not yet begun to explore the role of education in this new world. To that end, rather than simply attempting to identify which skills and jobs will remain resilient to automation, I ask what education might become when human work is no longer necessary. After tracing the history of American educational goals—religious, civic, and occupational—I propose three complementary possibilities for the future: 1) shifting emphasis from products and outcomes to processes and student experiences; 2) cultivating human excellence, growth, and mastery for their own sakes; and 3) developing taste, aesthetic discernment, and the capacity to use leisure and attention well. Together, these approaches reorient education away from instrumental economic outcomes toward a broader picture of what it means to live a meaningful, engaged, and distinctly human life. If education is to survive, it needs a new rationale for its existence—a rationale that can no longer be training for jobs.
