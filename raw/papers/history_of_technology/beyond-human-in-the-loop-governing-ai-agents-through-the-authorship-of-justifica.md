# Beyond human-in-the-loop: governing AI agents through the authorship of justification

**著者**: Matteo Cristofaro, Mie Augier, Alexis J. Bañón Gomis, Pier Luigi Giardino, Vijayakumar Parameswaran Unnithan
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 技術史

## Abstract

Purpose Artificial intelligence (AI) agents in organizations challenge a basic premise of decision governance, that keeping humans in the loop is sufficient to preserve meaningful control. AI agents increasingly frame problems, filter information, generate alternatives and produce the rationales that make recommendations appear reasonable, so humans may remain formally accountable while losing influence over how decisions are formed and justified. The central governance challenge is shifting from controlling autonomous actions to governing algorithmically mediated judgment. Design/methodology/approach This is a viewpoint article. The argument is conceptual, developed through a single running vignette, a manager approving an AI-generated workforce-reduction plan, with analogical anchors from safety engineering and clinical practice and foundations in the behavioral theory of decision-making and institutional theory. The vignette is a stylized composite. Findings Human-in-the-loop, human-on-the-loop and human-in-command locate control in a person's formal position leave open whether that person can reconstruct, challenge or alter the justification behind a decision. Each can therefore collapse into ratification, passive supervision and nominal authority. The paper develops epistemic governance to preserve human authorship of organizational justification. It specifies three enabling conditions (reconstructability, contestability, role separation), calibrates oversight intensity to irreversibility and answerability and introduces epistemic audits of organizational judgment rather than algorithmic output. Originality/value This viewpoint relocates the AI governance debate from decision loops to the authorship of justification, treating the reasoning behind a decision and the purpose it serves as two faces of one act of authorship and offers a practical mechanism (the epistemic audit) for testing whether human authorship is present before consequential decisions are approved.
