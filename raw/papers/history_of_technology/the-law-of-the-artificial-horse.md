# “The Law of the [Artificial] Horse”

**著者**: Paulius Astromskis
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 技術史

## Abstract

This chapter chronicles the genesis of the European Union&s;s Artificial Intelligence Act, framing the legislative journey through the lens of “The Law of the Horse” as a symbol for the adaptation of general legal principles to novel technologies. It traces the evolution of policy from the initial “sense of urgency” and the theoretical framework of “Trustworthy AI” to the hard law of the 2024 AI Act. Readers will gain a comprehensive understanding of the Act&s;s risk-based architecture, its extraterritorial “Brussels effect”, and the governance structures designed to enforce compliance. Beyond a historical overview, the text offers a sharp critique of the regulatory timeline. It exposes how a decade-long legislative process struggled to keep pace with exponential technological advancement, resulting in a reactive “patchwork” that nearly omitted critical safeguards for general-purpose AI models. By analysing the tension between innovation and fundamental rights, this chapter illustrates the inherent challenges of governing rapidly evolving risks and argues that future legal frameworks must abandon static rigidity for dynamic adaptability to avoid becoming obsolete before implementation.
