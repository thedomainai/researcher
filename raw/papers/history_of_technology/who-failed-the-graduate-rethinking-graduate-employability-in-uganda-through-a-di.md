# Who Failed the Graduate? Rethinking Graduate Employability in Uganda through a Dialectical and Historical Materialist Perspective

**著者**: Wasike David
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 技術史

## Abstract

Graduate employability has emerged as a critical issue in debates around higher education and the labour market in Uganda. This conceptual paper critically analyses the construct of graduate employability from a dialectical and historical-materialist perspective. It employs a critical narrative synthesis of relevant scholarly literature and secondary documentary evidence on the labour market, higher education and skills development systems in Uganda. It questions whether graduate unemployment and underemployment can be best explained by individual skill deficits or whether employability outcomes are also shaped by the productive structure of the economy, institutional practices, employer behaviour and demand in the labour market. The paper conceptualises employability, graduate capital, higher education and the world of work, skills mismatch, Competency-Based Education and Training (CBET), and the employment and higher education policy context in Uganda. It argues that employability is a relational construct, rather than an individual attribute. Individual skills gain employability value through their interaction with universities, curricula, employers, institutions regulating the labour market and the economy. It conceptualises and explains the contradiction of the co-existence of graduate unemployment and underemployment with reported skills shortages in the economy. This contradiction is explained by variations across occupations, sectors, locations, wages, experience requirements and productive capacities. While CBET may enhance labour capacity and competences, it cannot generate jobs or determine the volume and quality of labour demand. There is therefore a need for a complementary concept of productive absorption, which refers to an economy's capacity to generate and maintain productive employment in which graduates' skills, capacities and competences can be deployed, augmented and rewarded. The paper concludes that there is a need for a coordinated Skills-Production-Employment Nexus that integrates quality competency building with productive investments, enterprise development, innovation, governance of the labour market and decent job creation.
