# UNDERSTANDING BUSINESS AND THE MODERN ENTERPRISE

**著者**: Sixbert Sangwa, Placide Mutabazi
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 技術史

## Abstract

An enterprise is more than a legally registered firm, a production unit, or a collection of administrative departments. It is a historically situated arrangement for coordinating resources, relationships, decisions, and transactions to create and distribute economic and other forms of value under uncertainty. This chapter develops an integrative conceptual foundation for studying such arrangements across private companies, cooperatives, public enterprises, social ventures, nonprofit organizations, and informal economic units. It critically compares transaction-cost economics, property-rights and agency perspectives, resource-based explanations, stakeholder theory, institutional analysis, systems thinking, and business-model scholarship, showing what each illuminates and what each leaves unresolved. A structured synthesis of foundational literature and official secondary sources anchors the discussion in Rwanda, where the predominance of informal and microenterprise activity cautions against treating the large listed corporation as the universal model of business. An original enterprise-system map, ownership-accountability matrix, cross-functional order trace, financial illustration, and comparative enterprise cases translate theory into operational reasoning. The central argument is that viable enterprise performance emerges through contingent coordination among distinctive functions, rather than the optimization of any single function. Value creation, appropriation, distribution, and accountability must be distinguished analytically; reliable performance assessment therefore requires financial, operational, relational, social, and ecological evidence. The resulting framework is deliberately conceptual, with explicit boundary conditions and testable propositions, and establishes the vocabulary for the specialized chapters that follow.
