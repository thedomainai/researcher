# A short, selective history of work and jobs

**著者**: Ivan T. Robertson
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 技術史

## Abstract

This chapter provides a selective historical overview of work and jobs, tracing how occupational roles have evolved in response to technological, social and economic change. It reviews both continuity and disruption in the nature of work, from ancient societies structured around slavery and rigid hierarchies to the emergence of modern employment relationships. Particular attention is given to the transformative effects of technological innovation, including the Industrial Revolution, automation and more recent digital developments, showing how technology can eliminate, reshape and create jobs rather than simply destroy them. This chapter also explores resistance to change, from slave uprisings to Luddite machine-breaking, illustrating that conflict has long accompanied shifts in work organisation. Moving into the 20th century, it examines the rise of scientific management and the growing recognition of social and psychological factors at work. This chapter concludes by placing contemporary concerns about wellbeing, job quality and happiness within this long historical trajectory, arguing that while work remains central to individual identity and society, its relationship with income, wellbeing and fulfilment is complex and evolving.
