# From Ibn Khaldūn to Artificial Intelligence: The Historical Construction of Worlds of Intelligibility Language, Cognitive Infrastructures and the Invisible Conditions of Thought

**著者**: 
**年**: 2026 | **被引用数**: 0
**タイプ**: primary | **分野**: 技術史

## Abstract

This article proposes a rethinking of intelligence based on worlds of intelligibility in which objects, relations and transformations become thinkable.It begins with an observation in Ibn Khaldūn: the difference between the Muqaddimah, produced in a context of documentary withdrawal and the historical volumes of the Kitāb al-ʿIbar invites us to consider the book and the library not as mere repositories of information, but as cognitive infrastructures that configure the conditions for the production of knowledge.This perspective leads to a distinction between two ideal-typical regimes of intelligibility: starting from relations to bring forth structures and objects or starting from objects to classify and interpret them.The analysis is then extended to the relational conception of meaning in Ibn Jinnī and al-Jurjānī and subsequently to the notions of the invisible in Merleau-Ponty and the historical conditions of knowledge in Foucault.Language, classification, institutions, archives and technologies thus appear as historically constituted infrastructures that determine not only what can be seen, named and thought, but also what can remain invisible, unnamed and unthought.The shift from the library to the algorithm reveals a qualitative transformation: the algorithm becomes a dynamic cognitive infrastructure capable of selecting, classifying, linking, predicting and generating.Its black-box dimension then raises a question of power.Finally, the article examines the mathematics of manifolds, categories and topos as avenues that could open up new worlds of intelligibility, beyond the cognitive infrastructures of the book and contemporary architectures of artificial intelligence.This perspective shows that the boundaries between history, literature, mathematics and IT are not necessarily impermeable: they can be rethought as spaces of passage, dialogue and mutual transformation.
