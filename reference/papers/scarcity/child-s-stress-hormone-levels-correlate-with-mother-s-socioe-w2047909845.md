# Child’s stress hormone levels correlate with mother’s socioeconomic status and depressive state

**Authors**: SONIA J. LUPIEN, Suzanne King, Michael Joseph Meaney, Bruce S. McEwen | **Year**: 2000 | **Cited by**: 540 | **Kind**: article | **Relevance**: scarcity: supporting

## Abstract

Children with low socioeconomic status present significantly higher salivary cortisol levels than children with high socioeconomic status, and this socioeconomic status effect emerges as early as age 6, and a child's cortisol level is significantly correlated with his or her mother's extent of depressive symptomatology.
