# Frontal Lobe Metabolic Decreases with Sleep Deprivation not Totally Reversed by Recovery Sleep

**Authors**: Joseph C. Wu, J. Christian Gillin, Monte S. Buchsbaum, Phillip Chen, David B. Keator, Neetika Khosla Wu, Lynn A. Darnall, James H. Fallon, et al. | **Year**: 2006 | **Cited by**: 164 | **Kind**: article | **Relevance**: sleep: supporting

## Abstract

Sleep deprivation resulted in a significant decrease in relative metabolism of the frontal cortex, thalamus, and striatum and recovery sleep was found to have only a partial restorative effect on frontal lobe function with minimal reversal of subcortical deficits.
