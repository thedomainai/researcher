# Psychomotor Vigilance Performance: Neurocognitive Assay Sensitive to Sleep Loss

**Authors**: Jillian Dorrian, Naomi L. Rogers, David F. Dinges | **Year**: 2004 | **Cited by**: 373 | **Kind**: book | **Relevance**: sleep: supporting

## Abstract

This chapter reviews one neu-robehavioral assay that has met these criteria—the psychomotor vigilance task. To quantify the magnitude of such changes, as well as to track the temporal profile in neurobehavioral degradation as sleep loss accumulates, a wide array of neurocognitive assays have been used. The resulting neurocognitive performance measures provide an index of the degree of functional impairment present in an individual, and they have been used to answer both applied and theoretical questions about the nature of neurobehavioral capability in healthy people deprived of sleep. A prefrontal cortex-related function that has received particular study in the scientific literature is working memory. The lapse hypothesis marked a significant, conceptually consolidating advance in theoretical approaches to the effects of sleep deprivation on cognitive performance. The lapse hypothesis explicitly predicted that responses between lapses would be normal, which has not proven to be the case.
